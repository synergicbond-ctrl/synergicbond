21.2 Williamson ether synthesis

R–O−Na+ + R′–X → R–O–R′ + NaX. It is an SN2 reaction: the alkoxide attacks the alkyl halide from the back with inversion at R′. Because alkoxides are also strong bases, the reaction competes with E2 elimination. Rule of the Williamson synthesis: the halide (or tosylate) must be methyl or primary (or secondary only with a weakly basic nucleophile, e.g. phenoxide); the alkoxide may be primary, secondary, tertiary or aryl. Place the more hindered alkyl group on the alkoxide side.

Intramolecular Williamson (cyclic ethers): a halohydrin with base closes a ring. The rate depends on ring size: 3 (epoxide) and 5 (THF) fastest, then 6, while 4 and 7 are slow, because of strain and entropy. 2-Chloroethanol + NaOH → ethylene oxide; 4-chlorobutan-1-ol + NaOH → THF.
