Q40 [Medium] · Source: JEE Advanced-style paper, pages 18–19 (supplied images)

Which statements are CORRECT? (A) Cycloocta-1,3,5,7-tetraene is planar with alternating single and double bonds. (B) Tropylium bromide (C₇H₇Br) is an ionic compound, soluble in water, that gives a precipitate with aqueous AgNO₃. (C) Tropone (cyclohepta-2,4,6-trien-1-one) has an unusually large dipole moment because its dipolar resonance structure has aromatic character. (D) Cyclopentadienone is relatively unstable because its dipolar resonance structure contains an antiaromatic cyclopentadienyl cation.

Worked solution
  1. (A) False: cyclooctatetraene adopts a non-planar tub conformation; its double bonds are not part of a planar conjugated ring.
  2. (B) True: tropylium bromide is an ionic salt; Br⁻ precipitates AgBr with aqueous AgNO₃.
  3. (C) Unresolved, excluded from the key. The tropone dipole explanation was not found in any opened textbook, so it is not counted either way.
  4. (D) True: the dipolar form of cyclopentadienone has a 4π cyclopentadienyl cation, which is antiaromatic.

Answer: B, D (C unresolved and excluded from the key)

Q41 [Medium] · Source: JEE Advanced-style paper, pages 18–19 (supplied images)

Which statements are CORRECT? (A) The C2–C3 single bond in propene is shorter than the C–C bond in propane, partly because hyperconjugation gives it partial double-bond character. (B) The magnitude of the heat of hydrogenation of trans-but-2-ene is greater than that of but-1-ene. (C) Like the electromeric effect, hyperconjugation is a temporary effect that operates only in the presence of an attacking reagent. (D) Attached to a benzene ring, the order of electron release by hyperconjugation is CH₃⁻ > CH₃CH₂⁻ > (CH₃)₂CH⁻ > (CH₃)₃C⁻.

Worked solution
  1. (A) True: the sp²–sp³ bond is shorter than sp³–sp³ and conjugation/hyperconjugation adds partial double-bond character (standard qualitative account).
  2. (B) False: Ref. [2] (9th ed.) gives ΔH°(hyd) −125.9 kJ/mol for but-1-ene and −114.6 kJ/mol for trans-but-2-ene; the trans isomer releases less heat, so its magnitude is smaller.
  3. (C) False: hyperconjugation is described as an overlap of a filled σ orbital with an empty or partly filled p orbital (Ref. [2], pp. 222, 311). It needs no attacking reagent and is present in the ground state; the temporary effect is the electromeric effect.
  4. (D) True in the usual sense: the electron-release order by hyperconjugation follows the number of α-H, CH₃ (3) > CH₃CH₂ (2) > (CH₃)₂CH (1) > (CH₃)₃C (0).

Answer: A, D

Q42 [Hard] · Source: JEE Advanced-style paper, pages 18–19 (supplied images)

Number of aromatic species among (i)–(xii) (each structure drawn; ring sizes: (i) 9-membered anion, (ii) pentalene-type 5–5 fused, (iii) cycloheptatrienyl anion, (iv) pyridine, (v) thiophene, (vi) cyclooctatetraene, (vii) cyclopentadiene (CH₂), (viii) azulene, (ix) cyclobutadiene dication (2+), (x) 2H-pyran (CH₂), (xi) [12]annulene, (xii) cyclopropenyl anion).

Worked solution
  1. Huckel count, planarity and sp³ check for each: (i) 10π cyclononatetraenyl anion — listed as a 10π aromatic system (Ref. [2], p. 817). (ii) 8π: anti-aromatic. (iii) 8π (planar): anti-aromatic. (iv) 6π: aromatic. (v) 6π: aromatic. (vi) 8π, non-planar: non-aromatic. (vii) sp³ CH₂: non-aromatic. (viii) azulene 10π: aromatic. (ix) 2π cyclobutadiene dication: aromatic. (x) sp³ CH₂: non-aromatic. (xi) 12π, non-planar: non-aromatic. (xii) 4π cyclopropenyl anion: anti-aromatic.
  2. Aromatic: (i), (iv), (v), (viii), (ix) = 5. (ix) follows from the 2π count; the dication itself is not in the opened books.

Answer: 5

Q43 [Hard] · Source: JEE Advanced-style paper, pages 18–19 (supplied images)

Let a = Kekulé structures of tetracene, b = Kekulé structures of triphenylene, c = Kekulé structures of triphenylene with no C=C in the central ring. Find a + b + c.

Worked solution
  1. Enumerated all perfect matchings of the sp² carbon graph (own script on RDKit-parsed bonds): tetracene has 5 (a = 5); triphenylene has 9 (b = 9).
  2. Among the 9 triphenylene structures, exactly one has no double bond between two central-ring carbons (c = 1).
  3. a + b + c = 5 + 9 + 1 = 15.

Answer: 15

Q44 [Medium] · Source: JEE Advanced-style paper, pages 18–19 (supplied images)

P = number of groups that exert a +R/+M effect; Q = number exerting a −R/−M effect (attached to benzene). Find 10P + Q.

Worked solution
  1. +M (donors): –OCOCH₃, –SCH₃, –NHCOCH₃, –O⁻, –I (weak +M, halogens), –N(CH₃)₂ → P = 6.
  2. −M (acceptors): –CONH₂, –COOCH₃, –SO₂CH₃, –B(CH₃)₂ (empty p-orbital), –C≡N → Q = 5.
  3. No resonance effect: –NH₃⁺, –CF₃, –CH₂OH, –CH₂NH₂ (inductive or σ only).
  4. 10P + Q = 60 + 5 = 65. The boron entry counts as −M because of its empty p-orbital; this is the one judgement in the answer.

Answer: 65

Q45 [Medium] · Source: JEE Advanced-style paper, pages 18–19 (supplied images)

x = α-H atoms available for hyperconjugation with the C=C in 2,4-dimethylpent-2-ene; y = same for 1-methylcyclohex-1-ene. Find x + y.

Worked solution
  1. 2,4-dimethylpent-2-ene, CH₃–C(CH₃)=CH–CH(CH₃)₂: α-carbons are the two CH₃ on C-2 (3 + 3 H) and C-4 (1 H): x = 7.
  2. 1-methylcyclohex-1-ene: α-carbons are the CH₃ on C-1 (3 H), ring C-6 (2 H) and ring C-3 (2 H, attached to C-2): y = 7.
  3. x + y = 14.

Answer: 14

Q46 [Medium] · Source: JEE Advanced-style paper, pages 18–19 (supplied images)

Groups on benzene: –F, –OH, –NH₂, –OCH₃, –NHCOCH₃, –NO₂, –CN, –CHO, –COOH, –N⁺(CH₃)₃, –CCl₃, –CH₃, –C(CH₃)₃, –O⁻. x = number with −I and +R; y = number with both −I and −R. Find 10x + y.

Worked solution
  1. −I and +R (donor with σ-withdrawal): –F, –OH, –NH₂, –OCH₃, –NHCOCH₃ → x = 5.
  2. −I and −R (acceptors): –NO₂, –CN, –CHO, –COOH → y = 4. (–N⁺(CH₃)₃ and –CCl₃ are −I only.)
  3. 10x + y = 50 + 4 = 54.

Answer: 54

Q47 [Medium] · Source: JEE Advanced-style paper, pages 18–19 (supplied images)

Enthalpies of hydrogenation (kJ mol⁻¹, 298 K): cyclohexene −120; cyclohexa-1,4-diene −240; cyclohexa-1,3-diene −232; benzene −208. E₁ = resonance energy of benzene relative to a hypothetical cyclohexatriene with three isolated double bonds; E₂ = conjugation stabilisation of cyclohexa-1,3-diene. Find E₁ − E₂.

Worked solution
  1. E₁ = 3(−120) − (−208) = −360 + 208 = 152 kJ mol⁻¹ (Ref. [2] 9e gives 151).
  2. E₂ = 2(−120) − (−232) = −240 + 232 = 8 kJ mol⁻¹ (the 1,4-diene, −240, is the isolated reference).
  3. E₁ − E₂ = 152 − 8 = 144 kJ mol⁻¹.

Answer: 144

Q48 [Hard (paragraph I)] · Source: JEE Advanced-style paper, pages 18–19 (supplied images)

Bond length varies linearly with bond order: C–N 147 pm (BO 1), 127 pm (BO 2); C–O 143 pm (BO 1), 121 pm (BO 2). The amide R–CO–NH₂ has two contributors (neutral and dipolar). If the observed C–N bond is 135.2 pm, what is the percentage weight of the dipolar contributor?

Worked solution
  1. Linear fit: C–N length = 147 − 20(BO − 1).
  2. In the dipolar form C–N has BO 2; weight w gives BO = 1 + w.
  3. 135.2 = 147 − 20w ⇒ w = 11.8/20 = 0.59 ⇒ 59 %.

Answer: 59

Q49 [Hard (paragraph I)] · Source: JEE Advanced-style paper, pages 18–19 (supplied images)

Using the previous question: (i) the predicted sum of the C–N bond lengths in the guanidinium ion [C(NH₂)₃]⁺; (ii) the predicted C–O bond length in the amide of the previous question. Find the sum (in pm).

Worked solution
  1. Guanidinium: three equivalent C–N bonds, total bond order 4/3 each ⇒ length = 147 − 20(1/3) = 140.33 pm; sum = 421.0 pm.
  2. Amide C–O: BO = 2(1 − w) + 1·w = 2 − 0.59 = 1.41; C–O length = 143 − 22(0.41) = 133.98 pm (linear fit between BO 1 and BO 2).
  3. Sum = 421.0 + 134.0 = ≈ 555 pm.

Answer: ≈ 555

Q50 [Hard (paragraph II)] · Source: JEE Advanced-style paper, pages 18–19 (supplied images)

Predicted dipole moment (D) of 1-chloro-3-nitrobenzene using group moments μ(Cl) = 1.70 D and μ(NO₂) = 4.00 D (both negative end on the substituent) and the 60° rule given (meta = 120° between outward axes).

Worked solution
  1. Both vectors point outward from the ring. Meta axes differ by 120°.
  2. μ² = 1.70² + 4.00² + 2(1.70)(4.00)cos 120° = 2.89 + 16.00 − 6.80 = 12.09.
  3. μ = √12.09 = 3.48 D.

Answer: 3.48 D

Q51 [Hard (paragraph II)] · Source: JEE Advanced-style paper, pages 18–19 (supplied images)

Predicted dipole moment (D) of 2-chlorotoluene using μ(Cl) = 1.70 D (outward) and μ(CH₃) = 0.40 D (negative end toward the ring, so it points inward).

Worked solution
  1. Ortho outward axes differ by 60°; the CH₃ vector is opposite to its outward axis, so the angle between the two vectors is 180° − 60° = 120°.
  2. μ² = 1.70² + 0.40² + 2(1.70)(0.40)cos 120° = 2.89 + 0.16 − 0.68 = 2.37.
  3. μ = √2.37 = 1.54 D.

Answer: 1.54 D
