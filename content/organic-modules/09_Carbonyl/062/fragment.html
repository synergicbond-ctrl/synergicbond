53. Enamines and imines: which pair forms them?

Enamine formation needs (i) a carbonyl compound with at least one α-H, (ii) a secondary amine (primary amines give imines because the carbinolamine can lose H from N; tertiary amines have no N–H and cannot form the neutral hemiaminal–iminium route), (iii) acid catalysis and removal of water. Cyclic amines (pyrrolidine, piperidine, morpholine) work best. Aromatic aldehydes with no α-H cannot form enamines.

Example 56 Main

Which pair gives an enamine?

Which combination gives an enamine as the stable isolable product: (A) PhCHO + pyrrolidine (B) cyclohexanone + pyrrolidine, H+ (C) cyclohexanone + (CH3)3N (D) cyclohexanone + aniline (E) acetone + piperidine, H+

Show solution
  1. (B) and (E). (A) has no α-H (the iminium ion cannot lose an α-proton). (C) A tertiary amine has no N–H. (D) A primary aromatic amine gives an imine (Schiff base).
