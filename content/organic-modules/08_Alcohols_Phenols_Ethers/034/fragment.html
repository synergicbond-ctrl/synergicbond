5.2 Esterification (acylation of the alcohol)

Fischer esterification: RCOOH + R′OH ⇌ RCOOR′ + H2O (conc. H2SO4 or dry HCl catalyst; equilibrium constant about 4 for ethanol/acetic acid, so the ester is driven forward by removing water or using excess alcohol). Mechanism (PADPED): Protonation of the carbonyl O; Addition of the alcohol O to the carbonyl C to give a tetrahedral intermediate; Deprotonation; Protonation of one OH; Elimination of water; Deprotonation. The acyl–oxygen bond is broken: the alcohol oxygen is retained in the ester, shown by 18O labelling (R′–18OH gives RCO–18OR′). Therefore the configuration at the alcohol carbon is retained in esterification (and in ester hydrolysis).

Fischer esterification: ethyl ethanoate + water (equilibrium)

Rate order of alcohols in esterification: CH3OH > 1° > 2° > 3° (steric hindrance in the tetrahedral intermediate). Rate order of acids: HCOOH > CH3COOH > RCH2COOH > R2CHCOOH > R3CCOOH (steric effect in the acid; Newman’s rule of six for the atoms in the 6-position from the carbonyl O). Ortho-substituted benzoic acids esterify slowly (steric hindrance) but their esters hydrolyse also slowly. Faster, irreversible acylation: acid chlorides or anhydrides with a base (pyridine or Et3N), at room temperature: ROH + R′COCl → R′COOR + HCl (neutralised by pyridine). Acetic anhydride + ROH → ROCOCH3 + CH3COOH. With aqueous NaOH and an acid chloride: Schotten–Baumann (benzoylation; benzoate esters of phenols form this way). Inorganic esters: with conc. H2SO4 at 0 °C, ethanol gives ethyl hydrogen sulphate, C2H5OSO3H; with excess alcohol and heat, diethyl sulphate; with HNO3/H2SO4, glycerol gives glyceryl trinitrate (nitroglycerine); with POCl3 or phosphoric acid, alkyl phosphates; with CH3SO2Cl (MsCl) or TsCl and pyridine, sulphonate esters (see below).

Example 13 L2 Main

Retention in esterification

Optically active (R)-butan-2-ol is converted to its acetate with acetic anhydride/pyridine. The acetate is hydrolysed by aqueous NaOH. What is the configuration and optical purity of the recovered alcohol?

Show solution
  1. The C–O bond to the chiral carbon is never broken: in esterification the alcohol O attacks the carbonyl carbon; in alkaline hydrolysis OH− attacks the carbonyl carbon (BAc2) and the alkoxide leaves from the tetrahedral intermediate.
  2. Thus the stereocentre keeps its geometry throughout: the recovered alcohol is (R)-butan-2-ol with unchanged optical purity.
