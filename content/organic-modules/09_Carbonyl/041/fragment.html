35. Reduction of C=O to CH2

Two classical methods remove the oxygen completely. They are complementary: one needs acid, the other needs base, so the choice is dictated by what else is in the molecule. 35.1 Clemmensen reduction Zinc amalgam (Zn–Hg) with concentrated HCl, heated, reduces aldehydes and ketones (especially aryl alkyl ketones) to the hydrocarbon. The reaction occurs at the metal surface; it does not pass through the alcohol (the alcohol is not reduced under these conditions).

Zn–Hg, conc. HCl, Δ⟶
Acetophenone → ethylbenzene
Zn–Hg, conc. HCl, Δ⟶
Hexan-3-one → hexane

35.2 Wolff–Kishner reduction The carbonyl compound is converted into its hydrazone (N2H4), which is heated with strong base (KOH or potassium tert-butoxide) in a high-boiling solvent (ethylene glycol or diethylene glycol, 473 K, the Huang-Minlon modification) to give the alkane and nitrogen.

  • Step 1: R2C=O + N2H4 → R2C=N–NH2 (hydrazone) + H2O.
  • Step 2: base removes N–H; protonation at carbon gives R2CH–N=N−, the anion of a diazene.
  • Step 3: loss of N2 gives the carbanion R2CH−, which takes H+ from the solvent: R2CH2.
N₂H₄, KOH, ethylene glycol, 473 K⟶
Pentan-2-one → pentane. Driving force: formation of N₂ gas

Example 39 Main

Choose the reduction

Select Clemmensen or Wolff–Kishner (or neither) for converting C=O into CH2: (a) HOCH2CH2COCH3, (b) BrCH2CH2COCH3, (c) 4-nitroacetophenone, (d) CH3COCH2COOC2H5.

Show solution
  1. (a) Hot conc. HCl would convert the alcohol to the chloride or dehydrate it: use Wolff–Kishner.
  2. (b) The bromide would eliminate (or be displaced) in hot KOH: use Clemmensen.
  3. (c) Both methods reduce the nitro group, so neither is suitable; make ethylbenzene first and nitrate it afterwards.
  4. (d) The ester is sensitive to both hot acid and hot base: use the thioacetal/Raney Ni method.
