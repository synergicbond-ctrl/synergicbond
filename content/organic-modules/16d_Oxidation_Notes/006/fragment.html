7. Ozonolysis and permanganate cleavage of alkenes and alkynes

Ozone adds to C=C in a 1,3-dipolar cycloaddition to give the unstable primary ozonide, which fragments into a carbonyl compound and a carbonyl oxide and recombines to the secondary ozonide (a 1,2,4-trioxolane). This ozonide is not isolated; the work-up decides the products. Reductive work-up (dimethyl sulfide, Zn/AcOH or H2O, or PPh3) gives aldehydes and ketones. Oxidative work-up (H2O2) converts every aldehyde fragment into a carboxylic acid, while ketone fragments are unchanged. Hot, concentrated, acidic or alkaline KMnO4 gives the same pattern as oxidative work-up, and a terminal =CH2 is ultimately oxidised to CO2.

  1. Mark the double bond and break it; each carbon of the former C=C becomes a carbonyl carbon (C=O).
  2. Reductive work-up: R2C= gives a ketone R2C=O, RCH= gives an aldehyde RCHO, and =CH2 gives methanal.
  3. Oxidative work-up or hot KMnO4: RCH= gives RCOOH, R2C= still gives the ketone, and =CH2 gives CO2 (+ H2O).

Reactant:

Reagent: (i) O3, CH2Cl2, −78 °C; (ii) Me2S

Product:

A cyclic alkene opens to one chain carrying two carbonyl groups (6-oxoheptanal).

Reactant:

Reagent: (i) O3; (ii) H2O2

Product:

Oxidative work-up: adipic acid.

Counting rule: a molecule with n C=C bonds gives 2n carbonyl carbons, and an acyclic molecule gives n + 1 separate molecules; every ring that is opened by cleavage of one of its double bonds lowers the number of separate molecules by one, because a ring alkene yields a single chain bearing two carbonyl groups. Conjugated buta-1,3-diene (n = 2) gives methanal + ethanedial (glyoxal) + methanal, three molecules, the inner fragment retaining two carbonyl groups.

Alkynes are cleaved by O3 (then H2O) or by hot KMnO4 to two carboxylic acids, RC≡CR′ → RCOOH + R′COOH. A terminal alkyne gives RCOOH and CO2 because the ≡CH carbon is lost as carbon dioxide. Under carefully controlled neutral (buffered) KMnO4 conditions some internal alkynes can be stopped at the 1,2-diketone, but this is not general; unless a question says so, assume cleavage to acids.

Reactant:

Reagent: (i) O3; (ii) H2O (or hot KMnO4, H+)

Product:

Hex-3-yne is cleaved to two molecules of propanoic acid.

Key idea. Oxidative work-up never changes a ketone fragment, only an aldehyde fragment. To count products, count the double bonds, not the carbons.
Trap. Ozonolysis followed by NaBH4 gives alcohols, not carbonyl compounds; with Me2S the dimethyl sulfide is oxidised to DMSO and the fragments stay at the carbonyl stage.
