59. Favorskii rearrangement

Reagents: an α-halo ketone with at least one α′-hydrogen (the other α-carbon) + a base: NaOH (gives the carboxylic acid), NaOR (gives the ester), NH3 or RNH2 (gives the amide). The skeleton is rearranged; cyclic α-halo ketones contract by one carbon. Mechanism: (1) base removes the acidic α′-H (the side away from the halogen) giving an enolate; (2) the enolate carbon displaces the halide intramolecularly (SN2) to give a strained cyclopropanone; (3) the nucleophile (RO−, HO−) adds to the cyclopropanone C=O; (4) the three-membered ring opens at the bond that gives the more stable carbanion (usually the less substituted side), and protonation gives the product.

Intermediate: bicyclic cyclopropanone (bicyclo[3.1.0]hexan-6-one)
+ step 3–4: CH₃O⁻ adds, ring opens⟶

Trap Without an α′-hydrogen (α-halo ketone R3C–CO–CHXR) the cyclopropanone route is blocked and the quasi-Favorskii (semibenzilic) mechanism operates instead. For JEE: "α-halo ketone + alkoxide → ester with ring contraction" is the signature; 2-chlorocyclohexanone + NaOEt → ethyl cyclopentanecarboxylate; with NaOH, cyclopentanecarboxylic acid.

Ring contraction

Predict the product when (a) 2-bromocyclopentanone and (b) 2-chlorocycloheptanone are treated with sodium ethoxide.

Show solution
  1. (a) Cyclopentanone ring (5) contracts to cyclobutane: ethyl cyclobutanecarboxylate. (b) Seven → six: ethyl cyclohexanecarboxylate. The carbonyl carbon becomes the exocyclic ester carbon; the ring loses one carbon.
