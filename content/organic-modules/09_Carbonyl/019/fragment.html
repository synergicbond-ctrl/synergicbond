15. Reactivity order and the factors behind it

15.1 Steric effect Going from sp2 (angle 120°) to sp3 (angle 109.5°) pushes the substituents on carbon closer together. In formaldehyde they are two H atoms; in an aldehyde one H and one R; in a ketone two R groups. The bigger the groups, the more they crowd the new tetrahedral centre. 15.2 Electronic effect Alkyl groups donate electrons (+I effect, hyperconjugation) towards the carbonyl carbon, lowering its δ+ and stabilising the reactant. Aryl groups conjugate with C=O (+M from the ring, in effect), and again stabilise the reactant. Electron-withdrawing groups (–CCl3, –CF3, –NO2 on an aryl ring) destabilise the carbonyl and raise its electrophilicity.

Formula Reactivity towards nucleophiles: HCHO > CH3CHO > RCHO > C6H5CHO > CH3COCH3 > RCOR′ > C6H5COCH3 > C6H5COC6H5
Aldehydes are more reactive than ketones for both reasons: one fewer alkyl group (less donation) and less crowding.
15.3 Hydration equilibrium as a measure of electrophilicity
The reaction C=O + H2O ⇌ C(OH)2 is a clean measure of how electrophilic a carbonyl is, because water is a small nucleophile (the steric effect of Nu is small).

The numbers span nine powers of ten and show the reactivity order exactly. Hydrates of ordinary aldehydes cannot be isolated, because on removal of water the equilibrium moves back. Chloral hydrate and ninhydrin (indane-1,2,3-trione hydrate) are stable because the carbonyl carbon is flanked by strongly electron-withdrawing groups. Ketones that carry electron-withdrawing neighbours (1,2-diketones, α-keto acids, trifluoromethyl ketones) hydrate readily.

Trap Do not use "the hydrate is unstable so no addition happens". Hydration happens in water for every aldehyde and ketone; it is just reversible and, for most ketones, unfavourable. Evidence: acetone dissolved in H218O exchanges its oxygen for 18O, which proves that hydrate forms and loses water in either direction.
15.4 Effect of ring substituents in aryl carbonyls
In ArCHO an electron-donating substituent (p-OCH3, p-NMe2, p-OH) decreases the carbon's electrophilicity by +M donation into C=O; an electron-withdrawing one (p-NO2, p-CN, p-Cl) increases it.

15.5 Ring size: I-strain In cyclic ketones, the change sp2 → sp3 on addition alters angle and torsional strain. Cyclohexanone adds HCN much more readily than cyclopentanone or cycloheptanone (which like to keep their sp2 carbon in the ring); H. C. Brown called this I-strain (internal strain). Cyclopropanone and cyclobutanone, on the other hand, add nucleophiles very readily because the angle strain is relieved on becoming sp3 (sp3 angle 109.5° is closer to the ring angle). Practical rule: *small rings (3, 4) and six-membered rings are reactive; five- and seven-membered rings are less reactive towards addition.* 15.6 Steric effect of the nucleophile A bulky nucleophile (t-BuMgCl, a large amine) adds more slowly even to an unhindered carbonyl. A hindered ketone such as di-tert-butyl ketone or 2,2,4,4-tetramethylpentan-3-one adds almost nothing except the smallest nucleophiles (hydride, CN−); adding t-BuMgCl may give reduction (hydride transfer from the Grignard β-C–H) or enolisation instead of addition.

Example 10 Main

Reactivity order

Arrange in decreasing order of reactivity towards HCN: (a) CH3CHO, CH3COCH3, HCHO, (CH3)3CCOC(CH3)3; (b) benzaldehyde, p-nitrobenzaldehyde, p-tolualdehyde; (c) acetaldehyde, chloral, propanal.

Show solution
  1. (a) Methanal has two H on carbon (smallest, no donors): HCHO > CH3CHO > CH3COCH3 > (CH3)3CCOC(CH3)3 (extremely hindered).
  2. (b) EWG raises, EDG lowers electrophilicity: p-nitrobenzaldehyde > benzaldehyde > p-tolualdehyde.
  3. (c) CCl3 is strongly −I: chloral > acetaldehyde > propanal (the extra CH2 is more electron-donating and slightly larger).
Example 11 Main

Why is propanone less reactive than propanal?

Give two reasons.

Show solution
  1. (i) Electronic: two electron-donating CH3 groups reduce the positive character of the carbonyl carbon more than one CH3 and one H. (ii) Steric: the tetrahedral addition product of the ketone has two alkyl groups plus Nu and O on one carbon, which is more crowded than the aldehyde product. Both effects slow the addition and place the ketone equilibrium less in favour of the adduct.
