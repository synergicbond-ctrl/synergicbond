9A. Solubility classification: the first chemical test

Solubility in a fixed series of solvents places an unknown compound in a class before any specific test is applied. The scheme below is the standard laboratory classification (solvents tried in the order shown; each solvent is tested on a fresh portion).

Solubility classes of organic compounds
ClassSoluble inUsual membersNext tests
S (water soluble)Water (≤ about C5 polar)Low-mass alcohols, acids, amines, aldehydes, ketones, amidesTest the solution with litmus: acid, base or neutral
A1 (strong acids)Water-insoluble but soluble in aq. NaOH and NaHCO3Carboxylic acids, sulphonic acids, 2,4-dinitrophenol, picric acidEffervescence with NaHCO3; neutral FeCl3
A2 (weak acids)NaOH but not NaHCO3Phenols, enols, imides, some nitroalkanes, thiolsFeCl3, Br2 water, coupling
B (bases)Dilute HClAliphatic and aromatic aminesHinsberg, carbylamine, HNO2
N (neutral, O/N/unsaturated)Concentrated H2SO4 onlyAlcohols, aldehydes, ketones, esters, ethers, alkenes, alkynes, amides, nitro compounds2,4-DNP, Tollens', Lucas, Br2/CCl4, KMnO4
I (inert)None (insoluble even in conc. H2SO4)Alkanes, haloalkanes, aryl halides, simple arenesBeilstein/Lassaigne for halogens
IMP Why dissolution in an aqueous reagent means a chemical change: NaOH, NaHCO3 and HCl dissolve compounds by turning them into salts (RCOO−Na+, ArO−Na+, RNH3+Cl−), which are ionic and water-soluble; concentrated H2SO4 dissolves compounds that can be protonated on O, N or a π bond (oxonium or carbocation formation) and fails to dissolve alkanes, alkyl halides and arenes.
Example 15 Main

Using the solubility scheme

An unknown insoluble in water dissolves in NaOH but not in NaHCO3, and gives violet colour with neutral FeCl3. A second unknown is insoluble in water, NaOH and HCl but dissolves in conc. H2SO4 and gives an orange ppt with 2,4-DNP. Assign each class and group.

Show solution
  1. First: soluble in NaOH only ⇒ weak acid (A2); violet FeCl3 ⇒ phenol.
  2. Second: neutral, dissolves in conc. H2SO4 (N class) and carbonyl test positive ⇒ an aldehyde or ketone.
