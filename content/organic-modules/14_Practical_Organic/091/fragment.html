Calculate the degree of unsaturation of (a) C5H10, (b) C6H6, (c) C4H9Br, (d) C3H7NO2, (e) C7H5Cl3.

Show answer
  1. (a) (12 − 10)/2 = 1; (b) (14 − 6)/2 = 4; (c) (10 − 10)/2 = 0 (one X counts as H: C4H9Br ≡ C4H10); (d) (6 + 2 + 1 − 7)/2 = 1; (e) (16 − 5 − 3)/2 = 4.

Practice 153 Application

Why is the C≡C stretch of but-2-yne absent (or very weak) in the IR spectrum, while that of but-1-yne is observed?

Show answer
  1. A vibration is IR-active only if it changes the dipole moment. In symmetrical but-2-yne the stretch causes no net change of dipole. In but-1-yne the molecule is unsymmetrical, so the stretch changes the dipole (and the ≡C–H at ~3300 is also present).

Answer: no dipole change

Practice 155 Application

The O–H stretch of a dilute solution of ethanol in CCl4 appears as a sharp band at 3640 cm-1, while the pure liquid shows a broad band at 3350. Explain.

Show answer
  1. In dilute solution molecules are isolated, so O–H is free (3640, sharp). In the pure liquid O–H···O hydrogen bonds weaken the O–H bond and give a range of bond strengths, so the band shifts to lower ν̃ and broadens.

Answer: H-bonding

Practice 158 Application

Why does an organic compound with an odd molecular mass in its mass spectrum contain an odd number of nitrogen atoms?

Show answer
  1. Atoms of C, O, S have even mass numbers (12, 16, 32) and N is 14, so the parity of the molar mass equals the parity of the number of H (and halogen, odd mass) atoms. A neutral molecule needs an even total valence, so (number of H + X + N) must be even. Hence if N is odd, (H + X) is odd and the mass is odd; if N is even or zero, the mass is even. Examples: CH3NH2 = 31, aniline = 93, benzene = 78.

Answer: nitrogen rule

Practice 167 Application

In the ¹H NMR spectrum of ethanol obtained from a very dry sample, the OH signal is a triplet; in an ordinary sample it is a singlet. Explain.

Show answer
  1. In dry ethanol proton exchange is slow, so the OH proton couples with the two CH2 protons (n + 1 = 3, triplet); with traces of acid or water, rapid exchange averages out the coupling and the OH appears as a singlet (and shows no splitting to CH2).

Answer: proton exchange
