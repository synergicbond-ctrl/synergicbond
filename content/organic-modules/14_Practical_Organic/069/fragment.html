24. Putting it all together: a systematic route

A structure-deduction problem uses the same five steps every time.

  • Compute the DBE from the molecular formula. It limits the possible units (benzene ring needs 4; C=O needs 1).
  • From elemental tests decide which elements are present (N, S, X); from solubility decide acid/base/neutral.
  • From functional-group tests decide the group (Tollens, 2,4-DNP, FeCl3, NaHCO3, Lucas, carbylamine, Hinsberg).
  • Use IR (C=O, O–H, N–H) to confirm, and the ¹H NMR counts/shifts/splitting to assemble the carbon skeleton.
  • Check that the structure has the right formula and DBE, and that all observations (including negative ones) are satisfied.
Example 52 Adv

Full identification

A liquid X, C8H8O, (a) gives an orange precipitate with 2,4-DNP, (b) gives no silver mirror with Tollens', (c) gives a yellow precipitate with I2/NaOH, and (d) on vigorous oxidation gives benzoic acid. ¹H NMR: δ 2.6 (s, 3H), 7.4-8.0 (m, 5H); IR 1685 cm-1. Identify X and the IR shift.

Show solution
  1. DBE = (16 + 2 − 8)/2 = 5 ⇒ benzene ring (4) + C=O (1).
  2. 2,4-DNP: carbonyl; Tollens' negative: ketone; iodoform: methyl ketone CH3CO–. Oxidation to benzoic acid: the group on the ring is a single carbon-based substituent. So acetophenone, C6H5COCH3.
  3. NMR: CH3 singlet at 2.6 (on C=O, with a slight extra deshielding by the aryl ring), 5 aromatic H as a multiplet.
  4. IR: C=O at 1685 rather than 1715 because the carbonyl is conjugated with the ring.
Example 53 Adv

Another puzzle

Compound Y (C7H6O2) dissolves in aq. NaHCO3 with effervescence and gives a buff precipitate with neutral FeCl3. Its ¹H NMR shows δ 12.0 (br s, 1H) and 7.4-8.2 (m, 5H). Identify Y; why does it not give a violet colour?

Show solution
  1. DBE = (14 + 2 − 6)/2 = 5. NaHCO3 effervescence ⇒ –COOH; so Y = benzoic acid (benzene ring 4 + C=O 1). The NMR: COOH proton at 12, 5 aromatic H.
  2. A violet colour arises from a free phenolic –OH (as in the isomer salicylic C7H6O3); benzoic acid has no phenolic OH; its neutral FeCl3 test gives a buff (pinkish-buff) precipitate of ferric benzoate.
