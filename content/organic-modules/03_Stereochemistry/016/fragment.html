13. Meso compounds

13.1 What qualifies

A meso compound must have at least two stereocentres, be achiral, and (normally) contain an internal plane of symmetry or centre of inversion that exchanges two halves with opposite configurations (R on one half, S on the other). The ends must be identical, otherwise the halves cannot map onto each other.

Key idea Meso test: (1) are the two halves constitutionally identical? (2) is each centre the mirror image of its partner (R ↔ S)? If yes to both, meso. For a symmetric chain of n stereocentres: meso exists for any n ≥ 2; the count of meso forms is 2n/2−1 (n even) or 2(n−1)/2 (n odd).

13.2 Meso in rings

Shortcut In a 1,3-disubstituted ring, the cis isomer has a mirror plane through C-2 and C-5 (cyclohexane) so it is meso; the trans isomer has no mirror plane and exists as a pair. In 1,4-disubstituted cyclohexane both isomers have a mirror plane (through C-1 and C-4), so both are achiral: no optical isomers at all.
Example 40 Main

Meso or not?

Which are meso: (a) 2,3-dibromobutane (2R,3S); (b) (2R,3S)-2-bromo-3-chlorobutane; (c) cis-1,2-dimethylcyclopentane; (d) trans-1,2-dimethylcyclopentane; (e) (2R,4S)-2,4-dibromopentane?

Show solution
  1. (a) Yes: identical ends, centres R and S. (b) No: the ends are the same but the centres carry different halogens (Br versus Cl), so no mirror plane maps one centre onto the other: chiral. (c) Yes: cis with a mirror plane through the ring bond midpoint. (d) No: chiral (trans-1,2 has only C2). (e) Yes: identical ends, centres R and S.
