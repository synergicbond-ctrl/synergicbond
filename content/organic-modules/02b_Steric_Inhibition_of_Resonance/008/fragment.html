D. Resonance energy

Problem 89 [Application]

The heat of hydrogenation of benzene is 208 kJ mol⁻¹ and that of cyclohexene is 120 kJ mol⁻¹. Calculate the resonance energy of benzene.

Worked solution
  1. Hypothetical value for three isolated C=C bonds: 3 × 120 = 360 kJ mol⁻¹.
  2. Observed: 208 kJ mol⁻¹.

Answer: Resonance energy = 360 − 208 = 152 kJ mol⁻¹ (source value 151 kJ mol⁻¹; the difference is rounding in the enthalpies).

Problem 90 [Advanced]

Cyclohexa-1,3-diene conjugation: the hydrogenation enthalpy is about 232 kJ mol⁻¹ and cyclohexene is 120 kJ mol⁻¹ per C=C. Estimate the conjugation energy of the diene and say why it is much smaller than benzene’s resonance energy.

Worked solution
  1. Two isolated C=C bonds: 2 × 120 = 240 kJ mol⁻¹.
  2. Observed diene: 232 kJ mol⁻¹.

Answer: Conjugation energy ≈ 8 kJ mol⁻¹. A conjugated diene gains only a small delocalisation energy because its π-system is open-chain; benzene’s cyclic 6π system gains far more.
