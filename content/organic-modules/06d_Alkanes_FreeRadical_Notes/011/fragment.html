12. CFCs and ozone depletion; the peroxide effect

Chlorofluorocarbons such as CF2Cl2 are chemically inert near the ground and diffuse to the stratosphere. There, UV radiation cleaves the weakest bond, C–Cl, to give a chlorine atom, which catalyses the destruction of ozone in a radical chain. Natural ozone is formed by O2 + hν → 2 O followed by O + O2 → O3.

  1. Initiation: CF2Cl2 + hν → •CF2Cl + Cl•.
  2. Propagation 1: Cl• + O3 → ClO• + O2.
  3. Propagation 2: ClO• + O → Cl• + O2.
  4. Net: O3 + O → 2 O2. Cl• is regenerated, so it is a catalyst; one Cl atom can destroy about 105 ozone molecules before termination (for example, ClO• + NO2 → ClONO2).

Reagent: UV radiation (stratosphere)

Peroxide effect. HBr adds to an alkene in the presence of peroxides against Markovnikov's rule, by a radical chain: RO• abstracts H from HBr to give Br•; Br• adds to the less substituted carbon of the alkene to give the more stable (secondary or tertiary) carbon radical; this radical abstracts H from HBr to give the product and Br•. Using D(H–X) − D(C–H) = ΔH for the last step with D(C–H) of about 410 kJ mol−1: for HCl the step is endothermic by +21, for HBr exothermic by −44, for HI strongly exothermic but I• adds to the alkene too reluctantly (and I• readily recombines). Hence the effect is observed only for HBr.

Reactant:

Reagent: HBr, (C6H5COO)2

Product:

Anti-Markovnikov product, 1-bromopropane.

Key idea. In both examples a species (Cl• or Br•) is a chain carrier: it is consumed in one step and regenerated in the next. Count the steps of a cycle to find the number of propagation steps.
