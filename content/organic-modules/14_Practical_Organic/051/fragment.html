15. Estimation of carbon and hydrogen: Liebig's combustion method

A weighed amount of the pure organic compound is burned completely in a stream of pure dry oxygen over red-hot copper(II) oxide (which ensures complete oxidation of carbon to CO2 and of hydrogen to H2O). The products pass through two weighed absorption tubes in a fixed order:

  • First a U-tube of anhydrous calcium chloride (or Mg(ClO4)2), which absorbs water.
  • Then a bulb apparatus (Liebig bulbs) or tube containing concentrated KOH solution (or soda lime), which absorbs CO2.

The increases in mass of the two tubes are m(H2O) and m(CO2).

Formula CxHyOz + O2 → xCO2 + (y/2)H2O %C = (12/44)(wCO2/w) × 100 %H = (2/18)(wH2O/w) × 100
IMP The order matters: KOH absorbs both CO2 and water, so CaCl2 must come first. Also CaCl2 must not be placed after KOH (water vapour from the KOH solution). If the compound contains nitrogen, oxides of nitrogen form; a spiral of red-hot copper gauze reduces them to N2 so they do not enter the KOH. If it contains halogens, silver gauze retains the halogen; sulphur is retained by lead chromate (PbCrO4) as PbSO4.

Why the oxygen stream is purified: moisture or CO2 carried in with the oxygen would add to the mass of the absorption tubes and be credited to the compound, so the oxygen is first passed through KOH and a drying agent. Why the compound must be weighed in a boat and burnt slowly: to avoid incomplete combustion (soot, CO).

Example 24 Adv

Wrong order of absorbers

A compound has C 40.00 % and H 6.67 % by mass (CH2O). 0.300 g is burnt in a Liebig train, but the KOH bulbs are connected before the CaCl2 tube. Assuming each absorbent takes up everything it can, what %C and %H does the student report?

Show solution
  1. Aqueous KOH absorbs CO2 and water. CaCl2 then receives nothing.
  2. Mass of CO2 = 0.300 × 0.4000 × 44/12 = 0.4400 g; mass of H2O = 0.300 × 0.0667 × 18/2 = 0.1800 g; gain of KOH = 0.6200 g, attributed wholly to CO2.
  3. Reported %C = (12/44)(0.6200/0.300) × 100 = 56.4 % (true 40.0 %); reported %H = 0 (CaCl2 gains nothing).
Example 25 Main

Percentages from combustion data

0.150 g of an organic compound (containing C, H and O only) on combustion gave 0.330 g CO2 and 0.0600 g H2O. (a) Find the percentage of C, H and O. (b) Suggest the empirical formula.

Show solution
  1. %C = (12/44)(0.330/0.150) × 100 = 0.2727 × 2.200 × 100 = 60.0 %.
  2. %H = (2/18)(0.0600/0.150) × 100 = 0.1111 × 0.400 × 100 = 4.44 %.
  3. %O = 100 − 60.0 − 4.44 = 35.6 %.
  4. Relative moles: C 5.00; H 4.44; O 2.22. Divide by 2.22: 2.25 : 2 : 1 ⇒ ×4: 9 : 8 : 4. Empirical formula C9H8O4 (aspirin, M = 180).
Example 26 Adv

Hydrocarbon from combustion mass

0.2 g of a hydrocarbon gives 0.6286 g CO2. Its vapour density is 14 (relative to H2). Find the molecular formula.

Show solution
  1. Mass of C = 0.6286 × 12/44 = 0.1714 g; mass of H = 0.2 − 0.1714 = 0.0286 g.
  2. Moles: C 0.1714/12 = 0.01429; H 0.0286/1 = 0.0286: ratio 1 : 2. Empirical formula CH2 (14).
  3. M = 2 × 14 = 28 ⇒ n = 2, formula C2H4.
