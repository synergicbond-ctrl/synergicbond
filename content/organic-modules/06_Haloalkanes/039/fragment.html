38. A catalogue of carbocation rearrangements

Rearrangements are the single most common cause of a "wrong" product in SN1/E1 questions. A shift occurs if it converts a less stable cation into a more stable one (or relieves ring strain); the migrating group moves with its bonding pair to the adjacent positive carbon.

Ag+, H2O⟶
1,2-Hydride shift: 2° → 3° (3-methylbutan-2-yl bromide gives 2-methylbutan-2-ol).
HBr⟶
1,2-Methyl shift: 3,3-dimethylbutan-2-ol gives 2-bromo-2,3-dimethylbutane (via the 2,3-dimethylbut-2-yl cation).
HBr⟶
Neopentyl system: the primary cation is never formed; the methyl shifts as water leaves.
H2SO4, Δ⟶
Ring expansion: the cation next to a cyclobutane ring expands the ring (strain relief) to a cyclopentyl cation, which ends as 1-methylcyclopentene.
Trap A cation adjacent to a strained small ring (cyclopropyl- or cyclobutylcarbinyl) often expands the ring to relieve strain, even when the shift only converts a 2° cation into another 2° cation. Rearrangements are driven by stability, not by the number of carbons moved.
Rules of thumb for rearrangement:
> 1. Draw the first-formed cation. Ask: is a more stable cation one carbon away (neighbouring carbon with H or alkyl and the cation becomes 2° → 3°, or allylic/benzylic)?
> 2. Hydride shifts are faster than alkyl shifts (but 1,2-methyl shifts beat leaving a 2° cation when a 3° cation results).
> 3. Primary cations are not formed in SN1/E1; shifts occur as the leaving group departs (neopentyl).
> 4. If the base or nucleophile is strong and concentrated (E2, SN2), there is no cation and no rearrangement.
> 5. In an HX reaction with a 2° alcohol, expect a mix of rearranged and unrearranged halides; use SOCl2/PBr3 to avoid it.
Example 44 Adv

Rearrangement prediction

Predict the major product of 2-bromo-3,3-dimethylbutane with silver acetate in acetic acid.

Show solution
  1. Ag+ removes Br−: 2° cation (CH3)3C–CH+–CH3.
  2. 1,2-methyl shift gives the 3° cation (CH3)2C+–CH(CH3)2.
  3. Acetate captures it: 2,3-dimethylbutan-2-yl acetate (major). Elimination gives 2,3-dimethylbut-2-ene (minor).
Example 45 Adv

Rearrangement prediction

What alkene is formed on heating 3-methylbutan-2-ol with concentrated H2SO4?

Show solution
  1. Protonation, loss of water gives the 2° cation CH3CH+CH(CH3)2; hydride shift gives the 3° cation CH3CH2C+(CH3)2; loss of H+ (Zaitsev) gives 2-methylbut-2-ene (major), with 2-methylbut-1-ene and 3-methylbut-1-ene (minor, unrearranged) as by-products.
