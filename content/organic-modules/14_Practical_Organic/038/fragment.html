Iodoform test for acetophenone (a methyl ketone)
Why It Happens The α-hydrogens of CH3CO– are acidic, so enolate formation and α-halogenation repeat three times; the CI3 group is then a good leaving group (stabilised carbanion), and hydroxide attack on the carbonyl expels CI3−, giving CHI3 and the carboxylate. Ethanol and CH3CH(OH)R respond because hypoiodite first oxidises them to CH3CHO or CH3COR.
IMP Which compounds give the iodoform test? Those with CH3–CO–(C or H) or CH3–CH(OH)–(C or H) units: acetaldehyde (yes), ethanol (yes), acetone, butan-2-one, acetophenone, isopropyl alcohol (yes); formaldehyde, methanol, benzophenone, diethyl ketone, acetic acid, ethyl acetate, propan-1-ol (no).
Exam Trap Common errors: (1) benzaldehyde does NOT reduce Fehling's or Benedict's solution but DOES reduce Tollens'; (2) ketones do not respond to Tollens', but α-hydroxyketones (fructose) do, because they rearrange to aldoses under the basic conditions; (3) 2,4-DNP is a carbonyl test, not an aldehyde test; (4) acetic acid has CH3CO– but not the right oxidation level, so no CHI3; (5) acetaldehyde is the only aldehyde giving iodoform.
Compare & Distinguish Fehling's versus Tollens': Tollens' (silver(I) ammonia complex, basic) oxidises both aliphatic and aromatic aldehydes; Fehling's (copper(II) tartrate, strongly alkaline) is a milder oxidant and, empirically, oxidises aliphatic but not aromatic aldehydes.
Example 18 Main

Identification

Compound A (C3H6O) gives an orange precipitate with 2,4-DNP, a silver mirror with Tollens' reagent and a red precipitate with Fehling's solution but no yellow precipitate with I2/NaOH. Identify A and explain the last result.

Show solution
  1. The 2,4-DNP shows C=O; Tollens' and Fehling's show an aliphatic aldehyde: C3H6O = propanal CH3CH2CHO.
  2. No iodoform: propanal has no CH3CO– group (acetaldehyde is the only aldehyde that does).
  3. The isomer acetone also gives 2,4-DNP, but gives iodoform and no Tollens'/Fehling's reaction.
