12. Benzoin condensation

Reagents: 2 ArCHO, KCN or NaCN (catalytic) in aqueous ethanol (or the thiazolium carbene of thiamine). Product: benzoin, PhCH(OH)–C(=O)Ph (α-hydroxy ketone, 2-hydroxy-1,2-diphenylethanone). Mechanism (umpolung): (1) CN− adds to PhCHO to give the cyanohydrin alkoxide; (2) proton shift gives a carbanion stabilised by the nitrile and the phenyl ring: this is an acyl-anion equivalent (the carbonyl carbon has been turned from electrophile into nucleophile); (3) this carbanion attacks a second PhCHO; (4) proton transfer and loss of CN− give benzoin and regenerate the catalyst.

+ KCN, EtOH/H₂O⟶
Two benzaldehydes → benzoin (iso: no atoms are lost)
Cu²⁺ or HNO₃⟶
Benzoin → benzil (oxidation of the secondary alcohol)
Trap Benzoin requires aromatic aldehydes without strongly donating groups. p-(CH3)2N–C6H4CHO does not give a homobenzoin (the carbanion is too destabilised), but it does condense with benzaldehyde in a crossed benzoin. Aliphatic aldehydes (aldol competes) and formaldehyde do not work well with cyanide.
Example 20 Adv

Benzoin to benzilic acid

Starting from benzaldehyde, give a three-step route to benzilic acid, naming the reaction at each step.

Show solution
  1. 1) 2 PhCHO + KCN (cat.) → benzoin (benzoin condensation).
  2. 2) Benzoin + Cu(OAc)2/NH4NO3 or conc. HNO3 → benzil (oxidation of the CHOH group).
  3. 3) Benzil + KOH, then H3O+ → benzilic acid, (C6H5)2C(OH)COOH (benzilic acid rearrangement).
