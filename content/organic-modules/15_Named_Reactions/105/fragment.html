Block K. Tests and identification

Practice 215 Test

Distinguish propanal and propanone with one reagent.

Show answer
  1. Tollens' reagent (or Fehling's): propanal gives a silver mirror (red Cu2O); propanone does not.
Practice 216 Test

Distinguish pentan-2-one from pentan-3-one.

Show answer
  1. I2/NaOH: pentan-2-one (CH3CO–) gives yellow CHI3; pentan-3-one does not.
Practice 217 Test

Distinguish benzaldehyde from acetophenone (two tests).

Show answer
  1. Benzaldehyde gives Tollens' silver mirror; acetophenone does not. Acetophenone gives the iodoform test; benzaldehyde does not.
Practice 218 Test

Distinguish ethanol from methanol.

Show answer
  1. I2/NaOH: ethanol (oxidised to CH3CHO) gives yellow CHI3; methanol does not.
Practice 219 Test

Distinguish propan-1-ol from propan-2-ol (two tests).

Show answer
  1. Iodoform (I2/NaOH): propan-2-ol (CH3CH(OH)–) positive, propan-1-ol negative. Lucas: propan-2-ol turbid in ≈ 5 min; propan-1-ol no turbidity at room temperature.
Practice 220 Test

Distinguish aniline, N-methylaniline and N,N-dimethylaniline.

Show answer
  1. Hinsberg (PhSO2Cl, NaOH): aniline gives a sulfonamide that dissolves in NaOH; N-methylaniline gives an insoluble sulfonamide; N,N-dimethylaniline does not react. Also only aniline gives the carbylamine (isocyanide) test.
Practice 221 Test

Distinguish phenol from benzyl alcohol.

Show answer
  1. Neutral FeCl3: phenol gives a violet colour; benzyl alcohol does not. (Br2/H2O gives a white precipitate of 2,4,6-tribromophenol only with phenol.)
Practice 222 Test

Distinguish benzoic acid from phenol.

Show answer
  1. NaHCO3: benzoic acid (pKa 4.2) liberates CO2; phenol (pKa 10) does not.
Practice 223 Test

Distinguish methanoic acid from ethanoic acid.

Show answer
  1. Tollens' (or Fehling's): methanoic acid, which has an aldehyde-like H–C(=O)OH, reduces it; ethanoic acid does not.
Practice 224 Test

Distinguish glucose from fructose.

Show answer
  1. Br2/H2O: glucose (an aldose) is oxidised to gluconic acid and decolourises it; fructose (ketose) does not. Seliwanoff's reagent (resorcinol/HCl): fructose gives a rapid cherry-red colour. (Both reduce Tollens' in basic solution through enediol isomerisation.)
Practice 225 Test

Distinguish cyclohexene from cyclohexane.

Show answer
  1. Br2 in CCl4 (decolourised by cyclohexene) or cold dilute alkaline KMnO4 (pink → brown MnO2).
Practice 226 Test

Distinguish ethanal from methanal.

Show answer
  1. I2/NaOH: ethanal gives CHI3; methanal does not.
Practice 227 Test

How do the Lucas test and the Victor Meyer test classify butan-1-ol, butan-2-ol and 2-methylpropan-2-ol?

Show answer
  1. Lucas (298 K): butan-1-ol no turbidity; butan-2-ol turbid in ≈ 5 min; 2-methylpropan-2-ol immediately. Victor Meyer (NaOH colour): primary red, secondary blue, tertiary colourless.
Practice 228 Test

Distinguish butanal from butan-2-one.

Show answer
  1. Tollens' (or Fehling's): butanal reduces it, butanone does not. (Iodoform also separates them in the other direction: butanone is positive, butanal negative.)
Practice 229 Test

Distinguish ethylamine from diethylamine.

Show answer
  1. Carbylamine test (CHCl3/KOH): only ethylamine gives the foul-smelling isocyanide. Or Hinsberg: the ethylamine sulfonamide is soluble in NaOH; the diethylamine sulfonamide is insoluble.
Practice 230 Test

A compound C4H8O gives an orange precipitate with 2,4-DNP and a yellow precipitate with I2/NaOH, but no silver mirror with Tollens' reagent. Identify it.

Show answer
  1. C=O present (2,4-DNP); no CHO (Tollens negative) → ketone; CH3CO– (iodoform). C4H8O: butan-2-one, CH3COCH2CH3.
