22. Stereochemistry of substitution

22.1 The vocabulary: stereospecific, stereoselective

  • Stereoselective: a reaction that can give two or more stereoisomers but gives one (or one pair) predominantly.
  • Stereospecific: the stereochemistry of the reactant determines the stereochemistry of the product: different stereoisomers of the reactant give different stereoisomers of the product. Every stereospecific reaction is stereoselective, but not every stereoselective reaction is stereospecific.
  • Retention, inversion, racemisation: descriptions of what happens to the spatial arrangement at the reacting stereocentre. Whether the letter R/S changes also depends on the priorities of the groups before and after the reaction.

22.2 SN2: inversion (Walden inversion)

The nucleophile attacks from the side opposite the leaving group (backside attack), and the three other groups flip like an umbrella turned inside out in the wind. The spatial arrangement is inverted at the reacting carbon. The rate depends on both reactants (second order); it is fastest for methyl > primary > secondary, and impossible at a tertiary carbon.

Trap Inversion of arrangement does not always mean R → S. If the new group has a different priority rank than the leaving group, the letter can stay the same. Always rank the groups in the reactant and in the product.
Example 64 Main

Walden inversion

(S)-2-Bromooctane is treated with NaOH (SN2). Predict the configuration of octan-2-ol and say whether the optical rotation sign must change.

(S)-2-bromooctane (S) (R)-octan-2-ol (R)
Show solution
  1. Priorities are parallel (Br or OH first, then C6H13, then CH3, then H). Inversion changes the label: (R)-octan-2-ol.
  2. The sign of rotation is not predictable from the label: (R)-octan-2-ol happens to be (−) and (S) is (+), but the sign cannot be deduced from R/S alone; it follows only from experiment. (This was the historical point of the Walden-cycle experiments.)

22.3 SN1: racemisation (with some inversion)

The leaving group departs first, leaving a planar carbocation; the nucleophile attacks either face. The product is a racemate (at least in principle; in practice a slight excess of inversion arises when the leaving group still shields one face as an ion pair). Rates depend on the carbocation stability: 3° > 2° (benzylic, allylic best).

Shortcut Quick identification: a reaction at a stereocentre that gives a racemate is SN1 (or goes through a planar intermediate); single inversion is SN2; retention needs double inversion, neighbouring-group participation, or SNi.

22.4 Retention: SNi, neighbouring groups, double inversion

  • SNi (internal return): an alcohol reacts with SOCl2 without base to give the chlorosulfite R–O–S(=O)Cl; the leaving group delivers the chloride to the same face as it departs, so the alkyl chloride is formed with retention. With pyridine added, free chloride attacks from the back (SN2): inversion.
  • Neighbouring-group participation: a nearby group with a lone pair (CO2−, OR, SR, Br) displaces the leaving group first (inversion #1) forming a cyclic intermediate, and the external nucleophile then opens it (inversion #2): net retention. Example: (S)-2-bromopropanoate with dilute NaOH and Ag2O gives (S)-lactate (retention) because the carboxylate forms an α-lactone intermediate.
  • Double inversion: two successive SN2 steps (e.g. Br− replaces OTs with inversion; OH− replaces Br with inversion again) give net retention.
  • Tosylation (ROH + TsCl/pyridine) does not touch the C–O bond: retention at carbon. The subsequent SN2 on the tosylate then inverts.

Example 65 Adv

A Walden cycle

(R)-Octan-2-ol is converted to the tosylate (TsCl, pyridine), then treated with NaOAc (SN2), and the acetate is hydrolysed. What is the configuration of the final alcohol, and what is the net result?

Show solution
  1. Step 1, tosylation: retention at carbon (R tosylate). Step 2, SN2 by acetate: inversion (S acetate). Step 3, hydrolysis of the ester: the C–O bond to the carbon is not broken (acyl–oxygen cleavage): retention. The final alcohol is (S)-octan-2-ol: the enantiomer of the starting material, obtained by a net single inversion.
