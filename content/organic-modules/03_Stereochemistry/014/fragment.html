Key idea In a Fischer projection of a compound whose two ends carry identical groups (and whose two centres carry identical groups), identical groups on the same side at both centres means meso (the horizontal mirror line lies between the centres). Opposite sides means a chiral pair (the molecule has only a C2 axis). The labels (R,S) printed above on the figures confirm which is which.

11.2 The rules of the Fischer projection

  • Convention: the main chain is vertical; the most highly oxidised carbon (CHO, COOH) or the carbon with the lowest IUPAC locant is at the top.
  • At every stereocentre, the two vertical bonds go away from the viewer; the two horizontal bonds come toward the viewer.
  • The centre of the cross is the stereocentre.

Allowed operations (configuration unchanged):

  • 1. Rotate the whole projection by 180° in the plane of the paper.
  • 2. Hold one group fixed and cyclically rotate the other three (two swaps).
  • 3. Any even number of swaps of two groups.

Forbidden operations (give the enantiomer):

  • 1. Rotate by 90° (or 270°) in the plane.
  • 2. A single swap of two groups (any odd number of swaps).
  • 3. Lifting the projection out of the plane and turning it over.

Trap Rotating a Fischer projection by 90° looks harmless but gives the enantiomer. If a question shows a Fischer projection turned sideways, turn it back by 90° twice in your head only if the total is 180°; a single 90° turn changes the answer.

11.3 R/S from a Fischer projection

  • If the lowest-priority group (usually H) is on a vertical bond (pointing away), read 1 → 2 → 3 directly: clockwise R, anticlockwise S.
  • If the lowest-priority group is on a horizontal bond (pointing toward you), read 1 → 2 → 3 and reverse the answer.
  • Shortcut for H on a horizontal bond: write the priorities of the other three groups around the cross and reverse.

11.4 D and L

D/L is a relative configuration based on glyceraldehyde: draw the Fischer projection with the CHO (or the most oxidised group) at the top. If the OH on the stereocentre is on the right, it is D; on the left, L. For compounds with several centres the D/L descriptor refers to the stereocentre farthest from the most oxidised end (the highest-numbered stereocentre in sugars; the α-carbon in amino acids, where NH2 on the left = L).

11.5 Erythro, threo, like, unlike, syn, anti

For two adjacent stereocentres drawn in a Fischer projection:

  • Erythro: two similar (or identical) substituents on the same side. Threo: on opposite sides. The names come from the sugars erythrose and threose.
  • like (l) / unlike (u): descriptors of the pair (R,R) or (S,S) = like; (R,S) = unlike. They apply to any pair of centres.
  • syn / anti: the main chain is drawn in zig-zag in the plane; two substituents both on wedges (or both on dashes) = syn; one wedge, one dash = anti.

Trap Erythro/threo are not the same as meso/chiral. Erythro = identical groups on the same side in a Fischer projection; this gives a meso compound only when the two ends are also identical (tartaric acid, 2,3-dibromobutane). 2-Bromo-3-chlorobutane in its erythro form is chiral. 11.6 Converting between projections: the recipes

Recipe 1 (wedge → Fischer). Put the stereocentre at the cross. Two bonds (chosen to be the vertical ones) must point away; the other two toward you. If the molecule as drawn has the main chain in the plane with groups on wedge/dash, rotate the molecule so that the chain is vertical and curling away from you: the groups on wedges become horizontal.

(S)-lactic acid, wedge-dash
One wedge-dash drawing: compare with the Fischer projection in the next figure
(S)-lactic acid, Fischer

Recipe 2 (Fischer → Newman). Choose two adjacent centres, C-1 above C-2. Look from the top down the C-1 to C-2 bond. The Fischer projection is eclipsed: the vertical group of C-1 is eclipsed with the vertical group of C-2 (both away from you, at 12 o'clock); the left groups are eclipsed with each other; the right groups are eclipsed with each other. Going clockwise from 12 o'clock the groups of each carbon read: vertical group, right group, left group. Rotating the rear carbon by 180° gives the staggered anti conformation. (The program used exactly this rule and the figures above agree with the Fischer label every time.) Recipe 3 (Newman → Fischer). First rotate the rear carbon until the Newman projection is eclipsed with the vertical groups (the groups to become the top and bottom) at 12 o'clock. Then read off, for each carbon, the right and left groups (clockwise: top group, then right group, then left group).

Three-group rotation

Show that a three-group rotation of a Fischer projection keeps the configuration: start with COOH top, OH left, H right, CH3 bottom; rotate OH → COOH → H clockwise holding CH3 fixed.

start
after: top ← right (H), left ← top (COOH), right ← left (OH)
Show solution
  1. Holding CH3 fixed, H moves from the right to the top, COOH from the top to the left, OH from the left to the right. This is two swaps (a cyclic rotation of three objects), so the configuration is unchanged: S → S.

11.7 Fischer projections of several centres: chains and rings

For a chain with several stereocentres, each centre is read separately: the vertical neighbours go back, the horizontal ones come forward, at every centre. This means the whole chain curls away from you like a "C". A ring drawn flat (Haworth) places groups above (up) or below (down) the ring plane; a Fischer substituent on the right goes down in the Haworth structure, a substituent on the left goes up (for the D-series; reversed when the ring is read in the opposite direction, so check with the labels).

Show solution

Example 37 Adv

Epimers and anomers

D-glucose and D-mannose differ only at C-2. What is the relationship? D-glucose and D-galactose differ only at C-4. What are α-D-glucopyranose and β-D-glucopyranose?

Show solution
  1. D-Glucose/D-mannose: C-2 epimers; D-glucose/D-galactose: C-4 epimers: epimers are diastereomers that differ in configuration at exactly one stereocentre.
  2. α and β glucopyranose differ only at C-1, the new stereocentre created on ring closure (the hemiacetal carbon): they are anomers, and also diastereomers. Both are D-sugars because C-5 is unchanged.

11.9 Summary sheet: projections

  • Fischer: vertical = away, horizontal = toward. 180° turn fine, 90° turn gives the enantiomer; one swap inverts, two swaps retain.
  • H on horizontal: reverse the apparent sense.
  • D/L: OH (or NH2) on the right (left) at the reference centre; independent of R/S and of (+)/(−).
  • Meso test (identical ends): identical groups on the same side in Fischer.
  • Fischer → Newman: vertical group, right group, left group clockwise from 12 o'clock (eclipsed).
  • Erythro: same side; threo: opposite; like (RR/SS); unlike (RS); syn/anti for zig-zag.
