50. Isotopic labelling in carbonyl chemistry

Labelling experiments with 18O (written O*) test mechanisms and are a favourite problem type.

  • Oxygen exchange. Shaken with H2O* and a trace of acid or base, a ketone exchanges its oxygen: R2C=O + H2O* ⇌ R2C(OH)(O*H) ⇌ R2C=O* + H2O. This proves that the gem-diol (hydrate) forms reversibly. The α-hydrogens also exchange (D2O/OD−), through the enol or enolate.
  • Acetal hydrolysis. In H2O*/H+, a dimethyl acetal hydrolyses to the carbonyl compound containing O\* (water adds to the oxocarbenium ion) and two molecules of methanol that contain no label: R–CH(OCH3)2 + H2O* → RCH=O* + 2 CH3OH.
  • Baeyer–Villiger. The carbonyl oxygen of the ketone is retained as the ester C=O; the new ether-type oxygen (the one inserted) comes from the peracid. R–C(=O*)–R′ + R″CO3H → R–C(=O*)–OR′.
  • Cannizzaro in D2O. The carbinol carbon of the alcohol carries H (from the other aldehyde molecule), not D.

Example 52 Adv

Where does the label go?

Propanone (C=O, unlabelled) is stirred with H218O (large excess) and a drop of HCl for several hours. (a) What is the isotopic composition of the ketone? (b) How many hydrogens of propanone exchange if the water is D2O* instead?

Show solution
  1. (a) The oxygen exchanges through the hydrate until it mirrors the water composition, so the ketone becomes (CH3)2C=18O (almost fully labelled with a large excess of H218O).
  2. (b) In D2O with acid all six α-hydrogens exchange by enolisation: CD3COCD3.
Example 53 Adv

Baeyer–Villiger label

Cyclohexyl methyl ketone labelled with 18O on the carbonyl is oxidised with mCPBA, then the ester is hydrolysed with H2O (no label). Where is 18O found?

Show solution
  1. BV gives cyclohexyl ethanoate with the label in the C=O (original carbonyl oxygen retained; the inserted O comes from the peracid). Acid- or base-catalysed hydrolysis cleaves the acyl–oxygen bond (C–OR′) of the ester, so the label stays in the acid: CH3C(=18O)OH (or its oxygen-exchanged form in water). The cyclohexanol formed carries no 18O.
