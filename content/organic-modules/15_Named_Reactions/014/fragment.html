11. Knoevenagel condensation

Reagents: an aldehyde or ketone + a doubly activated CH2 (malonic ester CH2(COOEt)2, malononitrile CH2(CN)2, ethyl cyanoacetate, ethyl acetoacetate, nitromethane) + weak base (piperidine, pyridine, ammonium acetate). The weak base is enough because the active methylene is acidic (pKa ≈ 9–13); a strong base would cause self-condensation of the aldehyde. Doebner modification: malonic acid + pyridine + a trace of piperidine, heat: the Knoevenagel adduct (an alkylidene malonic acid) loses CO2 and gives the α,β-unsaturated monoacid directly.

Knoevenagel with a ketone and a ketoester

Predict the product of acetone with ethyl cyanoacetate (NCCH2CO2Et) and ammonium acetate, then state what happens on hydrolysis and heating.

Show solution
  1. Condensation at CH2: (CH3)2C=C(CN)CO2Et (ethyl 2-cyano-3-methylbut-2-enoate), losing H2O.
  2. Hydrolysis of ester and nitrile then decarboxylation of the geminal diacid-type group would leave (CH3)2C=CH–COOH (3-methylbut-2-enoic acid) after strong heating. The Knoevenagel route thus converts a ketone R2C=O to R2C=CH–COOH, adding two carbons.
