9. Family 4: C–C bond formation giving ketones

9.1 Acid chloride with organometallics Grignard reagents add to acid chlorides, but the ketone formed is more reactive than the acid chloride towards RMgX; over-addition gives a tertiary alcohol. Less reactive organometallics stop at the ketone:

  • Dialkylcadmium R2Cd (from RMgX + CdCl2) + R′COCl → RCOR′.
  • Lithium dialkylcuprate (Gilman reagent) R2CuLi + R′COCl → RCOR′ (does not attack the ketone, nor esters, nitriles; selective).
+ 1 equiv, −78 °C, Cu(I)⟶
RMgX + RCOCl with copper catalysis gives the ketone
(CH₃)₂CuLi⟶
Benzoyl chloride + Gilman reagent → acetophenone

9.2 Nitriles with Grignard reagents RC≡N + R′MgX gives the magnesium salt of an imine, which does not add a second R′ (it is a negatively charged, unreactive N–Mg species); aqueous acid hydrolyses it to the ketone. The carbonyl carbon of the ketone comes from the nitrile carbon.

+ (i) ether (ii) H₃O⁺⟶
Benzonitrile + MeMgBr → acetophenone
+ (i) ether (ii) H₃O⁺⟶
Ethanenitrile + EtMgBr → butanone

9.3 Heating calcium salts of carboxylic acids Dry distillation of the calcium salt of a carboxylic acid gives a ketone R–CO–R plus CaCO3. With calcium formate alone the product is methanal; with a mixture of calcium formate and another calcium carboxylate the product is an aldehyde.

dry distillation, Δ⟶
Calcium ethanoate → propanone + CaCO₃
+ dry distillation, Δ⟶
Calcium methanoate + calcium ethanoate → ethanal (cross reaction; methanal and propanone also form)
Trap Dry distillation of (HCOO)2Ca gives HCHO (not an acid or alcohol). The cross-distillation with another salt gives RCHO but as a mixture of three carbonyl compounds: the pure aldehyde is not obtained, so it is not a good synthesis. Ketonic decarboxylation of acids over MnO or ThO2 at 573 K also gives ketones.
