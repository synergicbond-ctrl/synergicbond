9. Ethers as protecting groups

A hydroxyl group is acidic and nucleophilic, so it interferes with Grignard reagents, hydrides and strong bases. It can be masked as an ether (or acetal) and released later. A good protecting group goes on in high yield, survives the intervening reactions and comes off under mild conditions that leave the rest of the molecule untouched.

  • THP ether (tetrahydropyranyl): ROH + 3,4-dihydro-2H-pyran with catalytic acid. It is an acetal, stable to bases, Grignard reagents and LiAlH4, and removed by aqueous acid. It creates a new stereocentre, so a chiral alcohol gives a mixture of diastereomers.
  • TBS ether (tert-butyldimethylsilyl): ROH + TBSCl, imidazole. Stable to bases and many oxidants; removed by fluoride (TBAF) because of the strong Si–F bond, or by acid.
  • Benzyl ether: ROH + NaH, then BnBr (Williamson). Stable to acid and base; removed by catalytic hydrogenolysis, H2/Pd–C, giving ROH and toluene.
  • MOM ether (methoxymethyl, ROCH2OCH3): an acetal, stable to base and removed by aqueous acid.

A typical use: 4-bromobutan-1-ol cannot be converted into a Grignard reagent because the O–H group would destroy the organometallic as it forms. The alcohol is first protected as its THP ether, the Grignard reagent is made and used, and the THP group is removed in the final step with aqueous acid.

Trap. Acetal-type protecting groups (THP, MOM) are removed by acid, silyl ethers by fluoride, benzyl ethers by hydrogenolysis. They are not interchangeable; the reagent must be matched to the group.
