1. The map of isomerism

Two compounds are isomers when they have the same molecular formula but are different compounds. The classification below is the backbone of the chapter; keep it in your head.

Two more words you will need. Stereogenic unit: a feature whose change (swap of two groups, or inversion) gives a stereoisomer. Stereocentres, C=C bonds with different groups on each carbon, and chiral axes are stereogenic units. Chirotopic / chiral: a molecule is chiral if its mirror image is not superimposable on it. A chiral molecule need not have a stereocentre (allenes, biphenyls, helicenes), and a molecule with stereocentres need not be chiral (meso compounds).

Trap "Stereoisomers" does not mean "optical isomers". Cis- and trans-but-2-ene are stereoisomers that are not optically active. "Optical isomers" means enantiomers (and, loosely, any compound that shows optical activity).
