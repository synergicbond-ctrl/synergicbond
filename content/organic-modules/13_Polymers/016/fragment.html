12. Condensation (step-growth) polymerisation: the principles

In step-growth polymerisation every molecule carrying two reactive end groups can react with every other such molecule, so growth is by random coupling of monomers, then dimers, trimers, oligomers, and finally long chains, with a small molecule (H2O, HCl, CH3OH, NH3) expelled at each coupling. The monomers are A–A + B–B (diacid + diamine, diacid + diol) or A–B (amino acid, hydroxy acid). Each coupling is an ordinary functional-group reaction (acylation of N or O), so the mechanisms are those of carboxylic-acid chemistry applied twice per unit.

IMP Two big differences from chain growth. (1) High molar mass appears only at very high conversion, not from the start. (2) The reaction is an equilibrium: to reach high mass the small molecule must be removed (heat, vacuum, a water separator) and the two functional groups must be in exact 1:1 ratio.
Why It Happens Why step-growth needs very high conversion. Any two molecules with reactive ends can join, so at p = 0.5 the chains are mostly dimers; chains of useful length (Xn ≥ 100) exist only after 99 % of the groups have reacted. Because each coupling is an equilibrium (esterification, amidation), the small molecule has to be removed to keep p rising.

12.1 The Carothers equation

Let p = fraction of the original functional groups (of the limiting type) that have reacted. For stoichiometric A–A + B–B (or A–B) the number of molecules falls from N0 to N0(1 − p), so

Formula Xn = N0/N = 1/(1 − p) p = 0.90 → Xn = 10; p = 0.99 → 100; p = 0.999 → 1000
Here Xn counts monomer molecules per chain (for A–A + B–B, two monomers per repeat unit, so repeat units = Xn/2). Mass 104–105 therefore needs p > 0.99, and every impurity matters.
Validity Check The Carothers equation assumes: equimolar bifunctional A–A and B–B (or A–B); every group equally reactive whatever the chain length; no cyclisation; no side reactions; no loss of monomer by evaporation. If any assumption fails, the real Xn is lower.

12.2 Stoichiometric imbalance and monofunctional impurities

With NA A-groups and NB B-groups (NB ≥ NA), r = NA/NB ≤ 1:

Formula Xn = (1 + r)/(1 + r − 2rp) at p → 1: Xn = (1 + r)/(1 − r)
  • Exactly r = 1 → Xn = 1/(1 − p) (Carothers).
  • A 1 % excess of diamine (r = 100/101 = 0.990) limits Xn to (1 + 0.990)/(1 − 0.990) = 201 even at complete reaction: this is how the chain length of nylon is controlled, by adding a slight excess of diacid or a monofunctional acid (acetic acid) that caps chains.
  • A monofunctional impurity terminates a chain end permanently (it has one reactive group, so it cannot continue growth).
Exam Trap Do not mix up the two equations. Carothers Xn = 1/(1 − p) is for exact stoichiometry. If a reactant is in excess or a monofunctional compound is present, use the r-form. In JEE-level questions the r-form appears as “1 mol of diacid is used with 1.02 mol of diamine ...”.

12.3 Counting what is lost

  • Linear polymer from n repeat units of A–A + B–B: 2n monomer molecules joined by 2n − 1 links → 2n − 1 molecules of H2O lost (for a diacid and a diamine or diol).
  • Linear polymer from n molecules of an A–B hydroxy- or amino-acid: n − 1 molecules of water.
  • Polymer mass M = n × M(repeat unit) + M(end groups) (end groups: –H and –OH, i.e. 18 g mol-1 for a polyamide with free end groups). Mass of the polymer = total monomer mass − (number of H2O lost) × 18.
Example 12 Main

Water lost in nylon-6,6

How many H2O molecules are eliminated in forming a nylon-6,6 chain of 100 repeat units?

Show solution
  1. 100 repeat units = 100 adipic acid + 100 hexamethylenediamine = 200 monomers. They are linked by 199 amide bonds.
  2. 199 H2O are lost (not 200 and not 100).
Example 13 Adv

Nylon chain mass and DP

A nylon-6,6 sample has average M = 22 600 g mol-1. Estimate the number of repeat units and the number of amide links per chain. (Repeat unit C12H22N2O2 = 226.3 g mol-1.)

Show solution
  1. n = 22 600/226.3 ≈ 100 repeat units (end groups, 18 g mol-1, change this by < 0.1 %).
  2. Monomers = 2n = 200; amide links = 2n − 1 = 199.
