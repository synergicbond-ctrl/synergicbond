7. Reactions of the OH group: ethers, esters, Schotten–Baumann, Williamson

Phenol is converted into its phenoxide with aqueous alkali; the phenoxide is a good nucleophile at oxygen toward primary alkyl halides. This is the Williamson synthesis of aryl alkyl ethers. The halide must be primary or methyl: a secondary or tertiary halide undergoes E2 elimination because phenoxide is also a base. The other disconnection, an aryl halide with an alkoxide, fails because an unactivated aryl halide does not undergo SN2.

Reactant:

Reagent: (i) NaOH; (ii) CH3I

Product:

Anisole.

Esterification of a phenol with a carboxylic acid is unfavourable at equilibrium because phenols are weak nucleophiles. Use an acid chloride or anhydride instead. With benzoyl chloride in aqueous NaOH (Schotten–Baumann conditions), the phenoxide is acylated and the base neutralises the HCl formed; with acetic anhydride (acid or base catalysis) phenyl acetate forms.

Reactant:

Reagent: C6H5COCl, aq. NaOH

Product:

Phenyl benzoate.

Reactant:

Reagent: (CH3CO)2O, trace H2SO4 or pyridine

Product:

Acetylation of salicylic acid occurs at the phenolic OH, giving aspirin.

The OH group of phenol cannot be replaced by halide with HX or ZnCl2/HCl (Lucas reagent) because the C–O bond has partial double-bond character and an SN2 attack at an sp2 carbon, or the formation of a phenyl cation, is not feasible. Phenol reacts with PCl5 to give only a little chlorobenzene (mostly phosphate esters). In contrast, heating anisole with HI cleaves the alkyl–oxygen bond because iodide attacks the methyl group of the protonated ether (SN2); the products are phenol and iodomethane, never iodobenzene.

Reactant:

Reagent: conc. HI, heat

Product:

Cleavage always occurs at the alkyl–O bond of an aryl alkyl ether.

Trap. Phenyl esters hydrolyse much faster than alkyl esters because phenoxide (pKa of conjugate acid ≈ 10) is a far better leaving group than alkoxide (≈ 16). Hydrolysis of a phenyl ester with excess NaOH consumes one equivalent for the acid portion and one more for the phenol that is released.
