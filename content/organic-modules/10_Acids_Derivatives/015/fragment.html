15. α-Hydrogens, enolates and the synthesis of C–C bonds

The carbon–carbon bond forming reactions of acid derivatives (Claisen, Dieckmann, malonic and acetoacetic ester syntheses) all depend on one thing: the acidity of the C–H bond α to a carbonyl group and the nucleophilicity of the enolate that results. Learn the acidity scale first, then every reaction becomes a question of “is the base strong enough, and what is the driving force?”

Why is a 1,3-dicarbonyl so much more acidic than a simple ketone (10.7 against 19)? The negative charge in the anion is delocalised over both carbonyl oxygens (and the carbon), and the anion is further stabilised by a planar W-shaped conformation. Why is an ester α-H (25.6) less acidic than a ketone (19.2)? The ester carbonyl is already stabilised by resonance donation from the OR oxygen, so it has less capacity to stabilise the enolate.

Key idea Sodium ethoxide in ethanol (pKa of ethanol 15.9) is the standard base. It completely deprotonates diethyl malonate (13) and ethyl acetoacetate (10.7). It deprotonates ethyl acetate (25.6) only to the extent of about 10−10, which is enough to start the Claisen condensation because the subsequent irreversible deprotonation of the β-keto ester product (10.7) pulls the equilibrium over. For simple ketones (19) and esters one needs a strong non-nucleophilic base (LDA) to make the enolate quantitatively.
<h4>15.2 Keto–enol tautomerism and its signatures</h4>

Ethyl acetoacetate exists as an equilibrium mixture of keto (about 92 %) and enol (about 8 %) forms; the enol is stabilised by conjugation and an intramolecular O–H···O=C hydrogen bond. In non-polar solvents the enol content is higher; in water it is lower (water H-bonds the keto form). Evidence for both forms:

  • Keto: gives a 2,4-DNP derivative, an oxime, a cyanohydrin, and an addition compound with NaHSO3.
  • Enol: gives a violet-red colour with neutral FeCl3, decolorises bromine water (by addition), and reacts with Na to give H2.

The two forms interconvert rapidly (the old name “tautomerism” and the dynamic equilibrium are the reason both sets of tests are positive). Acetylacetone, with greater enol content (≈ 80 % neat), gives a very strong FeCl3 test.

Example 40 Main

Tests

(a) Which of CH3COCH3, CH3COCH2COOEt, CH3COCH2COCH3, CH3COOEt gives a violet colour with neutral FeCl3? (b) Which decolorise bromine water immediately?

Show solution
  1. (a) Only compounds with a significant amount of enol: ethyl acetoacetate and acetylacetone (simple ketone and ester have a negligible enol fraction, about 10−6 %).
  2. (b) The same two react quickly with Br2 (by addition to the enol C=C, followed by loss of HBr: α-bromination); acetone brominates slowly because enolisation is slow without a catalyst.
