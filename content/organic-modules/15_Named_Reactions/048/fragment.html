43. Clemmensen and Wolff–Kishner reductions (C=O → CH2)

Clemmensen vs Wolff–Kishner
FeatureClemmensenWolff–Kishner
ReagentsZn–Hg amalgam, conc. HCl, refluxN2H4, KOH (or NaOEt), ethylene glycol, 473 K (Huang–Minlon modification)
Mediumstrongly acidicstrongly basic
Substrate must tolerateacid (no acetals, no acid-sensitive groups)base (no esters, halides that eliminate)
Best foraryl ketones (Ar–CO–R from Friedel–Crafts acylation)aliphatic, base-stable, acid-sensitive ketones
Mechanismzinc-surface, carbenoid-like (not fully understood)hydrazone → deprotonation → loss of N2 gives carbanion → protonation
Leaves aloneC=C, C–X (mostly), COOHC=C, COOH (as salt)

Acetophenone → ethylbenzene
N₂H₄, KOH, ethylene glycol, 473 K (Wolff–Kishner)⟶
Pentan-3-one → pentane
Trap Pick the method from the other groups in the molecule. Acid-sensitive groups (acetals, tertiary alcohols that would eliminate) call for Wolff–Kishner; base-sensitive groups (esters, β-halo ketones) call for Clemmensen. Zn/HCl would also reduce an aromatic –NO2 group to –NH2.
