10. Glycerol (propane-1,2,3-triol)

Glycerol (glycerine), HOCH2–CH(OH)–CH2OH, is a viscous, sweet, hygroscopic, non-toxic liquid (bp 290 °C with decomposition; mp 18 °C; miscible with water; three H-bonding OH groups). Preparation: (i) by-product of soap manufacture: fat + 3NaOH → glycerol + 3RCOONa (saponification; the glycerol is recovered from “spent lye”); (ii) hydrolysis of oils with superheated steam; (iii) synthetic: propene → (Cl2, 773 K) allyl chloride → (HOCl) glycerol dichlorohydrin → (NaOH) epichlorohydrin → hydrolysis → glycerol.   • With oxalic acid at 383 K: glycerol 1-oxalate → decarboxylates to glycerol 1-formate → hydrolysis gives formic acid; at 533 K the monooxalate loses CO2 and water to give allyl alcohol (prop-2-en-1-ol).
  • Esterification: with 3 mol of a fatty acid gives a triglyceride (fat or oil); with phthalic anhydride gives the cross-linked polyester glyptal; with acetic anhydride gives glyceryl triacetate (triacetin).
  • Oxidation: dil. HNO3 gives glyceric acid and tartronic acid; mild oxidation (Fenton’s reagent) gives glyceraldehyde and dihydroxyacetone (the simplest aldose and ketose).
  • Dehydration mechanism (acrolein): the central (secondary) OH is protonated and lost; removal of H+ from a terminal carbon gives the enol HOCH=CH–CH2OH, which tautomerises to 3-hydroxypropanal, HOCH2CH2CHO. This β-hydroxy aldehyde loses water readily (E1cB, as in any aldol dehydration) to give acrolein, CH2=CH–CHO.

Uses: nitroglycerine, cosmetics, glycerol-based lubricants, humectant in tobacco and food, antifreeze, solvent, glyptal resins, medicine (suppositories).

Example 31 L1 Foundation

Glycerol reactions

Write the product of glycerol with (a) KHSO4 (heat), (b) HNO3/H2SO4, (c) excess HI (heat). Give the chemical test for glycerol.

Show solution
  1. (a) Acrolein, CH2=CH–CHO, a pungent vapour (the test: acrolein’s acrid smell and its reaction with Tollens’ reagent or with Schiff’s reagent).
  2. (b) Glyceryl trinitrate (nitroglycerine), C3H5(ONO2)3.
  3. (c) 2-Iodopropane: C3H8O3 + 5HI → CH3CHICH3 + 3H2O + 2I2.
  4. Tests: (i) acrolein test (heating with KHSO4); (ii) it consumes 2 mol HIO4; (iii) it gives a blue colour with Cu(OH)2/NaOH (like all polyhydroxy alcohols, because of the soluble copper–glycerol complex).
Example 32 L2 Main

Nitroglycerine stoichiometry

Nitroglycerine decomposes explosively: 4 C3H5(NO3)3 → 12 CO2 + 10 H2O + 6 N2 + O2. How many litres of gas (at STP) are evolved from 227 g? (Molar mass 227 g mol−1; at the temperature of explosion water is a gas, but the question asks for the STP volume of all gases formed.)

Show solution
  1. 227 g is exactly 1.00 mol of nitroglycerine.
  2. Per 4 mol: 12 + 10 + 6 + 1 = 29 mol of gas. Per mole: 29/4 = 7.25 mol.
  3. At STP, using 22.4 L mol−1 and treating all as gases: 7.25 × 22.4 = 162.4 L (if water is condensed, the gases are 19/4 = 4.75 mol, 106.4 L). The enormous volume change, together with the heat released, causes the explosion.
