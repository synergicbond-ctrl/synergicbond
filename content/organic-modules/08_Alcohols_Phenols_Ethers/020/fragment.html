30.3 Ethers

Practice 127 L2 ETH

Give the products and mechanism type for the cleavage of (a) CH3OCH(CH3)2, (b) (CH3)3COCH2CH3, (c) C6H5CH2OCH3, each with HI.

Show answer
  1. (a) SN2 at CH3: CH3I + (CH3)2CHOH. (b) SN1 at the tertiary carbon: (CH3)3CI + C2H5OH. (c) SN1 (benzyl cation): C6H5CH2I + CH3OH.
Practice 128 L2 ETH

(R)-2-Methoxybutane + HI (1 mol) gives which products? What is the optical activity of the alcohol?

Show answer
  1. CH3I + (R)-butan-2-ol (the C–O at the stereocentre is not broken; configuration retained). The alcohol is optically active, dextro/levo according to its (R) sign.
Practice 129 L2 ETH

Why do ethers cleave faster with HI than with HBr, and HBr than HCl?

Show answer
  1. Acid strength and nucleophilicity of the halide ion both increase down the group (I− is the best nucleophile and weakest base, HI the strongest acid).
Practice 130 L2 ETH

Why does anisole give C6H5OH + CH3I and not C6H5I + CH3OH with HI?

Show answer
  1. After protonation, I− attacks the carbon that can undergo SN2: the methyl group. The phenyl carbon is sp2, the C–O bond partially double bonded; neither SN1 nor SN2 can occur there.
Practice 131 L2 ETH

Write the best Williamson combination for each: (a) ethyl isopropyl ether; (b) methyl neopentyl ether; (c) benzyl tert-butyl ether; (d) cyclohexyl methyl ether.

Show answer
  1. (a) (CH3)2CHONa + C2H5Br. (b) (CH3)3CCH2ONa + CH3I (neopentyl halide is too hindered for SN2). (c) (CH3)3CONa + C6H5CH2Cl. (d) cyclohexoxide + CH3I.
Practice 132 L2 ETH

Explain why the intramolecular Williamson reaction of 4-chlorobutan-1-ol (giving THF) is much faster than the intermolecular reaction of ethoxide with chloroethane under similar conditions.

Show answer
  1. The alkoxide and the C–Cl are in the same molecule: the effective molarity is very high (entropy of cyclisation is small for a 5-membered ring and the transition state is unstrained).
Practice 133 L2 ETH

From phenol and ethanol as the only organic starting materials, prepare ethoxybenzene.

Show answer
  1. C2H5OH + HBr (or PBr3) → C2H5Br. Phenol + NaOH → C6H5ONa. Then SN2: C6H5ONa + C2H5Br → C6H5OC2H5 (aryl halide cannot be used, so the phenoxide must be the nucleophile).
Practice 134 L2 ETH

Compare the nitration of benzene, anisole and nitrobenzene. Arrange in order of reactivity.

Show answer
  1. OCH3 activates (+M), NO2 deactivates: anisole > benzene > nitrobenzene. Anisole gives o + p products; nitrobenzene gives meta.
Practice 135 L2 ETH

Why is the para isomer the major product in bromination of anisole in acetic acid?

Show answer
  1. Both o and p positions are electronically activated; the ortho position is hindered by the OCH3 group (and its solvated oxygen), so para predominates (about 90 %).
Practice 136 L2 ETH

An ether A (C5H12O) is cleaved by cold HI to give B and methanol. B on treatment with alcoholic KOH gives an alkene that on ozonolysis gives CH2O and CH3COCH3. Identify A and B.

Show answer
  1. The alkene is 2-methylpropene, so B = tert-butyl iodide, (CH3)3CI, and A = methyl tert-butyl ether, (CH3)3COCH3 (C5H12O ✓). SN1 cleavage at the tertiary carbon gives B + CH3OH.
Practice 137 L2 ETH

Give an example of an ether that is chiral and one that is optically inactive but has a stereocentre.

Show answer
  1. Chiral: 2-methoxybutane (CH3OCH(CH3)C2H5). Meso example with stereocentres: cis-2,3-dimethyloxirane (meso epoxide, two stereocentres, plane of symmetry).
Practice 138 L2 ETH

Why does diethyl ether form peroxides but tert-butyl methyl ether does so only very slowly?

Show answer
  1. Autoxidation abstracts an α-H adjacent to O to give a stabilised radical. Diethyl ether has secondary α-H atoms; in MTBE the α carbons are CH3 (primary, less easily abstracted) and the quaternary carbon (no α-H).
