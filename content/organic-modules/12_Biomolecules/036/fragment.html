6.9 Enediol and the Lobry de Bruyn–van Ekenstein transformation

Step 1: HO− removes H-2⟶ Step 2: H2O protonates O− / C-1⟶
The enolate and the 1,2-enediol are the central intermediates
  • Step 1. Hydroxide removes the α-C–H, which is acidic because the resulting carbanion is delocalised onto the carbonyl oxygen (enolate). MO view: the C2–H σ bond must lie parallel to the π* of C=O for the developing carbanion to be delocalised: it is the stereoelectronic requirement for enolisation. C-2 becomes planar, losing its configuration (the only centre that does).
  • Step 2. The enolate oxygen is protonated to give the 1,2-enediol. From the enediol, protonation at C-2 gives glucose or mannose, protonation of the C-1 oxygen... and tautomerisation of the C-1 OH to C=O with H added at C-1 gives fructose.
Formula Equilibrium composition in dilute NaOH (aldose/ketose): D-glucose ≈ 63 %, D-fructose ≈ 31 %, D-mannose ≈ 3 %: the C-3 to C-6 centres remain unchanged.
