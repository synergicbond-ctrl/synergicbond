Exercise set 1: Foundation

Short direct questions that test each rule once: recall of definitions, data and standard reactions. Solutions are given under each question; attempt before opening them. Sections A to H follow the order of the chapter. The previous-year-question section is intentionally empty in this edition because none could be verified.

A. Language of mechanisms, reagents (Questions 1 to 9)

Question 1 Basic

Electrophile count

How many of the following are electrophiles (Lewis acids): BF3, NH3, CN−, NO2+, H2O, AlCl3, CH3+, :CCl2, Br−, SO3?

Show answer

6. Electrophiles (electron-pair acceptors): BF3, NO2+, AlCl3, CH3+, :CCl2 (empty p orbital), SO3. Nucleophiles: NH3, CN−, H2O, Br−.

Question 2 Basic

Steps and states

A reaction proceeds in three elementary steps. How many transition states and how many intermediates does its energy profile show?

Show answer

3 transition states and 2 intermediates (n steps give n transition states and n − 1 intermediates).

Question 3 Basic

Activation energies

For an exothermic reaction the forward activation energy is 80 kJ mol−1 and ΔH = −30 kJ mol−1. What is the activation energy of the reverse reaction?

Show answer

Ea(reverse) = Ea(forward) − ΔH = 80 − (−30) = 110 kJ mol−1. Reverse barrier is larger because the products lie lower than the reactants.

Question 4 Basic

Nucleophilicity trend

Which is the better nucleophile (a) in methanol, (b) in DMF: F− or I−?

Show answer

(a) I− in methanol: F− is strongly hydrogen-bonded (solvated) by the protic solvent; the large, polarisable I− is not. (b) F− in DMF (polar aprotic): neither anion is H-bonded, and the order follows basicity (F− > I−).

Question 5 Basic

Leaving group

Arrange in increasing order of leaving-group ability: F−, Br−, Cl−, I−.

Show answer

F− < Cl− < Br− < I−. The weaker the base (the stronger the acid H–X: HF pKa 3.2, HCl −7, HBr −9, HI −10) and the weaker and more polarisable the C–X bond, the better the leaving group.

Question 6 Basic

Bond fission

Classify each as heterolytic or homolytic: (a) t-BuBr in aqueous ethanol; (b) Cl2 in sunlight; (c) benzoyl peroxide at 90 °C; (d) CH3COOH in water.

Show answer

(a) heterolytic (ionisation to t-Bu+ + Br− in polar protic solvent); (b) homolytic (hν); (c) homolytic (weak O–O bond, heat); (d) heterolytic (O–H, gives H+ and acetate).

Question 7 Basic

Molecularity

The SN1 hydrolysis of t-BuBr is first order overall. Is the rate-determining step unimolecular or bimolecular? What is the rate law?

Show answer

Unimolecular (ionisation of t-BuBr alone). Rate = k[t-BuBr], independent of [OH−].

Question 8 Basic

Curved arrow tail

In the curved-arrow notation, where should the tail of an arrow start and where should the head point?

Show answer

The tail starts at the electrons (a lone pair or the middle of a bond); the head points to the atom or bond where the electrons end up (a new bond or lone pair). It never starts on an atom or nucleus as such.

Question 9 Basic

Solvent and mechanism

Which solvent favours SN2 with an anionic nucleophile: acetone or water? Which favours SN1?

Show answer

SN2: acetone (polar aprotic; leaves the nucleophile unsolvated and reactive). SN1: water (polar protic; stabilises the carbocation and the leaving group).

B. Carbocations (Questions 10 to 30)

Question 10 Basic

Structure

State the hybridisation, shape and number of valence electrons on carbon in the methyl cation. How many valence electrons does the carbon have in the methyl radical and the methyl anion?

Show answer

CH3+: sp2, trigonal planar, 6 electrons (empty p orbital). CH3•: 7 electrons. CH3−: 8 electrons (pyramidal sp3).

Question 11 Basic

Stability order of alkyl cations

Using the gas-phase hydride affinities CH3+ 314, CH3CH2+ 274, (CH3)2CH+ 247, (CH3)3C+ 230 kcal mol−1, arrange the cations in decreasing stability and give the number of α C–H bonds of each.

Show answer

(CH3)3C+ (230) > (CH3)2CH+ (247) > CH3CH2+ (274) > CH3+ (314). The smaller the hydride affinity, the more stable the cation. The numbers of α C–H bonds are 9, 6, 3 and 0, which parallels the order: more σ bonds available to donate into the empty p orbital.

Question 12 Basic

Hyperconjugation

Count the α C–H bonds of the isopropyl cation and of the 2-methylbutan-2-yl cation, (CH3)2C+CH2CH3, and explain in orbital terms what hyperconjugation is.

Show answer

Isopropyl: two CH3 groups × 3 H = 6. (CH3)2C+CH2CH3: 3 + 3 + 2 = 8. Hyperconjugation is the overlap of a filled σ(C–H) orbital on a carbon next to the cationic carbon with the empty p orbital: two electrons are delocalised into the empty orbital, which lowers the energy of the cation. It is strongest when the C–H bond lies parallel to the p orbital.

Question 13 Basic

Allyl cation

Draw the contributing structures of the allyl cation. According to the Hückel treatment, on which carbon atoms is the LUMO concentrated?

Show answer

Each terminal carbon carries about half a positive charge and the C–C bonds are equivalent (bond order 1.5). The LUMO is the nonbonding orbital, with coefficients 0.707, 0 and −0.707, so it is concentrated on the two terminal carbons and has a node at the central carbon: nucleophiles attack the ends.

Question 14 Basic

Benzylic

Why is the benzyl cation more stable than the ethyl cation although both are primary?

Show answer

The empty p orbital of the benzyl cation overlaps with the ring π system; the positive charge is delocalised onto the ortho and para carbons (four contributors). The ethyl cation has only 3 hyperconjugative interactions.

Question 15 Basic

Substituents on the benzyl cation

Arrange in decreasing stability: C6H5CH2+, p-CH3OC6H4CH2+, p-O2NC6H4CH2+.

Show answer

p-CH3OC6H4CH2+ > C6H5CH2+ > p-O2NC6H4CH2+. The methoxy oxygen donates a lone pair into the ring and onto the benzylic carbon (a contributor in which every atom has an octet); the nitro group withdraws electron density and destabilises the cation. The same trend is seen in the pKR+ values of substituted triphenylmethyl cations: 4-methoxy −3.40 > H −6.63 > 4-nitro −9.15.

Question 16 Basic

Aromatic cations

Which is more stable: the cycloheptatrienyl cation or the cyclopentadienyl cation? Give the π electron count.

Show answer

Cycloheptatrienyl (tropylium), 6 π electrons, aromatic. Cyclopentadienyl cation has 4 π electrons (antiaromatic), very unstable.

Question 17 Basic

Vinyl and aryl cations

The gas-phase hydride affinities (kcal mol−1) are CH3+ 314, C6H5+ 298, CH2=CH+ 287, CH3CH2+ 274. Arrange the four cations in decreasing stability and give an orbital reason why the vinyl and phenyl cations are less stable than ethyl.

Show answer

Decreasing stability: ethyl > vinyl > phenyl > methyl (smaller hydride affinity means greater stability). The vinyl and phenyl cations are less stable than a primary alkyl cation because the empty orbital is held in an orbital of high s character (vinyl: a p orbital perpendicular to the π bond with an sp-hybridised carbon; phenyl: an sp2 orbital in the ring plane that cannot overlap with the π system), and they have no adjacent σ bonds aligned for hyperconjugation. They remain more stable than the methyl cation, which has no stabilising groups at all.

Question 18 Basic

Rearrangement product

3-Methylbutan-2-ol is heated with concentrated HBr. Write the product.

Show answer

Protonation, loss of water gives CH3CH+CH(CH3)2 (2°); 1,2-H shift gives the tertiary cation; bromide capture: 2-bromo-2-methylbutane.

HBr⟶
2-Bromo-2-methylbutane (after a 1,2-hydride shift)
Question 19 Basic

Rearrangement of cations

Which one rearranges: (a) CH3CH2CH2+, (b) (CH3)3C+? Give the product cation.

Show answer

(a) Yes: a 1,2-hydride shift gives the secondary cation CH3CH+CH3. (b) No: it is already tertiary; no shift gives a more stable cation.

Question 20 Basic

Methyl shift

Write the cation formed when the 3,3-dimethylbutan-2-yl cation undergoes a 1,2-methyl shift, and state why the shift is favourable.

Show answer

(CH3)2C+–CH(CH3)2, the 2,3-dimethylbutan-2-yl cation: the secondary cation becomes tertiary (more hyperconjugative C–H/C–C bonds).

Question 21 Basic

Product count

Treatment of 2-methylbut-2-ene with HCl gives which major product? Is rearrangement possible?

Show answer

2-Chloro-2-methylbutane (protonation of C-3 gives the tertiary cation directly). No rearrangement is needed (cannot improve).

Question 22 Basic

Friedel–Crafts

Why does the Friedel–Crafts reaction of benzene with 1-chlorobutane/AlCl3 give mainly sec-butylbenzene?

Show answer

The incipient primary cation rearranges (1,2-hydride shift, as the C–Cl bond breaks) to the secondary butan-2-yl cation, which attacks benzene to give sec-butylbenzene (as major product).

Question 23 Basic

Acylium

Why does Friedel–Crafts acylation not involve rearrangement?

Show answer

The acylium ion RC≡O+ is stabilised by the lone pair on oxygen (octet on every atom); it has no adjacent carbocation centre to shift to, and rearrangement would give a less stable ion.

Question 24 Basic

Lucas test

Explain why tertiary alcohols give turbidity at once in the Lucas test whereas primary alcohols do not react at room temperature.

Show answer

The reaction goes through the alkyl cation: ROH2+ → R+. A tertiary cation forms rapidly (most stable); a primary cation is too unstable, so primary alcohols react only on heating (and by SN2).

Question 25 Basic

Racemisation

(S)-3-Bromo-3-methylhexane is warmed in aqueous acetone. State the stereochemical outcome of the substitution and give the reason.

Show answer

Racemisation (with a slight excess of inversion): the tertiary bromide ionises to a planar carbocation, which water attacks from either face.

Question 26 Basic

Hydration

Name the major product of the acid-catalysed hydration of propene and 2-methylpropene.

Show answer

Propene: propan-2-ol; 2-methylpropene: 2-methylpropan-2-ol (Markovnikov, via the more stable cation).

Question 27 Basic

Pinacol

Write the product of the pinacol rearrangement of 2,3-dimethylbutane-2,3-diol. What is the name?

Show answer

3,3-Dimethylbutan-2-one (pinacolone) after loss of water, 1,2-methyl shift and loss of H+.

Question 28 Basic

Which cation?

In the acid-catalysed hydration of but-1-ene, which carbocation forms, the 1-butyl or the 2-butyl cation, and what is the product?

Show answer

The 2-butyl (secondary) cation, CH3CH+CH2CH3, because protonation at C-1 follows Markovnikov's rule (the primary 1-butyl cation is not formed). Water adds: butan-2-ol (racemic).

Question 29 Basic

Bridgehead

Why is 1-bromobicyclo[2.2.1]heptane inert to SN1 solvolysis?

Show answer

The bridgehead cation cannot become planar (rigid bicyclic skeleton): the empty orbital cannot overlap with C–C σ bonds properly, so the cation is of very high energy (also no SN2 backside attack is possible).

Question 30 Basic

Hydride shift

Predict the product of HCl addition to vinylcyclohexane, CH2=CH–C6H11.

Show answer

Protonation at CH2 gives CH3CH+–C6H11 (secondary); a 1,2-hydride shift from the ring C–H (the carbon bearing the side chain) gives the tertiary 1-ethylcyclohexyl cation. Chloride capture: 1-chloro-1-ethylcyclohexane (major, rearranged).

C. Carbanions (Questions 31 to 43)

Question 31 Basic

Hybridisation

Order the carbanions by decreasing stability: CH3CH2−, CH2=CH−, HC≡C−.

Show answer

HC≡C− > CH2=CH− > CH3CH2− (sp 50 %, sp2 33 %, sp3 25 % s-character; pKa 25, 44, 50).

Question 32 Basic

Alkyl order

Arrange in decreasing stability: (CH3)3C−, (CH3)2CH−, CH3CH2−, CH3−.

Show answer

CH3− > CH3CH2− > (CH3)2CH− > (CH3)3C− (alkyl groups are electron-donating and destabilise the negative charge; opposite to carbocations).

Question 33 Basic

Base choice

Which of the bases NaOH, NaOEt, NaNH2, Na2CO3 can convert propyne quantitatively into its sodium salt? Why?

Show answer

Only NaNH2. Propyne has pKa about 25 (acetylene 25). The conjugate acids of the other bases (H2O 15.7, EtOH about 16, HCO3− about 10) are far stronger acids than propyne, so those bases cannot deprotonate it appreciably; NH3 (pKa about 33 to 36) is a much weaker acid than propyne.

Question 34 Basic

Grignard

Give the product of CH3CH2MgBr with (a) CO2 then H3O+; (b) acetone; (c) D2O.

Show answer

(a) Propanoic acid CH3CH2COOH. (b) After work-up 2-methylbutan-2-ol. (c) CH3CH2D (monodeuterio ethane).

Question 35 Basic

Active hydrogen

How many moles of CH4 are evolved from 1 mole of salicylic acid (o-HOC6H4COOH) with excess CH3MgI?

Show answer

2 moles (the phenolic OH and the COOH each protonate one CH3MgI).

Question 36 Basic

Ester + Grignard

Ethyl benzoate is treated with excess CH3MgBr, then H3O+. Name the product and the equivalents of Grignard used.

Show answer

2-Phenylpropan-2-ol, using 2 equivalents (the first gives acetophenone after loss of ethoxide, the second adds to the ketone).

Question 37 Basic

Aldol

Write the product of the dilute NaOH catalysed self-condensation of acetaldehyde at low temperature, and its name.

Show answer

CH3CH(OH)CH2CHO, 3-hydroxybutanal (aldol); on heating it loses water to give crotonaldehyde (but-2-enal) by E1cB.

Question 38 Basic

Haloform

Which of these give the iodoform test: CH3CH2OH, CH3CH2CH2OH, CH3COCH3, C6H5COCH3, CH3CH(OH)CH3, HCHO?

Show answer

CH3CH2OH, CH3COCH3, C6H5COCH3, CH3CH(OH)CH3 (contain CH3CO– or CH3CH(OH)–). Propan-1-ol and HCHO do not.

Question 39 Basic

Kinetic enolate

Which base and conditions give the less substituted (kinetic) enolate of 2-methylcyclohexanone?

Show answer

LDA in THF at −78 °C (bulky, strong, non-nucleophilic base removes the less hindered α-H irreversibly).

Question 40 Basic

E1cB

Name the mechanism by which 4-hydroxy-4-methylpentan-2-one dehydrates in base to mesityl oxide, and write the intermediate.

Show answer

E1cB. The base removes the α-H (between C=O and the carbinol carbon) giving the enolate CH3C(O−)=CH–C(OH)(CH3)2 ↔ carbanion; hydroxide is then expelled to give CH3COCH=C(CH3)2.

Question 41 Basic

Decarboxylation

Why does β-ketoacid CH3COCH2COOH lose CO2 on gentle heating but CH3CH2CH2COOH does not?

Show answer

The β-keto acid decarboxylates through a cyclic six-membered transition state in which the keto oxygen takes the acidic proton, giving an enol (and CO2); the C=O group stabilises the developing negative charge. A simple acid has no such acceptor.

Question 42 Basic

Favorskii

2-Chlorocyclohexanone with NaOMe gives which product?

Show answer

Methyl cyclopentanecarboxylate (ring contraction through a bicyclic cyclopropanone intermediate).

Question 43 Basic

Cannizzaro

Predict the products when benzaldehyde is treated with concentrated NaOH.

Show answer

Benzyl alcohol and sodium benzoate (hydride transfer from the hydrate anion; no α-H, so no aldol).

D. Free radicals (Questions 44 to 57)

Question 44 Basic

Radical stability

Arrange in decreasing stability: CH3•, (CH3)3C•, C6H5CH2•, CH3CH2•.

Show answer

C6H5CH2• > (CH3)3C• > CH3CH2• > CH3• (BDE of the C–H bond: 90, 96, 101, 105 kcal mol−1; the weaker the bond, the more stable the radical).

Question 45 Basic

Chain steps

Identify the initiation, propagation and termination steps: (i) Cl2 → 2Cl•; (ii) Cl• + CH4 → HCl + •CH3; (iii) •CH3 + •CH3 → C2H6; (iv) •CH3 + Cl2 → CH3Cl + Cl•.

Show answer

(i) initiation; (ii) and (iv) propagation; (iii) termination.

Question 46 Basic

Reactivity of halogens

Why does the iodination of methane not occur while fluorination is explosive?

Show answer

Iodination is endothermic overall (ΔH ≈ +12 kcal mol−1 from the BDE table) and the first propagation step, I• + CH4, is +34 kcal mol−1, too slow to carry a chain. Fluorination is violently exothermic (ΔH ≈ −109 kcal mol−1); both steps are strongly exothermic, so heat builds up in a runaway chain and C–C bonds can be cleaved.

Question 47 Basic

NBS

What does NBS do, and why is a low concentration of Br2 important?

Show answer

NBS supplies a low steady concentration of Br2 (from NBS + HBr), allowing radical allylic or benzylic substitution; at high [Br2] ionic addition across the C=C competes.

Question 48 Basic

Allylic bromination

Give the product of NBS/hν with cyclohexene.

Show answer
NBS, hν⟶
3-Bromocyclohexene
Question 49 Basic

Peroxide effect

Write the major product of propene + HBr in the presence of benzoyl peroxide.

Show answer

1-Bromopropane (anti-Markovnikov, radical chain: Br• adds to the terminal carbon to give the secondary radical).

Question 50 Basic

Peroxide effect scope

For which HX does the peroxide effect operate: HF, HCl, HBr, HI?

Show answer

HBr only. HCl: the H–Cl bond is too strong (the abstraction R• + HCl is endothermic); HI: I• adds to the alkene reversibly (endothermic); HF: H–F too strong.

Question 51 Basic

Side-chain chlorination

What are the products of toluene with Cl2 (a) in sunlight and (b) with FeCl3 in the dark?

Show answer

(a) Benzyl chloride (and benzal chloride, benzotrichloride on further reaction): radical substitution at the benzylic C–H. (b) o- and p-Chlorotoluene: electrophilic substitution on the ring.

Question 52 Basic

Kolbe

What is formed at the anode on electrolysis of aqueous sodium acetate?

Show answer

Ethane and CO2 (2CH3COO− → CH3CH3 + 2CO2 + 2e−); H2 and NaOH at the cathode.

Question 53 Basic

Hunsdiecker

Convert butanoic acid into 1-bromopropane. Name the reaction.

Show answer

CH3CH2CH2COOH → silver salt (Ag2O) → CH3CH2CH2COOAg + Br2 (CCl4, reflux) → CH3CH2CH2Br + CO2 + AgBr. Hunsdiecker reaction (one carbon less).

Question 54 Basic

Inhibitors

Why does a trace of hydroquinone stop the polymerisation of styrene initiated by benzoyl peroxide?

Show answer

Hydroquinone donates a hydrogen atom to the growing radical (forming a resonance-stabilised phenoxyl radical that does not propagate), terminating chains: an inhibitor.

Question 55 Basic

Termination

Write one combination and one disproportionation termination for two ethyl radicals.

Show answer

Combination: 2CH3CH2• → CH3CH2CH2CH3 (butane). Disproportionation: CH3CH2• + •CH2CH3 → CH3CH3 + CH2=CH2.

Question 56 Basic

Isomers

How many structural isomers of C5H11Cl are formed on photochlorination of 2-methylbutane?

Show answer

4: 1-chloro-2-methylbutane, 2-chloro-2-methylbutane, 2-chloro-3-methylbutane and 1-chloro-3-methylbutane.

Question 57 Basic

Radical rearrangement

Can a 1,2-hydride shift convert a primary radical into a secondary radical? Why do radical reactions of alkanes show no skeletal rearrangement?

Show answer

No. 1,2-H and 1,2-alkyl shifts in radicals have high barriers (the transition state has three electrons in a three-centre bond; no electron-deficiency to drive it). Cations are electron-deficient and shift readily.

E. Carbenes and nitrenes (Questions 58 to 71)

Question 58 Basic

Singlet or triplet

Which spin state of a carbene gives stereospecific addition to alkenes? Why?

Show answer

Singlet: both electrons paired in one orbital; the addition is concerted (one step), so the alkene geometry is retained. Triplet carbenes add stepwise through a diradical that can rotate.

Question 59 Basic

Dichlorocarbene

Write the equations that generate dichlorocarbene from chloroform with KOH.

Show answer

CHCl3 + HO− ⇌ CCl3− + H2O; CCl3− → :CCl2 + Cl− (α-elimination).

Question 60 Basic

Carbylamine

Which gives a positive carbylamine test: CH3NH2, (CH3)2NH, C6H5NH2, (CH3)3N? Write the product for one.

Show answer

CH3NH2 and C6H5NH2 (primary amines). CH3NH2 + CHCl3 + 3KOH → CH3NC + 3KCl + 3H2O (isocyanide, foul smell).

Question 61 Basic

Reimer–Tiemann

Give the major organic product of phenol with CHCl3/aq. NaOH at 340 K followed by acidification.

Show answer

Salicylaldehyde (2-hydroxybenzaldehyde); a little 4-hydroxybenzaldehyde. The electrophile is :CCl2.

Question 62 Basic

Simmons–Smith

What is formed from cyclopentene with CH2I2 and Zn(Cu)?

Show answer

Bicyclo[3.1.0]hexane, formed by syn addition of the carbenoid ICH2ZnI.

Question 63 Basic

Cyclopropanation

What is the stereochemistry of the product from trans-2-butene and dichlorocarbene?

Show answer

trans-1,1-Dichloro-2,3-dimethylcyclopropane (the two methyls remain trans; singlet carbene addition is stereospecific).

Question 64 Basic

Arndt–Eistert

Convert benzoic acid to phenylacetic acid.

Show answer

PhCOOH → SOCl2 → PhCOCl → CH2N2 (2 equiv.) → PhCOCHN2 → Ag2O, H2O (Wolff rearrangement to ketene PhCH=C=O) → PhCH2COOH (one carbon longer).

Question 65 Basic

Diazomethane

What is the product of benzoic acid with diazomethane? What is the by-product?

Show answer

Methyl benzoate; nitrogen, N2.

Question 66 Basic

Hofmann

Give the amine from (a) propanamide, (b) 2-methylpropanamide, (c) phenylacetamide on treatment with Br2/NaOH.

Show answer

(a) Ethanamine. (b) Propan-2-amine (isopropylamine). (c) Benzylamine (C6H5CH2NH2). Each has one carbon fewer than the amide.

Question 67 Basic

Curtius

Write the products when benzoyl azide is heated and the resulting compound is hydrolysed with water.

Show answer

PhCON3 → PhN=C=O + N2 (phenyl isocyanate); + H2O → PhNHCOOH → aniline + CO2.

Question 68 Basic

Nitrene

What type of electron-deficient species is formed when an organic azide loses N2?

Show answer

A nitrene (R–N:, six electrons on nitrogen), singlet or triplet.

Question 69 Basic

Insertion

Which of the following does singlet methylene do: add to C=C, insert into C–H, abstract H? Which does the triplet prefer?

Show answer

Singlet :CH2 adds to C=C (stereospecifically) and inserts into C–H (indiscriminately). Triplet :CH2 abstracts hydrogen (radical-like), then recombines.

Question 70 Basic

Ketene

In the Wolff rearrangement, what is the intermediate formed from an α-diazoketone?

Show answer

A ketene (R–CH=C=O), formed by 1,2-shift of R to the carbene carbon after loss of N2.

Question 71 Basic

Carbenoid

Why is the Simmons–Smith reagent called a carbenoid rather than a carbene?

Show answer

The carbon is bound to zinc (ICH2ZnI) and transfers CH2 without ever being a free :CH2; it behaves like a carbene but is milder, selective and directed by OH groups.

F. Arynes (Questions 72 to 77)

Question 72 Basic

Benzyne from chlorobenzene

Name the intermediate formed when chlorobenzene reacts with NaNH2 in liquid ammonia and describe how it is formed.

Show answer

Benzyne (1,2-didehydrobenzene). NH2− removes the H ortho to Cl (acidic by −I of Cl); the aryl anion expels Cl−, forming the strained in-plane "triple bond".

Question 73 Basic

Cine substitution

Define cine substitution and give one example.

Show answer

The incoming group takes a position adjacent to that vacated by the leaving group. Example: o-chlorotoluene + NaNH2 gives m-toluidine (and o-toluidine).

Question 74 Basic

No ortho H

Will 2-bromo-1,3-dimethylbenzene react with NaNH2/liquid NH3? Explain.

Show answer

No. Both positions ortho to Br carry CH3, so no ortho hydrogen is available to eliminate; benzyne cannot form.

Question 75 Basic

Trapping

What is formed when benzyne is generated in the presence of furan?

Show answer

1,4-Dihydro-1,4-epoxynaphthalene (Diels–Alder adduct; furan is the diene, benzyne the dienophile).

Question 76 Basic

Triptycene

Which hydrocarbon forms from benzyne and anthracene?

Show answer

Triptycene (C20H14), by [4+2] across C-9/C-10.

Question 77 Basic

Products from p-chlorotoluene

How many toluidines are formed from p-chlorotoluene with NaNH2?

Show answer

Two: m-toluidine and p-toluidine (from 4-methylbenzyne, about 1 : 1).

G. Energy diagrams, Hammond, kinetic and thermodynamic control (Questions 78 to 83)

Question 78 Basic

Rate-determining step

For a reaction R → I → P with Ea1 = 100 kJ mol−1 and Ea2 = 60 kJ mol−1 (both barriers measured from the preceding well; the intermediate I lies 10 kJ mol−1 above R), which step is rate-determining?

Show answer

The first step (higher barrier): it controls the overall rate.

Question 79 Basic

Kinetic or thermodynamic

HBr adds to buta-1,3-diene at −80 °C giving mainly the 1,2-adduct and at 40 °C mainly the 1,4-adduct. Which is the kinetic product and which the thermodynamic?

Show answer

1,2-adduct (3-bromobut-1-ene) = kinetic (forms faster); 1,4-adduct (1-bromobut-2-ene) = thermodynamic (more stable, disubstituted alkene).

Question 80 Basic

Catalysis

Does a catalyst change ΔH or the equilibrium constant of a reaction? What does it change?

Show answer

No. It lowers the activation energy (a lower pathway), speeding up both forward and reverse reactions equally.

Question 81 Basic

Enolate

Which enolate of 2-methylcyclohexanone is the thermodynamic enolate and how can it be made?

Show answer

The more substituted one (the C-1/C-2 enolate double bond, trisubstituted): with NaH or NaOEt at 25 °C under equilibrating conditions.

Question 82 Basic

Temperature and selectivity

Two products form irreversibly via TS 5.7 kJ mol−1 apart. How does the product ratio at 400 K compare with that at 298 K?

Show answer

At 298 K: ratio 10 : 1; at 400 K: 5.5 : 1 (exp(5700/RT)). Higher temperature lowers the selectivity.

Question 83 Basic

Reversibility

A kinetic product is warmed with acid and slowly changes into an isomer. What does this show?

Show answer

The kinetic product re-forms the intermediate (the reaction is reversible at the higher temperature), so the system relaxes to the thermodynamic product, the more stable isomer.

H. Further foundation problems (Questions 84 to 94)

Question 84 Basic

Early or late transition state

For an exothermic one-step reaction, does the transition state resemble the reactants or the products? What would change for an endothermic step?

Show answer

The reactants: when the step is strongly exothermic the transition state lies close in energy to the reactants and therefore close in structure (an early transition state). For an endothermic step the transition state resembles the products (late).

Question 85 Basic

Aromatic anion

Why is cyclopentadiene (pKa 16 in water) so much more acidic than cyclohexa-1,3-diene?

Show answer

Its conjugate base is the cyclopentadienide ion, a planar cyclic anion with six π electrons (aromatic). The conjugate base of cyclohexa-1,3-diene still has an sp3 CH2 in the ring, so its conjugation is not cyclic and it has no aromatic stabilisation.

Question 86 Basic

An isocyanate from an amide

Name the species that forms from the N-bromoamide anion of benzamide when the phenyl group migrates and bromide leaves, and the product of its hydrolysis.

Show answer

Phenyl isocyanate, C6H5–N=C=O; hydrolysis gives the carbamate PhNHCOO−, which loses CO2 to give aniline.

Question 87 Basic

A persistent radical

Why is the triphenylmethyl radical long-lived, and what is the structure of its dimer?

Show answer

The unpaired electron is delocalised into three phenyl rings (spin density at the central carbon and at ortho and para ring carbons), and the central carbon is shielded sterically. The dimer is not hexaphenylethane but a cyclohexadiene derivative in which the central carbon of one radical is bonded to a para carbon of a ring of the other.

Question 88 Basic

Ambident anion

An enolate is treated with (a) iodomethane in THF and (b) dimethyl sulfate in DMSO. At which atom does each electrophile mainly react, and why? (For (b), compare with (a).)

Show answer

(a) Carbon: iodomethane is a soft electrophile and THF favours a tight lithium–enolate ion pair; the reaction is controlled by the HOMO, which is largest on carbon. (b) A larger share of oxygen than in (a): dimethyl sulfate is a hard electrophile (a sulfate ester) and DMSO separates the ion pair, so the reaction is increasingly controlled by charge, which is largest on oxygen. (For a simple ketone enolate, C-alkylation can still compete; O-alkylation dominates for more delocalised enolates such as those of 1,3-dicarbonyl compounds.)

Question 89 Basic

Terminology

Distinguish (a) a transition state from an intermediate, (b) a nucleophile from a base, (c) molecularity from order.

Show answer

(a) A transition state is the energy maximum of one elementary step and has no lifetime; an intermediate is an energy minimum between two steps and can in principle be detected or trapped. (b) A base is measured by its affinity for a proton (an equilibrium); a nucleophile by the rate at which it attacks an atom such as carbon. (c) Molecularity is the number of species in one elementary step (1, 2 or 3); order is the experimental sum of the exponents in the overall rate law, which may be zero or fractional.

Question 90 Basic

Which solvent?

For each, state the better solvent type: (a) SN2 reaction of CN− with 1-bromobutane; (b) solvolysis of tert-butyl chloride; (c) free-radical bromination of cyclohexane with NBS.

Show answer

(a) A polar aprotic solvent such as DMSO or DMF, which leaves the anion unsolvated. (b) A polar protic solvent (aqueous ethanol), which stabilises the ions formed. (c) A non-polar solvent (CCl4 or similar), which favours radical intermediates and disfavours ionic addition.

Question 91 Basic

Count the intermediates

The acid-catalysed hydration of propene to propan-2-ol is written with three steps: protonation, capture by water, deprotonation. How many transition states and how many intermediates are there, and name the intermediates?

Show answer

Three transition states and two intermediates: the isopropyl cation and the protonated alcohol (isopropyloxonium ion, (CH3)2CHOH2+).

Question 92 Basic

Radical or ionic?

State whether each is more likely ionic or radical and give the reason: (a) Cl2 and light with methane; (b) HBr and peroxide with propene; (c) HBr in the dark with propene; (d) Br2 in CH2Cl2 with cyclohexene.

Show answer

(a) Radical (light, non-polar medium, alkane). (b) Radical (peroxide initiator, anti-Markovnikov). (c) Ionic (Markovnikov addition through a cation or a termolecular mechanism). (d) Ionic, through a bromonium ion (anti addition).

Question 93 Basic

Carbocation from a diazonium ion

Why does an aliphatic primary amine with nitrous acid give a mixture of products, whereas an arenediazonium salt at 0 °C is stable enough to be used in the Sandmeyer reaction?

Show answer

The aliphatic diazonium ion loses N2 at once to give an alkyl cation, which is captured, rearranges or loses a proton to give mixtures of alcohols and alkenes. An aryl cation would be of very high energy (empty sp2 orbital in the ring plane, no stabilisation), so the arenediazonium ion survives at low temperature and reacts by other pathways (for example single-electron transfer from copper(I)).

Question 94 Basic

Conjugation and acidity

Explain why the hydrogens of the CH3 group of propene are more acidic than those of propane.

Show answer

Removing a proton from the CH3 of propene gives the allyl anion, whose lone pair is delocalised over two carbons (the Hückel nonbonding orbital with coefficients on the terminal carbons), whereas propane gives a localised alkyl anion. The pKa values (propene about 38 in DMF) show that propene is a stronger acid than methane (48) or ethane (about 51).
