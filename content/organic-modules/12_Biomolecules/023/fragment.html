4.2 Polynucleotides and the structure of DNA

Nucleotides are joined by 3′→5′ phosphodiester bonds: the 3′-OH of one nucleotide is esterified to the 5′-phosphate of the next. A chain thus has a free 5′-phosphate end and a free 3′-OH end; sequences are written 5′ → 3′ (e.g. 5′-ATGC-3′).

  • Sugar–phosphate backbone (hydrophilic, outside) and bases (inside, stacked).
  • Each internucleotide phosphate is a diester, with one acidic OH (pKa ≈ 1) so is fully ionised at pH 7.

The Watson–Crick double helix (B-DNA)

  • Two antiparallel strands (one 5′→3′, the other 3′→5′) wind into a right-handed double helix around a common axis.
  • Base pairing: A pairs with T by two hydrogen bonds; G pairs with C by three. Always a purine with a pyrimidine, so the width (≈ 20 Å = 2 nm diameter) is uniform.
  • Chargaff's rules: %A = %T, %G = %C, so purines = pyrimidines (A + G = T + C); but (A + T)/(G + C) varies between species. These rules hold for double-stranded DNA only, not for single-stranded or RNA.
  • B-form dimensions: 10 base pairs per turn (10.5 in solution), 3.4 Å rise per base pair, pitch 34 Å (3.4 nm), diameter ≈ 20 Å; major and minor grooves.
  • Stabilised by H-bonds between bases, π-stacking between adjacent base pairs (the main stabilising factor), the hydrophobic effect and electrostatic screening by cations (Mg2+, Na+).
  • Melting temperature (Tm): the temperature at which half the helix has separated. Higher G + C content → higher Tm (three H-bonds, better stacking). For short oligonucleotides (< 14 nt), the Wallace rule: Tm ≈ 2(A + T) + 4(G + C) °C.
adenine–thymine pair (2 H-bonds) guanine–cytosine pair (3 H-bonds)
The pairs drawn as separate molecules: A···T through N–H···O and N–H···N; G···C through three H-bonds
Shortcut Hydrogen-bond count for a duplex = 2 × (number of A–T pairs) + 3 × (number of G–C pairs). A 20-bp duplex with 30 % G + C has 6 G–C and 14 A–T pairs: 18 + 28 = 46 H-bonds.

Trap Hydrolysis products differ in conditions. Complete acid hydrolysis of DNA gives bases + deoxyribose + H3PO4. Controlled enzymatic hydrolysis (nuclease, phosphatase) goes polynucleotide → nucleotides → nucleosides → base + sugar. RNA is hydrolysed by cold dilute alkali (the 2′-OH attacks the neighbouring phosphate, forming a 2′,3′-cyclic phosphate), while DNA is stable to alkali. This is the chemical reason why the genetic material is DNA.
