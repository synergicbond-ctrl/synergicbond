6. Practice questions

Q1. Rank aniline, p-nitroaniline and N,N-dimethylaniline in increasing order of basicity.
Answerp-nitroaniline < aniline < N,N-dimethylaniline. The pKa values of the conjugate acids are 1.0, 4.60 and 5.06 (Ref. [1], Table 19-3).
Q2. Rank benzoic acid, p-methoxybenzoic acid and p-nitrobenzoic acid in increasing order of acid strength.
Answerp-methoxybenzoic acid < benzoic acid < p-nitrobenzoic acid. The pKa values are 4.46, 4.19 and 3.41 (Ref. [1], Table 20-4). A lower pKa means a stronger acid.
Q3. Why is aniline a weaker base than cyclohexylamine? (Cyclohexylamine has pKa 10.67 for its conjugate acid, Ref. [1], Table 19-3.)
AnswerIn aniline the nitrogen lone pair overlaps with the ring π system and is delocalised. On protonation this overlap is lost, so the lone pair is less stabilised in the free amine than in cyclohexylamine, and aniline is less basic (Ref. [1], Section 19-6).
Q4. A bulky ortho group forces the NMe₂ group of a dimethylaniline out of the ring plane. Which effect changes, π or σ? What is the expected direction of the change in basicity?
AnswerOnly the π-effect changes: the lone pair is less delocalised into the ring, so it is more available for protonation. The σ-effect (inductive +I of the methyls) is unchanged. The expected direction is an increase in basicity. This is an SIR-based prediction; no measured value is given in this file.
Q5. Rank p-bromoaniline, aniline and p-methoxyaniline in increasing order of basicity, and say which substituent acts by resonance.
Answerp-bromoaniline < aniline < p-methoxyaniline (pKaH 3.8, 4.60, 5.3). The methoxy group acts by resonance donation (+M), which raises basicity.
Q6. Which of m-nitrobenzoic acid, p-nitrobenzoic acid and o-nitrobenzoic acid is the strongest acid, and what is its pKa?
Answero-nitrobenzoic acid, with pKa 2.16 (Ref. [1], Table 20-4). It has the lowest pKa of the three.
Q7. Why does the triphenylmethyl radical show only partial delocalisation, yet remain very stable?
AnswerIts phenyl rings are twisted about 30° out of a common plane, which reduces overlap and delocalisation. The high stability is attributed mainly to steric shielding of the central carbon by the twisted rings (Ref. [2], p. 979).
