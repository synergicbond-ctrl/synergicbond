2.1 The five-step method for a branched alkane

Definition. A branched alkane is named as a substituted straight-chain alkane: the parent is the longest continuous chain, and the branches are substituents.

  • Step 1, parent chain. Find the longest continuous chain of carbon atoms. It is the chain defined by connectivity, not the chain that happens to be drawn straight; bends in the drawing are irrelevant.
  • Step 2, ties. If two or more chains have the same length, take the one with the greater number of substituents.
  • Step 3, numbering. Number from the end that gives the lowest set of locants. Compare the sets from both directions term by term and stop at the first difference.
  • Step 4, naming. Name each substituent, group identical ones with di, tri, tetra and cite every locant (2,2-dimethyl, never 2-dimethyl).
  • Step 5, assembly. Write the substituents in alphabetical order before the parent name.
IMP The chain is chosen before the numbering, and the numbering is chosen before the alphabetical order. Alphabetical order decides the numbering only when every earlier criterion is tied (§2.4).
Why It Happens Choosing the longest chain as the parent makes the branches as small as possible, so that substituent names stay simple (methyl, ethyl). Choosing, among equal chains, the one with more substituents keeps the substituents themselves small for the same reason.

Example 4 Direct application

Name the alkane (the drawing hides the longest chain).

Show solution
  1. Two chains of 6 carbons exist. One runs through the isopropyl group: CH₃–CH(CH₃)–CH(Et)–CH₂CH₂CH₃, with substituents ethyl and methyl. The other runs through the ethyl group and carries a single isopropyl substituent.
  2. Tie in length → choose the chain with more substituents: the first one (2 substituents against 1).
  3. Numbering from the isopropyl end gives locants {2,3}; from the other end {4,5}. Choose {2,3}.
  4. Alphabetical order: ethyl (e) before methyl (m).

3-Ethyl-2-methylhexane — not 3-(propan-2-yl)hexane, which has the same chain length but fewer substituents.

Example 5 Standard

Name the alkane.

Show solution
  1. Longest chain: C1 CH₃, C2 CH₂, C3 CH(Et), C4 CH₂, C5 CH(CH₃), C6 CH₂, C7 CH₃ = 7 carbons.
  2. Locants from the left: ethyl 3, methyl 5 → {3,5}. From the right: methyl 3, ethyl 5 → {3,5}. A tie in the locant set.
  3. Tie-break: the substituent cited first in alphabetical order gets the lower locant. Ethyl precedes methyl, so ethyl = 3.

3-Ethyl-5-methylheptane.
