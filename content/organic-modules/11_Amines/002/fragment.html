2. Classification, nomenclature and isomerism

Amines are derivatives of NH3 in which one, two or three H atoms are replaced by alkyl or aryl groups. The class depends on the number of carbon groups attached to nitrogen, not on the carbon that bears it (contrast with alcohols).

Trap tert-Butylamine, (CH3)3C–NH2, is a primary amine – the carbon bearing N is tertiary but N has only one carbon attached. Likewise, benzylamine C6H5CH2NH2 is primary and aliphatic (the N is not on the ring), so it does not give a stable diazonium salt.

IUPAC rules for amines

  • Common-name system: alkyl groups are named alphabetically followed by "amine" as one word: ethylmethylamine, dimethylamine, triethylamine.
  • IUPAC substitutive name, 1° amines: replace the final –e of the parent alkane by –amine and give the locant of NH2 the lowest number consistent with the principal chain: propan-2-amine, butane-1,4-diamine (the final e is retained before a consonant).
  • 2° and 3° amines: the longest chain is the parent; the other groups are N-substituents (locant N): N-methylethanamine, N,N-dimethylpropan-2-amine. Several N-groups are cited alphabetically with N or N,N.
  • Aromatic amines: aniline is the retained name and is the parent (benzenamine is also allowed). Substituted: 4-methylaniline (p-toluidine), N-methylaniline, N,N-dimethylaniline, 2,4,6-tribromoaniline.
  • Priority: the amine ranks below carboxylic acid, ester, amide, nitrile, aldehyde, ketone and alcohol. When a higher group is present, –NH2 is cited as the prefix amino: H2N–CH2CH2–COOH is 3-aminopropanoic acid and H2N–CH2CH2CH(OH)CH3 is 4-aminobutan-2-ol (the alcohol outranks the amine).

Example 1 Main

Isomer count

How many amines (all classes) have the formula C3H9N, and how many of them are optically active?

Show solution
  1. Primary: CH3CH2CH2NH2 and (CH3)2CHNH2 – two.
  2. Secondary: CH3NHCH2CH3 – one. Tertiary: (CH3)3N – one.
  3. Total 4. None has a stereocentre (nitrogen inversion is fast and no carbon is bonded to four different groups), so 0 are optically active.
Example 2 Main

Naming

Give the IUPAC names of (a) CH3CH(NH2)CH2CH3, (b) (CH3CH2)2NCH3, (c) C6H5N(CH3)CH2CH3, (d) H2NCH2CH2CH2NH2.

Show solution
  1. (a) Butan-2-amine. (b) The longest chain is ethane on one ethyl: N-ethyl-N-methylethanamine. (c) Aniline is the parent: N-ethyl-N-methylaniline. (d) Propane-1,3-diamine.

Some nitrogen compounds that must not be confused with amines

  • Amides R–CO–NH2: the lone pair is delocalised into C=O; neutral, not basic like amines.
  • Nitriles R–C≡N: N is sp-hybridised; extremely weak base. Isocyanides R–N+≡C−.
  • Nitro compounds R–NO2; nitroso R–N=O; azo Ar–N=N–Ar; hydrazine H2N–NH2 and hydroxylamine NH2OH.
  • Imines C=N–R, oximes C=N–OH, enamines C=C–N.
