Number of alkenes

How many alkenes (counting stereoisomers) form on E2 elimination from 2-bromo-2,3-dimethylpentane?

Show answer

Practice 148 Adv

β-Branched primary halide

Isobutyl bromide gives ≈ 60 % alkene with NaOEt/EtOH while n-butyl bromide gives ≈ 10 %. Explain.

Show answer
  1. Both are primary, but in isobutyl bromide the β-carbon carries two methyl groups that hinder the back-side approach of EtO− to the α-carbon (SN2 slowed by ≈ 30-fold), whereas E2 requires attack on the β-hydrogen at the outside of the molecule and is little affected. The proportion of elimination therefore rises sharply.

Practice 151 Adv

Neopentyl

Why does neopentyl bromide, (CH3)3CCH2Br, not give an alkene with alcoholic KOH even though it is a bromide with β-carbon? How do hot solvolysis conditions change the picture?

Show answer
  1. The β-carbon is quaternary: it has no hydrogen to lose in E2. SN2 is blocked sterically. Under solvolysis (ethanol, heat) ionisation concerted with 1,2-methyl shift gives the tertiary cation and then E1 products (2-methylbut-2-ene, 2-methylbut-1-ene) together with the ether.
Practice 152 Adv

Stereochemistry of E1 vs E2

(2S,3S)-2-Bromo-3-methylpentane is heated (a) with NaOEt (b) in boiling ethanol with no base. Compare the alkene stereochemistry obtained.

Show answer
  1. (a) E2 is stereospecific: from the (S,S) diastereomer only the (E)-alkene forms (anti H/Br places CH3 and C2H5 trans). (b) E1 goes through a planar cation, so there is no stereospecificity: the more stable (E)-3-methylpent-2-ene predominates but the (Z)-isomer also forms. The 2° cation can also shift hydride from C3 to give the 3° cation CH3CH2C+(CH3)CH2CH3, which loses H+ to give 3-methylpent-2-ene and 2-ethylbut-1-ene.

Cyclic substrates

Predict the product of E2 on trans-1-bromo-2-methylcyclohexane and on cis-1-bromo-2-methylcyclohexane with NaOEt, and explain the difference.

Show answer
  1. trans: Br axial requires CH3 axial too (trans-diaxial); the C2–H is then equatorial, so only the C6 axial H can be lost: 3-methylcyclohexene. cis: Br axial, CH3 equatorial, so the axial C2–H is anti to Br: 1-methylcyclohexene (Zaitsev, major).
Practice 154 Adv

Bicyclic

In a rigid norbornane skeleton, an exo-2-bromo substituent has no anti-periplanar β-hydrogen. Which hydrogens are closest to the required geometry, and what does this predict for E2 of exo-2-bromonorbornane?

Show answer
  1. On C3 the exo-H is syn-periplanar to exo-Br (dihedral ≈ 0°) and the endo-H makes ≈ 120°: no H is anti (180°). The bridgehead H on C1 would give an anti-Bredt alkene and cannot be removed. E2 therefore either goes through the less favourable syn-periplanar transition state (exo-H, exo-Br) or is very slow; with a strong bulky base it occurs by syn elimination.

Dehydrohalogenation to alkyne

Predict the product and the number of equivalents of base: (a) 1,2-dibromopropane with NaNH2 (liquid NH3), then H2O (b) 2,2-dibromo-3-methylbutane with excess NaNH2 (c) 2,3-dibromobutane with a large excess of NaNH2 at high temperature, then H2O.

Show answer
  1. (a) Propyne. Two E2 steps (2 equiv); the terminal alkyne is deprotonated by the third equivalent to the acetylide, so 3 equiv are used. Water liberates propyne.
  2. (b) Only the CH3 on C1 and the C3–H are β to C2. An alkyne between C2 and C3 is impossible (C3 would be a quaternary alkyne carbon); the alkyne must be C1≡C2: 3-methylbut-1-yne (3 equiv, as the acetylide).
  3. (c) 2,3-Dibromobutane first gives but-2-yne (2 equiv). At high temperature and with excess NaNH2 the internal alkyne isomerises (through allene) to the sodium salt of but-1-yne, the thermodynamic sink in strong base; water gives but-1-yne.

Wurtz count

How many different alkanes can be formed by the Wurtz reaction of a mixture of CH3Br, CH3CH2Br and CH3CH2CH2Br?

Show answer
  1. Three halides give 3 + 3 = 6 coupling combinations: CH3–CH3 (ethane), CH3–C2H5 (propane), CH3–C3H7 (butane), C2H5–C2H5 (butane), C2H5–C3H7 (pentane), C3H7–C3H7 (hexane).

Corey–House

Outline the synthesis of 2-methylbutane from (a) CH3CH2Br and (CH3)2CHBr (b) explain why Wurtz coupling is poor here.

Show answer
  1. (a) CH3CH2Br + 2 Li → CH3CH2Li; + CuI → (CH3CH2)2CuLi; + (CH3)2CHBr → CH3CH2–CH(CH3)2 (2-methylbutane). A secondary halide as the electrophile gives moderate yield; better to make ((CH3)2CH)2CuLi and use CH3CH2Br (primary electrophile).

Grignard side-reactions

A hindered ketone (di-tert-butyl ketone) and a Grignard reagent with β-hydrogens (isopropylmagnesium bromide) give mostly recovered ketone and the reduced alcohol. Explain.

Show answer
  1. Addition is blocked sterically. Two other paths exist: (i) enolisation (Grignard as base) which returns the ketone after work-up; (ii) reduction by β-hydride transfer from the Grignard (six-membered cyclic transition state, as in a Meerwein–Ponndorf type), giving the secondary alcohol and propene.
