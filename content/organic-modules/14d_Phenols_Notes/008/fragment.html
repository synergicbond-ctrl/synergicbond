9. Coupling of phenols with diazonium salts

The diazonium ion is a weak electrophile, so it couples only with strongly activated rings. Phenols couple in mildly alkaline solution (pH about 9–10), where a substantial fraction is present as the very reactive phenoxide. Coupling is an electrophilic aromatic substitution at the para position (ortho if para is blocked), giving a brightly coloured azo compound, the basis of azo dyes. The proton lost is replaced by the –N=N–Ar group.

Reactant:

Reagent: aqueous NaOH, 273–278 K

Product:

p-Hydroxyazobenzene (orange). Benzenediazonium chloride + phenoxide.

The pH window matters. In strongly alkaline solution the diazonium ion is converted into the unreactive diazotate (Ar–N=N–O–), so coupling fails; in acidic solution phenol is not ionised, so its reactivity is too low. For aromatic amines the opposite window is used (pH about 4–5) so that the free amine is available and the diazonium ion is not lost as a diazoamino compound. 2-Naphthol couples at C-1, the ring position adjacent to the carbon bearing OH (same ring).

Reactant:

Reagent: aqueous NaOH, 273–278 K

Product:

1-(Phenylazo)-2-naphthol, an orange-red dye.

Reactant:

Reagent: aqueous NaOH, 273–278 K

Product:

The para position of p-cresol is occupied, so coupling occurs ortho to OH.

Key idea. Azo coupling follows the rule: attack para to the activating group if free, otherwise ortho. A diazonium ion will not couple with benzene, nitrobenzene or even with anisole under ordinary conditions; it needs –O–, –NR2 or –NH2 activation.
