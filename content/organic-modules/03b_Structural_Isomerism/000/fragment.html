Structural isomerism: what it is

Structural (constitutional) isomers have the same molecular formula but different connectivity of atoms. Stereoisomers, which differ only in the arrangement in space, are a separate topic.

Kinds of structural isomerism. Chain: different carbon skeletons (butane and 2-methylpropane). Position: the same skeleton, the group in a different place (butan-1-ol and butan-2-ol). Functional group: different functional groups (ethanol and dimethyl ether). Metamerism: different alkyl groups on either side of the same functional group (diethyl ether and methyl propyl ether). Tautomerism: two forms that interconvert by moving a proton (keto and enol). Ring-chain: a ring and an open chain (cyclopropane and propene).
Counting method. Compute the degrees of unsaturation (DBE) from the formula: DBE = (2C + 2 + N − H − X)/2. Then list skeletons systematically (longest chain first, then branches), place the functional group on every non-equivalent position, and remove duplicates by checking whether two drawings are the same structure rotated or flipped.
