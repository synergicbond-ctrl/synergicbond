6.6 DCC coupling, step by step

+ Step 1: acid adds to C=N⟶
Boc-Ala-OH + DCC → O-acylisourea (an activated "anhydride-like" ester)
+ Step 2: amine attacks the acyl C=O⟶ +
Step 3 collapses the tetrahedral intermediate, ejecting dicyclohexylurea (DCU): Boc-Ala-Gly-OMe
  • Step 1. The carboxylic acid O–H adds across the cumulene N=C=N. MO view: the central carbon of the carbodiimide has a low-lying π* (LUMO) in the plane of one C=N; the carboxylate lone pair (HOMO) fills it, while the N picks up the proton.
  • Step 2. The amine lone pair (HOMO) attacks the acyl carbonyl carbon (π*), now strongly activated: the leaving group is the urea (poor basicity, very stable C=O in DCU).
  • Step 3. The tetrahedral intermediate collapses: C=O re-forms, DCU leaves. Why it works: DCC converts the poor leaving group –OH into the excellent leaving group –OC(=NR)NHR, whose departure is driven by forming the strong C=O and a stable urea. The overall driving force is the hydration of DCC to DCU.
Trap The O-acylisourea can rearrange (O → N acyl shift) to an unreactive N-acylurea, or cyclise to an oxazolone and racemise the amino acid. HOBt intercepts the O-acylisourea as a less racemisation-prone active ester.
