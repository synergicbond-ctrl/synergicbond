6. Detection of carbon and hydrogen

Every organic compound contains carbon, and almost all contain hydrogen. Both are detected together: the dry compound is mixed with about ten times its mass of dry copper(II) oxide in a hard-glass tube and strongly heated. The oxide oxidises carbon to CO2 and hydrogen to water:

Formula C + 2CuO → CO2 + 2Cu 2H + CuO → H2O + Cu (overall for a hydrocarbon: CxHy + (2x + y/2) CuO → xCO2 + (y/2)H2O + (2x + y/2)Cu)
  • The gas is led through lime water, which turns milky because of CaCO3: CO2 + Ca(OH)2 → CaCO3↓ + H2O (excess CO2 dissolves the precipitate as Ca(HCO3)2).
  • Water vapour condenses on the cooler part of the tube and turns anhydrous (white) CuSO4 blue: CuSO4 + 5H2O → CuSO4·5H2O. (Or turns blue cobalt chloride paper pink.)
Exam Trap The compound and the CuO must be perfectly dry; a damp sample would give water even if the compound has no hydrogen. Carbonates and cyanides would also give CO2 without being an organic carbon test, which is why the test is used only on organic samples.
