(A) ethanal (B) ethanol (C) propan-2-ol (D) butan-2-ol (E) propanone (F) butanone
Show answer

  4. (D) butan-2-ol: positive – contains CH3CH(OH)– (oxidised in situ to CH3CO–).

Cannizzaro reaction: scope and products

Practice 52 Main

Does benzaldehyde undergo the Cannizzaro reaction with concentrated NaOH? If so give the two products (after acidification, the acid).

benzaldehyde
Show answer

Yes – the alcohol + benzoic acid (1 : 1)

  1. The aldehyde has no α-hydrogen, so no enolate can form and aldol is impossible; in conc. NaOH it disproportionates.
  2. Half the molecules are reduced to the alcohol (hydride acceptor), half are oxidised to the carboxylate (hydride donor).

Short-answer reasoning

Practice 207 Main

Why?

Why do aldehydes reduce Tollens' reagent but ketones do not?

Show answer

Aldehydes have an H on the carbonyl carbon (and form an easily oxidised hydrate); ketones have no such H, and oxidising them requires C–C bond cleavage.

Practice 208 Main

Why?

Why does benzaldehyde not reduce Fehling's solution, though it does reduce Tollens' reagent?

Show answer

Conjugation with the benzene ring lowers the reactivity of its CHO group, so the weaker, aqueous Cu2+ tartrate oxidant does not oxidise it, while the stronger Ag(I) complex does.

Practice 209 Main

Why?

Why are the α-hydrogens of carbonyl compounds acidic?

Show answer

The conjugate base (enolate) is stabilised by delocalising the negative charge onto the electronegative oxygen.

Practice 210 Main

Why?

Why is semicarbazide a nucleophile only through one nitrogen?

Show answer

The NH2 attached to C=O has its lone pair delocalised into the carbonyl (amide resonance); the other NH2 is attached to N and keeps its lone pair.

Practice 211 Main

Why?

Why does acetal formation need acid catalysis, while cyanohydrin formation needs a base?

Show answer

Alcohols are poor nucleophiles and need the carbonyl protonated (more electrophilic); cyanide is a strong nucleophile and needs to be generated from HCN by base.

Practice 212 Main

Why?

Why is it necessary to remove water in preparing a ketal from a ketone?

Show answer

Ketal formation is reversible and the equilibrium for ketones is unfavourable; removing water pushes it to products (Le Chatelier).

Practice 213 Main

Why?

Why are Grignard reagents prepared in dry ether?

Show answer

Water, alcohols and other protic sources destroy RMgX (R–MgX + H2O → RH); ether also solvates and stabilises the Mg centre.

Practice 214 Main

Why?

Why does acetone not undergo the Cannizzaro reaction but benzaldehyde does?

Show answer

Acetone has α-hydrogens and no aldehydic hydrogen; benzaldehyde has an aldehydic H and no α-H (so no competing enolate chemistry).

Practice 215 Main

Why?

Why is formaldehyde the most reactive carbonyl towards nucleophiles?

Show answer

No electron-donating alkyl groups to reduce δ+ on carbon, and no steric hindrance (two H atoms).

Practice 216 Main

Why?

Why do carboxylic acids not give iodoform though CH3COOH contains CH3CO–?

Show answer

The C=O of an acid (or ester, amide) is stabilised by the lone pair of OH/OR/NH2 and is much less electrophilic; the α-H is also much less acidic (pKa about 25 for esters); the haloform sequence does not occur.

Practice 217 Main

Why?

Why does the aldol of acetone lie on the side of the reactant at equilibrium?

Show answer

The addition product has a more crowded carbonyl-derived centre (tertiary alcohol); the entropy change of combining two molecules into one is also unfavourable.

Practice 218 Main

Why?

Why is a catalytic amount of cyanide enough for the benzoin condensation?

Show answer

Cyanide is regenerated in the last step (loss of CN− from the cyanohydrin carbon).

Practice 219 Main

Why?

Why does the Wittig reaction give a pure alkene while dehydration of an alcohol gives mixtures?

Show answer

The C=C forms exactly between the former carbonyl carbon and the ylide carbon; E1 dehydration can eliminate H from different β-carbons.
