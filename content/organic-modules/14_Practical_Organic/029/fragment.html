9. Functional group tests: general strategy

After the elements are known, the functional groups are found by systematic tests. Always start with the cheap, informative physical and solubility tests, then use specific chemical tests.

  • Physical state, colour, odour. Fruity odour: ester. Almond: benzaldehyde/nitrobenzene. Fishy: amine. Phenolic/carbolic: phenol. Pungent vinegar: acetic acid.
  • Burning (ignition) test. A smoky, sooty flame: aromatic or highly unsaturated (high carbon %). A clean, non-luminous blue flame: aliphatic or oxygen-rich (alcohol, acid).
  • Solubility: in water (small polar), in dilute NaOH (acids and phenols), in NaHCO3 (carboxylic acids and very acidic phenols), in dilute HCl (amines), in conc. H2SO4 (compounds containing O, N, or unsaturation).
  • Litmus/pH. Acidic: carboxylic acids, sulphonic acids, picric acid. Basic: amines. Neutral: hydrocarbons, alcohols, esters, ethers, ketones, aldehydes, halides, nitro.

HYF A positive test is meaningful only for the group it proves. State the *negative* class too: for example, a compound that gives no reaction with NaOH, HCl, NaHCO3 is neutral, so it is a hydrocarbon, halide, ether, alcohol, aldehyde, ketone, ester, nitro compound or amide.
