3.2 Enynes and the tie rule

Example 16 Direct application

Name the enyne.

Show solution
  1. Five-carbon chain with both multiple bonds.
  2. From the alkene end: C=C at 1, C≡C at 4 → {1,4}. From the alkyne end: C≡C at 1, C=C at 4 → {1,4}. Identical sets.
  3. Tie → the double bond takes the lower number.

Pent-1-en-4-yne (not pent-4-en-1-yne).

Example 17 Comparison

Name the enyne.

Show solution
  1. Chain: HC≡C–CH=CH–CH₃, five carbons.
  2. From the alkyne end: C≡C is 1, C=C is 3 → {1,3}. From the methyl end: C=C is 2, C≡C is 4 → {2,4}.
  3. The set {1,3} is lower than {2,4}, so number from the alkyne end even though the double bond then gets 3.

Pent-3-en-1-yne. The tie rule (double bond lower) applies only when the sets are equal.
