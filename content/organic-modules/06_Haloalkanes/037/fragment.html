Practice 261 Paragraph 1

Stereochemistry puzzle (Q1)

(S)-Butan-2-ol is converted to 2-chlorobutane A with SOCl2 (no base) and to 2-chlorobutane B with SOCl2 in pyridine. A reacts with NaI in acetone to give C; B reacts with NaCN in DMSO to give D (both SN2). Q1: What are the configurations of A and B?

Show answer
  1. A: SNi, retention: (S)-2-chlorobutane. B: pyridine releases free Cl−, which attacks from the back: inversion: (R)-2-chlorobutane.
SOCl2⟶
A: retention (S).
SOCl2, pyridine⟶
B: inversion (R).
Practice 262 Paragraph 1

Stereochemistry puzzle (Q2)

(Refer to the paragraph above.) What is the configuration of C?

Show answer
  1. SN2 on (S)-2-chlorobutane (A) by I−: inversion; priorities I > C2H5 > CH3 > H (same order): (R)-2-iodobutane. (With excess iodide it gradually racemises.)
Practice 263 Paragraph 1

Stereochemistry puzzle (Q3)

(Refer to the paragraph above.) What is D?

Show answer
  1. SN2 on (R)-2-chlorobutane (B) with cyanide: inversion. CIP priorities: CN > C2H5 > CH3 > H (same pattern), so the letter changes: (S)-2-methylbutanenitrile.
+ NaCN, DMSO⟶
Inversion at the stereocentre: (R)-chloride to (S)-nitrile.
Practice 264 Paragraph 2

E2 stereospecificity (Q1)

2-Bromo-3-methylpentane has two stereocentres, so four stereoisomers: (2R,3R), (2S,3S), (2R,3S), (2S,3R). Each is treated with NaOEt/EtOH. The H on C3 is lost anti to Br on C2. Q1: Which diastereomeric pair gives the (E)-3-methylpent-2-ene?

Show answer
  1. Conformer analysis (C3–H anti to C2–Br): in the (2R,3R) and (2S,3S) isomers the C2-methyl and C3-ethyl groups end up anti: (E)-alkene. In the (2R,3S) and (2S,3R) isomers they are gauche in the reactive conformer and become cis: (Z). Answer: (2R,3R) and (2S,3S).
Practice 265 Paragraph 2

E2 stereospecificity (Q2)

(Refer to the paragraph above.) A mixture of equal amounts of all four stereoisomers is subjected to E2 elimination, but only the C3–H (towards the more substituted alkene) is considered. How many different alkenes (counting E and Z) result?

Show answer
  1. Only 3-methylpent-2-ene forms: 2 (E and Z).
Practice 266 Paragraph 2

E2 stereospecificity (Q3)

(Refer to the paragraph above.) What is the effect on the product ratio if the same four-component mixture is heated in neutral ethanol (E1/SN1 conditions)?

Show answer
  1. E1 goes through a planar cation, so the stereochemical information is lost: all four isomers give the same distribution, with (E)-3-methylpent-2-ene most abundant (more stable). Stereospecificity disappears; rearrangement (hydride shift to the tertiary cation) can also occur.
Practice 267 Paragraph 3

Neighbouring group (Q1)

Hydrolysis of ClCH2CH2SCH2CH3 in water is much faster than that of ClCH2CH2CH2CH3, and the rate is independent of [HO−]. Q1: Name the intermediate and the phenomenon.

Show answer
  1. The sulfur lone pair attacks the carbon bearing Cl intramolecularly, forming a cyclic episulfonium (thiiranium) ion: anchimeric assistance (neighbouring-group participation).
Practice 268 Paragraph 3

Neighbouring group (Q2)

(Refer to the paragraph above.) Why is the rate independent of [HO−]?

Show answer
  1. The rate-determining step is the intramolecular ring closure (first-order in substrate); hydroxide or water attacks the cationic ring only in the fast second step.
Practice 269 Paragraph 3

Neighbouring group (Q3)

(Refer to the paragraph above.) How many inversions occur at the carbon that finally carries OH in a chiral analogue, and what is the net stereochemistry?

Show answer
  1. Two inversions (ring closure, then ring opening by water): net retention.
Practice 270 Paragraph 4

Unknown aromatic halides (Q1)

Isomers X and Z have the formula C7H7Cl. X gives an immediate precipitate with alcoholic AgNO3; Z does not give a precipitate. Oxidation of Z with hot KMnO4 gives 4-chlorobenzoic acid, and Z reacts with NaNH2/NH3 to give two isomeric aminotoluenes. Q1: Identify X.

Show answer
  1. Immediate precipitation shows easy ionisation: benzyl chloride, C6H5CH2Cl.
Practice 271 Paragraph 4

Unknown aromatic halides (Q2)

(Refer to the paragraph above.) Identify Z.

Show answer
  1. Ring chloride (no precipitate); oxidation to 4-chlorobenzoic acid shows CH3 and Cl are para: 4-chlorotoluene.
Practice 272 Paragraph 4

Unknown aromatic halides (Q3)

(Refer to the paragraph above.) Name the two aminotoluenes from Z with NaNH2, and explain.

Show answer
  1. Benzyne from loss of H ortho to Cl: C1≡C2 with CH3 at C4. The amide adds at C1 (→ p-toluidine, NH2 para to CH3) or at C2 (→ m-toluidine): p-toluidine and m-toluidine (≈ 1:1).
Practice 273 Paragraph 5

Grignard sequence (Q1)

An optically inactive bromide A (C3H7Br) is converted with Mg in dry ether into B. B reacts with dry ice and then H3O+ to give a branched-chain acid D (C4H8O2), and with formaldehyde followed by H3O+ to give an alcohol C (C4H10O). Q1: Identify A.

Show answer
  1. A branched C4 acid from a C3 Grignard (CO2 adds one carbon) means the C3 unit is isopropyl. A = 2-bromopropane, (CH3)2CHBr (1-bromopropane would give straight-chain butanoic acid).
Practice 274 Paragraph 5

Grignard sequence (Q2)

(Refer to the paragraph above.) Identify B, C and D.

Show answer
  1. B = (CH3)2CHMgBr. C = (CH3)2CHCH2OH (2-methylpropan-1-ol; one carbon added by HCHO). D = (CH3)2CHCOOH (2-methylpropanoic acid).
Practice 275 Paragraph 5

Grignard sequence (Q3)

(Refer to the paragraph above.) What is formed when B is treated with D2O, and with CH3CHO followed by H3O+?

Show answer
  1. D2O gives (CH3)2CH–D (2-deuteriopropane). With acetaldehyde: (CH3)2CH–CH(OH)CH3, 3-methylbutan-2-ol, a secondary alcohol with one stereocentre, formed as a racemate.
Practice 276 Paragraph 6

Chloroform chemistry (Q1)

Chloroform is stored in dark, full bottles containing 1 % ethanol. With KOH and aniline it gives a foul-smelling compound; with phenol and NaOH it gives a hydroxy aldehyde; on heating with silver powder it gives a hydrocarbon. Q1: Explain the storage conditions.

Show answer
  1. Air and light oxidise CHCl3 to phosgene (COCl2), a poisonous gas. Dark bottles exclude light, filling to the top excludes air, and ethanol converts any phosgene into diethyl carbonate.
Practice 277 Paragraph 6

Chloroform chemistry (Q2)

(Refer to the paragraph above.) Identify the foul-smelling compound and the hydroxy aldehyde, and name the reaction types.

Show answer
  1. Foul-smelling compound: phenyl isocyanide, C6H5N≡C (carbylamine reaction). Hydroxy aldehyde: salicylaldehyde (Reimer–Tiemann). Both proceed through dichlorocarbene, :CCl2, formed by α-elimination of HCl from CHCl3 with base.
Practice 278 Paragraph 6

Chloroform chemistry (Q3)

(Refer to the paragraph above.) What is the hydrocarbon from silver powder, and what is the balanced equation?

Show answer
  1. 2 CHCl3 + 6 Ag → HC≡CH + 6 AgCl (acetylene).
Practice 279 Paragraph 7

Ozone layer (Q1)

Chlorofluorocarbons such as CF2Cl2 are inert in the troposphere but are photolysed by UV in the stratosphere. Cl atoms catalyse O3 destruction: Cl• + O3 → ClO• + O2; ClO• + O → Cl• + O2. Q1: What is the net reaction?

Show answer
  1. Adding the two steps: O3 + O → 2 O2 (Cl• is regenerated: a catalyst).
Practice 280 Paragraph 7

Ozone layer (Q2)

(Refer to the paragraph above.) Which bond of CF2Cl2 breaks in the stratosphere and why?

Show answer
  1. The C–Cl bond (≈ 340 kJ mol−1) is much weaker than the C–F bond (≈ 480 kJ mol−1); CF2Cl2 absorbs short-wavelength UV (below about 230 nm) that reaches the stratosphere, and the C–Cl bond cleaves homolytically: CF2Cl2 → •CF2Cl + Cl•.
Practice 281 Paragraph 7

Ozone layer (Q3)

(Refer to the paragraph above.) How is CF2Cl2 made, and why was it considered an ideal refrigerant?

Show answer
  1. By the Swarts reaction: CCl4 + 2 HF (or SbF3) → CF2Cl2 + 2 HCl. It is non-toxic, non-flammable, chemically inert and easily liquefied under mild pressure.
Practice 282 Paragraph 8

Zaitsev and Hofmann (Q1)

2-Bromobutane gives 81 % but-2-ene (trans:cis = 3:1) and 19 % but-1-ene with NaOEt/EtOH, while with t-BuOK/t-BuOH it gives 53 % but-1-ene and 47 % but-2-ene. Q1: Which base gives the Hofmann alkene as the major product, and why?

Show answer
  1. t-BuOK: the bulky base abstracts the more accessible primary (terminal) hydrogen, avoiding the crowded CH2 hydrogens.
Practice 283 Paragraph 8

Zaitsev and Hofmann (Q2)

(Refer to the paragraph above.) How many grams of cis-but-2-ene are formed when 100 mmol of 2-bromobutane react with NaOEt (take 81 % but-2-ene as the total of cis + trans, and the stated ratio; M = 56)?

Show answer
  1. But-2-ene = 81 mmol; trans:cis = 3:1 → cis = 81/4 = 20.25 mmol → 20.25 × 56 = 1134 mg = 1.13 g.
Practice 284 Paragraph 8

Zaitsev and Hofmann (Q3)

(Refer to the paragraph above.) Why does trans-but-2-ene predominate over cis-but-2-ene?

Show answer
  1. The transition state leading to the trans alkene (methyl groups anti) has less steric strain (no gauche CH3/CH3 interaction), and trans-but-2-ene is itself more stable by about 4 kJ mol−1.
Practice 285 Paragraph 9

Radical halogenation (Q1)

Relative reactivities per hydrogen at 125 °C for bromination are 1° : 2° : 3° = 1 : 82 : 1640; for chlorination at 25 °C they are 1 : 3.8 : 5. Q1: Calculate the percentage of 2-bromo-2-methylbutane in the bromination of 2-methylbutane.

Show answer
  1. 2-Methylbutane: 9 primary H (six on the C2-methyls, three on C4), 2 secondary H, 1 tertiary H. Weights: 9 × 1 = 9; 2 × 82 = 164; 1 × 1640 = 1640. Total 1813. Tertiary = 1640/1813 = 90.5 %.
Practice 286 Paragraph 9

Radical halogenation (Q2)

(Refer to the paragraph above.) What is the percentage of 2-chloro-2-methylbutane in the chlorination?

Show answer
  1. 9 × 1 = 9 (two types, 6 + 3); 2 × 3.8 = 7.6; 1 × 5 = 5. Total 21.6. Tertiary = 5/21.6 = 23 %.
Practice 287 Paragraph 9

Radical halogenation (Q3)

(Refer to the paragraph above.) Explain the difference using the Hammond postulate.

Show answer
  1. H-abstraction by Cl• is exothermic (H–Cl 431 kJ vs C–H ≈ 410): early transition state, little radical character, small differences between C–H types. H-abstraction by Br• is endothermic (H–Br 366 kJ): late transition state resembling the alkyl radical, so the radical stability (3° > 2° > 1°) strongly affects the rate.
Practice 288 Paragraph 10

Allylic halide hydrolysis (Q1)

1-Chlorobut-2-ene and 3-chlorobut-1-ene both hydrolyse in aqueous acetone at similar rates (SN1) and give the same two alcohols. Q1: Name the common intermediate.

Show answer
  1. The delocalised allylic cation CH3CH=CH–CH2+ ↔ CH3CH+–CH=CH2 (1-methylallyl cation).
Practice 289 Paragraph 10

Allylic halide hydrolysis (Q2)

(Refer to the paragraph above.) Name the alcohols and say how many stereoisomers they have in total.

Show answer
  1. But-2-en-1-ol (E and Z) and but-3-en-2-ol (R and S, racemic): 4 stereoisomers in principle.
Practice 290 Paragraph 10

Allylic halide hydrolysis (Q3)

(Refer to the paragraph above.) Which is the thermodynamically more stable alcohol and why does it usually predominate?

Show answer
  1. (E)-But-2-en-1-ol: disubstituted internal C=C (more stable than a monosubstituted terminal alkene) and less steric strain. The product ratio often follows stability under reversible (acidic) conditions; under kinetic control the ratio depends on charge distribution (more positive charge at the secondary terminus).

Gabriel synthesis (Q1)

Potassium phthalimide reacts with 1-bromo-3-methylbutane to give P. P is treated with hydrazine hydrate to give amine Q and a cyclic by-product. Q gives a foul-smelling compound R with CHCl3/KOH. Q1: Identify Q.

Show answer
  1. Q = 3-methylbutan-1-amine (isopentylamine), (CH3)2CHCH2CH2NH2.
Practice 292 Paragraph 11

Gabriel synthesis (Q2)

(Refer to the paragraph above.) Name R and the cyclic by-product.

Show answer
  1. R = 1-isocyano-3-methylbutane (isopentyl isocyanide), (CH3)2CHCH2CH2N≡C; the by-product is phthalhydrazide (2,3-dihydrophthalazine-1,4-dione).
Practice 293 Paragraph 11

Gabriel synthesis (Q3)

(Refer to the paragraph above.) Why does the Gabriel synthesis give only the primary amine, and why can it not be used with bromobenzene?

Show answer
  1. After alkylation, the nitrogen of N-alkylphthalimide has no N–H and its lone pair is delocalised into two carbonyls, so it cannot be alkylated again. It fails with bromobenzene because aryl halides do not undergo SN2 (and the phthalimide anion is not a strong enough nucleophile for SNAr without activating groups).
Practice 294 Paragraph 12

E1cB (Q1)

3-Hydroxybutanal, CH3CH(OH)CH2CHO, loses water in warm dilute NaOH. Q1: Name the mechanism and the intermediate.

Show answer
  1. E1cB: hydroxide removes the acidic α-H (next to CHO) to give an enolate (carbanion stabilised by the carbonyl), which then expels HO− from the β-carbon.
Practice 295 Paragraph 12

E1cB (Q2)

(Refer to the paragraph above.) Name the product and give two reasons why the elimination is favourable.

Show answer
  1. But-2-enal (crotonaldehyde). Favourable because the C=C is conjugated with C=O (extra stabilisation) and because the enolate formation makes the poor leaving group HO− leave (the lone pair pushes it out).
Practice 296 Paragraph 12

E1cB (Q3)

(Refer to the paragraph above.) How can you distinguish E1cB from E2 by an isotope-exchange experiment?

Show answer
  1. Run the reaction in D2O/OD−. If the carbanion forms reversibly, the recovered starting β-hydroxy aldehyde has deuterium at the α-carbon before elimination is complete (exchange is faster than elimination): E1cB(R). In a concerted E2 there is no hydrogen exchange in the recovered substrate.

Three reactions are carried out: (i) 1-chloro-2,4-dinitrobenzene + aqueous Na2CO3, 368 K; (ii) chlorobenzene + NaNH2/NH3(l), −33 °C; (iii) 2-chloro-1,3-dimethylbenzene + NaNH2/NH3(l). Q1: Which reaction proceeds through a Meisenheimer complex?

  1. (i): addition–elimination activated by o- and p-nitro groups: 2,4-dinitrophenol.
Practice 298 Paragraph 13

SNAr and benzyne (Q2)

(Refer to the paragraph above.) Which proceeds through benzyne, and what is the product?

Show answer
  1. (ii): chlorobenzene with NaNH2: aniline via benzyne.
Practice 299 Paragraph 13

SNAr and benzyne (Q3)

(Refer to the paragraph above.) Why does (iii) not react?

Show answer
  1. Both positions ortho to Cl carry methyl groups, so no ortho hydrogen is available for the elimination step that generates benzyne; there is no activating nitro group for SNAr either. No reaction.
Practice 300 Paragraph 14

  1. (b): iodide is an excellent nucleophile and weak base, so it substitutes (2-iodobutane); acetone is aprotic.

(Refer to the paragraph above.) Which give mainly elimination, and with which regiochemistry?

  1. (a): E2, Zaitsev (but-2-ene major). (d): E2, bulky base, increased Hofmann (but-1-ene ≈ 53 %).
Practice 302 Paragraph 14

Competition (Q3)

(Refer to the paragraph above.) What is the major process in (c) and what stereochemistry?

Show answer
  1. Weak nucleophile (H2O), protic solvent, secondary substrate: SN1 (with E1) through the secondary cation: racemic butan-2-ol (slight inversion excess) plus a little butene.
