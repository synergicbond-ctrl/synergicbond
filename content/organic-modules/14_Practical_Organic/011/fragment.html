3.3 Azeotropes

A mixture that distils at a constant temperature with unchanged composition is an azeotrope. A solution that shows a large positive deviation from Raoult's law (ethanol–water, minimum b.p.) gives a minimum-boiling azeotrope; a solution with a large negative deviation (HNO3–water, 68 % HNO3, b.p. 393 K) gives a maximum-boiling azeotrope. Fractional distillation of 95.6 % alcohol cannot give absolute alcohol; commercial absolute alcohol is made by adding benzene (ternary azeotrope) or by removing water with CaO or molecular sieves.
