12. Worked examples on preparations

Example 4 Main

Choosing the method

Convert (a) propene to propanone, (b) propyne to propanal, (c) ethanoic acid to ethanal, (d) benzonitrile to benzaldehyde. Give reagents only.

Show solution
  1. (a) Wacker: PdCl2/CuCl2, O2, H2O (Markovnikov oxygen on the internal carbon). Alternatively H2O/H+ to propan-2-ol, then Cu/573 K.
  2. (b) Hydroboration–oxidation: (i) Sia2BH, (ii) H2O2, NaOH. (Hg2+ would give propanone.)
  3. (c) CH3COOH → CH3COCl (SOCl2) → Rosenmund (H2, Pd/BaSO4). Or reduce to the ester and use DIBAL-H.
  4. (d) Stephen reduction (SnCl2/HCl then H3O+), or DIBAL-H followed by hydrolysis.
Example 5 Main

Reverse ozonolysis

An alkene A (C7H12) on ozonolysis (Zn/H2O) gives a single product B, C7H12O2. B gives a yellow precipitate with I2/NaOH but does not reduce Tollens' reagent. Identify A and B.

Show solution
  1. A has two degrees of unsaturation (C7H16 is the saturated alkane). One is C=C. A single product with the same seven carbons and two oxygens means the alkene is cyclic: ozonolysis opens the ring and each former alkene carbon becomes a C=O.
  2. B must be a diketone (no Tollens' reaction, so no CHO) with CH3CO– groups (iodoform positive). Two CH3CO– groups plus three CH2 gives CH3CO(CH2)3COCH3: heptane-2,6-dione.
  3. Join the two carbonyl carbons by a double bond and close the ring: A = 1,2-dimethylcyclopent-1-ene.
  4. Check the alternatives: 1-methylcyclohexene gives 6-oxoheptanal (Tollens' positive), cycloheptene gives heptanedial (Tollens' positive, iodoform negative).

Example 6 Adv

Aromatic carbonyls

Predict the major product: (a) anisole (methoxybenzene) + CH3COCl/AlCl3; (b) phenol + CHCl3/NaOH; (c) benzene + CO/HCl/AlCl3; (d) nitrobenzene + CH3COCl/AlCl3.

Show solution
  1. (a) OMe is o,p-directing and activating; the bulky electrophile goes para: 4-methoxyacetophenone.
  2. (b) Salicylaldehyde (2-hydroxybenzaldehyde) via dichlorocarbene; 4-hydroxybenzaldehyde is the minor product.
  3. (c) Benzaldehyde (Gattermann–Koch).
  4. (d) No reaction: the ring is deactivated by NO2; Friedel–Crafts acylation fails (nitrobenzene is even used as a solvent for Friedel–Crafts reactions).

Example 7 Adv

Number of products

How many carbonyl products (counting only constitutional isomers) can form when pent-2-yne is hydrated with H2O/HgSO4/H2SO4, and what are they?

Show solution
  1. Water can add on either alkyne carbon: C-2 (giving pentan-2-one) or C-3 (giving pentan-3-one). The two carbons of C≡C are electronically almost identical (one bears methyl, the other ethyl), so both form: 2 products, pentan-2-one and pentan-3-one, in similar amounts.

Example 8 Adv

Oxidation: choose the reagent

Which reagent oxidises (i) PhCH2OH to PhCHO without touching a C=C elsewhere, (ii) CH3CH=CHCH2OH to CH3CH=CHCHO, (iii) cyclohexanol to cyclohexanone in aqueous acid?

Show solution
  1. (i) PCC in CH2Cl2, or MnO2 (benzylic), or Swern. (ii) MnO2 (selective for allylic alcohols), or PCC. (iii) CrO3/H2SO4 (Jones) or Na2Cr2O7/H2SO4.
Example 9 Adv

Tricky: toluene

Which reagents convert toluene to benzaldehyde in one step: KMnO4/H+; CrO2Cl2; CrO3/Ac2O followed by H3O+; Cl2/hν (1 equiv)?

Show solution
  1. KMnO4/H+ gives benzoic acid (over-oxidation). CrO2Cl2 (Etard) gives benzaldehyde. CrO3/Ac2O gives benzylidene diacetate, then H3O+ gives benzaldehyde (two operations, but one chemical sequence). Cl2/hν (1 equiv) gives benzyl chloride, which on hydrolysis gives benzyl alcohol, not the aldehyde (2 equiv Cl2 would give benzal chloride).
