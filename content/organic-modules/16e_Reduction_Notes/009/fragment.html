10. Reduction of nitro, azide, oxime and imine groups; reductive amination

Nitroarenes give different products with different reagents. Sn/HCl, Fe/HCl and H2/Pd–C go all the way to the aniline. Zn/NH4Cl (neutral) stops at the N-aryl hydroxylamine. Zn/NaOH (basic) gives hydrazobenzene through condensation steps, and LiAlH4 gives azobenzene. Aqueous Na2S or (NH4)2S (Zinin) typically reduces only one nitro group of a dinitroarene.

Reactant:

Reagent: (NH4)2S (or Na2S), H2O, heat

Product:

Organic azides are reduced cleanly to primary amines by LiAlH4, H2/Pd or PPh3 followed by H2O (Staudinger). Alkyl halide → azide → amine is a way to make a primary amine without over-alkylation. Oximes and nitriles also give primary amines on LiAlH4 or H2/Ni reduction.

Reductive amination joins a carbonyl compound to an amine in one pot. The amine condenses with the aldehyde or ketone to form an imine (or iminium ion), which is reduced in situ.

  1. RR′C=O + R″NH2 ⇌ carbinolamine ⇌ imine (acid catalysed, pH about 6).
  2. Protonation gives the iminium ion RR′C=N+HR″.
  3. NaBH3CN (or NaBH(OAc)3, or H2/Ni) transfers hydride to the iminium carbon. At pH 6–7 the C=O group itself is not reduced by NaBH3CN.

Reactant:

Reagent: NaBH3CN, pH 6

Product:

Key idea. Reductive amination avoids the over-alkylation of direct SN2 alkylation of amines: ammonia gives primary, primary amines give secondary, secondary amines give tertiary amines, with the carbon skeleton fixed by the carbonyl compound.
