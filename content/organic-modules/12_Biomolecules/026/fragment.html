5.2 Hormones

Hormones are chemical messengers secreted by endocrine glands into the blood, acting on distant target cells at very low concentrations. By chemistry:

Representative hormones: thyroxine (four iodines, diaryl ether), adrenaline (catechol amine), and four steroids built on the same four-ring (A, B, C, D) skeleton
  • Thyroxine is synthesised from tyrosine and iodine; its four I atoms give a mass fraction I = 4 × 126.9/776.87 = 65.3 %. Iodised salt prevents simple goitre (enlargement of the thyroid from iodine lack).
  • Adrenaline (R): a catecholamine made from tyrosine (tyrosine → DOPA → dopamine → noradrenaline → adrenaline); it has one stereocentre.
  • Insulin was the first protein sequenced (Sanger, 1955) and the first made by recombinant DNA technology (1978). Its A-chain intrachain disulfide Cys6–Cys11 and interchain Cys7–Cys7, Cys20–Cys19 are essential for activity.
  • Sex hormones are steroids; the 19-carbon testosterone and 18-carbon oestradiol differ in ring A (aromatic in oestradiol) and the C-10 methyl.
Trap Adrenaline is an amine hormone, not a steroid or peptide; thyroxine is an amino-acid derivative, not a peptide hormone. Insulin and glucagon are peptide hormones; testosterone, oestradiol, progesterone, cortisol and aldosterone are steroids; vitamin D is a secosteroid (a steroid with one ring opened).
