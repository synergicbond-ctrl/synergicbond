6.2 Bridged bicyclic compounds: the von Baeyer system

A bicyclic compound has two rings sharing two or more atoms. The shared atoms where the rings meet are the bridgeheads; the three paths connecting the bridgeheads are the bridges.

Why It Happens Counting only the atoms between the bridgeheads makes the descriptor [x.y.z] independent of the choice of the starting ring; the sum x + y + z + 2 then recovers the total number of ring atoms, which is why the alkane name is fixed by the descriptor.
  • Name: bicyclo[x.y.z]alkane, where x ≥ y ≥ z are the numbers of carbons in the bridges (not counting the bridgeheads); the alkane name counts all atoms = x + y + z + 2.
  • Numbering: start at a bridgehead (C-1), go around the longest bridge to the second bridgehead, continue around the next longest bridge back towards C-1, and finally number the shortest bridge starting from the atom nearer to C-1.
  • Heteroatoms, substituents and double bonds use these locants; the lowest locants go first to the principal group, then to double bonds, then to prefixes.

Bicyclo[1.1.0]butane consists of two cyclopropane-like rings sharing one bond and is very strained.
Special Case A bridge of zero atoms is a direct bond between the two bridgeheads, as in bicyclo[4.4.0]decane and bicyclo[1.1.0]butane. It is numbered last, and the bridgeheads are adjacent atoms.
IMP To name a bicyclic skeleton from the structure: (1) mark the two bridgeheads (atoms with three ring bonds); (2) count the carbons in each of the three bridges; (3) write them in decreasing order in brackets; (4) add all carbons: the alkane name. Bicyclo[2.2.1]: bridges 2, 2, 1; atoms 2 + 2 + 1 + 2 = 7 → heptane.
Example 41 Direct application

How many carbon atoms and rings in bicyclo[3.2.1]octane, and what are the bridges?

Show solution
  1. Bridges: 3, 2 and 1 carbons between the bridgeheads. Total atoms 3 + 2 + 1 + 2 = 8 (octane).
  2. "Bicyclo" always means two rings (SSSR): here a five-membered ring (2 + 1 + 2 atoms) and a six-membered ring (3 + 1 + 2 atoms); the seven-membered envelope round the outside (3 + 2 + 2) is not counted separately.

8 carbons, 2 rings.

Example 42 Standard

Name camphor, 1,7,7-trimethylbicyclo[2.2.1]heptan-2-one, from the structure and explain each locant.

Show solution
  1. Skeleton: bicyclo[2.2.1]heptane. The one-carbon bridge is C-7 (always the shortest bridge, here the carbon with two methyls).
  2. Principal group: ketone. C-1 must be a bridgehead; choose it so that C=O gets the lowest locant: C=O is on the carbon next to bridgehead C-1, so C=O = 2.
  3. Then the methyl on bridgehead C-1 and the gem-dimethyl on C-7.

1,7,7-Trimethylbicyclo[2.2.1]heptan-2-one. Prefixes: 1,7,7-trimethyl (the "tri" is not alphabetised).

Example 43 Standard

Name the compound.

Show solution
  1. Norbornane skeleton with one C=C in a two-carbon bridge: that bridge atoms are C2 and C3.
  2. The double bond takes the lowest locant, 2.

Bicyclo[2.2.1]hept-2-ene (norbornene, the monomer for ring-opening metathesis).
