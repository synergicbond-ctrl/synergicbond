Practice 169 Integration · integer

A hydrocarbon (C 85.71 %, H 14.29 %) is burnt in a Liebig train in which the KOH bulbs by mistake precede the CaCl2 tube. 0.280 g is burnt. What percentage of carbon is reported (nearest integer)?

Show answer
  1. CO2 formed = 0.280 × 0.8571 × 44/12 = 0.880 g; H2O = 0.280 × 0.1429 × 18/2 = 0.360 g.
  2. KOH absorbs both: gain = 1.240 g, taken as CO2.
  3. Reported %C = (12/44)(1.240/0.280) × 100 = 120.8 ⇒ 121.

Answer: 121

Practice 170 Integration · matching list

Match each procedural error (I-IV) with its consequence (P-S). I: KOH absorber placed before the CaCl2 tube in Liebig's method. II: copper gauze omitted in Dumas' method. III: the sodium extract is boiled with HNO3 before testing for sulphur with lead acetate. IV: too little sodium used with thiourea. P: no black PbS (S2− lost as H2S). Q: blood-red colour, no Prussian blue. R: %H reported zero, %C too high. S: nitrogen result too low (oxides of N absorbed by KOH).

Show answer
  1. I → R: KOH takes up water as well as CO2. II → S: oxides of nitrogen are not reduced to N2, and are absorbed by KOH in the nitrometer. III → P: HNO3 converts S2− to H2S, which escapes. IV → Q: insufficient Na leaves NaSCN.

Answer: I–R, II–S, III–P, IV–Q

Practice 171 Advanced · paragraph

A solid mixture of benzoic acid (0.0200 mol), phenol (0.0200 mol) and naphthalene (0.0300 mol) is dissolved in ether and separated by shaking successively with aqueous NaHCO3, then aqueous NaOH. (a) Give the masses of the three components. (b) What volume of CO2 (STP, mL) is released in the NaHCO3 step? (c) What volume of 0.500 M NaOH is just enough to extract the phenol? (d) What happens to the naphthalene? (e) How is phenol recovered from the NaOH extract?

Show answer
  1. (a) Benzoic acid 0.0200 × 122.1 = 2.44 g; phenol 0.0200 × 94.1 = 1.88 g; naphthalene 0.0300 × 128.2 = 3.85 g.
  2. (b) C6H5COOH + NaHCO3 → C6H5COONa + CO2 + H2O: 0.0200 mol CO2 = 448 mL at STP.
  3. (c) C6H5OH + NaOH → C6H5ONa + H2O: 0.0200 mol NaOH = 40 mL of 0.500 M.
  4. (d) Naphthalene is neutral; it stays in the ether layer and is recovered by evaporation (and can be sublimed).
  5. (e) Bubble CO2 into the NaOH extract or acidify with HCl: phenol separates (phenol is a weaker acid than H2CO3).

Answer: (b) 448 mL; (c) 40 mL

Practice 172 Integration

0.2500 g of a monobasic organic acid is neutralised by 20.0 mL of 0.100 N NaOH. Find the molar mass; and the mass of the silver salt (Ag+ replaces H+) that 0.2500 g of the acid would give.

Show answer
  1. Equivalents = 20.0 × 0.100/1000 = 2.00 × 10-3 ⇒ equivalent mass = 0.2500/2.00 × 10-3 = 125; monobasic ⇒ M = 125.
  2. Silver salt: M = 125 − 1 + 108 = 232; moles 2.00 × 10-3 ⇒ mass = 0.464 g.

Answer: M = 125; 0.464 g

Practice 173 Integration

Calculate for 0.150 g of urea (a) the volume of N2 at STP in a Dumas determination and (b) the volume of 0.50 N H2SO4 neutralised by the NH3 in a Kjeldahl determination.

Show answer
  1. n(urea) = 0.150/60 = 2.50 × 10-3 mol; each urea gives 1 N2 (Dumas): V = 2.50 × 10-3 × 22 400 = 56 mL.

Practice 175 Advanced · paragraph

Liquid K (C8H8O3) has a sweet, minty odour, does not dissolve in NaHCO3, dissolves in NaOH, gives a violet colour with neutral FeCl3, and with hot NaOH followed by acidification gives a solid acid that also gives a violet colour with FeCl3 and effervesces with NaHCO3. (a) DBE of K. (b) Identify K and the acid. (c) Why does K not effervesce with NaHCO3 although it dissolves in NaOH?

Show answer
  1. (a) DBE = (16 + 2 − 8)/2 = 5 (ring + one C=O).
  2. (b) Violet FeCl3 ⇒ phenolic –OH; alkaline hydrolysis (saponification) gives an acid with both groups: salicylic acid, so K is the methyl ester of salicylic acid: methyl salicylate (oil of wintergreen). Hydrolysis: o-HOC6H4COOCH3 + 2NaOH → o-NaOC6H4COONa + CH3OH + H2O; then HCl.
  3. (c) K has only the phenolic –OH (pKa ≈ 10), weaker than H2CO3; no carboxylic acid. NaOH is strong enough to form the phenoxide.

Answer: methyl salicylate

methyl salicylate

Practice 181 Advanced

Four liquids (C3H6O: propanal; C3H6O: acetone; C7H6O: benzaldehyde; C8H8O: acetophenone) are labelled randomly. Using Tollens', Fehling's and I2/NaOH only, devise a scheme and tabulate the expected results.

Show answer
  1. Tollens': propanal +, benzaldehyde +, acetone −, acetophenone −.
  2. Fehling's (on the Tollens'-positive pair): propanal +, benzaldehyde −.
  3. I2/NaOH (on the Tollens'-negative pair): acetone + (yellow ppt), acetophenone + also (methyl ketone): not discriminating; use instead NaHSO3: acetone gives the crystalline adduct, acetophenone does not. (Or 2,4-DNP colour; or boiling-point/odour.)
  4. So the full scheme needs a fourth test; an honest answer: Tollens' → Fehling's separates the aldehydes; NaHSO3 (not iodoform) separates the ketones.

Answer: Tollens → Fehling; NaHSO3 for the ketones

Practice 182 Integration

Place in order of increasing time to turbidity with Lucas reagent: pentan-1-ol, pentan-2-ol, 2-methylbutan-2-ol, benzyl alcohol.

Show answer
  1. Rate ∝ carbocation stability: 2-methylbutan-2-ol (3°) fastest; benzyl alcohol (benzylic) fast; pentan-2-ol (2°) slow (about 5 min); pentan-1-ol (1°) no reaction at room temperature.
  2. Increasing time: 2-methylbutan-2-ol ≈ benzyl alcohol < pentan-2-ol < pentan-1-ol.

Answer: 3° ≈ benzyl < 2° < 1°

Practice 183 Integration

Which sugar tests are positive for fructose: Molisch, Fehling, Tollens, bromine water, Seliwanoff?

Show answer
  1. Molisch +; Fehling + (α-hydroxyketone tautomerises to aldose in base); Tollens +; bromine water − (a ketose is not oxidised by Br2 water, unlike glucose); Seliwanoff + (cherry red in 1 min).

What is the purpose of (a) charcoal, (b) a seed crystal, (c) hot filtration, in crystallisation?
