Trap The FeCl3 test is also positive for enols (acetoacetic ester, acetylacetone), hydroxamic acids and some carboxylic acids (salicylic acid; also formic acid with a red colour, acetic acid with a red colour). Do not conclude “phenol” from FeCl3 alone if an enol or a salicylate is possible.
Example 52 L2 Main

Distinguishing tests

Give one test to distinguish: (a) phenol from benzyl alcohol; (b) phenol from benzoic acid; (c) o-cresol from benzyl alcohol; (d) salicylic acid from aspirin; (e) phenol from ethanol.

Show solution
  1. (a) Neutral FeCl3: phenol violet, benzyl alcohol none (also NaOH solubility, bromine water).
  2. (b) NaHCO3: benzoic acid evolves CO2; phenol does not.
  3. (c) FeCl3: o-cresol gives a blue/violet colour; benzyl alcohol none.
  4. (d) Neutral FeCl3: salicylic acid violet; fresh aspirin has no free phenolic OH, so no colour.
  5. (e) Bromine water: phenol gives a white precipitate; ethanol only decolourises Br2 slowly (oxidation); FeCl3 gives violet with phenol only.
