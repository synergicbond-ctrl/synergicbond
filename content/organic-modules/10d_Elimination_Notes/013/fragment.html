Practice 1 L1 Foundation

For a typical E2 elimination of an alkyl halide R–X with a base B−, the experimental rate law is

(A) rate = k[R–X]

(B) rate = k[R–X][B−]

(C) rate = k[B−]

(D) rate = k[R–X]2

Show answer
  1. E2 is a one-step bimolecular process: the base and the substrate both appear in the single transition state.
  2. Hence the rate is first order in each: rate = k[R–X][B−], second order overall.

Answer: B

Practice 2 L1 Foundation

The rate of an E1 reaction of a tertiary halide in ethanol depends on

(A) only the concentration of the halide

(B) the concentrations of both halide and base

(C) only the concentration of the base

(D) the square of the base concentration

Show answer
  1. The slow step is ionization of R–X to the carbocation; the base is involved only in the fast second step.
  2. Therefore rate = k[R–X], independent of base concentration.

Answer: A

Practice 3 L1 Foundation

Which arrangement of the β-hydrogen and the leaving group is required for the usual E2 reaction?

(A) syn-periplanar (dihedral angle 0°)

(B) gauche (dihedral angle about 60°)

(C) anti-periplanar (dihedral angle 180°)

(D) perpendicular (dihedral angle 90°)

Show answer
  1. The orbitals of the breaking C–H and C–X bonds must overlap with the developing p orbitals of the π bond.
  2. This overlap is best, and the staggered arrangement lowest in energy, when H and X are anti-periplanar, 180° apart.

Answer: C

Practice 4 L1 Foundation

How many constitutionally different alkenes can be formed by dehydrobromination of the compound shown?

Show answer
  1. The α-carbon is C2 (bearing Br). Its neighbours are C1 (CH3, with H), C3 (CH2, with H) and the 2-methyl group (CH3, with H, equivalent to C1).
  2. Removing H from a CH3 group gives 2-methylbut-1-ene; removing H from C3 gives 2-methylbut-2-ene. The two methyl groups are equivalent, so there are 2 alkenes.

Answer: 2

Practice 5 L1 Foundation

The major product of 2-bromo-2-methylbutane with sodium ethoxide in ethanol at 55 °C is

(B) 2-methylbut-2-ene

Show answer

Practice 6 L1 Foundation

The major alkene from 2-bromo-2-methylbutane with potassium tert-butoxide in tert-butanol is

(A) 2-methylbut-2-ene

(B) 3-methylbut-1-ene

(C) 2-methylbut-1-ene

(D) 2-methylbutane

  2. The Hofmann alkene 2-methylbut-1-ene therefore predominates.

Practice 7 L1 Foundation

Which halide undergoes E2 elimination fastest under identical conditions (same alkyl group, same base)?

(A) R–F

(B) R–Cl

(C) R–Br

(D) R–I

Show answer
  1. The leaving group departs in the rate-determining step, so a weaker C–X bond and a more stable X− give a faster reaction.
  2. Iodide is the weakest base and has the weakest C–X bond, so R–I is fastest: I > Br > Cl >> F.

Answer: D

What intermediate is formed in the slow step of an E1 reaction of a tertiary alkyl halide?

Practice 13 L1 Foundation

Which solvent condition most favours E1/SN1 over E2/SN2 for a tertiary halide?

(A) a polar protic solvent such as ethanol or water

(B) a polar aprotic solvent such as DMSO with a strong base

(C) a non-polar solvent such as hexane with a strong base

(D) concentrated sodium ethoxide

Show answer
  1. E1 and SN1 need ionization to a carbocation; polar protic solvents stabilise both the cation and the leaving group by solvation.
  2. Strong base (options B–D) promotes the bimolecular E2 route instead.

Answer: A

Practice 14 L1 Foundation

A primary kinetic isotope effect kH/kD clearly greater than 1 for the elimination of a deuterated halide indicates that

(A) the C–H bond is broken in the rate-determining step

(B) the C–X bond is broken before the C–H bond in the slow step

(C) the reaction proceeds through a carbocation

(D) the reaction follows SN2 kinetics

Show answer
  1. The C–D bond is stronger than the C–H bond, so a reaction that cleaves it in the slow step is slower for the deuterated substrate.
  2. This is the signature of E2 (C–H bond cleavage in the single slow step), not of E1 where kH/kD is close to 1.

Answer: A

Practice 15 L1 Foundation

Which sequence of reagents converts a primary amine R–CH2CH2–NH2 into an alkene by Hofmann elimination?

(A) excess CH3I; then Ag2O, H2O; then heat

(B) NaNO2, HCl; then heat

(C) PCl5; then NaOEt

(D) LiAlH4; then H2O

Show answer
  1. Exhaustive methylation gives the quaternary ammonium iodide, which is a salt with a good leaving group (NMe3).
  2. Ag2O/H2O exchanges iodide for hydroxide, and heating effects E2 elimination to the alkene and trimethylamine.

Answer: A

Practice 16 L1 Foundation

According to Zaitsev's rule, in a base-induced β-elimination the major product is

(A) the least substituted alkene

(B) the most substituted (most stable) alkene

(C) the product with the double bond at the end of the chain

(D) always the Z isomer

Show answer
  1. The transition state of E2 with a small base resembles the alkene, so the more stable, more highly substituted alkene is formed faster.
  2. Hence the Zaitsev product is the most substituted alkene; the exceptions (bulky base, poor leaving group) give the Hofmann product.

Answer: B

Practice 17 L1 Foundation

Which of the following is a strong but sterically hindered base that favours elimination?

(A) sodium ethoxide

(B) potassium tert-butoxide

(C) sodium iodide

(D) sodium acetate

Show answer
  1. tert-Butoxide has a bulky tertiary alkyl group, so it is a poor nucleophile (slow SN2) but a strong base (conjugate acid pKa about 17–18 for the alcohol).
  2. Iodide and acetate are weak bases and ethoxide is not hindered, so only potassium tert-butoxide fits.

Answer: B

Practice 19 L1 Foundation

Sodium ethoxide in ethanol converts tert-butyl bromide mainly into

(A) tert-butyl ethyl ether by SN2

(B) tert-butyl ethyl ether by SN1

Practice 20 L1 Foundation

Arrange in order of decreasing ease of acid-catalysed dehydration: ethanol (I), propan-2-ol (II), 2-methylpropan-2-ol (III).

(A) I > II > III

(B) III > II > I

(C) II > III > I

(D) III > I > II

Show answer
  1. Dehydration through the carbocation is fastest when the cation is most stable: tertiary > secondary > primary.
  2. Thus 2-methylpropan-2-ol (III) is easiest, then propan-2-ol (II), then ethanol (I): III > II > I.

Practice 21 L1 Foundation

Ethanol is heated with concentrated sulfuric acid at about 140 °C instead of 170 °C. What organic compound is the main product?

Practice 22 L1 Foundation

Which statement describes an E1cB reaction?

(A) a carbocation forms first, then a proton is lost

(B) a carbanion (conjugate base) forms first, then the leaving group departs

(C) a single concerted step removes H and X

(D) the leaving group and the proton leave on the same face through a cyclic transition state

Show answer
  1. E1cB stands for elimination, unimolecular, conjugate base: the base removes an acidic proton to give a stabilised carbanion.
  2. The leaving group then departs in the second step. Option A describes E1, C describes E2, D describes syn thermal elimination.

Answer: B

Practice 23 L1 Foundation

Which of the following statements about the E2 reaction are correct?

(A) It occurs in a single concerted step.

(B) It requires a syn-periplanar arrangement of H and the leaving group.

(C) It shows second-order kinetics.

(D) It involves a free carbocation intermediate.

Show answer
  1. E2 is concerted and bimolecular: statements A and C are correct.
  2. The usual E2 requires an anti-periplanar H and X (not syn), and no carbocation forms; B and D are therefore wrong.

Answer: A,C

Practice 25 L1 Foundation

For E2 reactions with a strong base such as sodium ethoxide, the order of reactivity of alkyl bromides is

(A) primary > secondary > tertiary

Practice 27 L2 Main

(2R,3R)-2-Bromo-3-methylpentane is treated with sodium ethoxide. The alkene formed by anti E2 removal of the hydrogen on C3 (the C2=C3 alkene) is

(A) (E)-3-methylpent-2-ene

(B) (Z)-3-methylpent-2-ene

(C) a 1:1 mixture of E and Z

(D) 3-methylpent-1-ene

Show answer
  1. The β-carbon C3 carries only one hydrogen, so the anti-periplanar arrangement H–C3–C2–Br fixes the geometry.
  2. In that conformer the C2 methyl group and the C3 ethyl group end up on opposite sides, so the higher-priority groups (CH3 on C2, ethyl on C3) are trans: the E isomer.

Answer: A

Practice 28 L2 Main

(2S,3R)-2-Bromo-3-methylpentane undergoes anti E2 with removal of the C3 hydrogen. The alkene formed is

(A) (E)-3-methylpent-2-ene

(B) (Z)-3-methylpent-2-ene

(C) 3-methylpent-1-ene

(D) a racemic mixture

Show answer
  1. The (2S,3R) isomer is the enantiomer of (2R,3S); the (2R,3R)/(2S,3S) pair gives E, and the (2R,3S)/(2S,3R) pair gives Z.
  2. The anti-periplanar conformer places the C2 methyl and the C3 ethyl groups on the same side, so the product is (Z)-3-methylpent-2-ene. An alkene has no chirality centre, so enantiomeric substrates give the same alkene.

Answer: B

Practice 29 L2 Main

(D) 1,2-diphenylpropane

Practice 30 L2 Main

  1. Anti elimination from the like pairs (1R,2R) and (1S,2S) puts the phenyl groups on the same side, giving the Z alkene.

Practice 31 L2 Main

Which diastereomer of 2,3-dibromobutane gives (Z)-2-bromobut-2-ene when it loses HBr by anti E2 with alcoholic KOH?

Practice 32 L2 Main

Menthyl chloride (all substituents equatorial in the stable chair) is treated with sodium ethoxide. The principal alkene formed is

Practice 33 L2 Main

Neomenthyl chloride eliminates much faster than menthyl chloride with sodium ethoxide because

Practice 34 L2 Main

Practice 35 L2 Main

cis- and trans-4-tert-Butylcyclohexyl bromide are each treated with NaOEt. Which statements are correct?

(A) The cis isomer reacts faster than the trans isomer.

(B) The trans isomer reacts faster than the cis isomer.

(C) In the preferred chair of the cis isomer, bromine is axial.

(D) Both isomers give the same alkene, 4-tert-butylcyclohexene.

(E) The trans isomer must adopt a chair with tert-butyl axial to react by E2.

Show answer
  1. The tert-butyl group locks the ring with itself equatorial. In the cis isomer this leaves Br axial, anti to axial hydrogens on both neighbours, so E2 is fast (A, C).
  2. In the trans isomer Br is equatorial; the ring would have to flip to a chair with the tert-butyl group axial (E), a high-energy conformer, so reaction is slow and B is false. Elimination from either isomer gives 4-tert-butylcyclohexene (D).

Answer: A,C,D,E

Practice 54 L2 Main
