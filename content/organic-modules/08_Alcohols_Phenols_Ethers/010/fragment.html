3.4 From alkyl halides, amines and other derivatives

  • Hydrolysis of RX with aqueous NaOH or moist Ag2O (SN2 for 1° halides; SN1 for 3°). Tertiary halides with strong base mainly eliminate, so use water/Ag+ or hot water. Aryl and vinyl halides do not react under these conditions.
  • Hydrolysis of esters (RCOOR′ + NaOH → RCOONa + R′OH): gives the alcohol part with retention of configuration at the carbinol carbon (acyl–oxygen cleavage). Useful for obtaining a chiral alcohol from its ester.
  • From primary aliphatic amines: RNH2 + HNO2 (NaNO2/HCl, 0–5 °C) → R–N2+ → R+ → ROH + alkenes + rearranged products + alkyl chloride. The reaction is not a good preparation for simple alcohols because of the mixture (propylamine gives mostly propan-2-ol and propene as well as propan-1-ol).
  • From aryl diazonium salts: ArN2+ + H2O, warm (warm, about 300–320 K), gives phenols (see Part II).
  • From epoxides: aqueous acid or base opening gives glycols; Grignard and LiAlH4 give mono-alcohols.
  • From the Cannizzaro reaction and Tishchenko: ArCHO (no α-H) with conc. NaOH gives ArCH2OH + ArCOONa (50 % alcohol).
  • From aldol reactions followed by reduction: the β-hydroxy carbonyl compounds are also alcohols (aldol = “ald-ol”).
