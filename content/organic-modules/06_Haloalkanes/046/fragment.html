46. Worked examples: a final round of mixed products

Each example below combines two or three ideas from the chapter. Predict before reading the solution.

Example 56 Adv

Benzylic bromination then chain extension

Convert toluene into phenylacetic acid using NBS.

Show solution
  1. NBS, hν (or peroxide), CCl4 → benzyl bromide (benzylic radical, low [Br2]). NaCN (aqueous ethanol or DMSO) → benzyl cyanide (SN2 on a benzylic primary halide). H3O+, heat → phenylacetic acid (one carbon more than toluene).
NBS, hν⟶ NaCN⟶ H3O+, Δ⟶
Toluene to phenylacetic acid in three steps.
Example 57 Adv

Wurtz versus Grignard coupling

Why is the Wurtz reaction a poor way to make 2,3-dimethylbutane from 2-bromopropane even though only one halide is used?

Show solution
  1. A secondary halide with sodium: the alkyl radical/organosodium partly disproportionates and the strongly basic organosodium eliminates HBr from the starting halide (giving propene); yields of the coupled product 2,3-dimethylbutane are low. The reaction is practical only for primary halides.
Na, dry ether⟶ +
Coupling product (low yield) and elimination by-product.
Example 58 Adv

Order of reactions

How can 4-iodonitrobenzene be prepared from benzene?

Show solution
  1. Direct iodination of nitrobenzene fails (NO2 deactivates and directs meta), and an aryl chloride cannot be exchanged for iodide by Finkelstein. Use the amine as a handle: benzene → nitrobenzene (HNO3/H2SO4) → aniline (Sn/HCl) → acetanilide (Ac2O; protects and moderates the amine) → 4-nitroacetanilide (nitration, para) → 4-nitroaniline (hydrolysis) → diazonium salt (NaNO2/HCl, 0–5 °C) → KI → 4-iodonitrobenzene.
Example 59 Adv

Retrosynthesis of an alcohol via a halide

Plan a synthesis of 2-methylpentan-2-ol from propene and any inorganic reagents.

Show solution
  1. Target: (CH3)2C(OH)CH2CH2CH3. Disconnect with a Grignard + acetone: CH3CH2CH2MgBr + acetone. n-Propyl bromide from propene: HBr/peroxide (anti-Markovnikov) → 1-bromopropane; Mg/ether → propylmagnesium bromide; acetone (from propene: H2O/H+ → propan-2-ol, oxidise) → addition and H3O+ work-up.
+ 1. Mg, ether 2. H3O+⟶
2-Methylpentan-2-ol from n-propylmagnesium bromide and acetone.
Example 60 Adv

Intramolecular alkylation with a carbon nucleophile

Diethyl malonate is treated with NaOEt and then 1,4-dibromobutane. What cyclic product is expected after hydrolysis and decarboxylation?

Show solution
  1. The malonate carbanion alkylates one end (SN2); the second enolate formation and an intramolecular SN2 forms a five-membered ring: diethyl cyclopentane-1,1-dicarboxylate. Hydrolysis then heating with acid loses CO2: cyclopentanecarboxylic acid.
Example 61 Adv

Elimination in a halohydrin

Explain why trans-2-bromocyclohexanol gives an epoxide with NaOH but cis-2-bromocyclohexanol does not.

Show solution
  1. The alkoxide must attack the carbon bearing Br from the side opposite to Br. In the trans isomer O− and Br are both axial in one chair (anti-periplanar): back-side attack is possible and cyclohexene oxide forms. In the cis isomer one group is axial and the other equatorial; they are gauche (≈ 60°), so the nucleophile cannot approach from behind: no epoxide forms (elimination or other products instead).
Example 62 Adv

Substitution competes with rearrangement

Compare the products of 3-methylbutan-2-ol with (a) HBr (b) PBr3 (c) TsCl then NaBr.

Show solution
  1. (a) 2-Bromo-2-methylbutane (via 2° cation then hydride shift). (b) 2-Bromo-3-methylbutane (SN2, inversion, no cation). (c) 2-Bromo-3-methylbutane, overall inversion (tosylate with retention, then SN2 by bromide).
Example 63 Adv

Bridging back to the aryl halide

Which reagent combinations convert benzene into 4-chloronitrobenzene and into 3-chloronitrobenzene? Explain the order of introduction.

Show solution
  1. 4-Chloronitrobenzene: chlorinate first (Cl2/FeCl3; Cl is an o,p-director) then nitrate; separate the para isomer.
  2. 3-Chloronitrobenzene: nitrate first (HNO3/H2SO4; NO2 is a meta-director) then chlorinate (Cl2/FeCl3).
Example 64 Adv

DDT dechlorination idea

Why does DDT persist in the environment, and which structural feature prevents easy SN-type degradation?

Show solution
  1. The chlorines are on aryl carbons (inert to SN) and on a CCl3 group (neopentyl-like: three C–Cl on a carbon whose neighbours are crowded, no easy back-side attack; and ionisation would give an unstable cation destabilised by the other chlorines). It is highly lipophilic, so it accumulates in fat rather than being hydrolysed.
Example 65 Adv

Stereochemistry puzzle

Trans-1,2-dibromocyclohexane reacts with NaI. Which product forms, and why must both bromines be axial?

Show solution
  1. I− attacks one Br (halophilic attack) in an anti elimination: the two Br atoms must be anti-periplanar (diaxial), the C–Br bonds fall out as Br2 equivalents and a C=C forms: cyclohexene. In the trans isomer the diaxial conformer exists, so the reaction proceeds; in the cis isomer (axial/equatorial) the anti arrangement is unavailable and the reaction is much slower.
