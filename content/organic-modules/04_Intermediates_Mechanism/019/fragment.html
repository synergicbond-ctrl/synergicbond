D. Mechanism writing (Questions 314 to 329)

Question 314 Mechanism

Hydration with rearrangement

Write the stepwise mechanism for the acid-catalysed hydration of 3-methylbut-1-ene to 2-methylbutan-2-ol.

Show answer
  1. Protonation of the terminal carbon (π electrons attack H+) gives the secondary cation CH3–CH+–CH(CH3)2.
  2. A 1,2-hydride shift from the tertiary C–H gives the tertiary cation (CH3)2C+–CH2CH3.
  3. Water attacks the planar cation to give the oxonium ion (CH3)2C(OH2+)CH2CH3.
  4. Loss of H+ to a water molecule gives 2-methylbutan-2-ol.
Question 315 Mechanism

Hofmann

Write the mechanism of the Hofmann bromamide reaction of benzamide.

Show answer
  1. HO− removes an N–H proton; the amide anion attacks Br2: C6H5CONHBr.
  2. A second deprotonation gives C6H5CON−Br.
  3. The phenyl group migrates from carbon to nitrogen as Br− leaves (concerted): C6H5–N=C=O (phenyl isocyanate).
  4. HO− adds to the isocyanate carbon; protonation gives carbamic acid C6H5NHCOOH.
  5. Carbamic acid loses CO2 (as carbonate in base) to give aniline.
Question 316 Mechanism

Reimer–Tiemann

Write the mechanism of the formation of salicylaldehyde from phenol, CHCl3 and NaOH.

Show answer
  1. CHCl3 + HO− → CCl3− + H2O; CCl3− → :CCl2 + Cl−.
  2. Phenol is deprotonated to phenoxide (a better nucleophile; the negative charge is shared with the ortho and para carbons).
  3. The ortho carbon of phenoxide attacks :CCl2 (electrophile) giving a cyclohexadienone anion with a CHCl2− group; proton transfer re-aromatises to ArCHCl2 (as the phenoxide).
  4. Loss of Cl− (assisted by the O−) gives an ortho-quinone methide-like intermediate which adds HO−: ArCH(OH)Cl; loss of Cl− and H+ gives the aldehyde. Acidification gives salicylaldehyde.
Question 317 Mechanism

Benzyne route

Write the mechanism for the conversion of o-bromoanisole into m-anisidine with NaNH2/NH3(l), explaining the regiochemistry.

Show answer
  1. With OMe on C-1 and Br on C-2, NH2− removes H-3 (the only H ortho to Br; C-1 carries OMe).
  2. The aryl anion loses Br−, giving 3-methoxybenzyne (the aryne carbons are the old C-Br carbon and the carbon that lost H; the OMe-bearing carbon is next to the former).
  3. NH2− adds to the aryne carbon *meta* to OMe (the carbon farther from the oxygen), putting the developing aryl anion on the carbon *adjacent to* OCH3, stabilised by the −I effect of oxygen.
  4. Protonation by NH3 gives m-anisidine (3-methoxyaniline).
Question 318 Mechanism

Haloform

Write the mechanism for the conversion of acetophenone, PhCOCH3, into benzoate and chloroform with NaOCl (excess).

Show answer
  1. HO− removes an α-H: enolate PhC(O−)=CH2. The enolate attacks the chlorine of OCl− (or Cl2) to give PhCOCH2Cl. Repeat twice (each Cl makes the remaining α-H more acidic) to give PhCOCCl3.
  2. HO− adds to C=O: tetrahedral alkoxide.
  3. Collapse expels −CCl3 (a stabilised carbanion, three −I chlorines), giving PhCOOH.
  4. Proton transfer from PhCOOH to −CCl3: PhCOO− + CHCl3 (irreversible).
Question 319 Mechanism

NBS

Write the chain mechanism for the allylic bromination of propene by NBS to give allyl bromide.

Show answer
  1. Initiation: Br2 (from NBS/HBr) → 2Br• (hν or peroxide).
  2. Propagation 1: Br• + CH3CH=CH2 → HBr + •CH2CH=CH2 (allyl radical, resonance-stabilised).
  3. Fast: HBr + NBS → Br2 + succinimide (keeps [Br2] low).
  4. Propagation 2: •CH2CH=CH2 + Br2 → BrCH2CH=CH2 + Br•.
  5. Termination: radical combination.
Question 320 Mechanism

Peroxide effect

Write the mechanism of the addition of HBr to 2-methylpropene with ROOR present.

Show answer
  1. RO–OR → 2RO•; RO• + HBr → ROH + Br•.
  2. Br• adds to CH2= of the alkene: (CH3)2C•–CH2Br (tertiary radical).
  3. (CH3)2C•–CH2Br + H–Br → (CH3)2CH–CH2Br (1-bromo-2-methylpropane) + Br•.
  4. Chain repeats; termination by radical coupling.
Question 321 Mechanism

Cannizzaro

Write the mechanism of the Cannizzaro reaction of benzaldehyde.

Show answer
  1. HO− adds to C=O: PhCH(O−)OH.
  2. This alkoxide transfers its hydride (H−) to the carbonyl carbon of a second benzaldehyde (rate-determining step).
  3. The first molecule becomes PhCOOH (then PhCOO−); the second becomes PhCH2O−.
  4. Proton exchange gives PhCOO−Na+ and PhCH2OH; there is no α-H, so no enolate forms.
Question 322 Mechanism

Favorskii

Write the mechanism for 2-chlorocyclohexanone + NaOCH3 → methyl cyclopentanecarboxylate.

Show answer
  1. MeO− removes the α′-H (C-6) to give an enolate.
  2. The enolate carbon displaces Cl− intramolecularly (SN2, 3-exo): bicyclic cyclopropanone intermediate.
  3. MeO− adds to the cyclopropanone C=O to give a tetrahedral alkoxide.
  4. The three-membered ring opens by cleavage of the C–C bond that gives the more stable carbanion (secondary), which is protonated by MeOH: methyl cyclopentanecarboxylate (ring contraction from six to five).
Question 323 Mechanism

Beckmann

Write the mechanism of the Beckmann rearrangement of cyclohexanone oxime to caprolactam.

Show answer
  1. H2SO4 protonates the oxime OH (good leaving group, H2O).
  2. The ring carbon *anti* to the leaving group migrates from C to N as water leaves (concerted), giving a nitrilium ion (ring-expanded).
  3. Water attacks the nitrilium carbon; proton transfers (tautomerisation) give ε-caprolactam (cyclic amide, 7-membered).
Question 324 Mechanism

Baeyer–Villiger

Write the mechanism of the oxidation of pinacolone (CH3COC(CH3)3) with mCPBA and state the product.

Show answer
  1. mCPBA adds to the protonated C=O to form the Criegee intermediate.
  2. The *tert*-butyl group (best migrating group, most able to bear positive charge) migrates from C to O as m-chlorobenzoate leaves, with retention of configuration.
  3. Loss of H+ gives tert-butyl acetate, CH3COOC(CH3)3.
Question 325 Mechanism

Wolff/Arndt–Eistert

Write the mechanism for the conversion of PhCOCl to PhCH2COOH by the Arndt–Eistert method.

Show answer
  1. PhCOCl + CH2N2 → PhCOCHN2·HCl (the nucleophilic carbon of diazomethane attacks C=O; Cl− leaves); a second CH2N2 removes HCl.
  2. Ag2O (or hν) loses N2 to give the α-keto carbene PhCO–CH:.
  3. Wolff rearrangement: Ph migrates from the carbonyl carbon to the carbene carbon: ketene PhCH=C=O.
  4. H2O adds to the ketene: PhCH2COOH.
Question 326 Mechanism

Claisen condensation

Write the mechanism for the formation of ethyl acetoacetate from ethyl acetate with NaOEt.

Show answer
  1. EtO− removes an α-H of ethyl acetate to give the ester enolate CH2=C(O−)OEt (small equilibrium concentration).
  2. The enolate carbon attacks the C=O of a second molecule of ethyl acetate: tetrahedral intermediate.
  3. EtO− is expelled, giving ethyl acetoacetate CH3COCH2COOEt.
  4. EtO− removes the very acidic CH2 proton (pKa 11) of the product to give the stabilised enolate: this irreversible step drives the reaction. Acidification gives ethyl acetoacetate.
Question 327 Mechanism

E1cB dehydration

Write the mechanism for the base-catalysed dehydration of 3-hydroxybutanal to crotonaldehyde.

Show answer
  1. HO− removes the acidic α-H: enolate CH3CH(OH)–CH=CH–O−.
  2. The lone pair of the enolate carbanion pushes out HO− from the β-carbon (E1cB): CH3CH=CH–CHO.
  3. The new C=C is conjugated with C=O (which drives the loss of the poor leaving group OH−).
Question 328 Mechanism

Fischer esterification

Write the mechanism for acid-catalysed esterification of ethanoic acid with ethanol and say how the equilibrium is driven.

Show answer
  1. Protonation of the carbonyl O makes the carbon more electrophilic.
  2. Ethanol adds to C=O (tetrahedral intermediate).
  3. Proton transfer from the ethanol-derived O to one OH.
  4. Loss of water (the better leaving group) regenerates C=O.
  5. Deprotonation gives the ester. All steps are reversible; drive by excess ethanol or removal of water (Dean–Stark).
Question 329 Mechanism

SNAr

Write the mechanism for the reaction of 1-fluoro-2,4-dinitrobenzene with NH3 and explain why F is a better leaving group here than I.

Show answer
  1. NH3 adds to the carbon bearing F (rate-determining), giving the Meisenheimer complex with the negative charge delocalised onto the ortho and para NO2 groups.
  2. Loss of F− (fast) restores aromaticity; deprotonation of the ammonium gives 2,4-dinitroaniline.
  3. The rate-determining addition step is accelerated by the strongly electron-withdrawing F (it makes the ipso carbon more δ+); breaking the C–F bond occurs after the slow step. Hence F > Cl > Br > I in SNAr.
