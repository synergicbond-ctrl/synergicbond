55. Problem bank: conformations, figures and extra counting

Practice 401 L2 Figure

Newman projection of butane

Identify the conformer and give its relative energy.

Show answer

A staggered conformer with the two CH3 groups at 60°: gauche, about 3.8 kJ mol-1 above anti (steric repulsion of the two methyls).

Practice 402 L2 Figure

Newman projection of butane

Identify the conformer and give its relative energy.

Show answer

The two CH3 groups are directly aligned (0°): the fully eclipsed (syn) conformer, the highest-energy form of butane, about 19 kJ mol-1 above anti (one CH3/CH3 eclipse of 11 kJ plus two H/H eclipses of 4 kJ each).

Practice 403 L2 Figure

Energy profile

Which dihedral angle corresponds to the global minimum of butane? Read the profile.

Show answer

The anti conformer (CH3–C–C–CH3 = 180°) is the global minimum (energy 0). Two gauche minima lie at 3.8 kJ mol-1, separated by the 19 kJ mol-1 syn maximum.

Practice 405 L2 Figure

Chair diagram

What is the stereochemistry of this 1,3-disubstituted cyclohexane, and does it ring-flip readily?

Show answer

Equatorial t-butyl, axial methyl at C-3: a trans-1,3 isomer (1,3 a,e is trans). Ring flip would put t-Bu axial (> 20 kJ), so the molecule is effectively locked in the shown chair; the axial CH3 costs about 7.6 kJ.

Practice 407 L2 Calc

Br2 + isoprene: how many products?

How many constitutional products and how many stereoisomers form if isoprene reacts with 1 equivalent of Br2?

Show answer

Three modes of addition: (i) across C1=C2: 3,4-dibromo-3-methylbut-1-ene (one stereocentre: 2 forms); (ii) across C3=C4: 3,4-dibromo-2-methylbut-1-ene (one stereocentre: 2 forms); (iii) 1,4-addition: 1,4-dibromo-2-methylbut-2-ene (E and Z: 2 forms). 3 structural, 6 stereoisomers (confirmed by enumeration).

Practice 408 L2 Calc

HBr + isoprene

How many structural and stereoisomeric products can HBr give with isoprene (1 equiv.) through the tertiary allylic cation?

Show answer

Two structural products: 3-bromo-3-methylbut-1-ene (C-3 carries CH3, Br, CH=CH2 and CH3: not a stereocentre) and 1-bromo-3-methylbut-2-ene (two CH3 on C-3: no E/Z). So 2 products and no stereoisomers.

Practice 409 L3 Integer

Stereoisomers of a D–A adduct

(2E,4E)-Hexa-2,4-diene + fumaric acid (trans). How many stereoisomers of the adduct (3,6-dimethylcyclohex-4-ene-1,2-dicarboxylic acid) can form?

Show answer

The trans diacid on a ring where the two CH3 (outside groups) are cis: the product has no mirror plane (trans diester) so it is chiral; a single diastereomer forms as its racemate: 2 stereoisomers (R,R... enantiomers).

Practice 410 L3 Integer

Stereoisomers of a D–A adduct

(2E,4E)-Hexa-2,4-diene reacts with acrylonitrile. The adduct is a dimethylcyclohexene carbonitrile (ring carbons: C–CN, CH2, two CH3-bearing carbons and the C=C). How many ring stereocentres does it have and how many stereoisomers form (endo and exo, counting enantiomers)?

Show answer

The CN-bearing carbon and the two CH3-bearing carbons are stereocentres: 3. The two CH3 are cis (E,E-diene). The endo and exo orientations of CN give two diastereomers, each chiral: 4 stereoisomers.

Show answer

The first step (H abstraction, ΔH = +2 kcal/mol) has the higher barrier, so it is rate-determining; the second is strongly exothermic with a very low barrier.

Practice 412 L2 Figure

Energy diagram for E1 dehydration

Which step is rate-determining for E1 dehydration of a tertiary alcohol, and what is the intermediate of highest energy?

Show answer

The loss of water from the protonated alcohol (TS2, highest barrier) is rate-determining; the carbocation is the highest-energy intermediate.

Practice 413 L2 Figure

Cis or trans?

Is this 2-methylcyclopentanol cis or trans, and which reagents form it from 1-methylcyclopentene?

Trans (the CH3 and OH are on opposite faces): formed by hydroboration–oxidation (BH3, then H2O2/NaOH), as a racemate. The cis isomer would need a different route (e.g. reduction of 2-methylcyclopentanone).

Practice 416 L3 Integer

Stereoisomers of a tribromide

Br2 adds to 3-bromocyclohexene to give 1,2,3-tribromocyclohexane. How many stereoisomers does 1,2,3-tribromocyclohexane have?

Show answer

Three stereocentres but C-1 and C-3 are equivalent by symmetry, so some forms are meso: 4 stereoisomers in all (enumeration): two meso forms and one pair of enantiomers.

Practice 420 L3 Integer

Kolbe counting

A mixture of CH3COONa, CH3CH2COONa and (CH3)2CHCOONa is electrolysed. How many alkanes in total (coupling products)?

Show answer

Radicals Me•, Et•, iPr•: 6 couplings (3 self + 3 cross): ethane, butane, 2,3-dimethylbutane, propane, isobutane, isopentane — all distinct: 6.

Practice 421 L3 Integer

Acidic H and Grignard

How many moles of CH4 are evolved when 1 mol of HC≡C–CH2–CH2–OH reacts with excess CH3MgBr?

Show answer

Two acidic hydrogens (≡C–H and O–H): 2 mol CH4 (Zerewitinoff determination).

Practice 422 L3 Integer

Zerewitinoff

How many moles of CH4 per mole of HOOC–C≡C–CH2OH with CH3MgBr (excess)?

Show answer

COOH (1) + OH (1) = 2 acidic hydrogens (no terminal ≡C–H); 2 mol CH4 first formed.

Practice 423 L3 Integer

Stereoisomers of trimethylcyclopropane

How many stereoisomers does 1,2,3-trimethylcyclopropane have?

Show answer

2: the all-cis isomer and the isomer in which one CH3 is trans to the other two. Both are achiral (the central carbon is a pseudoasymmetric centre); confirmed by enumeration.
