40. The Diels–Alder reaction

A [4+2] cycloaddition: a conjugated diene (4 π electrons) + a dienophile (2 π electrons) → a cyclohexene, forming two new σ bonds and one new π bond in a single concerted step through a cyclic six-electron aromatic-like transition state (thermal, suprafacial on both partners).

+ Δ, 200 °C, pressure⟶
Butadiene + ethene → cyclohexene (poor, unactivated)
+ Δ⟶
Butadiene + maleic anhydride → cis-1,2,3,6-tetrahydrophthalic anhydride (meso)

40.1 Requirements and reactivity

  • Diene must adopt the s-cis conformation (both C=C on the same side of the central single bond). s-trans dienes are more stable for acyclic butadiene (≈ 12 kJ) but they interconvert rapidly (barrier ≈ 30 kJ). Locked s-cis dienes (cyclopentadiene, cyclohexa-1,3-diene, furan) are very reactive; locked s-trans dienes (e.g. in a bicyclic ring) do not react at all. A diene with a Z substituent at C-1 (or C-4) is slow because the "inside" substituent clashes with the other terminus in s-cis. (Z,Z)-hexa-2,4-diene practically does not react.
  • Normal electron demand: electron-rich diene (EDG: –R, –OR, –NR2) + electron-poor dienophile (EWG: –CHO, –COR, –COOR, –C≡N, –NO2, –CO–O–CO–, maleic anhydride, quinones, tetracyanoethene). Rate rises with FMO gap reduction (HOMOdiene–LUMOdienophile).
  • Typical dienophiles: maleic anhydride, acrolein, methyl acrylate, acrylonitrile, benzoquinone, dimethyl acetylenedicarboxylate (gives 1,4-cyclohexadiene), ethene (poor).
  • Reversibility: the retro-Diels–Alder occurs at high temperature (cracking of dicyclopentadiene to cyclopentadiene at 170 °C).
Example 54 Foundation

Which diene reacts?

Which of the following will undergo Diels–Alder with maleic anhydride? (A) cyclopentadiene (B) (E,E)-hexa-2,4-diene (C) (Z,Z)-hexa-2,4-diene (D) penta-1,4-diene (E) furan

Show solution
  1. A: yes (locked s-cis, fastest). B: yes. C: no (Z substituents clash in s-cis). D: no (isolated diene, not conjugated). E: yes (furan, the 5-membered aromatic ring can act as diene; the adduct is an oxabicycle).
  2. Answer: A, B, E.

40.2 Stereochemistry

(i) Stereospecific syn addition with respect to the dienophile: the relative geometry of substituents on the dienophile is preserved. Cis dienophile → cis substituents on the new ring; trans → trans. Maleic acid/anhydride (cis) → cis-1,2-diacid; fumaric acid (trans) → trans-1,2-diacid (racemic).

+ Δ⟶
Butadiene + maleic acid → cis-cyclohex-4-ene-1,2-dicarboxylic acid (meso)
+ Δ⟶
Butadiene + fumaric acid → trans-cyclohex-4-ene-1,2-dicarboxylic acid (racemic)

(ii) The diene's terminal substituents: an (E,E)-1,4-disubstituted diene gives a product in which the two substituents from the diene termini are cis to each other (both "outside" groups point the same way). (E,Z) gives trans.

Example 55 Advanced

Stereochemistry of an E,E-diene

(2E,4E)-Hexa-2,4-diene reacts with maleic anhydride. Give the product (describe stereochemistry) and say how many stereoisomers form.

Show solution
  1. Both CH3 groups of the (E,E)-diene are "outside" substituents; they end up cis to each other on the new ring (C-3 and C-6). The anhydride is cis-fused (cis dienophile).
  2. The endo adduct has the anhydride ring cis to both methyl groups (all-cis). A mirror plane passes through the middle of the ring, so the product is meso: 1 stereoisomer (3,6-dimethylcyclohex-4-ene-cis-1,2-dicarboxylic anhydride).

(iii) Endo rule: with a cyclic diene or when the dienophile carries a π-substituent (C=O) the endo product (substituent under the diene, near the new C=C, favoured by secondary orbital overlap between the dienophile's C=O and the diene's C-2/C-3) forms faster (kinetic control). The exo product is more stable (less steric strain) and appears at high temperature (reversibility) or with furan.

+ Δ⟶
Cyclopentadiene + maleic anhydride → bridged endo adduct, cis-norbornene-5,6-endo-dicarboxylic anhydride (the anhydride ring lies under the C=C, anti to the CH₂ bridge)
+ 25 °C⟶
Cyclopentadiene dimerises (one molecule is diene, the other dienophile): endo-dicyclopentadiene; retro-Diels–Alder at 170 °C regenerates the monomer

(iv) Regiochemistry ("ortho–para rule"): for an unsymmetrical diene and dienophile:

  • A 1-substituted diene (e.g. 1-methoxybuta-1,3-diene or piperylene) + a monosubstituted dienophile → mainly the "ortho" (1,2) product.
  • A 2-substituted diene (isoprene) + a monosubstituted dienophile → mainly the "para" (1,4) product.

Rationale: the largest HOMO coefficient of the diene (at C-4 for a diene donor at C-1, and at C-1 for a donor at C-2) bonds to the dienophile carbon with the largest LUMO coefficient (the β carbon of the acrylate-type dienophile).

+ Δ⟶
Isoprene + methyl acrylate → "para" product: methyl 4-methylcyclohex-3-ene-1-carboxylate (1,4-relationship)
+ Δ⟶
Piperylene (1-methylbutadiene) + methyl acrylate → "ortho": methyl 2-methylcyclohex-3-ene-1-carboxylate (1,2-relationship)

40.3 Counting and drawing the product

  • Step 1: draw the diene in s-cis; number C-1 to C-4; the dienophile carbons are a and b.
  • Step 2: bonds form C-1–a and C-4–b; the new double bond is C-2=C-3.
  • Step 3: substituents: dienophile geometry is retained (cis stays cis); the two "outside" diene substituents end up cis to each other; in the endo adduct the dienophile's activating groups are cis to those outside groups (all-cis arrangement).
Example 56 Integer

Number of stereoisomers

How many stereoisomers does the Diels–Alder adduct of (a) buta-1,3-diene + maleic anhydride, (b) buta-1,3-diene + fumaric acid, (c) cyclopentadiene + maleic anhydride have?

Show solution
  1. (a) cis-fused, meso: 1 stereoisomer.
  2. (b) trans-1,2-diacid: 2 (R,R and S,S enantiomers).
  3. (c) Cyclopentadiene + maleic anhydride gives two diastereomers, endo (kinetic) and exo; each is achiral (mirror plane), so 2 stereoisomers in total. Kinetic control favours the endo adduct.
