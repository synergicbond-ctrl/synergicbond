Example 63 Main

Pick the reagent for the stated selectivity

Give a reagent that accomplishes each: (a) CH3CH=CH–CHO → CH3CH=CH–CH2OH, (b) CH3CH=CH–COOH → CH3CH=CH–CH2OH, (c) PhCH2CH2OH → PhCH2CHO, (d) CH3COCH2CH2COOCH3 → CH3CH(OH)CH2CH2COOCH3.

Show solution
  1. (a) NaBH4 (or Al(O-i-Pr)3/i-PrOH, MPV): reduces C=O only and leaves C=C.
  2. (b) LiAlH4 in ether at low temperature (or esterify, then DIBAL-H): reduces COOH and spares the isolated-type C=C. BH3·THF is unsuitable here because it would also hydroborate the C=C.
  3. (c) PCC in CH2Cl2 (stops at the aldehyde; Jones reagent would give the acid).
  4. (d) NaBH4 in ethanol at 25 °C: the ketone is reduced and the ester is untouched.
Example 64 Adv

Chemoselectivity with a protecting group

Convert ethyl 4-oxopentanoate, CH3COCH2CH2COOC2H5, into 5-hydroxypentan-2-one, CH3COCH2CH2CH2OH.

Show solution
  1. LiAlH4 would reduce both C=O groups, so mask the ketone: (i) HOCH2CH2OH, H+ (cyclic ketal, removing water); (ii) LiAlH4, dry ether, then H2O (ester → CH2OH; the ketal is untouched); (iii) dilute H3O+ removes the ketal and returns the ketone: 5-hydroxypentan-2-one (as the intramolecular hemiketal in equilibrium).
