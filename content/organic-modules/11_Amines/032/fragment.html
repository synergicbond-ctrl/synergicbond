32. Reactions of nitro compounds

32.1 Reduction (revisited)

Nitroalkane: Sn/HCl or H2/Ni → RNH2; Zn/NH4Cl → RNHOH (alkylhydroxylamine). Nitroarene: see Section 7 (medium-dependent). Hydrolysis of a 1° nitroalkane with hot conc. HCl or H2SO4 gives the carboxylic acid + hydroxylamine salt: RCH2NO2 + H2O → RCOOH + NH2OH. (Useful as a preparation of NH2OH.)

32.2 Electrophilic substitution of nitrobenzene

Nitrobenzene gives meta products: nitration → 1,3-dinitrobenzene; sulphonation (oleum, heat) → m-nitrobenzenesulphonic acid; bromination (Br2/Fe, heat) → m-bromonitrobenzene. No Friedel–Crafts.

32.3 Nucleophilic aromatic substitution (SNAr)

A halogen on a ring carrying o- or p-nitro groups is replaced by nucleophiles (OH−, NH3, OR−, N2H4) by addition–elimination: Nu− adds to the carbon bearing X, giving the resonance-stabilised Meisenheimer complex (negative charge delocalised onto the nitro oxygens), and then X− leaves. A meta nitro group cannot take part in the delocalisation, so m-halonitrobenzenes are not activated. The more nitro groups, the faster.

  • Rate order of the leaving group is F > Cl ≈ Br > I (rate-determining step is addition, favoured by the electron-withdrawing, highly polar C–F bond) – opposite to SN2.
  • Chlorobenzene is inert to NaOH at ordinary temperatures (needs 623 K, 300 atm: Dow process); p-chloronitrobenzene reacts with aq. NaOH at ≈ 433–443 K; 2,4-dinitrochlorobenzene at ≈ 368 K; 2,4,6-trinitrochlorobenzene (picryl chloride) at room temperature.

Example 32 Main

Alkyl halide + nitrite

Ethyl bromide reacts with (a) AgNO2 and (b) KNO2. Give the major products and state how you could distinguish them.

Show solution
  1. (a) Nitroethane CH3CH2NO2; (b) ethyl nitrite CH3CH2ONO (mainly). The reaction at O versus N is governed by the nature of the salt (covalent AgNO2: attack by N; ionic KNO2: attack by O).
  2. Distinguish: reduction with Sn/HCl gives ethylamine from nitroethane but ethanol (+ NH3) from ethyl nitrite; alkali hydrolysis converts the nitrite to ethanol + nitrite ion but leaves nitroethane unchanged (apart from forming the soluble nitronate). Nitroethane has the much higher b.p. (115 °C vs 17 °C).
Example 33 Adv

Order of SNAr reactivity

Arrange in decreasing rate of reaction with aqueous NaOH: chlorobenzene, 1-chloro-4-nitrobenzene, 1-chloro-2,4-dinitrobenzene, 1-chloro-3-nitrobenzene.

Show solution
  1. The rate depends on the stability of the Meisenheimer complex: two o/p nitro groups > one p-nitro > meta-nitro (only −I, no resonance) > none.
  2. 1-Chloro-2,4-dinitrobenzene > 1-chloro-4-nitrobenzene > 1-chloro-3-nitrobenzene > chlorobenzene.
Example 34 Adv

Products

Nitromethane is treated with one equivalent of benzaldehyde and a catalytic amount of base, and the product is heated with acid. Give the final product and name the reactions.

Show solution
  1. Henry reaction: PhCH(OH)CH2NO2; on heating with acid, dehydration (the CH2 α to NO2 is acidic; the benzylic OH is lost) gives β-nitrostyrene, PhCH=CH–NO2.
Example 35 Adv

Which tests?

Three liquids: nitromethane, 2-nitropropane and 2-methyl-2-nitropropane. Give a one-reagent test that separates all three.

Show solution
  1. NaNO2/H2SO4 followed by NaOH: nitromethane → nitrolic acid → blood-red; 2-nitropropane → pseudonitrole → blue (in organic layer/ with chloroform); 2-methyl-2-nitropropane → no colour.
