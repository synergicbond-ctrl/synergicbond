17. Claisen condensation (ester condensation)

Reagents: 2 RCH2COOR′ + NaOR′ (the alkoxide matching the ester, in stoichiometric amounts), then H+. Product: β-keto ester. Example: 2 CH3COOEt → CH3COCH2COOEt (ethyl acetoacetate) + EtOH. Mechanism: (1) ethoxide removes an α-H giving the ester enolate (pKa 25 vs EtOH 16: only a small equilibrium amount); (2) the enolate attacks the C=O of a second ester (tetrahedral intermediate); (3) it expels EtO− (addition–elimination); (4) the β-keto ester product (pKa ≈ 11) is deprotonated by EtO−: this step is irreversible and drives the whole condensation. Acidification liberates the neutral β-keto ester.

Key idea Reason a full equivalent of base is consumed: the product has the doubly activated CH2, whose conjugate base is stable. An ester with only one α-H (e.g. ethyl isobutanoate) gives a β-keto ester that has no acidic H between the two C=O, so the equilibrium is unfavourable and a stronger base such as Ph3CNa is required.

Diethyl oxalate: highly electrophilic, no α-H

Good non-enolisable partners for a crossed Claisen: ethyl benzoate, ethyl formate (introduces –CHO, gives a β-keto aldehyde), diethyl carbonate (introduces –CO2Et), diethyl oxalate.

Example 25 Main

Predict the Claisen products

Give the product of (a) ethyl propanoate with NaOEt (self-condensation), (b) ethyl formate + acetone with NaOEt.

Show solution
  1. (a) 2 CH3CH2COOEt → CH3CH2CO–CH(CH3)–COOEt (ethyl 2-methyl-3-oxopentanoate) + EtOH. The nucleophile is the α-carbon (CH2) of one ester; the electrophile is the acyl carbon of the other.
  2. (b) Ethyl formate is the electrophile (no α-H) and acetone is deprotonated: CH3COCH2CHO (3-oxobutanal, isolated as its sodium enolate) is formed.
