Practice 1 L1 Foundation

Ethyl bromide reacts with hydroxide ion in a single, concerted elementary step. The rate law is

(A) rate = k[CH3CH2Br]

(B) rate = k[OH–]

(C) rate = k[CH3CH2Br][OH–]

(D) rate = k[CH3CH2Br]2

Show answer
  1. A concerted one-step reaction between two species is an elementary bimolecular process.
  2. For an elementary step the rate law follows the molecularity: first order in each reactant, so rate = k[RBr][OH–].

Answer: C

Practice 2 L1 Foundation

Practice 3 L1 Foundation

The stereochemical result of a pure SN2 reaction at a stereogenic carbon is

(A) retention of configuration

(B) racemisation

(C) inversion of configuration

(D) formation of a meso compound only

Show answer
  1. The nucleophile approaches from the side opposite to the leaving group.
  2. The other three substituents flip across the carbon, so the spatial arrangement is inverted (Walden inversion).

Practice 28 L2 Main

(S)-2-Bromo-1-fluoropropane, CH3CHBrCH2F, reacts with NaCN in DMSO by a clean SN2 reaction. The product CH3CH(CN)CH2F has the descriptor

(A) R, because inversion always changes S to R

(B) S, because inversion of geometry is accompanied by an exchange of the two highest CIP ranks

(C) a racemic (RS) pair, because the nucleophile is achiral

(D) cannot be assigned

Show answer
  1. SN2 inverts the geometry. Substrate priorities: Br &gt; CH2F (F,H,H) &gt; CH3 &gt; H.
  2. Product priorities: CH2F (F,H,H) beats CN (N,N,N) at the first point of difference (F &gt; N), so CH2F &gt; CN &gt; CH3 &gt; H.
  3. The two highest ranks exchange, so the descriptor stays S even though the molecule is inverted.

Answer: B

Practice 29 L2 Main

The following initial-rate data were collected for CH3CH2CH2Br + OH– at constant temperature.
Exp 1: [RBr] = 0.10 M, [OH–] = 0.10 M, rate = 4.0 × 10–4 M s–1
Exp 2: [RBr] = 0.20 M, [OH–] = 0.10 M, rate = 8.0 × 10–4 M s–1
Exp 3: [RBr] = 0.10 M, [OH–] = 0.20 M, rate = 8.0 × 10–4 M s–1
The rate for [RBr] = 0.30 M and [OH–] = 0.20 M is

(A) 1.2 × 10–3 M s–1

(B) 2.4 × 10–3 M s–1

(C) 4.8 × 10–3 M s–1

(D) 7.2 × 10–3 M s–1

Show answer
  1. Exp 2 vs 1: doubling [RBr] doubles the rate, so first order in RBr; Exp 3 vs 1: doubling [OH–] doubles the rate, so first order in OH–. Rate = k[RBr][OH–].
  2. k = 4.0 × 10–4/(0.10 × 0.10) = 4.0 × 10–2 M–1 s–1.
  3. Rate = 4.0 × 10–2 × 0.30 × 0.20 = 2.4 × 10–3 M s–1.

Answer: B

Practice 30 L2 Main

The solvolysis of a tertiary halide is first order with k = 1.155 × 10–3 s–1. Taking ln 2 = 0.693, what is its half-life in minutes?

Show answer

  2. t½ = 0.693/(1.155 × 10–3 s–1) = 600 s = 10 min.

A substitution reaction has Ea = 83.1 kJ mol–1 (assume A is temperature independent, R = 8.314 J mol–1 K–1). By what factor, to the nearest integer, does the rate constant increase on raising the temperature from 300 K to 310 K?

Practice 32 L2 Main

The rate constant of an SN2 reaction doubles when the temperature is raised from 298 K to 308 K. Using R = 8.314 J mol–1 K–1 and ln 2 = 0.693, the activation energy in kJ mol–1 (nearest integer) is

Show answer

Practice 33 L2 Main

Which statements about the SN2 mechanism are correct?

(A) The rate depends on the concentrations of both the substrate and the nucleophile.

(B) A carbocation intermediate is formed in the slow step.

Practice 36 L2 Main

Methyl iodide reacts with sodium azide in DMSO and, separately, in methanol. The correct comparison is

(A) faster in methanol, because methanol hydrogen-bonds to azide and activates it

(B) much faster in DMSO, because azide is poorly solvated (more reactive) in a polar aprotic solvent
