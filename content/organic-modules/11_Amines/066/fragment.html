66. Level 1, set B — basicity

Practice 11 L1 Basicity

Arrange in increasing order of basic strength: NH3, CH3NH2, C6H5NH2.

Show answer
  1. CH3 (+I) increases the electron density on N and stabilises CH3NH3+: stronger than NH3. In aniline the lone pair is delocalised into the ring (and anilinium is not stabilised): weaker than NH3. pKb: 3.38, 4.75, 9.38.

C6H5NH2 < NH3 < CH3NH2.

Practice 12 L1 Basicity

In aqueous solution, which is the stronger base: (CH3)2NH or (CH3)3N? Which is stronger in the gas phase? Why do they differ?

Show answer
  1. In the gas phase only the +I effect matters; three methyls give the most electron-rich N. In water the ammonium ion is stabilised by hydrogen bonding: (CH3)3NH+ has one N–H (little solvation), (CH3)2NH2+ has two. The extra solvation outweighs the smaller +I effect.

Aqueous: (CH3)2NH (pKb 3.27 vs 4.22). Gas phase: (CH3)3N.

Practice 13 L1 Numerical

The pKb of aniline is 9.38 and that of ethylamine is 3.29. By what factor is Kb of ethylamine larger?

Show answer
  1. Kb(ethylamine)/Kb(aniline) = 10(9.38 − 3.29) = 106.09 = 1.23 × 106.

About 1.2 × 106.

Practice 14 L1 Numerical

Calculate the pH of 0.10 M methylamine (pKb = 3.38, 298 K).

Show answer
  1. Kb = 10−3.38 = 4.17 × 10−4. Let x = [OH−]: x²/(0.10 − x) = 4.17 × 10−4 → x = 6.25 × 10−3 M (quadratic solved; the approximation √(KbC) = 6.46 × 10−3 is 3 % high).
  2. pOH = −log(6.25 × 10−3) = 2.20, pH = 14 − 2.20 = 11.80.

pH ≈ 11.8.

Practice 15 L1 Numerical

Calculate the pH of 0.010 M aniline (pKb = 9.38).

Show answer
  1. Kb = 10−9.38 = 4.17 × 10−10. Very small, so x = √(KbC) = √(4.17 × 10−12) = 2.04 × 10−6 M.
  2. pOH = 5.69; pH = 8.31.

pH ≈ 8.3.

Practice 16 L1 Basicity

What is the pKa of the methylammonium ion if pKb of methylamine is 3.38?

Show answer
  1. pKa + pKb = 14 at 298 K for a conjugate acid–base pair, so pKa = 14 − 3.38 = 10.62.

10.62.

Practice 17 L1 Basicity

Which is the stronger base: aniline or p-toluidine? Explain.

Show answer
  1. The methyl group at para is electron donating (+I, hyperconjugation), increasing the electron density on N and the stability of the ion: pKa of the conjugate acid 5.1 versus 4.6.

p-Toluidine.

Practice 18 L1 Basicity

Why is p-nitroaniline a much weaker base than aniline?

Show answer
  1. In p-nitroaniline the structure H2N+=C6H4=NO2− contributes strongly; the lone pair is less available for protonation. pKa of the conjugate acid: 1.0 versus 4.6 for aniline.

The –NO2 group at para withdraws electrons by −I and −M effects; the N lone pair is delocalised on to the nitro group.

Practice 19 L1 Basicity

Arrange in decreasing basic strength: acetamide, aniline, pyridine, ammonia.

Show answer
  1. pKa of conjugate acids: 9.25, 5.2, 4.6, −0.5. Acetamide's N lone pair is delocalised into C=O: practically non-basic; pyridine is sp2; aniline's lone pair is delocalised into the ring.

NH3 > pyridine > aniline > acetamide.

Practice 20 L1 Basicity

Arrange pyrrole, pyridine and piperidine in increasing basicity.

Show answer
  1. Pyrrole: lone pair part of the aromatic sextet (non-basic). Pyridine: sp2 N, lone pair outside the π system (pKa 5.2). Piperidine: sp3 N, an ordinary secondary alkylamine (pKa 11.1).

Pyrrole < pyridine < piperidine.

Practice 21 L1 Basicity

Why does aniline dissolve in dilute HCl although it is a very weak base?

Show answer
  1. At pH 1, [ArNH3+]/[ArNH2] = 10(pKa − pH) = 103.6 ≈ 4000. So essentially all aniline is converted to the salt.

Even a weak base is protonated by a strong acid: C6H5NH2 + H+ → C6H5NH3+, which is a soluble ionic salt (pKa 4.6 means that at pH 1 anilinium is the dominant form by a factor of 103.6).

Practice 22 L1 Basicity

Compare cyclohexylamine and aniline: which is the stronger base and by roughly how many pK units?

Show answer
  1. In cyclohexylamine the N lone pair is localised and the alkyl group is +I. In aniline the lone pair is conjugated with the ring.

Cyclohexylamine; the conjugate-acid pKa is about 10.6 against 4.6 – a difference of about 6 units.

Practice 23 L1 Basicity

Benzylamine (pKb 4.70) and aniline (pKb 9.38) both contain C6H5–N. Why is benzylamine about 105 times more basic?

Show answer
  1. The CH2 insulates N from the ring: no resonance delocalisation, only a mild −I effect of the phenyl.

In benzylamine the nitrogen is attached to an sp3 carbon (CH2); its lone pair is not conjugated with the ring.

Practice 24 L1 Basicity

Arrange in decreasing basicity in water: C2H5NH2, (C2H5)2NH, (C2H5)3N, NH3.

Show answer
  1. pKb: 3.00, 3.25, 3.29, 4.75. Alkyl groups raise basicity (+I); the 3° ion is less solvated and sterically hindered, so the 2° amine is best in water.

(C2H5)2NH > (C2H5)3N > C2H5NH2 > NH3.

Practice 25 L1 Basicity

Which of o-toluidine and p-toluidine is the stronger base and why?

Show answer
  1. Ortho effect: the ortho substituent hinders solvation of the –NH3+ group (and protonation), so even an electron-releasing group at ortho weakens the base relative to the para isomer.

p-Toluidine (pKa 5.1 vs 4.4 for o-toluidine).

Practice 26 L1 Basicity

Which is more basic: m-nitroaniline or p-nitroaniline?

Show answer
  1. At para, NO2 withdraws by −I and −M; at meta, only by −I (no direct conjugation with the amino group). So the lone pair is more available in the meta isomer.

m-Nitroaniline (pKa 2.5 vs 1.0).

Practice 27 L1 Basicity

Arrange in decreasing order of gas-phase basicity: CH3NH2, (CH3)3N, (CH3)2NH, NH3.

Show answer
  1. Without solvent the order follows the number of +I alkyl groups (and their polarisability). Proton affinities (kJ mol-1): 949, 930, 899, 854.

(CH3)3N > (CH3)2NH > CH3NH2 > NH3.

Practice 28 L1 Basicity

Among guanidine, ammonia and aniline, which is the strongest base and why?

Show answer
  1. Protonation gives the guanidinium ion, stabilised by three equivalent resonance structures (Y-delocalisation); its conjugate acid has pKa 13.6 versus 9.25 (NH3) and 4.6 (aniline).

Guanidine.

Practice 29 L1 Acidity

Which is the stronger acid: NH4+ or C6H5NH3+? Explain using basicity.

Show answer
  1. The weaker the base, the stronger its conjugate acid. Aniline is a weaker base than NH3, so anilinium gives up H+ more readily.

C6H5NH3+ (pKa 4.6 vs 9.25).

Practice 30 L1 Basicity

Explain why amides are neutral whereas amines are basic.

Show answer
  1. Resonance: RC(=O)NH2 ↔ RC(–O−)=N+H2. Protonation of an amide occurs on O (not N), with pKa ≈ −0.5 for the conjugate acid.

In an amide the N lone pair is delocalised into the C=O group (n → π*), forming a partial C=N double bond; it is not available for protonation.

Practice 31 L1 Basicity

N,N-Dimethylaniline (pKb 8.92) is slightly more basic than aniline (9.38). What does this show?

Show answer
  1. Both compounds lose basicity through delocalisation into the ring; the difference is only the +I effect of two methyls (a modest increase of 0.46 pK units).

The +I effect of two methyl groups outweighs everything else in this pair; the lone pair of the 3° N is still conjugated with the ring, so the basicity remains low.

Practice 32 L1 Basicity

Why is ethylamine more basic than ammonia?

Show answer
  1. pKb 3.29 vs 4.75.

The ethyl group is electron releasing (+I); it raises the electron density on N and stabilises C2H5NH3+.

Practice 33 L1 Basicity

Which is more basic: diphenylamine or aniline? Which is more basic: N-methylaniline or aniline?

Show answer
  1. A second phenyl group delocalises the lone pair further (pKb of diphenylamine 13.1). A methyl group is +I and raises basicity slightly.

Aniline > diphenylamine; N-methylaniline ≈ aniline (slightly stronger, pKb 9.30 vs 9.38).

Practice 34 L1 Basicity

Rank NH3, CH3NH2 and CH3CONH2 by the pKa of their conjugate acids (largest first).

Show answer
  1. The strongest base has the conjugate acid with the largest pKa.

CH3NH3+ (10.6) > NH4+ (9.25) > protonated acetamide (≈ −0.5).

Practice 35 L1 Basicity

Which of the following solutions has the higher pH at equal concentration: dimethylamine or aniline?

Show answer
  1. pKb: 3.27 vs 9.38. For 0.05 M dimethylamine pH ≈ 11.7; for 0.05 M aniline pH ≈ 8.7.

Dimethylamine (a much stronger base).

Practice 36 L1 Basicity

Why is trimethylamine less basic than dimethylamine in water although it has more +I groups?

Show answer
  1. pKb: dimethylamine 3.27; trimethylamine 4.22.

(CH3)3NH+ has only one N–H bond for hydrogen bonding with water, and steric hindrance reduces its solvation; the loss of solvation energy outweighs the extra +I effect.

Practice 37 L1 Basicity

Place CH3NH2, C6H5NH2, C6H5CH2NH2 and (C6H5)2NH in decreasing order of basic strength.

Show answer
  1. pKb: 3.38, 4.70, 9.38, 13.1.

CH3NH2 > C6H5CH2NH2 > C6H5NH2 > (C6H5)2NH.

Practice 38 L1 Basicity

What is the pH of a 0.10 M solution of anilinium chloride? (pKa of anilinium 4.62)

Show answer
  1. Anilinium is a weak acid: [H+] = √(KaC) = √(2.40 × 10−5 × 0.10) = 1.55 × 10−3 M → pH = 2.81.

pH ≈ 2.8.

Practice 39 L1 Basicity

Which of these is not a Lewis base: NH3, (CH3)4N+, CH3NH2, pyridine?

Show answer
  1. A Lewis base needs a lone pair. The quaternary ion has no lone pair on N (all four electron pairs are in N–C bonds).

(CH3)4N+.
