18. Malonic ester synthesis

Diethyl malonate (diethyl propanedioate) is prepared from chloroacetic acid: ClCH2COOH → (NaCN) NCCH2COONa → (EtOH, H+) CH2(COOEt)2. The CH2 (pKa 13) is deprotonated by NaOEt; the enolate is alkylated by R–X (primary/methyl); repetition with R′–X gives the disubstituted ester; hydrolysis and decarboxylation of the substituted malonic acid (140–150 °C) gives a carboxylic acid RCH2COOH or RR′CHCOOH.

Key idea Carbon rule: RCH2COOH from malonic ester = R–X + 2 carbons (CH2COOH). Starting from the target acid R–CH2–COOH, disconnect the bond between the α-carbon and R: R–X is the alkylating agent. For R(R′)CH–COOH, disconnect both R and R′ (both from RX, R′X). Only 1° and methyl halides (SN2) can be used; benzyl and allyl halides are excellent.

Example 49 Main

Build the acid

Prepare from diethyl malonate: (a) pentanoic acid; (b) 2-methyl-3-phenylpropanoic acid; (c) cyclopentanecarboxylic acid; (d) succinic acid.

Show solution
  1. (a) Pentanoic acid CH3CH2CH2CH2COOH = R–CH2COOH with R = n-propyl: NaOEt, n-C3H7Br; hydrolysis, decarboxylation.
  2. (b) PhCH2CH(CH3)COOH: dialkylation with CH3I and PhCH2Cl (either order), then hydrolysis, decarboxylation.
  3. (c) 1,4-Dibromobutane with 2 equiv. of base: ring closure; hydrolysis and decarboxylation.
  4. (d) Succinic acid HOOCCH2CH2COOH: alkylate with ethyl chloroacetate ClCH2COOEt (or I2 oxidative coupling); the triester (EtOOC)2CH–CH2COOEt is hydrolysed and decarboxylated → HOOC–CH2–CH2–COOH.
Example 50 Adv

Malonic ester with epoxides and aldehydes

Diethyl malonate, NaOEt, then ethylene oxide, then acid and heat gives a lactone. Identify it.

Show solution
  1. The enolate opens the epoxide at the less hindered carbon: (EtOOC)2CH–CH2CH2O−. The alkoxide attacks one ester intramolecularly to give a five-membered lactone (α-carbethoxy-γ-butyrolactone). Acid hydrolysis and decarboxylation give γ-butyrolactone (oxolan-2-one).

18.3 Barbiturates

Diethyl 2,2-disubstituted malonates condense with urea and NaOEt to give barbiturates: diethyl diethylmalonate + urea → barbital (5,5-diethylbarbituric acid), the first barbiturate sedative. Malonic ester + urea (no substituents) gives barbituric acid.
