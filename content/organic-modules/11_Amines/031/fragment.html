31. Acidity of nitroalkanes: nitro–aci tautomerism

A nitroalkane with an α-hydrogen is a carbon acid much stronger than expected, because the conjugate base (nitronate/aci-nitro anion) delocalises the negative charge onto O. Approximate pKa: CH3NO2 10.2; CH3CH2NO2 8.6; (CH3)2CHNO2 7.7; compare acetone 19–20 and ethyl acetate 25. Nitromethane is therefore soluble in aqueous NaOH, and the nitronate on acidification gives first the aci form (nitronic acid) R2C=N(O)OH, a tautomer which slowly reverts to the nitro form.

  • The nitro compound with no α-H (e.g. 2-methyl-2-nitropropane (CH3)3CNO2, nitrobenzene) cannot form an aci form and is not soluble in alkali.
  • Reactions of the nitronate carbanion:
  • Henry (nitro-aldol) reaction: nitronate + aldehyde/ketone → β-nitro alcohol; dehydration gives a nitroalkene. CH3NO2 + PhCHO → PhCH(OH)CH2NO2 → PhCH=CHNO2 (β-nitrostyrene).
  • Michael addition to α,β-unsaturated carbonyls and nitriles.
  • Nef reaction: the nitronate (from 1°/2° nitroalkane) poured into cold dilute H2SO4 gives the aldehyde (from 1°) or ketone (from 2°): R2C=NO2− → R2C=O + N2O.
