10. Claisen and Fries rearrangements

Both rearrangements convert an O-substituted phenol into a C-substituted phenol, but they are mechanistically unrelated.

The Claisen rearrangement is a thermal [3,3]-sigmatropic shift of an allyl aryl ether. Heating allyl phenyl ether to about 470 K moves the allyl group from oxygen to the ortho carbon. Six atoms (the ortho and ipso ring carbons, O, CH2, CH=CH2) and six electrons take part in a cyclic chair-like transition state. The bond between O and the allyl CH2 breaks while a new bond forms between the ring ortho carbon and the far terminal carbon of the allyl group, so the allyl unit is inverted (the carbon that was the terminal =CH2 becomes attached to the ring). The initial cyclohexadienone then tautomerises to the aromatic phenol.

  1. Step 1: the six atoms (the ring ortho carbon, the ring carbon bearing O, O, the allylic CH2, and the two alkene carbons) line up in a cyclic six-membered transition state.
  2. Step 2: C–O σ bond breaks and the new C–C σ bond forms at the ring ortho position, giving 6-allylcyclohexa-2,4-dienone.
  3. Step 3: enolisation (keto → enol tautomerism) restores the aromatic ring: o-allylphenol.

Reactant:

Reagent: heat, about 470 K

Product:

Allyl phenyl ether → o-allylphenol.

Reactant:

Reagent: heat

Product:

Both ortho positions blocked: a second [3,3] shift (Cope) moves the allyl group to the para position, and 4-allyl-2,6-dimethylphenol is formed.

The Fries rearrangement converts a phenyl ester into a hydroxyaryl ketone using a Lewis acid (AlCl3). The acyl group migrates, as an acylium-type species, to the ortho or para position. At lower temperature (about 298 K or less) the para isomer predominates; at higher temperature (about 430 K or more) the ortho isomer predominates, stabilised by chelation of aluminium between the carbonyl and phenoxide oxygens. When the para position is blocked only the ortho ketone can form.

Reactant:

Reagent: AlCl3, heat

Product:

p-Tolyl acetate → 2-hydroxy-5-methylacetophenone.

Trap. In the Claisen rearrangement the allylic carbon that bonds to the ring is the one at the other end of the allyl group. For a crotyl ether (O–CH2CH=CH–CH3) the product is therefore the branched o-(1-methylprop-2-enyl)phenol, not the straight-chain o-crotylphenol.
