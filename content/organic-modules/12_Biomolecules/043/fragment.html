A.1 Sources actually opened

All problems and explanations in this chapter are original; the books below were used to confirm conventions and to find problem types that the chapter was missing.

  1. Organic Chemistry, 9th edition (L. G. Wade): Chapters 23 (carbohydrates and nucleic acids), 24 (amino acids, peptides, proteins). Pages consulted (printed page numbers 1182–1183, 1223–1230, and the Chargaff and sequencing problem sections): Haworth conventions and the "α is trans, β is cis to the terminal CH2OH" rule; the specific rotations +112.2° (α), +18.7° (β) and the equilibrium value (+52.6° in that book; +52.7° in many Indian texts: both appear); the L-configuration / (S) labelling of amino acids; the isoelectric points of the standard amino acids; the titration curve of glycine (pKa1 2.3, pKa2 9.6, pI 6.0); electrophoresis; Chargaff's rule (G = C and A = T does not imply G = A); Sanger, Edman, carboxypeptidase, trypsin and chymotrypsin cleavage conventions. Used to cross-check facts; no text or problem was reproduced.
  2. Advanced Problems in Organic Chemistry for JEE (M. S. Chouhan), 16th edition, Biomolecules chapter, Level 1 and Level 2 (printed pages 592–603). Used only to find problem types: pI from pKa/pKb data, reagent selection for a dipeptide, osazone relationships, anomer/epimer recognition, counting acetals and hemiacetals in a disaccharide, which sugars do not mutarotate, isomer counts of D-glucose, matrix matches of D/L with R/S. Every problem here uses different numbers, structures or sequences.
  3. Himanshu Pandey, Advanced Problems: searched for biomolecule content (carbohydrate, amino acid, osazone, Edman and related terms); no biomolecule section was found, so nothing was taken from it.
  4. Not used: McMurry, Finar Vol. 2 and other Google Drive titles named in the brief could not be downloaded (the Drive mount timed out), and Clayden, Sykes, Klein, Nasipuri and Carey–Sundberg were not opened for this chapter. They are therefore not claimed.
