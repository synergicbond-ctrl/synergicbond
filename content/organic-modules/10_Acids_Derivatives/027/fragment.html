26. Practice set, Level 1: Foundation (single correct and short answer)

Practice 1 L1 · Naming

Name CH3CH(CH3)CH2COOH.

(A) 2-methylbutanoic acid (B) 3-methylbutanoic acid (C) isopentanoic acid (IUPAC) (D) 2,2-dimethylpropanoic acid

Show answer
  1. Number from the COOH carbon as C-1: C-1 COOH, C-2 CH2, C-3 CH(CH3), C-4 CH3. The methyl is on C-3 and the parent chain is four carbons.
  2. (B) 3-methylbutanoic acid (isovaleric acid). (C) is a trivial name, not IUPAC; (A) would have the methyl on C-2; (D) is pivalic acid.
Practice 2 L1 · Naming

Give the IUPAC name of CH3CH2COOCH(CH3)2.

Show answer
  1. The ester name is “alkyl alkanoate”: the alkyl comes from the alcohol (the O–R part) = CH(CH3)2 = propan-2-yl (isopropyl); the acid part CH3CH2CO– = propanoate.
  2. Propan-2-yl propanoate (isopropyl propanoate).
Practice 3 L1 · Naming

Name CH3CONHCH3.

Show answer
  1. Parent amide from CH3CO–: ethanamide; the substituent on nitrogen is a methyl group, given the locant N.
  2. N-methylethanamide (N-methylacetamide).
Practice 4 L1 · Counting

How many carbon atoms are present in ethyl 3-oxobutanoate?

Show answer
  1. Butanoate chain = 4 C (C-1 ester carbon, C-2 CH2, C-3 C=O, C-4 CH3); the ethyl group adds 2 C. Total 6 carbons (formula C6H10O3).
Practice 5 L1 · Isomers

How many carboxylic acids have the formula C4H8O2?

Show answer
  1. C3H7COOH: the C3H7 group is n-propyl or isopropyl. Acids: butanoic acid and 2-methylpropanoic acid. 2.
Practice 6 L1 · Acidity

The strongest acid among FCH2COOH, ClCH2COOH, BrCH2COOH and CH3COOH is

Show answer
  1. The –I effect decreases F > Cl > Br: pKa values 2.59, 2.86, 2.90 versus 4.76 for acetic acid. FCH2COOH is the strongest.
Practice 7 L1 · Acidity

Which is the weakest acid? HCOOH, CH3COOH, C6H5COOH, ClCH2COOH.

Show answer
  1. pKa: formic 3.75; benzoic 4.20; chloroacetic 2.86; acetic 4.76. Largest pKa = weakest: CH3COOH.
Practice 8 L1 · Acidity

Arrange benzoic acid (H), p-chloro-, p-nitro- and p-methoxybenzoic acid in decreasing acid strength.

Show answer
  1. p-NO2 (3.44) > p-Cl (3.98) > H (4.20) > p-OCH3 (4.47).
  2. The nitro group is –M and –I: it stabilises the carboxylate most; Cl is –I > +M; OCH3 is a +M donor at para position and destabilises the anion.
  3. p-nitro > p-chloro > benzoic > p-methoxy.
Practice 9 L1 · pH

Calculate the pH of 0.10 M acetic acid (pKa = 4.76).

Show answer
  1. For a weak acid pH = ½(pKa − log c) = ½(4.76 + 1) = 2.88. (Check: α = [H+]/c = 1.3 × 10−3/0.1 = 1.3 %, so the approximation is valid.)
Practice 10 L1 · Tests

Which of these will NOT liberate CO2 from aqueous NaHCO3? (A) C6H5COOH (B) CH3COOH (C) C6H5OH (D) 2,4,6-trinitrophenol

Show answer
  1. NaHCO3 reacts with acids stronger than carbonic acid (pKa ≈ 6.35 for the CO2/H2O system). Carboxylic acids (4–5) and picric acid (0.4) do. Phenol (pKa 10) does not. (C).
Practice 11 L1 · Dibasic acids

Which has the lowest pKa1? Oxalic, malonic, succinic, adipic acid.

Show answer
  1. The two COOH groups withdraw electrons from each other; the effect falls off with distance. Oxalic acid has them adjacent: pKa1 = 1.25 (malonic 2.85; succinic 4.21; adipic 4.43). Oxalic acid.
Practice 12 L1 · Dibasic acids

Between maleic and fumaric acid, which has the larger Ka1, and why?

Show answer
  1. Maleic acid (pKa1 1.90 against 3.03). In the maleate monoanion the carboxylate oxygen is hydrogen-bonded to the neighbouring COOH of the cis isomer, which stabilises it; fumaric acid (trans) cannot form that internal H-bond.
Practice 13 L1 · Acidity

Among o-, m- and p-hydroxybenzoic acid, which is the strongest acid, and why?

Show answer
  1. Ortho (salicylic acid, pKa 2.97). The salicylate ion is stabilised by an intramolecular O–H···O− hydrogen bond (six-membered ring). Meta 4.08; para 4.58 (+M of OH weakens it).
Practice 14 L1 · Acidity

Which is a stronger acid: CCl3COOH or CF3COOH?

Show answer
  1. Fluorine is more electronegative, and its –I effect is larger: CF3COOH (pKa ≈ 0.5) is stronger than CCl3COOH (pKa ≈ 0.7). CF3COOH.
Practice 15 L1 · Ortho effect

Which is stronger: benzoic acid or o-toluic acid?

Show answer
  1. The ortho CH3 twists the COOH out of the ring plane (steric inhibition of resonance), so the acid is stronger: o-toluic acid pKa 3.91 against 4.20 for benzoic. o-Toluic acid.
Practice 16 L1 · Properties

Arrange in increasing boiling point: CH3COOCH3, CH3CH2CH2OH, CH3COOH, CH3CONH2.

Show answer

  2. CH3COOCH3 < CH3CH2CH2OH < CH3COOH < CH3CONH2.
Practice 17 L1 · Properties

Acetic acid dissolved in benzene shows a molar mass of about 120 g mol−1 from freezing-point depression. Why?

Show answer
  1. In benzene (non-polar, cannot H-bond to the acid) acetic acid exists as hydrogen-bonded dimers, so the number of particles is halved and the apparent molar mass doubles (60 × 2 = 120). In water the dimer is broken by solvent H-bonding.

Practice 37 L1 · Reactivity

Why is an amide the least reactive carboxylic acid derivative?

Show answer
  1. The nitrogen lone pair is strongly delocalised into the C=O (amide resonance), which lowers the electrophilicity of the carbonyl carbon and gives the C–N bond double-bond character; the leaving group would be NH2−, a very strong base and poor leaving group.

Practice 43 L1 · Esters

Alkaline hydrolysis of an ester goes to completion, but acid hydrolysis does not. Why?

Show answer

Practice 114 L1 · Conceptual

Why does 2,4,6-trinitrobenzoic acid decarboxylate far more easily than benzoic acid?

Show answer
  1. Loss of CO2 leaves an aryl anion at the ring carbon; the three nitro groups (two ortho) stabilise this carbanion by –I (and –M for the ortho/para nitro groups), and steric crowding of the ortho NO2 groups twists COOH out of the plane and relieves strain on decarboxylation.
Practice 115 L1 · Conceptual

What is the function of red phosphorus in the HVZ reaction?

Show answer
  1. Red P + Br2 generates PBr3 in situ, which converts a little of the acid to the acyl bromide. The acyl bromide enolises easily and is brominated at the α-carbon; exchange with more RCOOH propagates the cycle.

Why does benzoic acid not undergo the HVZ reaction?

State two reasons why the Claisen condensation needs at least one full equivalent of base.

Show answer
  1. (1) The final deprotonation of the β-keto ester product (pKa 10.7) consumes one equivalent of base. (2) That deprotonation is what drives the otherwise unfavourable equilibrium to the product.
Practice 118 L1 · Conceptual

Explain why the ethyl ester of isobutyric acid does not give a good Claisen product with NaOEt.

Show answer
  1. Ethyl isobutyrate has only one α-H. The Claisen product has no acidic hydrogen between the carbonyls, so the final driving deprotonation cannot occur and the equilibrium lies on the side of the starting ester.
