2. Structural isomerism: the six patterns

2.1 Chain (skeletal) isomerism

Same functional group and same type of position, but a different carbon skeleton: butane / 2-methylpropane; pentane / 2-methylbutane / 2,2-dimethylpropane.

butane 2-methylpropane pentane 2-methylbutane neopentane
Chain isomers of C4H10 and C5H12

The numbers of alkane isomers (acyclic, connectivity only) grow slowly at first: C4: 2, C5: 3, C6: 5, C7: 9. (These were generated by exhaustive computer enumeration, not recalled.) The sequence 1, 1, 1, 2, 3, 5, 9, 18, 35 for C1 to C9 is worth knowing (the C8 and C9 terms, 18 and 35, are standard tabulated values; they were not enumerated here).

2.2 Position isomerism

Same skeleton, same functional group, different location of the functional group or substituent: propan-1-ol / propan-2-ol; but-1-ene / but-2-ene; the three xylenes (o-, m-, p-); 1-chloropropane / 2-chloropropane.

propan-1-ol propan-2-ol but-1-ene but-2-ene 1-chloropropane 2-chloropropane

o-xylene m-xylene p-xylene ethylbenzene
The four C8H10 aromatic isomers: three position isomers plus a chain-type isomer

Trap In exams, "how many isomers of C3H6O" is usually answered with a filter: if the question says "carbonyl compounds" the answer is 2 (propanal, propanone); "stable, open-chain" gives 4 (propanal, propanone, allyl alcohol, methyl vinyl ether); "all, including enols and rings" gives 9. Read the filter in the question before you count.

2.4 Metamerism

Different alkyl groups on either side of the same polyvalent functional group (–O–, –CO–, –NH–, –COO–, –S–): diethyl ether / methyl propyl ether / isopropyl methyl ether (all C4H10O); pentan-2-one / pentan-3-one / 3-methylbutan-2-one; N-methylpropylamine / diethylamine.

CH₃OCH₂CH₂CH₃ C₂H₅OC₂H₅ CH₃OCH(CH₃)₂ pentan-2-one pentan-3-one
Metamers: the ethers (C4H10O) and two ketones (C5H10O)

Metamers are a special kind of position/chain isomers around a central atom; JEE questions use the word, so know it.

2.5 Ring–chain isomerism

An open-chain compound and a ring compound of the same formula: propene / cyclopropane; but-1-ene / cyclobutane; propanal / oxetane (C3H6O); and, important for carbohydrates, open-chain glucose / its cyclic hemiacetal.

propene cyclopropane but-1-ene cyclobutane methylcyclopropane
Ring–chain isomers of C3H6 and C4H8

2.6 Tautomerism

Tautomers are structural isomers in rapid equilibrium by migration of a proton (and a shift of a double bond). They are different compounds in principle but interconvert so fast that they usually cannot be isolated separately. Important types:

⇌⟶
Keto–enol tautomerism of acetone: the keto form is >99.9 % at equilibrium
⇌⟶
Pentane-2,4-dione: the enol is strongly stabilised (intramolecular H-bond + conjugation), about 80 % enol in non-polar solvents
⇌⟶
Phenol / cyclohexa-2,4-dienone: aromaticity makes the phenol side overwhelmingly favoured
⇌⟶
Imine–enamine (the enamine is the minor tautomer)
⇌⟶
Nitro–aci-nitro (nitronic acid) tautomerism of nitromethane
⇌⟶
Lactam–lactim: 2-pyridone is the favoured form
Key idea Condition for keto–enol tautomerism: the carbonyl compound must have at least one α-hydrogen. Benzaldehyde, formaldehyde, trimethylacetaldehyde (pivalaldehyde) and benzophenone have no α-H, so they exist only in the keto form. A hydrogen on a bridgehead α-carbon (as in bicyclic ketones such as bicyclo[2.2.1]heptan-2-one on the bridgehead side) cannot enolise towards the bridgehead because an alkene there would violate Bredt's rule.
Trap Tautomers differ in the position of an atom (H), resonance structures differ only in the position of electrons. Keto and enol are different compounds (different atom connectivity); the two Kekulé structures of benzene are not.
Example 1 Main

Identify the relationship

Classify each pair as chain, position, functional, metamers, ring–chain or tautomers (use "metamers" for isomers that differ in the alkyl groups on the two sides of the same functional group): (a) CH3CH2CH2OH and CH3OCH2CH3; (b) CH3COCH2CH2CH3 and CH3CH2COCH2CH3; (c) CH3CH2CH2NO2 and CH3CH2CH2ONO; (d) CH3CH=CH2 and cyclopropane; (e) CH3CH2CHO and CH3CH=CHOH.

Show solution
  1. (a) Alcohol versus ether: functional group isomers (both C3H8O).
  2. (b) Pentan-2-one versus pentan-3-one: the carbonyl group moves, and the two alkyl groups on either side change (methyl + propyl versus ethyl + ethyl): these are metamers (also describable as position isomers; the textbook word for different alkyls about CO is metamerism).
  3. (c) Nitroalkane versus alkyl nitrite: functional group isomers.
  4. (d) Ring–chain isomers (C3H6).
  5. (e) Propanal and prop-1-en-1-ol differ by the position of one H and one double bond, and interconvert: tautomers (keto–enol).
