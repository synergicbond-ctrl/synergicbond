Level 2 problems are at JEE Advanced depth: multi-step reasoning, several correct options, stereochemical bookkeeping and numerical work. Write the mechanism before looking at the answer.

Practice 115 Adv MCQ

Several correct: SN2

Which statements about the SN2 reaction are correct? (A) The rate is doubled when the concentrations of both substrate and nucleophile are doubled. (B) The reaction at a stereogenic carbon proceeds with inversion of spatial arrangement. (C) A rearranged carbon skeleton is often obtained. (D) The rate is higher in DMF than in methanol for an anionic nucleophile. (E) The reaction has a negative entropy of activation.

Show answer
  1. (A) False: the rate is quadrupled (rate = k[RX][Nu], both doubled). (B) True. (C) False: no carbocation, so no rearrangement. (D) True: DMF does not solvate the anion by H-bonding. (E) True: two species form one ordered transition state.
  2. B, D, E.
Practice 116 Adv MCQ

Several correct: SN1

Which statements about SN1 are correct? (A) First-order kinetics. (B) Optically active tertiary halide gives completely racemic product in every case. (C) The reaction rate increases with the ionising power of the solvent. (D) The nucleophile does not appear in the rate expression. (E) Rearranged products may be formed.

Show answer
  1. (A) True. (B) False: ion-pair effects give a slight excess of inversion; "completely racemic" is not guaranteed. (C) True. (D) True. (E) True (carbocation).
  2. A, C, D, E.
Practice 117 Adv

Product count

(R)-3-Bromo-3-methylhexane is heated in methanol. List all products from SN1 and E1 and state the total number of distinct compounds (counting stereoisomers separately).

Show answer
  1. The planar cation CH3CH2–C+(CH3)–CH2CH2CH3 is captured by methanol: 3-methoxy-3-methylhexane (chiral: R and S, racemic): 2.
  2. E1 (loss of H+): from the methyl group, 2-ethylpent-1-ene (no stereoisomers): 1; from the ethyl CH2, 3-methylhex-2-ene (E and Z): 2; from the propyl CH2, 3-methylhex-3-ene (E and Z): 2.
  3. Total = 2 + 1 + 2 + 2 = 7 compounds.
Practice 118 Adv

Optical purity

(S)-1-Phenylethyl chloride is hydrolysed in aqueous acetone to 1-phenylethanol. The product has an optical purity of 20 % with the (R) configuration. What percentages of the molecules are formed with inversion and with retention?

Show answer
  1. Let I % be inverted and (100 − I) % retained. Optical purity (ee) = I − (100 − I) = 2I − 100 = 20 → I = 60.
  2. 60 % inversion (R) and 40 % retention (S). This is typical of SN1 through ion pairs (a stabilised benzylic cation so the net inversion is small).
Practice 119 Adv

Optical purity

Reaction of (R)-2-bromooctane gives octan-2-ol as 85 % (S) and 15 % (R). Calculate the enantiomeric excess and the specific rotation of the product mixture, given that pure (S)-octan-2-ol has [α]D = +9.9°. How much of the (S) alcohol came by a clean SN2 path, if the remaining material is racemic?

Show answer
  1. ee = 85 − 15 = 70 %. [α]observed = 0.70 × 9.9° = +6.9°.
  2. Let x be the fraction formed by pure inversion (all S) and (1 − x) the fraction racemic (half S, half R): S = x + (1 − x)/2 = 0.85 → x = 0.70. So 70 % clean SN2 inversion, 30 % racemic (SN1-like), which also equals the ee.
Practice 120 Adv

Arrhenius

A bimolecular substitution has an activation energy of 80 kJ mol−1. By what factor does the rate constant increase on raising the temperature from 300 K to 310 K? (R = 8.314 J K−1 mol−1.)

Show answer
  1. ln(k2/k1) = (Ea/R)(1/T1 − 1/T2) = (80000/8.314)(1/300 − 1/310) = 9622 × 1.075 × 10−4 = 1.035.
  2. k2/k1 = e1.035 = 2.8.
Practice 121 Adv

Partial rate constants

tert-Butyl bromide in 80 % ethanol at 25 °C reacts with an overall first-order rate constant k = 3.0 × 10−4 s−1, giving 83 % substitution products and 17 % isobutene. Calculate kS_{N1} and kE1, and the half-life of the bromide.

Show answer
  1. kS_{N1} = 0.83 × 3.0 × 10−4 = 2.5 × 10−4 s−1; kE1 = 0.17 × 3.0 × 10−4 = 5.1 × 10−5 s−1.
  2. t½ = 0.693/k = 0.693/(3.0 × 10−4) = 2.3 × 103 s (≈ 38.5 min).
Practice 122 Adv

Energy profile

Which energy diagram feature is correct for the hydrolysis of tert-butyl bromide? (A) One maximum, no intermediate. (B) Two maxima and one intermediate, the first barrier being higher. (C) Two maxima and the second barrier higher. (D) Three maxima.

Show answer
  1. Ionisation (step 1) is rate-determining, so it has the higher barrier; the carbocation is an intermediate in the valley; capture by water has a smaller barrier. (Strictly a third small barrier exists for deprotonation, but the profile is classified as two main steps.) (B).
Practice 123 Adv

Bridgehead

Arrange in order of decreasing SN1 solvolysis rate (80 % ethanol, 25 °C): (a) tert-butyl bromide (b) 1-bromoadamantane (c) 1-bromobicyclo[2.2.2]octane (d) 1-bromobicyclo[2.2.1]heptane (1-bromonorbornane).

t-BuBr 1-Bromoadamantane 1-Bromobicyclo[2.2.2]octane 1-Bromonorbornane
Show answer
  1. The cation must flatten as much as possible. tert-Butyl: fully planar. 1-Adamantyl is rigid but the three-ring strain in the cation is only moderate (about 10−3× t-Bu). The bicyclo[2.2.2]octyl cation (about 10−6×) and the norbornyl bridgehead (about 10−13×, extremely strained) are far worse.
  2. (a) > (b) > (c) > (d) (rates approximate). Bridgehead halides are inert in SN2 as well (no back-side approach).

List all the isomeric C3H5Cl compounds (including E/Z), and say which gives an immediate precipitate with alcoholic AgNO3 at room temperature.

Practice 125 Adv

Solvent effect on rate

For the reaction of CH3I with N3−, the rate constant in DMF is 106 times that in methanol. If the activation free energy in methanol is 90 kJ mol−1, estimate that in DMF at 298 K.

Show answer
  1. k ∝ e−ΔG^{‡/RT}. A factor 106 corresponds to ΔΔG‡ = RT ln 106 = 8.314 × 298 × 13.82 = 34.2 kJ mol−1.
  2. ΔG‡(DMF) ≈ 90 − 34 = 56 kJ mol−1 (assuming the same pre-exponential factor).
Practice 126 Adv

Neighbouring group

Compare the hydrolysis rates in water of (A) ClCH2CH2SC2H5 (B) ClCH2CH2OC2H5 (C) ClCH2CH2CH2CH3. Explain.

Show answer
  1. (A) is far faster: sulfur, a good nucleophile with a lone pair β to the leaving group, displaces chloride intramolecularly to form a cyclic episulfonium (thiiranium) ion (anchimeric assistance). Oxygen in (B) is a much weaker nucleophile and assists little; (C) has no participating group.
  2. A ≫ B > C (A is about 103–104 times faster than a normal primary chloride).
Practice 127 Adv

NGP and stereochemistry

Explain why the acetolysis of trans-2-acetoxycyclohexyl tosylate is hundreds of times faster than that of the cis isomer, and gives the trans-diacetate with retention.

Show answer
  1. In the trans isomer the acetoxy group can be diaxial with OTs: the carbonyl oxygen attacks C–OTs from the back to give a bridged acetoxonium (1,3-dioxolan-2-ylium) ion; acetate then opens the ring at the other carbon, by a second inversion: net retention (trans-diacetate). The cis isomer cannot place the carbonyl oxygen anti to the tosylate (it would have to attack from the same face), so there is no assistance and it reacts slowly with different stereochemistry.
Practice 128 Adv

Double inversion

Starting from (S)-2-bromopropanoate, which sequence gives (R)-lactate: (A) NaOH (high conc.) (B) Ag2O, H2O (C) dilute NaOH then acid? State the overall stereochemistry of each.

Show answer
  1. Priorities Br > COO− > CH3 > H become OH > COO− > CH3 > H (same order). (A) Direct SN2: inversion → (R)-lactate. (B) Ag+ assists ionisation and the carboxylate neighbouring group attacks (inversion), water opens the α-lactone (second inversion): retention → (S)-lactate. (C) Low [OH−] favours NGP: retention → (S)-lactate. Only (A) gives (R)-lactate.

Practice 129 Adv

Competing mechanisms

Both (R)-2-bromooctane + NaOH (aq. acetone) and (R)-2-bromooctane in aqueous acetone with no base give 2-octanol. Describe the stereochemistry in each and the effect of adding NaOH on the rate.

Show answer
  1. With NaOH: SN2, rate = k[RBr][OH−]; the product is (S)-octan-2-ol (inversion), and the rate rises in proportion to [OH−].
  2. Without base (water/acetone, 2° bromide): SN1 with solvent, rate independent of added nucleophile; product mostly racemic with a slight excess of the inverted (S) alcohol. At high [OH−] the rate law becomes mixed: rate = k1[RBr] + k2[RBr][OH−].
Practice 130 Adv

Aziridinium

2-(Diethylamino)ethyl chloride, Et2NCH2CH2Cl, is hydrolysed in water about 104 times faster than 1-chlorobutane, and its rate is independent of [HO−]. Explain.

Show answer
  1. The amine nitrogen (lone pair) is β to the leaving group; it attacks the carbon bearing Cl intramolecularly, expelling chloride to form a strained, cationic aziridinium ion. Because this ring closure is rate-determining and unimolecular, the overall hydrolysis is first-order and independent of hydroxide; water (or hydroxide) then opens the aziridinium ring rapidly. This is the mode of action of nitrogen mustards (alkylating anticancer agents).

Anchimeric assistance by nitrogen: aziridinium ion intermediate.
Practice 131 Adv

Ambident

A student treats ethyl bromide with each of NaCN in DMSO and AgCN in ether. The first product on acid hydrolysis gives propanoic acid and NH4+; the second gives ethylamine and formic acid. Identify the products and reasoning.

Show answer
  1. NaCN: ionic cyanide attacks through carbon: CH3CH2C≡N; hydrolysis (H3O+, heat) → CH3CH2COOH + NH4+.
  2. AgCN: covalent Ag–C bond, so the nitrogen lone pair attacks: CH3CH2–N≡C (ethyl isocyanide); dilute acid hydrolysis → CH3CH2NH2 + HCOOH (isocyanides are hydrolysed by acid but stable to base).
Practice 132 Adv

Order of reactivity

Arrange the following in increasing order of SN2 reaction rate with NaN3/DMF: (a) 1-bromobutane (b) bromocyclohexane (c) 1-bromo-2,2-dimethylpropane (d) benzyl bromide (e) 2-bromopropane.

Show answer
  1. Benzyl bromide is fastest (allylic/benzylic acceleration), then 1-bromobutane (primary), then the secondary bromides (2-bromopropane, then bromocyclohexane, slowed further by ring strain/hindrance), and neopentyl bromide slowest.
  2. (c) < (b) < (e) < (a) < (d).
Practice 133 Adv

Back-side attack in rings

Which compound will undergo SN2 with I− faster: cis- or trans-4-tert-butylcyclohexyl tosylate? Explain using the conformations.

Show answer
  1. t-Bu is equatorial. In the cis isomer the OTs is axial: the nucleophile attacks from the equatorial side (the back side of an axial leaving group is open): SN2 faster. In the trans isomer OTs is equatorial; the back-side attack must come through the axial direction, hindered by the 1,3-diaxial hydrogens: slower. Also an equatorial leaving group makes the axial approach crowded.
  2. cis is faster in SN2 (the axial leaving group is displaced from the equatorial direction); with the product being the trans-iodide (axial-to-equatorial inversion).
Practice 134 Adv

Tertiary alkyl halide, nucleophile varied

For (CH3)3CBr, rate of disappearance in aqueous ethanol is 5.0 × 10−4 s−1 regardless of added NaN3 (0 to 0.1 M), but the product contains tert-butyl azide. Explain.

Show answer
  1. Rate-determining ionisation gives (CH3)3C+; azide, being a good nucleophile, merely competes with solvent for the cation in the fast second step: so the product distribution changes (more azide) but not the rate. Hence SN1.
Practice 135 Adv

Selectivity of a carbocation

A carbocation is generated in 80 % aqueous ethanol containing N3−. Why does the azide/solvent product ratio not equal [N3−]/[solvent]?

Show answer
  1. Azide is a much better nucleophile than water or ethanol by a factor of ≈ 103; the ratio of products reflects relative nucleophilicity times concentration. (For very unstable cations, selectivity vanishes, since capture is diffusion controlled.)
Practice 136 Adv

Leaving group abilities

Arrange as leaving groups in SN1 solvolysis of a tertiary R–X: X = F, Cl, Br, I, OTs, OH2+ (from ROH + H+), OH.

Show answer
  1. A good leaving group is a weak base (conjugate base of a strong acid): TsO− (pKa of TsOH ≈ −3), I− (−10), Br− (−9), H2O (from H3O+, −1.7), Cl− (−7), F− (3.2), HO− (15.7).
  2. Rates: OTs ≳ I > Br > Cl ≈ OH2+ (acid-promoted) ≫ F ≫ OH. (The position of OH2+ is conditional on strong acid being present; HO− itself never leaves.)

Alkyl halide to alcohol with retention

Design a route from (S)-2-chlorobutane to (S)-butan-2-ol (overall retention) in which only the SN2 steps change configuration.

Show answer
  1. Step 1: (S)-2-chlorobutane + CH3COO−K+ (DMF): SN2 → (R)-2-butyl acetate (inversion #1).

Practice 139 Adv

Intramolecular competition

HO–(CH2)n–Br (n = 2 to 6) is treated with dilute NaOH. Give the ring size formed for each n and say for which n the cyclic ether forms fastest.

Show answer
  1. Ring sizes (including O): n = 2 → 3 (oxirane), n = 3 → 4 (oxetane), n = 4 → 5 (oxolane, THF), n = 5 → 6 (oxane, THP), n = 6 → 7 (oxepane).
  2. Fastest is n = 4 (five-membered ring), then n = 2 and n = 5; n = 3 (ring strain) and n = 6 (unfavourable entropy for 7-membered ring formation) are slow, so intermolecular products (ethers, diols) compete.
