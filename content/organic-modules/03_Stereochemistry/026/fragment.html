23. Stereochemistry of elimination

23.1 E2: anti-periplanar elimination

E2 needs the β-hydrogen and the leaving group to be anti-periplanar (dihedral angle 180°, the best orbital overlap) in the transition state. (Syn-periplanar elimination, 0°, occurs only when anti is impossible, as in some rigid rings.) Consequences: the reaction is stereospecific: different diastereomers of the substrate give different alkene isomers. The program below places each substrate in a conformation with H and Br anti-periplanar, removes both, and records the geometry of the new double bond: the product's E/Z label was computed.

substrate (R,S) alkene (E)
meso-2,3-dibromobutane with base: the new C=C has the two CH₃ cis, giving the (E)-2-bromobut-2-ene
substrate (R,R) alkene (Z)
(R,R)-2,3-dibromobutane with base: the CH₃ groups end up trans, giving the (Z)-2-bromobut-2-ene
Key idea Anti-elimination from a meso vicinal dibromide and from the racemic dibromide gives different bromoalkene diastereomers. Likewise anti-debromination (NaI, acetone; or Zn) of meso-2,3-dibromobutane gives trans-but-2-ene and of the (±) dibromide gives cis-but-2-ene. This is the classic test of anti stereochemistry.
Example 66 Adv

Anti elimination in the Newman projection

Meso-2,3-dibromobutane has C-2 (front) and C-3 (rear). Show with a Newman projection that E2 loss of H from C-2 and Br from C-3 gives the (E)-bromoalkene.

meso-2,3-dibromobutane with H (front, 8 o'clock) anti to Br (rear, 2 o'clock)
Show solution
  1. The Newman projection has front C-2: CH3 (12 o'clock), Br (4 o'clock), H (8 o'clock); rear C-3: CH3 (10 o'clock), Br (2 o'clock), H (6 o'clock). It is the meso isomer (identical groups related by the Fischer projection with both Br on the same side), and the front H (8 o'clock) is anti to the rear Br (2 o'clock).
  2. Remove H and Br. Groups on the same side of the 2–8 o'clock axis become cis: the two CH3 groups (12 and 10 o'clock) are on the same side; the front Br (4 o'clock) and the rear H (6 o'clock) are on the other side.
  3. So the product has the two methyl groups cis. At C-2 the priorities are Br > CH3; at C-3, CH3 > H. Br is trans to the C-3 methyl: (E)-2-bromobut-2-ene, as the computed result also shows.

23.2 Anti-periplanar elimination in cyclohexanes: the diaxial requirement

In a chair, an anti-periplanar H/X pair is trans-diaxial. A leaving group that is equatorial cannot undergo E2 until the ring flips to put it axial; if the flip puts the group in an axial position with no axial anti H on one side, elimination goes to the other side. The classic comparison is menthyl chloride (all substituents equatorial) and neomenthyl chloride (Cl axial):

menthyl chloride: Cl equatorial in the stable chair
menthyl chloride after ring flip: all axial
neomenthyl chloride: Cl already axial
  • Neomenthyl chloride has Cl axial in its stable chair, and C-2 has an axial H anti to it (the isopropyl at C-2 is equatorial). E2 is fast and gives mainly the more substituted alkene (3-menthene, Zaitsev).
  • Menthyl chloride has Cl equatorial. It must flip to an all-axial chair, which is strongly disfavoured. In that chair the group axial at C-2 is the isopropyl, so there is no axial H on C-2 anti to Cl; the only anti H is on C-6, giving the less substituted alkene (2-menthene). E2 is slow (about 200 times slower).
Example 67 Main

Cyclohexane E2

Why does trans-1-bromo-2-methylcyclohexane eliminate to give 3-methylcyclohexene (the less substituted alkene) as the main product, whereas cis-1-bromo-2-methylcyclohexane gives 1-methylcyclohexene (Zaitsev)?

trans: Br and CH₃ both axial (the H on C-2 is equatorial, not anti to Br)
cis: Br axial, CH₃ equatorial (the H on C-2 is axial and anti)
Show solution
  1. E2 needs an axial Br and an anti axial H. In the trans isomer the reactive chair puts Br axial, which forces the adjacent CH3 axial as well (trans-diaxial); the H on C-2 is then equatorial, not anti to Br. The only anti H is on C-6, so the less substituted alkene (3-methylcyclohexene) forms.
  2. In the cis isomer, with Br axial the CH3 is equatorial and the H on C-2 is axial and anti to Br: elimination toward C-2 gives the more substituted 1-methylcyclohexene.

23.3 E1, Zaitsev and Hofmann

E1 goes through the carbocation: the stereochemistry of the substrate is lost; the more stable (more substituted, trans) alkene predominates (Zaitsev). E2 with a small base also follows Zaitsev; a bulky base (t-BuOK) or a poor leaving group like −N+R3 (Hofmann elimination) gives the less substituted alkene. In E2 giving a disubstituted alkene from a CH2 group, both E and Z can form; the E isomer is normally the major product because it is less crowded in the transition state.
