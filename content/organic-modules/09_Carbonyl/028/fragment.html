24. The haloform reaction

24.1 Mechanism Methyl ketones (CH3CO–R, also ethanal) react with excess halogen in base (NaOX, X = Cl, Br, I) by repeated enolate halogenation to give R–CO–CX3. Hydroxide adds to the carbonyl, the CX3− (a good leaving group because three halogens stabilise the carbanion) is expelled, and the carboxylic acid immediately transfers a proton to CX3− to give the haloform CHX3 and the carboxylate.

  • Step 1: CH3COR + OH− ⇌ enolate; enolate + I2 → ICH2COR (fast). Repeat to give CI3COR.
  • Step 2: OH− adds to C=O of CI3COR (strongly electrophilic).
  • Step 3: tetrahedral intermediate expels CI3−; RCOOH + CI3− → RCOO− + CHI3.

Iodoform test (+ yellow ppt; − no ppt)
Trap Carboxylic acids and their derivatives containing CH3CO– (CH3COOH, CH3COOEt, CH3CONH2) give no iodoform test: their α-H are much less acidic and the carboxylic C=O is not electrophilic enough. Acetic acid is a standard negative. Acetyl chloride hydrolyses to acetic acid and also fails.
Trap A methyl group on a ring-bearing quaternary or non-carbonyl carbon does not count: CH3CH(OH)– must have the CH3 on the carbinol carbon. 1-Phenylethanol is positive; 2-phenylethanol (PhCH2CH2OH) is negative. Propan-2-ol is positive; butan-1-ol negative.
24.3 Other haloforms
With NaOCl or NaOBr (chloroform, bromoform) the reaction also works; I2/NaOH is used in the laboratory because CHI3 is a yellow solid (m.p. 119 °C) with an antiseptic smell and thus easy to see. CHCl3 and CHBr3 are colourless liquids and are not used as a visual test.
Example 24 Main

Iodoform: yes or no, and the carboxylic acid formed

Which of these give iodoform with I2/NaOH; for those that do give the organic product: (a) CH3CH2CH(OH)CH3 (b) PhCOCH3 (c) CH3CH2CH2OH (d) CH3CHO (e) HCHO (f) CH3COCH2COOC2H5 (g) CH3CH2COCH2CH3

Show solution
  1. (a) Butan-2-ol: CH3CH(OH)– unit; oxidised to butanone then haloform: yes, propanoate (CH3CH2COO−).
  2. (b) Yes, benzoate. (c) Propan-1-ol: no CH3CH(OH). No.
  3. (d) Yes (the only aldehyde), methanoate HCOO− (formate). (e) No.
  4. (f) Ethyl acetoacetate has CH3CO–: yes; the acid formed is the malonic half-ester, i.e. −OOC–CH2–COOC2H5.
  5. (g) Pentan-3-one has no CH3CO– group: No.
Example 25 Adv

Mechanism check

Why does the haloform reaction not stop after the first iodination, while acid-catalysed iodination does?

Show solution
  1. In base the reactive species is the enolate. Replacing H by I makes the remaining α-H on the same carbon more acidic (−I of iodine stabilises the enolate), so the second and third iodinations are faster than the first. In acid the reactive species is the enol; the first halogen reduces the basicity of the carbonyl oxygen, so the next protonation (needed to form the enol) is slower; reaction stops at monosubstitution.
Example 26 Adv

Unsymmetrical ketone

Predict the major product of (a) CH3COCH2CH3 + Br2/H+; (b) CH3COCH2CH3 + excess Br2/NaOH; (c) 2-methylcyclohexanone + Br2/CH3COOH.

Show solution
  1. (a) Acid: more substituted enol (CH3C(OH)=CHCH3): 3-bromobutan-2-one.
  2. (b) Base, excess: haloform cleavage: propanoate + CHBr3 (bromoform).
  3. (c) Acid: bromine goes to the more substituted α-carbon: 2-bromo-2-methylcyclohexanone.
