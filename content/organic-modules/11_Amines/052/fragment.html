52. Mechanism: Eschweiler–Clarke methylation (why it stops at the tertiary amine)

  • Step 1 – iminium formation. A 1° amine adds to formaldehyde (nN → π*C=O) to give the carbinolamine; protonation and loss of water gives the iminium ion R–N+H=CH2 (formaldehyde is a very reactive carbonyl, so this is fast).
  • Step 2 – hydride transfer. Formic acid (as HCOO−) is a hydride donor: its C–H bond donates H− (σ C–H → π*C=N+, the iminium LUMO) to the CH2 carbon of the iminium ion, producing the N-methylamine and CO2. Why irreversible: CO2 escapes.
  • Step 3 – repeat. The 2° amine R–NHCH3 adds to a second HCHO, forms the iminium R–N+(CH3)=CH2, and is reduced to R–N(CH3)2.
  • Step 4 – stop. A 3° amine has no N–H and cannot form an iminium ion with HCHO, so there is no pathway to the quaternary salt.
