12. Selectivity: which reagent for which job

A good choice of reagent is made by asking three questions: what must be oxidised, what must survive, and whether the product must stop at an intermediate oxidation level. The summary below is limited to standard, textbook selectivities.

  • Primary alcohol → aldehyde: PCC, PDC, Collins, Swern, Dess–Martin, TEMPO/NaOCl (buffered). Water-free conditions are the common factor for the chromium reagents.
  • Primary alcohol → carboxylic acid: Jones reagent, hot KMnO4, acidified K2Cr2O7 (excess, reflux).
  • Secondary alcohol → ketone: any of the above, as well as Oppenauer (non-acidic, leaves C=C intact).
  • Allylic or benzylic alcohol → carbonyl without touching other alcohols: activated MnO2.
  • Aldehyde → acid under mild conditions: Tollens reagent (silver mirror), Fehling (aliphatic only), Ag2O; also KMnO4, CrO3.
  • Ketone → ester or lactone: peroxy acid (Baeyer–Villiger). Methyl ketone → acid with one carbon fewer: NaOX (haloform).
  • Alkene → epoxide: mCPBA. Alkene → syn diol: OsO4/NMO or cold dilute alkaline KMnO4. Alkene → anti diol: mCPBA then H3O+.
  • Alkene → two carbonyl compounds: O3, then Me2S (or Zn), or OsO4/NaIO4. Alkene → acids/ketones: O3, then H2O2, or hot KMnO4.
  • Terminal alkene → methyl ketone: Wacker (PdCl2/CuCl2/O2). Alkene → anti-Markovnikov alcohol: BH3, then H2O2/NaOH.
  • Allylic C–H → C–OH: SeO2. α-CH2 of a ketone → C=O: SeO2 (Riley).
  • Alkyl side chain → ArCOOH: hot KMnO4 (needs a benzylic H). Methylarene → ArCHO: CrO2Cl2 (Étard).
  • 1,2-Diol → two carbonyls: HIO4/NaIO4 in water, Pb(OAc)4 in organic solvents.
  • Sulfide → sulfoxide → sulfone: 1 then 2 equiv of H2O2/peracid; tertiary amine → N-oxide: H2O2 or peracid.
Key idea. When two functional groups compete, the selective reagents are: MnO2 (allylic OH), mCPBA (most electron-rich C=C, and S before C=C), PCC (alcohol, with C=C intact), HIO4 (vicinal diols and α-hydroxy carbonyl compounds).
Trap. Strong, aqueous, hot reagents (KMnO4, Jones, HNO3) are almost never selective. If a question asks for a selective oxidation, the answer is one of the mild, specific reagents in the list above.
