12. Hydride opening of epoxides

Epoxides are strained ethers, and LiAlH4 opens them by an SN2 reaction. Hydride attacks the less hindered carbon from the back, with inversion at that carbon, and the oxygen remains on the carbon that was not attacked. The product is therefore the alcohol in which OH is on the more substituted carbon (Markovnikov-type alcohol): propylene oxide gives propan-2-ol, and 2,2-dimethyloxirane gives tert-butyl alcohol. NaBH4 is much slower and is normally not used for this purpose.

Attack at CH2 (less hindered), not at the benzylic carbon.

Reactant:

Reagent: LiAlH4, ether; then H2O

Product:

  1. The Al–H bond delivers hydride to the less substituted epoxide carbon (backside attack, SN2).
  2. The C–O bond to that carbon breaks and the oxygen becomes an alkoxide on the other carbon.
  3. Aqueous work-up protonates the alkoxide to give the alcohol.
Key idea. The C–O bond to the carbon that is not attacked is never broken, so a stereocentre there keeps its three-dimensional arrangement. Its R/S label may nevertheless change because the CIP priorities of its substituents change (the epoxide CH2O group becomes CH3).
