46. Mechanism: the Henry reaction and nitro–aci tautomerism

  • Step 1 – deprotonation (carbon acid). OH− removes the α-H of CH3NO2. The resulting carbanion mixes with π(N–O): the nitronate CH2=N+(O−)2 places the charge on oxygen (pKa 10.2). MO lens: the lone pair on C overlaps with the low-lying π*(N=O) – a strongly stabilising conjugation, the reason the α-H is enormously more acidic than a simple alkane C–H (pKa ≈ 50).
  • Step 2 – C-attack on the aldehyde. The nitronate carbon (HOMO) attacks the carbonyl carbon (LUMO π*C=O) along the Bürgi–Dunitz angle: alkoxide intermediate.
  • Step 3 – protonation. The alkoxide takes H+ from water: β-nitro alcohol. Acid or heat eliminates water (the remaining α-H is acidic, E1cB) giving the nitroalkene.

Nef reaction. The nitronate is protonated on O (aci-nitro form), then on the other O to give an iminium-like cation that is attacked by water at carbon; loss of HNO (→ N2O + H2O) leaves the carbonyl compound.
