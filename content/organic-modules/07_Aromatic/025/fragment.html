Practice 86 L2 · multiple correct

Select the aromatic species: (A) cyclopropenyl cation (B) cycloheptatrienyl cation (C) cyclooctatetraene dianion (D) pentalene (C8H6) (E) azulene

Show answer
  1. (A), (B), (C), (E). (A) 2π; (B) 6π; (C) 10π, planar on accepting the two electrons; (E) azulene 10π (the dipolar form shows a 6π cyclopentadienyl anion fused to a 6π tropylium cation).

Practice 87 L2 · multiple correct

Which statements about [n]annulenes are correct? (A) [14]annulene is aromatic when planar (B) [16]annulene is non-aromatic/antiaromatic (C) [18]annulene is aromatic (D) [10]annulene (all-cis) is planar and aromatic

Show answer
  1. (A), (B), (C). (A) 14π = 4(3) + 2; (B) 16π = 4n (n = 4): it avoids antiaromaticity by non-planar, alternating bond lengths; (C) 18π, planar enough, inner H at δ −3, outer at δ +9.3 (ring current).
  2. (D) [10]annulene would be planar and aromatic by count, but the angle strain (144° for a regular decagon) and the clash of the two inner H prevent planarity, so it is not aromatic.

Arrange in decreasing pKa (weakest acid first): cyclopentadiene (A), cycloheptatriene (B), propene (C), cyclohexane (D).

Show answer

Choose the species with the largest dipole moment and explain: (A) naphthalene (B) azulene (C) benzene (D) toluene

Practice 91 L2 · structure

The C–C bond length in benzene is 139 pm. Which is true? (A) it lies between C–C (154 pm) and C=C (134 pm) (B) all six bonds are equal (C) it is shorter than C=C because the π electrons are delocalised (D) the bond order is 1.5

Show answer

Practice 92 L2 · resonance energy

The heat of hydrogenation of 1,3-cyclohexadiene is −232 kJ mol-1 and that of benzene −208 kJ mol-1; the heat of hydrogenation of cyclohexene is −120. (a) Resonance energy of benzene. (b) Is hydrogenation of benzene to cyclohexadiene exothermic?

Show answer

Practice 95 L2 · aromaticity

Cyclopentadienone and tropone are isomeric in the sense that both have a ring ketone with the carbonyl in conjugation. Why is tropone stable while cyclopentadienone is not?

Show answer
  1. The C=O carbon of tropone bears a partial positive charge in the polarised form C+–O−, which leaves a ring with 6π electrons (tropylium cation analogue) — aromatic stabilisation. In cyclopentadienone the same polarisation would give a cyclopentadienyl cation (4π, antiaromatic), so the compound is destabilised, very reactive, and dimerises at once (Diels–Alder) even at low temperature.

Write the mechanism of Friedel–Crafts acylation of benzene with acetyl chloride in four steps, and give the structure of the species that is hydrolysed in the work-up.

Practice 112 L2 · explain

Take OCH3 on C1 and let the electrophile attack C3 (meta). The positive charge is then on C2, C4 and C6. Why is no oxonium form possible?

Show answer
  1. The oxygen lone pair can stabilise a carbocation only on the carbon it is attached to (C1). In meta attack the charges are on C2, C4 and C6, none of them C1, so the oxygen lone pair is not conjugated with a cationic centre. Only the –I effect of OCH3 acts, and it destabilises the arenium ion: meta substitution in anisole is slower than in benzene, while o/p substitution is much faster.
Practice 113 L2 · explain

Explain why para-nitration of fluorobenzene is only slightly slower than that of benzene (fp ≈ 0.8), whereas chlorobenzene is about 7 times slower at the para position.

Show answer
  1. The para position of fluorobenzene benefits from efficient 2p–2p resonance donation by F that compensates for its strong –I effect, giving fp near 1. For Cl the 3p–2p overlap is poorer, so +M is weaker and does not offset the –I (fp ≈ 0.14).
Practice 114 L2 · explain

The nitration rates relative to benzene are toluene ≈ 25, ethylbenzene ≈ 22, cumene ≈ 15, t-butylbenzene ≈ 15. Give the usual explanation of why the order is not the same as the inductive order (t-Bu > i-Pr > Et > Me).

Show answer
  1. The first atoms of the alkyl group that can hyperconjugate with the positive charge are C–H bonds: CH3 has three, CH2R two, CHR2 one, CR3 none (only the weaker C–C bonds). The hyperconjugation order (Baker–Nathan) is Me > Et > i-Pr > t-Bu. Steric hindrance to solvation of the cation near a larger group also lowers the rate. Hyperconjugation thus outweighs the inductive effect in this reaction.
