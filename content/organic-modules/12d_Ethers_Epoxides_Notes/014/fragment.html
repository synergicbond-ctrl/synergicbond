15. Tests and identification of ethers

An ether is identified largely by what it fails to do. It shows no reaction with sodium metal (no acidic hydrogen), with aqueous base or with acyl chlorides, which distinguishes it from isomeric alcohols. It dissolves in cold concentrated sulfuric acid by forming a dialkyloxonium hydrogensulfate, while alkanes and alkyl halides do not.

  • Sodium test: alcohols evolve H2; ethers do not. Among the isomers of C4H10O, four alcohols evolve hydrogen and three ethers do not.
  • HI cleavage: heating with excess HI and identifying the alkyl iodides, or the Zeisel method for methoxy and ethoxy groups, in which the volatile RI is passed into alcoholic AgNO3 to precipitate AgI that is weighed.
  • Phenol or ether? A phenol gives a violet or green colour with neutral FeCl3 and dissolves in NaOH; an aryl alkyl ether does neither, but on heating with HI it gives a phenol that then responds.
  • Peroxides: acidified KI turns brown; starch gives a blue-black colour.
  • Spectroscopy: IR shows a strong C–O–C stretch in the region 1050–1150 cm−1 and no O–H band. In the 1H NMR spectrum the protons on carbon next to oxygen appear at about δ 3.3–4.0, and epoxide ring protons appear further upfield at about δ 2.5–3.5.
