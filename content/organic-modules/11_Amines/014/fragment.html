14. Alkylation, quaternary salts and the Hofmann elimination

14.1 Alkylation

Amines react with alkyl halides by SN2 (see §8). With excess CH3I every amine is converted to the quaternary ammonium iodide: exhaustive methylation.

  • Number of CH3I molecules consumed: 1° amine 3, 2° 2, 3° 1 (the number of N–H bonds plus one – i.e. until N has four carbon groups). This count determines the class of an unknown amine.
  • Quaternary ammonium hydroxides (R4N+OH−) are as strongly basic as KOH. They are prepared from the iodide with moist Ag2O (AgI precipitates).

Exhaustive methylation of ethylamine (3 CH₃I) and conversion to the hydroxide
14.2 Hofmann elimination

When a quaternary ammonium hydroxide having a β-hydrogen is heated (typically 373–473 K, often under reduced pressure) it undergoes E2 elimination to an alkene, a tertiary amine and water. The alkene is the least substituted one: the Hofmann rule (opposite to Zaitsev).

  • Why? The leaving group NR3 is large and carries a full positive charge. The base (OH−) attacks the most accessible and most acidic β-hydrogen – a CH3 or CH2 on the less-branched side – and the transition state has carbanion character (E1cB-like), which favours the least-substituted β-carbon.
heat⟶
sec-Butyltrimethylammonium hydroxide → but-1-ene (major, Hofmann) + trimethylamine + water; but-2-ene is minor

Rules to apply:

  • The β-hydrogens that can be removed are those on carbons attached to the α-carbon (the carbon carrying N). The methyl groups on N have no β-carbon and never give alkene.
  • Among several β-carbons, the order of H-abstraction is CH3 > CH2 > CH (primary β-H most easily removed).
  • Exceptions (acidic β-H wins). A β-hydrogen activated by a phenyl group or a carbonyl group is removed first even if on a more substituted carbon: PhCH2CH2N+Me3 gives styrene (conjugated).
  • Stereochemistry: anti-periplanar E2 (as with halides).
  • Using Hofmann elimination to find the structure: the alkene(s) formed and the number of CH3I consumed identify the original amine (classical structure-determination method).
Trap Hofmann elimination is not about the "amine class". It requires the quaternary hydroxide to be heated. Tertiary amines themselves do not eliminate; and quaternary halides (not hydroxides) on heating give SN2 dealkylation (RX + R3N) rather than E2.
heat⟶
N,N-Dimethylpiperidinium hydroxide → N,N-dimethylpent-4-en-1-amine (the first ring opening)
heat⟶
A second methylation/elimination cycle removes nitrogen entirely: penta-1,4-diene + trimethylamine

A cyclic amine therefore needs two rounds of exhaustive methylation/elimination (in total 4 CH3I) before nitrogen is expelled as NMe3; the number of rounds equals the number of rings that contain N.

Example 15 Adv

Structure by Hofmann degradation

An amine A (C4H11N) consumes three equivalents of CH3I. Its quaternary hydroxide, heated, gives trimethylamine and a mixture of but-1-ene (major) and but-2-ene (minor). Identify A.

Show solution
  1. Three CH3I → A is a 1° amine. The carbon chain is unbranched (butenes), so A is butan-1-amine or butan-2-amine.
  2. Butan-1-amine can only give but-1-ene (the single β-carbon is C2, the product CH2=CH–CH2CH3). A mixture of 1-ene and 2-ene requires two different β-carbons: N on C2, with β-carbons C1 (CH3, Hofmann, major → but-1-ene) and C3 (CH2, minor → but-2-ene).
  3. A = butan-2-amine.
Example 16 Adv

Hofmann rule

Predict the major alkene on heating (a) (CH3)3N+CH(CH3)CH2CH3OH−, (b) (CH3)3N+CH2CH2C6H5OH−, (c) (CH3)3N+–C(CH3)2CH2CH3 OH−.

Show solution
  1. (a) β-carbons: C1 (CH3) and C3 (CH2). Hofmann: remove H from CH3 → but-1-ene.
  2. (b) The only β-carbon is the benzylic CH2; this H is also acidic: styrene.
  3. (c) β-carbons: two equivalent CH3 groups on the α-carbon, and the CH2 of the ethyl group. Primary β-H preferred: 2-methylbut-1-ene.
Example 17 Adv

Integer

How many moles of CH3I are needed in total to remove the nitrogen of one mole of piperidine as trimethylamine (two Hofmann cycles)?

Show solution
  1. First cycle: piperidine (2°) takes 2 CH3I to give N,N-dimethylpiperidinium; heating its hydroxide opens the ring to N,N-dimethylpent-4-en-1-amine (3°).
  2. Second cycle: 1 more CH3I quaternises it; elimination gives penta-1,4-diene and NMe3.
  3. Total = 3.
14.3 Cope elimination

A tertiary amine is oxidised (H2O2 or a peracid) to the N-oxide; on heating (≈ 373–423 K) it eliminates syn through a five-membered cyclic transition state (Ei: intramolecular, no external base) to give an alkene and an N,N-dialkylhydroxylamine.

Cope elimination: N-oxide → alkene + N,N-dimethylhydroxylamine (syn elimination, five-membered TS)

Contrast with Hofmann elimination: Hofmann is anti E2 with an external base, and the amine product is NR3; Cope is syn, intramolecular, and gives hydroxylamine.
