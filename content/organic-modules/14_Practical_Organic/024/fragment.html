7.4 Compounds containing both nitrogen and sulphur

If the compound contains N and S and the amount of sodium used is insufficient, sodium thiocyanate NaSCN forms instead of NaCN and Na2S. Fe3+ then gives the blood red colour of the thiocyanato complex and no Prussian blue forms:

Formula Fe3+ + SCN− → [Fe(SCN)]2+ (blood red) (and for excess ligand, [Fe(SCN)n]3−n)

If sodium is in excess, the thiocyanate is decomposed: NaSCN + 2Na → NaCN + Na2S, and the tests for N (Prussian blue) and for S (nitroprusside, PbS) are then positive separately.

IMP Blood red with FeCl3 but not Prussian blue ⇒ N and S are both present in the compound and the sodium was insufficient to decompose NaSCN. To distinguish, repeat with more sodium: the pattern changes to Prussian blue + violet nitroprusside + black PbS.
Example 10 Adv

Interpreting observations

A compound gives the following with its sodium extract. (i) FeSO4 + boil + H2SO4: no Prussian blue. (ii) FeCl3: blood-red colour. (iii) Sodium nitroprusside: no violet colour. Deduce the elements present, and explain (iii).

Show solution
  1. Blood red with Fe3+ ⇒ SCN− ⇒ N and S are both present as thiocyanate. No Prussian blue because no free CN− (it is all SCN−).
  2. (iii) Nitroprusside reacts with free S2−; there is no free sulphide ion in the extract because sulphur is in the thiocyanate. So that test is negative even though S is present.
  3. Elements: C (organic), N and S.
