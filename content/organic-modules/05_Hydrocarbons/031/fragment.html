30. Alkynes: structure and acidity

Alkynes CnH2n−2 contain a C≡C bond: one σ (sp–sp) and two π bonds at right angles. Each sp carbon is linear (180°); H–C≡C–H is a straight molecule. C≡C = 120 pm, ≡C–H = 106 pm, C≡C bond enthalpy ≈ 839 kJ mol-1. Because the carbon in an sp orbital (50 % s-character) holds its electrons closer to the nucleus than sp2 (33 %) or sp3 (25 %), the ≡C–H bond is polarised towards carbon and the conjugate base (acetylide) is stabilised.

Acid–base series (pKa): H2O (15.7) > ROH (16–18) > HC≡CH (25) > NH3 (38) > alkene C–H (44) > alkane C–H (50). Therefore:

  • NaOH or RO− cannot deprotonate a terminal alkyne appreciably (their conjugate acids are stronger acids than the alkyne); NaNH2 (pKa of NH3 = 38), NaH, Na metal, or RLi/RMgX can.
  • Terminal alkynes liberate H2 with Na: 2 HC≡CH + 2 Na → 2 HC≡C−Na+ + H2.
  • Acidity order of hydrocarbons: alkyne > alkene > alkane; and for substituted alkynes the order of acidity: HC≡CH > CH3C≡CH > CH3C≡CCH3 (the latter has no acidic ≡C–H).

Tests for terminal alkyne (≡C–H): ammoniacal AgNO3 (Tollens' reagent) → white precipitate of silver acetylide; ammoniacal Cu2Cl2 → red precipitate of copper(I) acetylide. Dry metal acetylides explode; decompose with dil. HNO3.

Which will react?

Which of the following react with (a) NaNH2 (b) ammoniacal AgNO3 (c) NaOH(aq): ethyne, propyne, but-2-yne, ethene?

Show solution
  1. (a) Ethyne and propyne (terminal ≡C–H, pKa 25); not but-2-yne (no ≡C–H), nor ethene (pKa 44 is higher than NH3's 38, so the equilibrium lies on the left).
  2. (b) Ethyne and propyne.
  3. (c) None: hydroxide (pKa of water 15.7) is too weak a base.
