Further problems: orientation, stereochemistry and competition (part B)

The problems in this set extend the basic ideas of elimination to the situations that decide the product in practice: which β-hydrogen is lost, which geometry the new double bond adopts, how the ring conformation of a cyclohexane controls an E2 reaction, and how conditions tip the balance between E1, E2, substitution and the syn eliminations of onium and ester derivatives.

Each problem is fully solved. Where stereochemistry matters, the structures carry wedge bonds, and the solutions describe the reacting conformation in words (anti-periplanar or syn-periplanar H and leaving group, axial or equatorial positions). Treat the worked examples as models for the setting-out of an answer.
