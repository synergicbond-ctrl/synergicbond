1. What a polymer is: the vocabulary

A polymer (Greek poly = many, meros = part) is a very large molecule, a macromolecule, built by joining a very large number of small molecules called monomers through covalent bonds. Molar masses typically run from about 104 to 106 g mol-1. A chain with only a few to a few dozen units is an oligomer; it does not yet show polymer properties such as fibre-forming strength or rubbery elasticity.

  • Monomer: the small molecule that is actually put into the reactor (ethene, vinyl chloride, adipic acid, hexamethylenediamine ...).
  • Repeat unit (structural unit, constitutional repeating unit, CRU): the smallest group of atoms that, repeated, generates the chain. For an addition polymer it has the same atoms as the monomer. For a condensation polymer it is the monomer residue(s) after the small molecule has been lost.
  • Degree of polymerisation (DP, Xn): the number of repeat units in the chain. For nylon-6,6 count repeat units (one diacid residue + one diamine residue), but when you count monomer molecules you get 2 per repeat unit. The question always has to be read carefully.
  • End groups: the two ends of a chain are chemically different from the middle (initiator fragments in addition polymers; –COOH and –NH2 in polyamides). They matter only when the chain is short, but they are the basis of end-group analysis for Mn.
  • Functionality of a monomer is the number of sites through which it can bond: ethene (as a monomer) is bifunctional because one C=C can open into two bonds; adipic acid is bifunctional (two –COOH); glycerol is trifunctional (three –OH); phenol is trifunctional in the Bakelite reaction (two ortho and one para position).
IMP Functionality decides architecture. All monomers bifunctional → linear chains (thermoplastic). Some monomers of functionality ≥ 3 → branched, then cross-linked three-dimensional network (thermoset). Bakelite, melamine–formaldehyde, glyptal (glycerol–phthalic anhydride) and vulcanised rubber are all networks for this reason.

Addition: repeat unit has the same atoms as the monomer; only the C=C π bond has become two σ bonds. Open bonds on * continue the chain.
caprolactam repeat unit of nylon-6 ethylene glycol terephthalic acid repeat unit of PET
Nylon-6 is made by ring-opening, so its repeat unit also equals the monomer. The PET repeat unit is one glycol residue + one diacid residue (2 H2O lost per pair joined, once the chain grows on both ends).
Exam Trap The repeat unit of an addition polymer of CH2=CHX is –CH2–CHX–, not –CH=CX–. A very common error is to write the polymer with the double bond intact. Addition polymerisation consumes the double bond; the polymer is saturated (except for diene polymers, where one C=C survives per unit).
Compare & Distinguish Monomer, repeat unit, DP. The monomer is the molecule you put in; the repeat unit is what appears in the chain; DP counts repeat units. In nylon-6,6 one repeat unit contains two monomer residues, so DP = 100 means 200 monomer molecules. Always read which of the two counts a question asks for.
Why It Happens Why functionality fixes architecture. A bifunctional monomer can extend a chain at only two ends, so the product is a chain. A trifunctional monomer has a third bonding site that branches the chain; when branches of different chains meet, a network forms. It is purely a counting argument about bonding sites.
