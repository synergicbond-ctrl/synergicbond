16.2 Kolbe–Schmitt reaction (ortho-carboxylation)

Sodium phenoxide (dry) is heated with CO2 at 400–410 K under 4–7 atm pressure; acidification gives salicylic acid (2-hydroxybenzoic acid). CO2 is a weak electrophile, so the very nucleophilic phenoxide (not phenol) is required. In the transition state Na+ chelates the phenoxide O and one oxygen of CO2, directing attack to the ortho carbon through a six-membered cyclic arrangement; the intermediate keto-carboxylate then tautomerises (a proton shift) to the aromatic salicylate.

Key idea Sodium phenoxide gives the ortho isomer (kinetic, chelation control); potassium (or rubidium, caesium) phenoxide at higher temperature (above 500 K) gives mostly the para isomer, p-hydroxybenzoic acid (thermodynamic product, no chelation). The large K+ chelates less strongly and the ortho carboxylate can rearrange to para at high temperature.

  • Aspirin is hydrolysed by moist air to salicylic acid and ethanoic acid (vinegar odour in old tablets); it gives a violet colour with FeCl3 only after hydrolysis (free phenolic OH). It is an analgesic, antipyretic and anti-inflammatory, and an anticoagulant in low dose.
  • Salicylic acid heated with soda lime or with excess alkali decarboxylates to phenol.
Example 47 L2 Main

Kolbe–Schmitt versus Reimer–Tiemann

Compare the two reactions with respect to the electrophile, the attacking species, the conditions and the product.

Show solution
  1. Reimer–Tiemann: electrophile :CCl2 (generated in situ from CHCl3/NaOH); nucleophile phenoxide; aqueous alkali, 340 K, atmospheric pressure; product salicylaldehyde.
  2. Kolbe–Schmitt: electrophile CO2; nucleophile dry sodium phenoxide; 400 K, 4–7 atm; product (after acid) salicylic acid.
  3. Both substitute at the ortho position through the phenoxide ion; Na+ guides both to the ortho carbon.
Example 48 L2 Main

Aspirin synthesis

Starting from phenol, give the reagents for a 3-step synthesis of aspirin and calculate the mass of aspirin from 94 g of phenol if each step has 100 % yield, then if the overall yield is 60 %.

Show solution
  1. Phenol → (NaOH) sodium phenoxide → (CO2, 400 K, 5 atm; H+) salicylic acid → ((CH3CO)2O, H+) aspirin.
  2. Phenol 94 g = 1.00 mol → 1.00 mol aspirin (C9H8O4, 180 g mol−1) = 180 g at 100 % yield and 108 g at 60 %.
