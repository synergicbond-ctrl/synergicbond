25. Crown ethers and related macrocycles

Crown ethers (C. Pedersen, 1967; Nobel Prize 1987 with D. Cram and J.-M. Lehn) are macrocyclic polyethers made of –CH2CH2O– repeat units. They are named x-crown-y: x = total number of atoms in the ring, y = number of oxygens: 12-crown-4 (C8H16O4), 15-crown-5 (C10H20O5), 18-crown-6 (C12H24O6, M = 264.3), dibenzo-18-crown-6 (two benzene rings fused).

Binding. The ring folds so that all the oxygen lone pairs point into the cavity, which is lined by oxygens (polar, ion-binding) while the outside is a hydrocarbon surface (CH2 groups). A metal cation that fits the cavity is held by ion–dipole interactions with all the oxygens and carried into non-polar solvents, with its counter-anion “naked”. Selectivity follows the match between ion size and cavity size:

12-crown-41.2–1.5 ÅLi+ (1.5 Å)
15-crown-51.7–2.2 ÅNa+ (2.0 Å)
18-crown-62.6–3.2 ÅK+ (2.7 Å)
21-crown-73.4–4.3 ÅCs+ (3.4 Å)

Applications

  • Dissolving inorganic salts in organic solvents: KMnO4 + 18-crown-6 in benzene gives “purple benzene”, an organic-soluble oxidant that oxidises alkenes and alcohols under mild conditions; KF/18-crown-6 in acetonitrile gives “naked fluoride” (F− is a much stronger nucleophile and base when not solvated or ion-paired): halide exchange R–Cl → R–F; KCN/18-crown-6 for SN2 in organic solvent.
  • Phase-transfer catalysis: the crown ether carries the cation (and so the anion) across the water–organic interface.
  • Template synthesis: a large cation organises the ring closure in a Williamson synthesis (K+ template for 18-crown-6 from triethylene glycol and the corresponding dichloride, KOH/THF).
  • Ion-selective electrodes; separation of isotopes and of metal ions; models for biological ionophores (valinomycin, nonactin carry K+ across membranes).
  • Cryptands (Lehn) are bicyclic analogues, e.g. [2.2.2]cryptand, which encapsulate the cation in a three-dimensional cavity and bind far more strongly than crown ethers. Cyclodextrins and calixarenes are related host molecules.
Key idea The crown ether complexes the cation, not the anion, and does so by size match. When an exam says “K+ is transported/dissolved in benzene by 18-crown-6”, the logic is: polar inside (binds cation), non-polar outside (dissolves in hydrocarbons).
Example 72 L1 Foundation

Crown ether facts

(a) What is the molecular formula and mass of 18-crown-6? (b) Which alkali metal ion is best bound by 15-crown-5? (c) Why does KF dissolve in benzene in the presence of 18-crown-6?

Show solution
  1. (a) 18-Crown-6 is (CH2CH2O)6 = C12H24O6, M = 12 × 12.01 + 24 × 1.008 + 6 × 16.00 = 264.3 g mol−1.
  2. (b) Na+ (ionic diameter about 2.0 Å matches the 1.7–2.2 Å cavity).
  3. (c) K+ sits inside the polar cavity, ion–dipole bound to six O atoms, and the ring’s non-polar CH2 exterior makes the complex soluble in benzene. F− follows to preserve charge neutrality; it is poorly solvated (“naked”) and therefore very reactive.
Example 73 L2 Main

Crown ether counting

How many ring atoms, oxygen atoms, carbon atoms and hydrogen atoms are present in 15-crown-5? What is its molar mass?

Show solution
  1. 15-Crown-5 consists of five –CH2CH2O– units: ring atoms 5 × 3 = 15, O = 5, C = 10, H = 20; formula C10H20O5.
  2. M = 10 × 12.01 + 20 × 1.008 + 5 × 16.00 = 220.3 g mol−1.
