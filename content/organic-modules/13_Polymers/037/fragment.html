Appendix: Sources cross-checked and what was not verified

Opened and used for this chapter (page numbers are PDF page numbers):

  1. Wade, Organic Chemistry, 9th ed., Chapter 26 “Synthetic Polymers”: PDF pp. 1328–1333 and 1341. Used to cross-check: definitions of isotactic, syndiotactic and atactic chains and the stereochemical control by Ziegler–Natta catalysts (TiCl4 + triethylaluminium); latex of Hevea brasiliensis, vulcanisation by –S–S– cross-links and the sulphur ranges (about 1–2 % soft, 2–5 % medium, 10–30 % hard rubber); Saran as a vinyl chloride–vinylidene chloride copolymer; ABS; nylon salt formation and heating to give nylon-6,6; nylon-6 from caprolactam with a trace of water; PET by transesterification of dimethyl terephthalate; polyacetylene and the 2000 Nobel Prize; poly(vinyl alcohol) made through poly(vinyl acetate). The problem types on acid hydrolysis of nylon, alkaline hydrolysis of polyester and the PVA route gave ideas for original problems here.
  2. Clayden, Greeves, Warren, Organic Chemistry (2012): PDF p. 1103, migratory insertion as the chain-extension step that, repeated indefinitely, gives Ziegler–Natta polymers (basis of the Cossee–Arlman description in section 5.5).
  3. The repository’s own earlier Polymers notes (NCERT-style content: reaction conditions, temperatures, catalysts and uses) were the starting syllabus and were kept as the baseline for conditions.
  4. Structures, formulae, repeat-unit masses and CIP/tacticity labels were checked with RDKit; every numerical answer in the practice sets was recomputed in Python (script `verify13.py`).

Searched but not used: M.S. Chouhan’s Advanced Problems in Organic Chemistry contains only incidental mentions of polymers (PDF pp. 184, 592, 602); no problem from it was used or adapted.

Not opened for this chapter: McMurry, Finar, Eliel, Kalsi, the ICHO volumes, Klein, Sykes, the coaching test papers and previous JEE Advanced papers. No claim is made that they were consulted.

Stated from standard knowledge, not matched to a book in this session (treat numbers as typical values): melting points and glass-transition temperatures (LDPE, HDPE, PP, PS, PMMA, PVC, PET, nylon-6, nylon-6,6, nylon-6,10), densities and crystallinities, the ceiling temperature of α-methylstyrene, the industrial routes to monomers (PTFE monomer from CHClF2, acrylonitrile ammoxidation, chloroprene from vinylacetylene), Kevlar and Nomex properties, the 1,5-hydrogen back-biting description of LDPE branching, the C–F bond energy, the PDI values for different mechanisms and the reactivity-ratio picture of copolymers. The orbital explanations (SOMO–π* control, Bürgi–Dunitz attack, nN→π* amide delocalisation, π-band picture of polyacetylene) are standard frontier-orbital arguments, stated qualitatively.
