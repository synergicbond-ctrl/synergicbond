35. Practice set, Level 3 (Advanced): matrix-match problems

In each matrix problem, one entry in Column I may match one or more entries in Column II.

Practice 248 Matrix

Mechanisms

Column I: (A) (CH3)3CBr in warm ethanol (B) CH3CH2CH2Br + NaCN in DMSO (C) (CH3)3CBr + t-BuOK in t-BuOH (D) CH3CH(OH)CH2COCH3 + NaOH, heat.

Column II: (p) SN1 (q) SN2 (r) E2 (s) E1 (t) E1cB.

Show answer
  1. (A) Tertiary halide in a protic solvent with no strong base: ionisation then capture/loss of H+: p, s. (B) Primary halide, good nucleophile, aprotic solvent: q. (C) Strong bulky base on a tertiary halide: r. (D) β-Hydroxy ketone: enolate formation then loss of HO−: t.
Practice 249 Matrix

Ambident nucleophiles

Column I: (A) KCN (B) AgCN (C) AgNO2 (D) KNO2. Column II: (p) R–N≡C (q) R–C≡N (r) R–NO2 (s) R–O–N=O.

Show answer
  1. Ionic cyanide attacks through carbon: (A) → q. Covalent AgCN attacks through nitrogen: (B) → p. Covalent AgNO2: nitro compound: (C) → r. Ionic KNO2: through the more electronegative oxygen: (D) → s.

Practice 251 Matrix

Reactivity of halides

Column II: (p) immediate precipitate with alcoholic AgNO3 at room temperature (q) hydrolysed by aqueous NaOH at about 368 K by addition–elimination (r) practically inert to hydroxide at 100 °C (s) cannot undergo E2 (no β-hydrogen).

Practice 254 Matrix

Solvent polarity effects

Column I: (A) (CH3)3CCl → [(CH3)3C+Cl−]‡ (B) HO− + CH3Br (C) (C2H5)3N + C2H5I (D) (CH3)3S+ + HO−. Column II: (p) rate increases greatly with solvent polarity (q) rate decreases slightly (r) rate decreases markedly.

Show answer
  1. Hughes–Ingold rules. (A) neutral → charge-separated TS: p. (B) anion → TS with dispersed charge: q. (C) neutral + neutral → ion pair (Menshutkin): p. (D) cation + anion → neutral products (charge destroyed): r.
