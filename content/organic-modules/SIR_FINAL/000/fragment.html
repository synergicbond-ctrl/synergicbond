1. The core idea

A resonance (π) effect needs the conjugating atom's p-orbital to overlap with the ring's π system. Overlap is largest when the two are coplanar and falls as the twist angle θ between them grows. At θ = 90° the overlap is zero and the π effect is lost.

Bulky groups in the ortho positions (CH₃, t-Bu) force a conjugating group out of the ring plane. This is steric inhibition of resonance.

What SIR changes. Only the π-part of a substituent: +M, −M, and resonance stabilisation of an ion or lone pair.
What SIR does not change. The σ-part: −I and +I (inductive) effects act through σ bonds and do not need coplanarity.

Test for any problem: (1) Does the substituent act by resonance? (2) Is it bulky and ortho to the conjugating group? If both are yes, the π-part is at risk. Then check whether the question's answer depends on π-donation, σ-withdrawal, or both.
