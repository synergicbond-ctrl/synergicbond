6.3 The Victor Meyer test (primary, secondary, tertiary)

The alcohol is converted to the alkyl iodide (red P + I2), then to the nitroalkane (AgNO2), which is treated with nitrous acid (NaNO2 + H2SO4) and then made alkaline (NaOH).

  • 1° alcohol → RCH2I → RCH2NO2 → with HNO2 gives nitrolic acid, RC(=NOH)NO2; with NaOH: blood-red colour (sodium salt).
  • 2° alcohol → R2CHI → R2CHNO2 → pseudonitrole, R2C(NO)NO2; with NaOH: blue colour.
  • 3° alcohol → R3CI → R3C–NO2 (or mostly alkene/nitrite: tertiary iodides eliminate with AgNO2); no α-H, so with HNO2 no reaction; with NaOH: colourless.
Trap Memorise as “Red – Primary, Blue – Secondary, Colourless – Tertiary” (RP, BS, CT). The key chemistry is the number of α-hydrogens on the nitroalkane: two (nitrolic acid), one (pseudonitrole), none (nothing).
