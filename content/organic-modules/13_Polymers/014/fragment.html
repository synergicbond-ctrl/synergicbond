10. Rubbers and diene polymers

10.1 Natural rubber

Natural rubber is the latex of Hevea brasiliensis: an aqueous colloid with about 30–40 % rubber. Coagulation with acetic or formic acid gives crude rubber. Its repeat unit is cis-1,4-isoprene, with all the –CH2– groups on the same side of every C=C. Average M ≈ 105–106 (DP in the thousands).

isoprene cis-1,4-polyisoprene unit (natural rubber)
The chain is kinked at every cis double bond.

Natural rubber (raw) is soft, sticky, has low tensile strength and becomes brittle in the cold; it has weak van der Waals forces, so the chains slide past each other irreversibly when stretched hard.

10.2 Vulcanisation

Charles Goodyear (1839) heated rubber with sulphur. Modern vulcanisation: rubber + 3–5 % sulphur + accelerator (ZnO, benzothiazole derivatives) at 373–415 K. Sulphur attacks the allylic C–H (and the C=C) and forms S–S (and Sn) bridges between chains. Hard rubber (ebonite) uses 10–30 % S.

model: two allylic segments joined by –S–S–
Model of a cross-link: sulphur bridges attach at the allylic carbon next to a C=C.

Effects: higher tensile strength and hardness, better abrasion and solvent resistance, elasticity over a wider temperature range, no stickiness, and reversible stretching (the cross-links pin the chains so that stress cannot cause permanent flow). The amount of sulphur sets the property: 1–2 % soft (rubber bands, inner tubes), 2–5 % medium (tyres), 10–30 % hard rubber.

Exam Trap Vulcanised rubber is not a thermoset in the usual sense of “hard and brittle”: at 3–5 % S it is still a soft elastomer. It is a lightly cross-linked network (one cross-link per few hundred units), which is why it is insoluble but swells and cannot be reshaped by heating.

Example 10 Main

Chloroprene preparation

Chloroprene is made from vinylacetylene by addition of HCl. Show the route from ethyne.

Show solution
  1. 2 HC≡CH → (CuCl/NH4Cl) CH2=CH–C≡CH (vinylacetylene, but-1-en-3-yne).
  2. CH2=CH–C≡CH + HCl (CuCl) → CH2=C(Cl)–CH=CH2. H goes to the terminal alkyne carbon and Cl to the internal one (Markovnikov-type orientation), giving 2-chlorobuta-1,3-diene, chloroprene. (The industrial pathway is believed to go through a 1,4-adduct that rearranges; the net result is what is examined.)
Compare & Distinguish Natural rubber vs gutta-percha. Same constitution (head-to-tail polyisoprene); different C=C geometry. cis (rubber): kinked chains, amorphous, elastic. trans (gutta-percha): straight chains, crystalline, hard. The properties differ because the geometry decides whether chains can pack.
Experimental Connection Stretching a rubber band quickly and holding it against the lip shows warming; letting it relax shows cooling. The mechanical work done on stretching is released as heat because the stretched, more ordered chains have lower entropy (§19).
Example 11 Think Further

Why not vulcanise with 50 % sulphur?

Hard rubber (ebonite) uses 10–30 % sulphur. What property would you expect at much higher cross-link density, and why does elasticity disappear?

Show solution
  1. Each cross-link ties two chain segments together. At low density the segments between cross-links are long, so the chains can uncoil and recoil: elasticity.
  2. As cross-link density rises, the segments become so short that they cannot change conformation appreciably; the material resembles a rigid network: hard and brittle, with almost no elastic range.
  3. Limit: ebonite (hard rubber) is that regime; it is a rigid black plastic.
Concept Check C13 Checkpoint

Rubbers

Name the monomers of Buna-S, Buna-N and neoprene, and give one use of each.

Show answer

Buna-S: buta-1,3-diene + styrene (tyres). Buna-N: buta-1,3-diene + acrylonitrile (oil seals, fuel hoses). Neoprene: chloroprene (2-chlorobuta-1,3-diene) (gaskets, hoses, wet-suits).
