34. The orbital picture of the nitrogen lone pair

Everything about amines reduces to the non-bonding lone-pair orbital nN – the HOMO of an amine (an sp3-type orbital, mostly N-centred, with some s-character).

  • Protonation/basicity: H+ has an empty 1s orbital (LUMO). The better the energy match and the more localised nN, the stronger the N–H bond formed. Lower s-character in nN means higher energy, a better donor: sp3 (25 % s) > sp2 (33 %) > sp (50 %). That is the MO statement of the basicity order amine > pyridine > nitrile.
  • Nucleophilicity: nN (HOMO) overlaps with the empty σ*(C–X) of an alkyl halide or π*(C=O) of a carbonyl compound (LUMO).
  • Alkyl groups donate electron density by σ-hyperconjugation and polarisation, raising the energy of nN: alkylamines are better donors than NH3.
  • Pyramidal inversion: the pyramid passes through a planar transition state in which the lone pair becomes a pure p orbital (sp2 + p). Cost ≈ 25 kJ mol-1. Anything that favours planarity (conjugation with a π system, as in amides and anilines) lowers the barrier.
34.1 Conjugation: aniline, amide, and why they are weak bases

When nN overlaps with a neighbouring π system its energy is lowered (it becomes the out-of-phase partner of a stabilised bonding combination) and delocalised over more atoms: the electrons are held more tightly and are less available for H+.

Two-way consequence of conjugation (the central idea):

  • The N lone pair becomes less available to H+ (lower basicity, weaker nucleophile at N).
  • The π system gains electron density: the ring HOMO of aniline lies higher than that of benzene, with large coefficients on N and on the ortho/para carbons. Hence electrophiles attack ortho/para very fast.

Amide (three-centre, four-electron π system O–C–N): ψ1 (bonding over all three atoms), ψ2 (non-bonding, mainly O and N), ψ3 (anti-bonding). The N lone pair enters ψ1/ψ2, giving a C–N bond order about 1.4–1.5 and a rotation barrier of 65–85 kJ mol-1; the amide N is planar. The lowest empty orbital π*(C=O) is raised by the donation, so amides are poor electrophiles (hydrolysis is slow) and LiAlH4 (a very strong H− donor) is needed to reduce them. Pyridine vs pyrrole: in pyridine the lone pair lies in the ring plane in an sp2 orbital orthogonal to the π system (no conjugation) – available. In pyrrole the N lone pair is the p orbital that forms part of the 6π-electron aromatic sextet: protonating it would destroy the aromatic stabilisation.

34.2 The aniline HOMO and electrophilic substitution

In the arenium ion formed by attack of E+ at the para (or ortho) carbon, the N lone pair is adjacent to the positive charge and can donate into it, giving a fourth resonance structure in which every atom (except H) has an octet (iminium ion). With attack at meta there is no such structure.

Br⁺ (as Br₂) at para⟶
Para attack of an electrophile (here Br⁺): the σ-complex has an iminium-ion resonance form (N lone pair donates into the carbocation) – a large stabilisation

Energetic reason: the transition state resembles the σ-complex, so stabilising it lowers the activation energy; this is the origin of the strong o/p-directing, activating effect of –NH2 (and of –OH, –OR, –NHCOR, weaker).
