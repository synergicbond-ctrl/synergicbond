0. How to read this chapter

This chapter is the engine room of organic reaction mechanisms. Almost every later chapter (alcohols, ethers, amines, carbonyl chemistry, named reactions) quietly uses a substitution or an elimination step, so the time spent here repays itself many times. The chapter is built in six movements:

  • Movement A (sections 1–3): what a C–X bond is, how it is named, and how every class of halide is made.
  • Movement B (sections 4–9): the substitution manifold: SN2, SN1, SNi, neighbouring-group participation, ambident nucleophiles and the full list of synthetic transformations.
  • Movement C (sections 10–14): elimination: E2, E1, E1cB, Zaitsev/Hofmann, stereochemistry, cyclohexanes, and the SN/E competition map.
  • Movement D (sections 15–18): organometallics, allyl/benzyl/vinyl halides, aryl halides (SNAr, benzyne), polyhalogen compounds.
  • Movement E: one-page summary sheets, a reagent dictionary, and traps.
  • Movement F: three graded problem sets with full solutions: Level 1 (Main), Level 2 (Advanced single/multiple correct) and Level 3 (integer, matrix, paragraph).
Shortcut Study order that works: read a section, attempt its worked examples before opening the solution (every solution is hidden behind a button on screen and printed in full on paper), then do the matching practice problems at the end. Mechanism arrows are not decoration: for every product you write, you should be able to say which bond broke first and why.
