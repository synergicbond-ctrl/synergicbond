Practice 40 L1 Gabriel

Give the product when potassium phthalimide reacts with 1-bromopropane and the product is then heated with hydrazine.

Show answer
  1. Step 1: SN2 of the phthalimide anion on CH3CH2CH2Br gives N-propylphthalimide.
  2. Step 2: hydrazinolysis cleaves both C–N(acyl) bonds, releasing the primary amine.

Propan-1-amine (CH3CH2CH2NH2) and phthalhydrazide.

N₂H₄⟶
N-Propylphthalimide → propan-1-amine
Practice 41 L1 Gabriel

Which of the following cannot be prepared by Gabriel's phthalimide synthesis? (a) benzylamine (b) aniline (c) isobutylamine (d) tert-butylamine

Show answer
  1. Gabriel is an SN2 reaction of the phthalimide anion with an alkyl halide. Aryl halides do not undergo SN2 (b), and tertiary halides eliminate (d). Benzyl and isobutyl (1°) halides work.

(b) and (d).

Practice 42 L1 Hofmann

Write the product of the Hofmann bromamide reaction of (a) propanamide, (b) benzamide, (c) 2-methylpropanamide, (d) phenylacetamide.

Show answer
  1. The amine has one carbon fewer than the amide: the carbonyl carbon is lost as carbonate; the R group migrates from C to N.

(a) ethylamine, (b) aniline, (c) isopropylamine, (d) benzylamine.

Practice 43 L1 Hofmann

Balance: CH3CONH2 + Br2 + KOH → CH3NH2 + K2CO3 + KBr + H2O. How many moles of KOH are needed per mole of amide?

Show answer
  1. Atom check: K: 4 = 2 (K2CO3) + 2 (KBr) ✓; Br: 2 = 2 ✓; O: 1 + 4 = 5 = 3 + 2 ✓; H: reactants 5 (amide) + 4 (KOH) = 9, products 5 (CH3NH2) + 4 (2 H2O) = 9 ✓.

4 mol of KOH (CH3CONH2 + Br2 + 4KOH → CH3NH2 + K2CO3 + 2KBr + 2H2O).

Practice 44 L1 Hofmann

A compound A (C7H7NO) is heated with Br2/KOH to give B (C6H7N), which gives a positive carbylamine test and a red dye with β-naphthol after NaNO2/HCl at 273 K. Identify A and B.

Show answer
  1. A has one more C than B and is an amide: C6H5CONH2 (C7H7NO ✓). B = C6H5NH2 (C6H7N ✓), a 1° aromatic amine: carbylamine and the diazonium–coupling test fit.

A = benzamide; B = aniline.

Practice 45 L1 Reduction

What are the products when nitrobenzene is reduced with (a) Sn/HCl, (b) Zn/NH4Cl, (c) Zn/NaOH?

Show answer
  1. Acidic medium: complete reduction to the amine. Neutral: stops at the hydroxylamine. Alkaline: bimolecular products from condensation of PhNO and PhNHOH.

(a) aniline, (b) N-phenylhydroxylamine, (c) azoxybenzene → azobenzene → hydrazobenzene.

Practice 46 L1 Reduction

How many moles of Sn are needed to reduce one mole of nitrobenzene completely to aniline in HCl?

Show answer
  1. NO2 → NH2 takes 6 electrons; each Sn gives 2 electrons: 6/2 = 3 Sn.

3 mol of Sn (Sn → Sn2+).

Practice 47 L1 Reduction

Predict the product: CH3CH2C≡N with (a) LiAlH4, (b) H3O+, heat, (c) SnCl2/HCl followed by H2O.

Show answer
  1. (a) Full reduction C≡N → CH2NH2. (b) Hydrolysis via the amide to the acid. (c) Stephen reduction stops at the imine, which is hydrolysed to the aldehyde.

(a) propan-1-amine, (b) propanoic acid, (c) propanal.

Practice 48 L1 Reduction

What is obtained on reducing CH3CONHCH3 with LiAlH4?

Show answer
  1. C=O of the amide becomes CH2; the carbon count is unchanged. NaBH4 would not reduce an amide.

Ethylmethylamine, CH3CH2NHCH3 (N-methylethanamine).

Practice 49 L1 Ammonolysis

Why does the reaction of ethyl bromide with ammonia not give pure ethylamine? How is the yield of the primary amine improved?

Show answer
  1. Each alkylation makes a more electron-rich (more nucleophilic) N, so the reaction runs on. High [NH3] makes the halide meet NH3 rather than the amines.

The product ethylamine is more nucleophilic than NH3 and reacts further with C2H5Br, giving diethylamine, triethylamine and the quaternary salt. A large excess of NH3 favours the primary amine.

Practice 50 L1 Reductive amination

What is formed when acetone reacts with NH3 in the presence of H2/Ni? What if CH3NH2 is used instead of NH3?

Show answer
  1. Carbonyl + amine → imine → H2/Ni reduces C=N.

With NH3: propan-2-amine (isopropylamine). With CH3NH2: N-methylpropan-2-amine.

Practice 51 L1 Chain length

Starting from ethyl bromide, how would you prepare (a) propan-1-amine, (b) methylamine?

Show answer
  1. The nitrile route ascends; the Hofmann route descends.

(a) CH3CH2Br → KCN → CH3CH2CN → LiAlH4 → CH3CH2CH2NH2 (one carbon more). (b) CH3CH2Br → hydrolysis → CH3CH2OH → oxidation → CH3COOH → NH3/heat → CH3CONH2 → Br2/KOH → CH3NH2 (one carbon fewer than the acid).

Practice 52 L1 Eschweiler–Clarke

What is obtained when ethylamine is heated with HCHO and HCOOH (excess)? Can a quaternary salt form?

Show answer
  1. The primary amine forms an imine/iminium with HCHO, which is reduced by HCOOH (hydride donor, CO2 lost); the process repeats on the secondary amine. A tertiary amine cannot form an iminium, so the reaction stops.

N,N-Dimethylethanamine; no quaternary salt forms.

Practice 53 L1 Carbon count

Which method gives an amine with one carbon fewer than the starting material: (a) nitrile reduction, (b) Gabriel, (c) Hofmann bromamide, (d) reductive amination?

Show answer
  1. RCONH2 → RNH2. Nitrile reduction gives RCH2NH2 (one more C than R), Gabriel and reductive amination keep carbon number.

(c) Hofmann bromamide.

Practice 54 L1 Mechanism

Name the intermediate common to Hofmann, Curtius and Lossen rearrangements.

Show answer
  1. In each reaction R migrates from carbon to an electron-deficient nitrogen as a leaving group (Br−, N2, OH−-derived) departs; the isocyanate is hydrolysed to the carbamic acid which loses CO2.

The isocyanate, R–N=C=O.

Practice 55 L1 Preparation

What reagent converts CH3CH2CH2Br to the amine with one more carbon? Name two other routes giving a 1° amine free of 2° and 3° amines.

Show answer
  1. All avoid the polyalkylation of direct ammonolysis.

KCN then LiAlH4. Other routes: Gabriel phthalimide synthesis; Hofmann bromamide (carbon-descending); reductive amination of an aldehyde with NH3.
