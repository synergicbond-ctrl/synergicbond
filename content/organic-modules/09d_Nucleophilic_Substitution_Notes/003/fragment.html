4. Carbocation rearrangements in substitution

Whenever a carbocation is an intermediate (or when the leaving group departs with concerted migration), the cation may rearrange by a 1,2-hydride or 1,2-alkyl (methyl) shift to a more stable cation before it is trapped. A shift is favoured when it converts a primary into a secondary or tertiary cation, or a secondary into a tertiary or resonance-stabilised cation. Products carrying the nucleophile on a different carbon from the one that held the leaving group are the signature of such a pathway, and are a diagnostic of SN1 character.

Reactant:

Reagent: Ethanol, heat (solvolysis; concerted methyl shift as bromide leaves)

Product:

Neopentyl bromide gives 2-ethoxy-2-methylbutane (via the tertiary 2-methylbut-2-yl cation), essentially none of the unrearranged neopentyl ethyl ether, together with some alkene.

Reactant:

Reagent: Hot water (SN1 with 1,2-hydride shift)

Product:

The secondary cation from 3-methylbutan-2-yl bromide takes a hydride from the neighbouring CH to give the tertiary cation; water then gives 2-methylbutan-2-ol as the substitution product (alkenes from E1 also form).

  • Hydride shift: secondary → tertiary cation.
  • Methyl shift: primary (β-quaternary) → tertiary cation, as in neopentyl systems.
  • Rearranged product ⇒ cationic intermediate or cation-like transition state; SN2 never rearranges the carbon skeleton.
Trap. Do not apply rearrangement to SN2: a concerted backside displacement has no cation to rearrange. A rearranged product therefore argues against SN2.
