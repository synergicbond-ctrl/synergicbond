Paragraph 1. An organic solid P (C8H9NO) is boiled with aqueous NaOH. A volatile oily liquid Q (C6H7N) distils over, and the residue contains the sodium salt of an acid R. Q gives a white precipitate (M = 330) with bromine water.

Practice 244 L3 Paragraph 1.1

Identify Q.

Show answer
  1. The precipitate of M = 330 is 2,4,6-tribromoaniline, so Q = C6H5NH2 (C6H7N ✓).

Aniline.

Practice 245 L3 Paragraph 1.2

Identify P and R.

acetanilide
Show answer
  1. Alkaline hydrolysis of the amide: CH3CONHC6H5 + NaOH → CH3COONa + C6H5NH2. The formula C8H9NO fits acetanilide.

P = acetanilide (N-phenylacetamide); R = acetic acid (isolated as sodium acetate).

Practice 246 L3 Paragraph 1.3

What is the major product when P is nitrated with HNO3/H2SO4 at 288 K and then hydrolysed?

Show answer
  1. The acetamido group is an o/p director, not protonated, and its bulk disfavours the ortho position.

p-Nitroaniline (via p-nitroacetanilide), with a little o-nitroaniline.

Paragraph 2. Aniline is diazotised at 273 K to give D. D is divided into three portions: (i) CuBr/HBr gives E, (ii) KI gives F, (iii) β-naphthol/NaOH gives G (a red-orange solid).

Practice 247 L3 Paragraph 2.1

Identify E and F.

Show answer
  1. (i) Sandmeyer bromination; (ii) iodide displaces N2 without catalyst.

E = bromobenzene; F = iodobenzene.

Practice 248 L3 Paragraph 2.2

Write the formula and the molar mass of G.

Show answer
  1. The azo coupling retains both N atoms: C6H5–N=N–C10H6OH.

1-(Phenylazo)naphthalen-2-ol, C16H12N2O, M = 248.3 g mol-1.

Practice 249 L3 Paragraph 2.3

Which of E, F, G still contains nitrogen? Why is G coloured while E and F are colourless?

Show answer

Only G. G has the extended conjugated chromophore Ar–N=N–Ar'(OH) (with the –OH auxochrome), whose π→π* and n→π* absorptions lie in the visible region; E and F absorb only in the UV.

Paragraph 3. The following table gives the basic strength of conjugate acids: (I) diethylamine pKa 11.0; (II) ammonia 9.25; (III) aniline 4.6; (IV) p-nitroaniline 1.0; (V) p-toluidine 5.1.

Practice 250 L3 Paragraph 3.1

Arrange I–V in increasing basic strength.

Show answer
  1. The larger the pKa of the conjugate acid, the stronger the base: 1.0 < 4.6 < 5.1 < 9.25 < 11.0.

IV < III < V < II < I.

Practice 251 L3 Paragraph 3.2

Calculate the pH of 0.10 M aqueous diethylamine (pKa of the conjugate acid 11.0, so pKb = 3.0).

Show answer
  1. Kb = 10−3.0 = 1.0 × 10−3. Solve x2/(0.10 − x) = 1.0 × 10−3: x = (−Kb + √(Kb2 + 4KbC))/2 = (−0.001 + 0.020025)/2 = 9.51 × 10−3 M.
  2. pOH = 2.02; pH = 14 − 2.02 = 11.98.

pH ≈ 12.0 (11.98).

Practice 252 L3 Paragraph 3.3

Which statement is incorrect? (a) IV is a weaker base than III because of −I and −M of NO2; (b) V is stronger than III because of +I of CH3; (c) I is stronger than II because of +I of two ethyl groups; (d) III is a stronger base than II because the phenyl group is electron donating.

Show answer
  1. The phenyl group (sp2 carbons, and conjugation of N lone pair with the ring) lowers the basicity; aniline is much weaker than ammonia.

(d).

Paragraph 4. Benzoyl chloride is treated with sodium azide to give an acyl azide W, which on heating in toluene loses N2 and gives X. X reacts with water to give Y (an amine) and CO2; with ethanol to give Z, a stable carbamate.

Practice 253 L3 Paragraph 4.1

Identify W and X.

benzoyl azide phenyl isocyanate
Show answer

W = benzoyl azide, C6H5CON3; X = phenyl isocyanate, C6H5N=C=O (Curtius rearrangement).

Practice 254 L3 Paragraph 4.2

Identify Y and Z.

aniline ethyl N-phenylcarbamate
Show answer
  1. Water adds to the isocyanate to give the unstable carbamic acid → aniline + CO2; ethanol adds to give the stable ester (urethane).

Y = aniline; Z = ethyl N-phenylcarbamate, C6H5NHCOOC2H5.

Practice 255 L3 Paragraph 4.3

Is the rearrangement W → X accompanied by retention or inversion if the migrating group is a chiral carbon? What are the driving forces?

Show answer

Retention. The loss of the very stable N2 molecule drives the reaction; the migrating group moves with its bonding electrons through a three-centre transition state without becoming free.

Paragraph 5. 1-Fluoro-2,4-dinitrobenzene (Sanger's reagent) reacts with methylamine at room temperature to give a yellow solid S; with 1-chloro-2,4-dinitrobenzene the reaction is somewhat slower; with 1-chloro-3-nitrobenzene there is no reaction under these conditions.

Practice 256 L3 Paragraph 5.1

Give the structure of S and the type of mechanism.

S
Show answer

S = N-methyl-2,4-dinitroaniline; nucleophilic aromatic substitution by addition–elimination (SNAr).

Practice 257 L3 Paragraph 5.2

Which step is rate-determining and why does fluorine give the faster reaction?

Show answer

Addition of the nucleophile to form the Meisenheimer complex. Fluorine's electronegativity increases the electrophilicity of the ipso carbon and stabilises the anionic intermediate; breaking C–F occurs after the rate-determining step.

Practice 258 L3 Paragraph 5.3

Explain why 1-chloro-3-nitrobenzene does not react.

Show answer

The negative charge in the Meisenheimer complex cannot be delocalised onto the meta nitro group (no resonance path); the intermediate is not stabilised.

Paragraph 6. Two isomeric compounds M and N have the formula C3H5N. M is hydrolysed by hot aqueous acid to propanoic acid. N has a very unpleasant smell and is hydrolysed by dilute acid to a base and formic acid. On strong heating N isomerises to M.

Practice 259 L3 Paragraph 6.1

Identify M and N.

M N
Show answer

M = propanenitrile (ethyl cyanide); N = ethyl isocyanide.

Practice 260 L3 Paragraph 6.2

Give the product of M with CH3MgBr followed by H3O+ and the product of N with H2/Ni.

Show answer
  1. Grignard adds once to the nitrile carbon; the resulting ketimine hydrolyses. Hydrogenation converts the isocyanide carbon into a methyl group on nitrogen.

M → butan-2-one (CH3CH2COCH3); N → N-methylethanamine (CH3CH2NHCH3).

Practice 261 L3 Paragraph 6.3

Give the two products of the acid hydrolysis of N and state the one test that proves the nitrogen was attached to the ethyl group.

Show answer

Ethylamine (as ethylammonium salt) and formic acid. The formation of a 1° amine whose carbon count equals that of the alkyl group (C2), together with HCOOH, shows that the isocyanide carbon, not the alkyl carbon, was bonded to N; with a nitrile the nitrogen would be lost as NH4+ and the acid would keep all three carbons.

Paragraph 7. A liquid mixture contains butan-1-amine (A), N-ethylethanamine (B) and N,N-dimethylethanamine (C). The mixture is shaken with excess benzenesulphonyl chloride and aqueous KOH, then the mixture is steam-distilled. The distillate (D) is separated, and the residue is filtered. The filtrate is acidified.

Practice 262 L3 Paragraph 7.1

Which compound is found in the steam distillate D, and which in the residue after filtration?

Show answer
  1. A's sulphonamide stays in the filtrate as the potassium salt; B's sulphonamide, N,N-diethylbenzenesulphonamide, is insoluble in alkali (an oil or low-melting solid that is collected by filtration or extraction); C does not react and distils.

D = C (the unreacted tertiary amine is volatile with steam). The insoluble material remaining = B's sulphonamide.

Practice 263 L3 Paragraph 7.2

What precipitates when the filtrate is acidified?

N-butylbenzenesulphonamide
Show answer

N-Butylbenzenesulphonamide, C4H9NHSO2C6H5 (from A).

Practice 264 L3 Paragraph 7.3

How would the free primary and secondary amines be recovered?

Show answer

Heat the sulphonamides with concentrated HCl or H2SO4 (hydrolysis) and then basify to liberate the amines. The 1° and 2° sulphonamides are hydrolysed slowly and under forcing conditions.

Paragraph 8 (sulpha drugs). Acetanilide is treated with chlorosulphonic acid (ClSO3H) to give 4-acetamidobenzenesulphonyl chloride (T). T is treated with ammonia to give U. Hydrolysis of U in dilute acid or alkali gives sulphanilamide (V).

Practice 265 L3 Paragraph 8.1

Write the structures of T, U and V.

T U V (sulphanilamide)
Show answer

T = p-CH3CONH–C6H4–SO2Cl; U = p-CH3CONH–C6H4–SO2NH2; V = p-H2N–C6H4–SO2NH2 (C6H8N2O2S, M = 172).

Practice 266 L3 Paragraph 8.2

Why is the amino group protected as the acetamide before chlorosulphonation?

Show answer

Free aniline would react with ClSO3H at nitrogen and be oxidised/protonated; and the sulphonyl chloride of free aniline would react with itself (the –NH2 of one molecule attacks the –SO2Cl of another). The acetamide is stable to the acid, directs para, and is removed at the end.

Practice 267 L3 Paragraph 8.3

In the hydrolysis of U, why is the sulphonamide group not hydrolysed?

Show answer

The amide C–N bond (acetamide) is hydrolysed under conditions in which the sulphonamide S–N bond is stable; sulphonamides are hydrolysed only under much more forcing conditions.
