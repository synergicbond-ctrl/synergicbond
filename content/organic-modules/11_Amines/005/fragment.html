5. Basicity — the central topic

5.1 Definitions and the scale

An amine behaves as a Brønsted base through its lone pair, and also as a Lewis base. In water

+ ⇌ in H₂O⟶ +
RNH₂ + H₂O ⇌ RNH₃⁺ + OH⁻ ; K_b = [RNH₃⁺][OH⁻]/[RNH₂]
  • Kb and pKb = −log Kb: larger Kb, smaller pKb = stronger base.
  • Equivalent and more useful: the pKa of the conjugate acid RNH3+: larger pKa = stronger base. pKa + pKb = 14 (at 298 K).
  • Basicity = tendency to accept a proton. Nucleophilicity = tendency to attack a carbon. They usually go together but steric hindrance reduces nucleophilicity more than basicity.

Master rule: an amine is a stronger base the more available and electron-rich its lone pair is, and the more stable its conjugate ammonium ion is (stabilised by +I groups and by solvation through hydrogen bonding with water). Four factors operate, and in questions you must decide which dominates:

  • Inductive effect (+I / −I) of groups on N.
  • Resonance (+M / −M) involving the lone pair.
  • Solvation of the ammonium ion in water (number of N–H bonds that can hydrogen-bond).
  • Steric hindrance to solvation and, with large groups, to protonation (and to planarity, in "steric inhibition of resonance").
Shortcut Quick recipe: aliphatic amine > ammonia > aromatic amine > amide; then rank within the family using EDG/EWG and, for aliphatic 1°/2°/3° comparisons in water, the solvation argument.

5.3 Gas phase versus aqueous solution

In the gas phase there is no solvent, and only the inductive effect (and polarisability) acts: R3N > R2NH > RNH2 > NH3. Proton affinities (kJ mol-1): NH3 ≈ 854, CH3NH2 ≈ 899, (CH3)2NH ≈ 930, (CH3)3N ≈ 949. In water the order becomes irregular because the number of N–H bonds in the ammonium ion matters:

  • RNH3+ has three N–H bonds → three H-bonds to water → greatest solvation stabilisation.
  • R2NH2+: two N–H → intermediate; R3NH+: one N–H → least solvation.
  • Larger R also makes solvation harder (steric shielding). For methyl groups the 3° falls below 1° and 2°; for ethyl groups (bigger +I but greater steric hindrance to solvation) the 3° is below 2° but above 1°.

So the observed order is a compromise between +I, solvation and steric hindrance. For JEE Advanced, memorise the experimentally fixed orders above; reason with the three factors when the groups are unfamiliar.

Trap A common mistake is to give "3° > 2° > 1°" for aqueous solutions. In water the 2° amine is the strongest in both the methyl and ethyl series. In the gas phase, or in a non-polar/aprotic solvent such as chlorobenzene, 3° > 2° > 1° > NH3.

5.4 Aromatic amines: why aniline is a much weaker base

In aniline the nitrogen lone pair is delocalised into the ring (+M effect of –NH2). Resonance structures place negative charge on the ortho and para carbons and positive charge on N:

resonance⟶
The lone pair is delocalised: partial double bond C=N⁺ and negative charge at ortho and para carbons

When aniline is protonated, the lone pair is used to form the N–H bond and can no longer take part in resonance, so anilinium has no resonance stabilisation – only the Kekulé structures of the benzene ring. Therefore:

  • Aniline (stabilised by 5 resonance forms) is more stable relative to its ion → protonation unfavourable → pKb 9.38, i.e. Kb about 105 times smaller than for methylamine.
  • The sp2 carbon attached to N is also more electronegative than sp3 C (−I of the phenyl group).
Key idea Aniline: delocalised lone pair + (−I of the sp2 carbon) + no resonance in anilinium → pKb 9.4. Cyclohexylamine (no ring conjugation) has pKb ≈ 3.3, behaving as an ordinary alkylamine.
5.5 Substituted anilines

EDG (alkyl, –OCH3, –NH2) at para push electron density to N and increase basicity; EWG (–NO2, –CN, –X, –COR) at para or meta decrease it. Para –NO2 is far more effective than meta because it withdraws through resonance as well as induction:

Points to read from the table (these are the typical exam traps):

  • p-Toluidine > aniline (CH3: +I and hyperconjugation).
  • p-Anisidine > aniline > m-anisidine. At para, OCH3 is +M (dominant) so the base is strengthened; at meta, OCH3 can only exert −I (it is not in conjugation with the NH2 position) so the base is weakened. So the same substituent can raise or lower basicity depending on position.
  • m-Nitroaniline > p-nitroaniline (m: −I only; p: −I and −M). Do not use m > p blindly for all EWG; e.g. halogens are similar in both positions.
  • Ortho effect: almost any ortho substituent, even an electron donor such as CH3, makes aniline a weaker base than the para (and often than parent aniline). Reasons: steric hindrance to solvation/protonation of –NH3+ (which is bulkier than –NH2) and, with H-bond donors/acceptors, intramolecular effects. o-Nitroaniline is the weakest of the three nitroanilines (pKa ≈ −0.3).
  • Steric inhibition of resonance (SIR) in the base. When two ortho groups force the NR2 group out of the plane of the ring (e.g. N,N-dimethyl-o-toluidine vs N,N-dimethylaniline), the lone pair cannot conjugate with the ring, so the base is stronger than the planar analogue. The opposite effect in the nitro group: a nitro group twisted out of the plane by two ortho groups cannot withdraw by −M, so it affects the basicity less.

5.6 Effect of N-substitution on aniline

Aniline (9.38) < N-methylaniline (9.30) < N,N-dimethylaniline (8.92) in pKb (i.e. DMA is the strongest base of the three): +I of CH3 wins. Diphenylamine (pKb 13.1) and triphenylamine (practically non-basic) fall far lower: each phenyl takes part in delocalising the lone pair. Order of basic strength: (CH3)2NH > C6H5CH2NH2 > NH3 > C6H5NH2 > (C6H5)2NH > (C6H5)3N. Benzylamine (pKb 4.70) is almost exactly as basic as NH3 (4.75): the sp2 phenyl ring is electron-withdrawing by −I, cancelling the +I of the CH2. When numbers are given in an exam, use them rather than intuition.

Heterocyclic and special bases
  • Pyrrole vs pyridine. In pyrrole the N lone pair is part of the 6π aromatic system; protonation would destroy aromaticity, so pyrrole is practically non-basic and in fact is a weak acid (N–H, pKa ≈ 17.5). In pyridine the lone pair lies in the plane of the ring in an sp2 orbital, outside the π system, and is available.
  • Piperidine > pyridine because sp3 N holds its lone pair less tightly than sp2 N.
  • Imidazole has both a pyrrole-type N–H (not basic) and a pyridine-type N (basic site).
  • Guanidine is protonated on the =NH nitrogen; the guanidinium ion is stabilised by three equivalent resonance structures.
  • Amide: protonates at O, because the cation C(OH)=NH2+ is resonance-stabilised.
  • Aniline vs pyridine vs NH3: NH3 (9.25) > pyridine (5.2) > aniline (4.6).

5.8 Two more cases examiners like
  • Quinuclidine (1-azabicyclo[2.2.2]octane), pKa 11.0, vs triethylamine (10.8). In the bicycle the three alkyl arms are tied back, so the lone pair is not shielded: more available. This shows the importance of steric hindrance to solvation and attack.
  • Proton sponge (1,8-bis(dimethylamino)naphthalene), pKa 12.1. Strongly crowded ground state with lone-pair repulsion relieved on protonation; protonated form has an intramolecular N–H···N bond. Very strong base but very poor nucleophile.

Example 4 Main

Order of basicity (aqueous)

Arrange in decreasing order of basic strength in aqueous solution: (CH3)3N, CH3NH2, (CH3)2NH, NH3, C6H5NH2.

Show solution
  1. Alkylamines > NH3 > aniline.
  2. Within the methylamines: (CH3)2NH (pKb 3.27) > CH3NH2 (3.38) > (CH3)3N (4.22) – a compromise between +I and solvation of the ammonium ion.
  3. Final order: (CH3)2NH > CH3NH2 > (CH3)3N > NH3 > C6H5NH2.
Example 5 Main

Gas-phase order

Arrange the same set in decreasing basic strength in the gas phase.

Show solution
  1. No solvation: only +I/polarisability. Each added methyl group increases the proton affinity.
  2. (CH3)3N > (CH3)2NH > CH3NH2 > NH3 for the alkylamines.
  3. Aniline is a special case: its gas-phase proton affinity (≈ 882 kJ mol-1) is actually above NH3 (≈ 854) because the phenyl group stabilises the cation by polarisation. The weak basicity of aniline is therefore mainly a solution phenomenon (hydration of anilinium is poor). Exam questions on the gas phase are restricted to alkylamines.
Example 6 Adv

Substituted anilines

Arrange in decreasing basic strength: aniline, p-nitroaniline, p-toluidine, p-chloroaniline, m-nitroaniline.

Show solution
  1. p-Toluidine (EDG) is the strongest. Aniline is next.
  2. Among EWG: −Cl (−I > +M) weakens moderately; m-NO2 (−I) weakens more; p-NO2 (−I, −M) weakens most.
  3. p-Toluidine > aniline > p-chloroaniline > m-nitroaniline > p-nitroaniline (pKa 5.1 > 4.6 > 4.0 > 2.5 > 1.0).
Example 7 Adv

Position matters

Which is the stronger base, p-anisidine or m-anisidine? Compare both with aniline.

Show solution
  1. At para, OCH3 donates by +M (stronger than its −I) so the NH2 is enriched. At meta, resonance of OCH3 cannot reach the carbon bearing NH2 (the lone-pair donation goes to positions ortho and para to OCH3, i.e. the carbons 2, 4, 6 relative to OCH3); only −I operates.
  2. p-Anisidine > aniline > m-anisidine (5.3 > 4.6 > 4.2).
Example 8 Adv

Steric inhibition

Explain why N,N-dimethyl-o-toluidine (2-methyl-N,N-dimethylaniline) is a stronger base than N,N-dimethylaniline, whereas the ortho effect usually weakens bases.

Show solution
  1. The ortho CH3 pushes the bulky –N(CH3)2 out of the plane of the ring. The lone pair is no longer parallel to the π orbitals, so resonance (the main reason for the low basicity of arylamines) is switched off — the lone pair is more available.
  2. The usual ortho effect (weaker base) comes from hindrance to solvating –NH3+; in N,N-dimethylaniline the ion is –NH(CH3)2+, but the loss of resonance in the free base outweighs this. Net result: the o-methyl compound is the stronger base.
Example 9 Adv

Pyrrole and pyridine

Rank in decreasing basic strength: pyridine, pyrrole, piperidine, aniline. Justify.

Show solution
  1. Piperidine (sp3 N, 11.1) > pyridine (sp2 N, 5.2) > aniline (4.6) > pyrrole (−3.8).
  2. Piperidine is an alkylamine; pyridine's lone pair has 33 % s-character; aniline's is delocalised into the ring; pyrrole's lone pair is part of the aromatic sextet and cannot be protonated without losing aromaticity.

Summary sheet — basicity

Key idea Order of preference when ranking bases: (1) Is the lone pair delocalised? (amide, pyrrole, aniline ↓ strongly). (2) sp3 > sp2 > sp. (3) EDG raise, EWG lower. (4) In water: 2° ≥ 3° ≈ 1° > NH3 for alkyl; in gas phase 3° > 2° > 1°. (5) Ortho substituent weakens aniline; SIR in the base can make it stronger. (6) pKb small = strong base.
