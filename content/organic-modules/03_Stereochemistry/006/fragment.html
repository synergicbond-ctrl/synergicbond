3. Counting structural isomers: a method that does not miss anything

Random listing loses isomers. Use a ladder:

  • Step 1: DBE = (2C + 2 + N − H − X)/2 tells you how many rings + π-bonds there are.
  • Step 2: choose the skeleton (carbon framework) and list all distinct skeletons first.
  • Step 3: put the functional group / substituent on every non-equivalent position of each skeleton (symmetry check).
  • Step 4: only now consider functional-group isomers of a different class, then ring-containing isomers.
  • Step 5: apply the question's filter (acyclic only? include stereoisomers? include enols?).
Formula DBE = (2C + 2 + N − H − X)/2. C4H10O: 0. C4H8O: 1. C6H6: 4. C8H10: 4. C3H6O2: 1.

3.1 Monosubstituted alkanes (alkyl halides, alcohols, amines)

The number of monosubstituted derivatives of an alkane equals the number of distinct alkyl groups (distinct kinds of hydrogen). Propane has 2 kinds of H → 2 chloropropanes; butane 2 kinds, 2-methylpropane 2 kinds → 4 butyl chlorides; the three pentanes have 3 + 4 + 1 = 8 kinds of H → 8 chloropentanes (structural), of which some are chiral.

3.3 Aromatic isomers

3.4 Disubstituted benzenes and the "count by symmetry" trick

For a benzene ring: two identical substituents → 3 isomers (o, m, p); two different → 3 as well; three identical substituents → 3 (1,2,3; 1,2,4; 1,3,5); three substituents of which two are identical (X, X, Y) → 6; three all different (X, Y, Z) → 10; four identical → 3; tetrasubstituted X2Y2 → 6.

Show solution
