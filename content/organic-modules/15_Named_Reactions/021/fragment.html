18. Dieckmann condensation (intramolecular Claisen)

A diester with NaOEt gives a cyclic β-keto ester. Only 1,6- and 1,7-diesters work, because they form five- and six-membered rings: diethyl adipate (hexanedioate, 1,6) → ethyl 2-oxocyclopentanecarboxylate; diethyl pimelate (heptanedioate, 1,7) → ethyl 2-oxocyclohexanecarboxylate.

1. NaOEt 2. H⁺⟶ +
Diethyl adipate → 2-ethoxycarbonylcyclopentanone (C-2 attacks the C-6 ester carbonyl, so the ring is C-2 … C-6, five atoms)
Shortcut Count the ring: the enolate carbon (α to one ester) attacks the carbonyl of the other ester. Number the diester carbons from one ester carbon as 1: ring atoms are C-2 … C-n, where C-n is the other ester carbonyl. For adipate (n = 6) the ring has C-2, 3, 4, 5, 6 = 5 atoms. Then hydrolysis and decarboxylation of the β-keto ester gives the cycloalkanone (cyclopentanone from adipate).
Example 26 Main

Dieckmann to ketone

Show how cyclopentanone is prepared from diethyl adipate, and which ketone from diethyl pimelate.

Show solution
  1. Diethyl adipate → (1) NaOEt (Dieckmann) → ethyl 2-oxocyclopentanecarboxylate → (2) aq. H2SO4, Δ (hydrolysis then β-keto acid decarboxylation) → cyclopentanone (+ CO2 + EtOH).
  2. Diethyl pimelate → the same sequence → cyclohexanone. (Alternatively, strong heating of barium adipate gives cyclopentanone and of barium pimelate gives cyclohexanone.)
