4.1 The seniority table

Definition. The principal group of a compound is the functional group highest in the seniority table below. Only the principal group is expressed as the suffix of the name; every other group is expressed as a prefix.

IMP The order is acid > anhydride > ester > acid halide > amide > nitrile > aldehyde > ketone > alcohol > thiol > amine > imine > C=C > C≡C, with ethers, halides, nitro compounds and alkyl groups as prefixes only. Carboxylate salts rank just above anhydrides (§9.1).
Why It Happens The order is a convention, but it is not random: groups with carbon bonded to three heteroatoms (acids, esters, amides, nitriles) come first, then carbonyl compounds, then single-bonded O, S and N groups. The sequence therefore tends to follow how many heteroatom bonds the carbon carries, which is why acids outrank aldehydes and alcohols outrank amines.
Exception & Its Reason The tendency is not exact. In a ketone the carbonyl carbon has a higher formal oxidation number (+2) than in an aldehyde (+1), yet the aldehyde is senior. Seniority is fixed by the agreed table, not derived from oxidation state, so the table must be used directly.
Exam Trap In numbering, unsaturation (-ene, -yne) ranks below the principal group but above all prefixes. That is why CH₂=CH–CH(OH)–CH₃ is but-3-en-2-ol: the OH takes the lowest locant (2), and the double bond then has the locant its position dictates (3).
