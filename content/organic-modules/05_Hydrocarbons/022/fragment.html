21. Addition of halogens and related reagents

21.1 Bromination and chlorination: bridged halonium ion

Alkene + Br2 (or Cl2) in an inert solvent (CCl4, CH2Cl2) → vicinal dihalide. The polarisable Br2 approaches the π cloud; Br–Br is polarised (Brδ+–Brδ−); a bromonium ion (three-membered ring) forms with loss of Br−. The bromide then attacks a carbon of the ring from the back side (SN2-like) → anti addition.

+ CCl₄⟶
Ethene + bromine (reddish-brown colour disappears: test for unsaturation)

Evidence for the bridged ion: (i) overall anti addition (cyclohexene → trans-1,2-dibromocyclohexane only); (ii) no rearrangement in Br2 additions even for 3,3-dimethylbut-1-ene; (iii) in the presence of NaCl or in water, products from nucleophile attack on the bromonium ion (bromochloride, bromohydrin) form.

Key idea Anti addition gives, from a cis alkene: (for 2,3-disubstituted alkene RCH=CHR) the racemic (threo/d,l) dibromide; from a trans alkene: the meso dibromide (when both carbons carry identical substituent sets).
Example 35 Advanced

Stereochemistry counting

How many stereoisomeric products result when each of the following reacts with Br2/CCl4: (a) cis-but-2-ene (b) trans-but-2-ene (c) cyclopentene (d) 1,2-dimethylcyclohexene (e) (Z)-1,2-diphenylethene? Say if each product is optically active.

Show solution
  1. (a) 2 (R,R and S,S, formed 1:1 → racemic, optically inactive overall).
  2. (b) 1 (meso, optically inactive).
  3. (c) trans-1,2-dibromocyclopentane: (1R,2R) + (1S,2S): racemate (2 stereoisomers).
  4. (d) anti addition to the tetrasubstituted alkene gives trans-1,2-dibromo-1,2-dimethylcyclohexane: 2 enantiomers (racemic).
  5. (e) cis-stilbene → racemic (1R,2R)/(1S,2S)-1,2-dibromo-1,2-diphenylethane; trans-stilbene → meso.

21.2 What decides regiochemistry and which carbon is attacked?

In an unsymmetrical alkene the bromonium ion is unsymmetrical: the more substituted carbon bears more positive charge and a longer C–Br bond, so the nucleophile attacks there. With Br− as the nucleophile, both carbons give the same product (a vicinal dibromide), so regiochemistry is invisible. It becomes visible when the nucleophile is different from Br−:

Example 36 Advanced

Halohydrin products

Predict the major product, with stereochemistry where relevant: (a) styrene + Br2/H2O (b) 1-methylcyclohexene + Br2/H2O (c) cyclopentene + NBS/H2O.

Show solution
  1. (a) Br+ at the terminal CH2; water at the benzylic carbon: PhCH(OH)CH2Br (2-bromo-1-phenylethanol).
  2. (b) Br at C-2 (less substituted), OH at C-1 (tertiary), anti: trans-2-bromo-1-methylcyclohexan-1-ol (Br and OH anti). Racemic.
  3. (c) trans-2-bromocyclopentan-1-ol (racemic).

21.3 Chlorination at high temperature and NBS: see section 25 (allylic substitution)

Chlorine at 500 °C (or low concentration of Br2) substitutes the allylic hydrogen instead of adding (radical). Propene + Cl2 at 500 °C gives allyl chloride (industrial route to glycerol and epichlorohydrin). At room temperature in CCl4 propene + Cl2 gives the 1,2-dichloropropane by electrophilic addition.

Example 37 Advanced

Test for unsaturation

Which compounds decolourise (a) Br2/CCl4 (b) cold dilute alkaline KMnO4 (Baeyer's reagent)? Cyclohexene, cyclohexane, ethyne, benzene, cyclopropane, toluene.

Show solution
  1. (a) Cyclohexene and ethyne decolourise Br2 in the dark. Cyclohexane, benzene and toluene do not (substitution needs light or FeBr3). Cyclopropane reacts only slowly (ring opening); at JEE level it is treated as not decolourising Br2/CCl4.
  2. (b) Cyclohexene and ethyne decolourise cold alkaline KMnO4 (purple → brown MnO2); the others do not.
