22.1 Cleavage by hydrogen halides (HI > HBr > HCl)

Ethers are cleaved by concentrated, hot HI or HBr (HCl is too weak; the halide is too poor a nucleophile). Mechanism: (1) protonation of the ether oxygen gives a dialkyloxonium ion, which turns the poor alkoxide leaving group into an alcohol (a good leaving group); (2) the halide attacks the carbon that is more susceptible: either SN2 at the less hindered carbon or SN1 where a stable carbocation (3°, benzylic, allylic) can form.

Key idea Aryl alkyl ethers always cleave to a phenol and an alkyl halide, never to an aryl halide plus alcohol, because the Ar–O bond (sp2 carbon, partial double bond) cannot be broken by SN1/SN2; the oxygen is protonated and the halide attacks the alkyl carbon. Diaryl ethers are not cleaved by HX.

Trap With cold dilute acid a tert-butyl ether cleaves by E1/SN1 even though HX is not a strong nucleophile (the tert-butyl cation is formed); with anhydrous CF3COOH it gives isobutene. Primary alkyl ethers need hot concentrated HI. If a question says “cold HI” and a tert-alkyl ether, expect SN1 at the tertiary carbon; if “hot HI” with a primary/secondary ether, SN2 at the less substituted carbon.
Example 59 L1 Foundation

Cleavage products

Give the products of (a) CH3OCH2CH2CH3 + HI (1 mol); (b) C6H5OC2H5 + HBr (hot); (c) (CH3)3COC2H5 + HI (cold); (d) C6H5OC6H5 + HI.

Show solution
  1. (a) SN2 at the less hindered CH3: CH3I + CH3CH2CH2OH.
  2. (b) C6H5OH + C2H5Br.
  3. (c) SN1 at the tertiary carbon: (CH3)3CI + C2H5OH.
  4. (d) No reaction.
Example 60 L2 Main

Retention or inversion?

(S)-2-Methoxybutane is heated with one equivalent of HI. Give the organic products and the configuration of the chiral product.

Show solution
  1. The oxonium ion is attacked at the less hindered methyl carbon (SN2): CH3I + butan-2-ol.
  2. The C–O bond at the stereocentre is not broken, so the configuration is retained spatially; CIP priorities at the stereocentre (O > C2H5 > CH3 > H) are unchanged, so the product is (S)-butan-2-ol.
Example 61 L2 Main

Zeisel determination

0.424 g of a compound (M = 212 g mol−1, trimethoxybenzoic acid) is heated with excess HI; the CH3I formed is passed into alcoholic AgNO3 to give 1.409 g of AgI (M = 234.8 g mol−1). How many OCH3 groups are present per molecule?

Show solution
  1. Moles of compound = 0.424/212 = 0.00200 mol. Moles of AgI = 1.409/234.8 = 0.00600 mol = moles of CH3I = moles of OCH3 groups.
  2. Groups per molecule = 0.00600/0.00200 = 3.
  3. Zeisel’s method: R–OCH3 + HI → CH3I + ROH; CH3I + AgNO3 (alcoholic) → AgI↓ + CH3NO3/CH3OEt; the AgI is weighed. (Ethoxy groups can be estimated similarly as C2H5I.)
