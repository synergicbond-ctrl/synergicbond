13. Chemoselectivity: which group reduces first

In a molecule with several reducible groups, the reagent determines the outcome. Always identify the groups, then pick the reagent that reduces only the target.

  • Ketone in presence of ester, nitrile or nitro: NaBH4.
  • Ester in presence of ketone: protect the ketone as an acetal, reduce with LiAlH4, then deprotect with aqueous acid.
  • Nitro group in presence of ketone: SnCl2/HCl in ethanol, or Fe/HCl.
  • C=O of an enone without touching C=C: NaBH4/CeCl3 or MPV.
  • C=C of an enone without touching C=O: H2 (1 equiv), Pd/C or Wilkinson catalyst.
  • Less hindered C=C in presence of a tetrasubstituted one: H2 (1 equiv), Pd/C.
  • Alkyne to alkene in presence of a terminal alkene: Lindlar H2 (1 equiv).

Reactant:

Reagent: NaBH4, EtOH

Product:

Ketone reduced, nitro retained.

Reactant:

Reagent: SnCl2, HCl, EtOH

Product:

Nitro reduced, ketone retained.

  1. Protect: CH3COCH2CH2CO2Me + HOCH2CH2OH, H+ → ketal ester.
  2. Reduce: LiAlH4 converts the ester into CH2OH; the ketal is stable to hydride.
  3. Deprotect: H3O+ regenerates the ketone, giving CH3COCH2CH2CH2OH.
Trap. LiAlH4 and H2/Pd–C are both able to reduce several groups at once. An answer that retains a group should first check whether it appears in the vulnerable list of the proposed reagent. Aryl nitro groups with LiAlH4 give azo compounds rather than anilines.
