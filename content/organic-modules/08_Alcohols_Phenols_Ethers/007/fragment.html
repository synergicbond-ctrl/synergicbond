3.1 From alkenes

(a) Acid-catalysed hydration (H3O+, dilute H2SO4 or H3PO4). Markovnikov addition of water. The mechanism has three steps: (1) the alkene π bond protonates to give the more stable carbocation (rate-determining step); (2) water attacks the cation; (3) the oxonium ion loses H+. Because a carbocation is formed, rearrangement is possible, the reaction is reversible, and there is no stereochemical control (racemic product if a stereocentre results).

H₃O⁺⟶
Isobutene → 2-methylpropan-2-ol (3° cation, Markovnikov)
H₃O⁺ (dil. H₂SO₄)⟶
3,3-Dimethylbut-1-ene gives 2,3-dimethylbutan-2-ol: a methyl shift converts the 2° cation to the 3° cation before water attacks

Trap Acid-catalysed hydration is reversible: the same conditions (low temperature, excess water) hydrate an alkene; (high temperature, conc. acid, little water) dehydrate an alcohol. Equilibrium position is controlled by temperature and water concentration. Ethene and propene are hydrated industrially at about 570 K and 70 atm over phosphoric acid on silica, because the free-energy change is small.
(b) Oxymercuration–demercuration (Hg(OAc)2, H2O/THF; then NaBH4, NaOH). Gives the Markovnikov alcohol without rearrangement. The mercuric ion adds to the alkene forming a cyclic mercurinium ion (bridged, like a bromonium ion), a carbocation is never formed free; water attacks the more substituted carbon from the side opposite the bridging Hg (anti addition of Hg and OH). NaBH4 then replaces Hg by H by a radical process, which is not stereospecific.
1. Hg(OAc)₂, H₂O/THF 2. NaBH₄, NaOH⟶
Same alkene, no rearrangement: 3,3-dimethylbutan-2-ol
1. Hg(OAc)₂, H₂O 2. NaBH₄⟶
1-Methylcyclohexene → 1-methylcyclohexan-1-ol (Markovnikov)

(c) Hydroboration–oxidation (BH3·THF or B2H6; then H2O2, NaOH). Gives the anti-Markovnikov alcohol with syn addition of H and OH. Boron (the electrophile) adds to the less hindered, less substituted carbon; hydrogen (as hydride) goes to the more substituted carbon in a concerted four-centre transition state. Oxidation by HOO− replaces C–B by C–OH with retention of configuration. No carbocation, so no rearrangement.

1. BH₃·THF 2. H₂O₂, NaOH⟶
Propene → propan-1-ol
1. BH₃·THF 2. H₂O₂, NaOH⟶
3,3-Dimethylbut-1-ene → 3,3-dimethylbutan-1-ol: anti-Markovnikov, no rearrangement
1. BH₃·THF 2. H₂O₂, NaOH⟶
1-Methylcyclohexene → trans-2-methylcyclohexanol (racemic): H and OH add syn, so the methyl ends up trans to OH

(d) Other alkene routes. Epoxidation followed by hydride opening (LiAlH4 attacks the less hindered carbon, giving the more substituted alcohol, Markovnikov-type); hydroboration of alkynes (with disiamylborane) gives aldehydes, which can be reduced; ozonolysis with reductive work-up using NaBH4 gives alcohols.

Example 5 L2 Main

Choose the method

How would you make (a) 3-methylbutan-2-ol from 3-methylbut-1-ene without rearrangement, (b) 3-methylbutan-1-ol from the same alkene?

Show solution
  1. The alkene is (CH3)2CH–CH=CH2. Direct acid hydration would give the secondary cation (CH3)2CH–CH+–CH3 which undergoes a hydride shift to the tertiary cation, finally 2-methylbutan-2-ol. Avoid it.
  2. (a) Hg(OAc)2/H2O then NaBH4: Markovnikov OH at C-2, no cation, so 3-methylbutan-2-ol.
  3. (b) BH3·THF, then H2O2/NaOH: anti-Markovnikov OH on the terminal carbon: 3-methylbutan-1-ol.
Example 6 L2 Main

Stereochemistry of hydroboration

1-Methylcyclopentene undergoes hydroboration–oxidation. Give the name and say how many stereoisomers form.

Show solution
  1. Boron attaches to C-2 (the CH= carbon, less substituted); hydride goes to C-1 (bearing CH3); both on the same face of the ring.
  2. The CH3 at C-1 is therefore pushed to the opposite face, trans to the OH at C-2. Product: trans-2-methylcyclopentan-1-ol.
  3. The alkene is planar and achiral, and boron can approach either face equally, giving a racemic mixture of (1R,2R) and (1S,2S) enantiomers; the cis diastereomer is not formed.
