1.5 Mutarotation

A freshly dissolved crystal of α-D-glucose ([α]D = +112°) shows a falling rotation; the β-form ([α]D = +18.7°) shows a rising rotation; both converge to +52.7°. This is mutarotation: the rings open through the free aldehyde (acid–base catalysed, fast with both acid and base) and reclose to give an equilibrium mixture.

Formula [α]eq = xα[α]α + xβ[α]β, xα + xβ = 1 (ignoring furanose and open chain)
Mutarotation is not a racemisation: the C-2 to C-5 centres never change. It proves that the two forms are diastereomers (anomers) with different rotations, not enantiomers.
Shortcut The change of rotation follows first-order kinetics: [α]t − [α]∞ = ([α]0 − [α]∞) e−kt, where k is the sum of forward and backward rate constants, k = kf + kb.
Example 8 Adv

Mutarotation percentages

α-D-Glucose has [α] = +112°, β-D-glucose +18.7°; the equilibrium value is +52.7°. Find the composition (ignore furanose and open chain). Then predict the rotation of a solution that is 50 % α and 50 % β.

Show solution
  1. x(112) + (1 − x)(18.7) = 52.7 → 93.3x = 34.0 → x = 0.364.
  2. 36.4 % α, 63.6 % β.
  3. For 50:50: 0.5(112) + 0.5(18.7) = +65.35°.
Example 9 Adv

Equilibrium constant

Using the data above, find K = [β]/[α] and ΔG° at 298 K.

Show solution
  1. K = 63.6/36.4 = 1.75.
  2. ΔG° = −RT ln K = −8.314 × 298 × ln 1.75 = −8.314 × 298 × 0.560 = −1.39 kJ mol−1 (β favoured).
Example 10 Adv

Kinetics of mutarotation

Pure α-D-glucose is dissolved and the optical rotation is followed. The rotation falls from +112° to +82.35° in 10 minutes. The final value is +52.7°. Find the rate constant and the time for the rotation to reach +60°.

Show solution
  1. [α]0 − [α]∞ = 112 − 52.7 = 59.3°.
  2. At 10 min: 82.35 − 52.7 = 29.65 = 59.3 e−10k → e−10k = 0.5 → k = 0.0693 min−1 (half-life 10 min).
  3. For +60°: 60 − 52.7 = 7.3 = 59.3 e−kt → t = ln(59.3/7.3)/0.0693 = 2.095/0.0693 = 30.2 min.
Example 11 Main

Which conformer?

In β-D-glucopyranose, how many substituents are axial and how many equatorial in the stable chair? What about α-D-galactopyranose?

Show solution
  1. β-D-glucopyranose: 0 axial, 5 equatorial (four OH + CH2OH).
  2. α-D-Galactopyranose: C-1 OH (α) axial and C-4 OH axial: 2 axial, 3 equatorial.
Example 12 Adv

Trans or cis in Haworth

In α-D-glucopyranose, which pairs of adjacent substituents are cis in the Haworth projection?

Show solution
  1. Haworth directions (D-glucose): C-1 OH down (α), C-2 OH down, C-3 OH up, C-4 OH down, C-5 CH2OH up.
  2. Adjacent cis pairs: C-1/C-2 (both down) and no others; C-2/C-3 trans, C-3/C-4 trans, C-4/C-5 trans.
  3. Only C-1/C-2 is cis. This is why α-glucose is the diol that forms boronate/acetonide derivatives at C-1,2 (cis diol) readily, while β-glucose with trans C-1,2 does not.
