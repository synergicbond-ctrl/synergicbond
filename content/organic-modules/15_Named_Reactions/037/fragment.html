33. Azo coupling

Reagents: ArN2+ (a weak electrophile) + an electron-rich arene: phenol (weakly alkaline, pH 9–10, so that the phenoxide forms but the diazonium is not converted to a diazotate) or an aromatic amine (weakly acidic, pH 4–5, so that the free amine is available and diazonium is intact). Coupling is at the para position of the activating group, at ortho only if para is blocked. Product: azo compound Ar–N=N–Ar′, brightly coloured because of extended conjugation (the basis of azo dyes and indicators: methyl orange, congo red).

+ NaOH, 273–278 K⟶
Phenol → 4-hydroxyazobenzene (p-hydroxyazobenzene, orange)
+ pH 4–5⟶
N,N-Dimethylaniline → 4-(dimethylamino)azobenzene (yellow, "butter yellow")
Trap Primary aromatic amines are tested by diazotisation then coupling with 2-naphthol in NaOH, giving a scarlet-red azo dye (the dye test). Aliphatic primary amines give no dye. pH matters: in strongly acidic solution phenols do not couple (no phenoxide), in strongly alkaline solution the diazonium becomes unreactive diazotate Ar–N=N–O−.
