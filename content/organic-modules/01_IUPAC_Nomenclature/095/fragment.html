11G · Molecular-orbital lens and bonding insights

MOT Lens Naming is a convention and needs no orbital picture. The orbital picture becomes useful in the two places where geometry matters: the planarity of an sp² carbon, which keeps a double bond rigid and makes E/Z isomerism possible, and the linear geometry of an sp carbon, which fixes the shape of an alkyne. A molecular-orbital description of these geometries is not needed to name a compound. (§3.1, §8.3)
Bonding Insight Hybridisation sets the shape of each carbon, and that shape is what the names describe: sp³ carbon gives a tetrahedral centre (stereocentres, alkyl branches), sp² gives the planar alkene and aromatic ring, and sp gives the linear alkyne and nitrile. (§1.5, §3.1)
Bonding Insight A double bond counts once for the sigma framework and once for the pi overlap. The pi overlap is why rotation is restricted, and it is the reason E/Z descriptors exist for alkenes but not for alkanes. (§8.3)
