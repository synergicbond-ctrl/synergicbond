8. Other tests, summary and exam-style cases

Beilstein's test (halogens). A clean copper wire loop is heated in a Bunsen flame until it no longer colours the flame, dipped in the compound and heated again: a green (blue-green) flame is produced by volatile copper(II) halides. It indicates a halogen (not F), but gives false positives with some N-containing compounds (urea, thiourea, salicylic acid, some pyridine derivatives) that form volatile copper cyanide and similar species. It is confirmed by Lassaigne's test. Soda-lime test for N in amides and ammonium salts. Heating a primary amide or an ammonium salt with soda lime (CaO + NaOH) liberates NH3, which turns red litmus blue and gives white fumes with HCl.

Example 12 Adv

Match the observation with the compound

Compounds: (1) aniline, (2) chlorobenzene, (3) thiourea, (4) iodoform, (5) benzenesulphonic acid, (6) hydrazine hydrate.

Observations in Lassaigne's extract: (P) Prussian blue only; (Q) yellow, insoluble-in-ammonia precipitate with AgNO3 (after HNO3 boil); (R) black ppt with lead acetate only; (S) blood red with FeCl3 (insufficient Na); (T) white ppt soluble in ammonia; (U) nothing.

Show solution
  1. aniline C6H5NH2 (C, N): P. Chlorobenzene (C, Cl): T. Thiourea (NH2)2C=S has both N and S; with insufficient sodium: S (with excess sodium it would give P and R as well). Iodoform CHI3: Q. Benzenesulphonic acid (C, S, no N): R. Hydrazine N2H4·H2O has no carbon, so no CN−: U.
Example 13 Main

Why boil with nitric acid?

In testing a compound containing nitrogen, sulphur and chlorine, what would be observed on adding AgNO3 to the sodium fusion extract without prior acidification and boiling? How is this avoided?

Show solution
  1. NaCN gives white AgCN, Na2S gives black Ag2S, and the NaCl gives white AgCl. The black Ag2S dominates and the AgCN is a white precipitate similar to AgCl, which falsely suggests chlorine in a sulphur or nitrogen compound.
  2. Boiling the extract with HNO3 converts CN− to HCN and S2− to H2S (and to some extent elemental S), which escape; only Cl− remains to precipitate as AgCl.
Example 14 Adv

Which tests fail?

For each compound state whether Lassaigne's test for nitrogen succeeds: (a) NH2OH·HCl; (b) C6H5N2+Cl−; (c) urea; (d) nitrobenzene; (e) pyridine; (f) H2N–NH2.

Show solution
  1. (a) fails: NH2OH has no carbon (the hydrochloride as well). (b) fails: diazonium salt loses N2 on heating before it can form NaCN. (c) works (C and N present; CN− forms). (d) works. (e) works. (f) fails: hydrazine contains no carbon.
  2. Rule: need C and N in the same molecule, and N must remain until it meets sodium.
