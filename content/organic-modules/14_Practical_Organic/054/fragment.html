18. Estimation of halogens, sulphur and phosphorus: Carius method

A known mass of the compound is heated in a strong sealed hard-glass tube (the Carius tube) in a furnace with fuming HNO3 (and, for halogens, a few crystals of AgNO3). Carbon and hydrogen are oxidised to CO2 and H2O.

Carius method
ElementReagent in tubeConversionFinal weighed substance
Cl, Br, IFuming HNO3 + AgNO3X → AgX (precipitate in the tube)Dry AgX (filter, wash, dry, weigh)
SFuming HNO3S → H2SO4; add BaCl2BaSO4
PFuming HNO3P → H3PO4; add magnesia mixture (MgCl2 + NH4Cl + NH3) → MgNH4PO4; igniteMg2P2O7
Formula 2MgNH4PO4 → Mg2P2O7 + 2NH3 + H2O %P = (62/222)(wMg2P2O7/w) × 100 if weighed as ammonium phosphomolybdate (M = 1877): %P = (31/1877)(wppt/w) × 100
IMP For a compound with several halogens only the precipitate mass is measured, not the individual halide; if both Cl and Br are present, one needs two equations (total mass of AgX and total Ag). A sulphur-containing compound that also contains halogens: the halogen precipitated as AgX is accompanied by sulphate that remains in solution, so Ag+ is added first and removed before BaCl2.
Example 31 Main

Halogen

0.30 g of an organic compound containing bromine gave 0.47 g of AgBr in a Carius determination. Find %Br. (Br = 80, Ag = 108)

Show solution
  1. %Br = (80/188)(0.47/0.30) × 100 = 0.4255 × 1.5667 × 100 = 66.7 %.
Example 32 Main

Sulphur

0.25 g of a sulphur compound gave 0.5825 g of BaSO4. Find %S. (Ba = 137)

Show solution
  1. %S = (32/233)(0.5825/0.25) × 100 = 0.13734 × 2.330 × 100 = 32.0 %.
Example 33 Adv

Phosphorus

0.500 g of an organic phosphate gave 0.222 g Mg2P2O7. Find %P.

Show solution
  1. %P = (62/222)(0.222/0.500) × 100 = 0.2793 × 0.444 × 100 = 12.4 %.
Example 34 Adv

A compound with two different halogens

0.200 g of a compound containing equal numbers of Cl and Br atoms per molecule gave 0.400 g of mixed AgCl + AgBr in a Carius estimation. Find %Br.

Show solution
  1. One weighing gives one equation, so the extra relation is the 1 : 1 ratio of Cl : Br, i.e. equal moles of AgCl and AgBr.
  2. Let x mol each: 143.5x + 188x = 0.400 ⇒ x = 1.207 × 10-3 mol.
  3. Mass of Br = 80 × 1.207 × 10-3 = 0.0965 g ⇒ %Br = 0.0965/0.200 × 100 = 48.3 %.
Example 35 Adv

Number of sulphur atoms

0.156 g of a compound (M = 156) gave 0.466 g BaSO4. How many S atoms per molecule?

Show solution
  1. Mass of S = 0.466 × 32/233 = 0.0640 g ⇒ %S = 41.0 %.
  2. Atoms per molecule = 156 × 0.410/32 = 2.0. (The factor is 32/233 because BaSO4 contains one S, whereas Mg2P2O7 contains two P, giving 62/222.)
Example 36 Adv

Molar mass from a Kjeldahl titration

0.2700 g of a compound with one nitrogen atom per molecule liberated ammonia that neutralised 20.0 mL of 0.100 M HCl. Find the molar mass.

Show solution
  1. Moles NH3 = 20.0 × 10-3 × 0.100 = 2.00 × 10-3 = moles N = moles of compound (one N per molecule).
  2. M = 0.2700/2.00 × 10-3 = 135 g mol-1.
Example 37 Adv

Molar mass from halogen percentage

0.2865 g of a chlorine compound gave 0.5740 g of AgCl. The molecule contains two Cl atoms. Find its molar mass.

Show solution
  1. Mass of Cl = 35.5/143.5 × 0.5740 = 0.1420 g; %Cl = 0.1420/0.2865 × 100 = 49.6 %.
  2. Molar mass = (2 × 35.5)/0.496 = 143 g mol-1 (for example, the molecular mass of C6H4Cl2 is 147; of C3H6Cl2, 113 – the data fit a compound near 143).
