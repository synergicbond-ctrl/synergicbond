Learning objectives

After working through the module a student should be able to:

  • identify the principal functional group of any compound and justify it with the seniority table;
  • choose the parent chain or ring by the stated criteria, and number it so that the locants satisfy the rules in the correct order;
  • cite prefixes in alphabetical order and use di/tri/bis/tris correctly;
  • name bicyclic (von Baeyer), spiro, fused and common heterocyclic parents and place substituents on them;
  • name benzene derivatives, side-chain compounds and the common retained aromatic parents;
  • assign E/Z and R/S descriptors from structures and write them correctly inside a name;
  • translate between the trivial and reagent-type names listed in Part 9 and their systematic names;
  • extract numerical quantities (sums of locants, carbon counts, isomer counts) from a correct name;
  • recognise where two different rule-sets give different names, and know which one a problem is using.
IMP A systematic name is correct only if (a) it describes the structure, (b) the parent was chosen by the rules, (c) the locants are the lowest permitted in the stated order, and (d) the prefixes are in alphabetical order. A name that fails any of these is wrong even if a reader can redraw the molecule from it.
