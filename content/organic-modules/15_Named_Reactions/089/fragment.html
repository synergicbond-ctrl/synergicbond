H1. Order of steps on a benzene ring

Toluene → m-bromobenzoic acid. CH3 is o,p-directing; COOH is m-directing. To put Br meta to the carboxyl, make the carboxyl first, then brominate.

KMnO₄, OH⁻; then H⁺⟶ Br₂, FeBr₃⟶
Oxidation first (CH3 → COOH), then bromination meta
Trap Reverse order (brominate toluene first) gives o- and p-bromotoluenes, from which oxidation can only give o- and p-bromobenzoic acids: wrong isomer.
Benzene → p-bromoaniline. –NH2 is too activating (tribromination) and is protonated in acid; so protect it as the amide, brominate para, then deprotect.
HNO₃, H₂SO₄⟶ Sn, HCl; then OH⁻⟶ (CH₃CO)₂O⟶ Br₂, CH₃COOH⟶ H₃O⁺, Δ⟶
Five steps: nitration, reduction, acetylation (protection), para-bromination, hydrolysis

Aniline → p-nitroaniline. Direct nitration in H2SO4 protonates the amine to the meta-directing –NH3+ and oxidises it (about half of the product is the meta isomer); protecting as the acetamide avoids both.

(CH₃CO)₂O⟶ HNO₃, H₂SO₄, < 283 K⟶ H₃O⁺, Δ⟶
p-Nitroaniline (the o-isomer is a minor by-product that can be removed because it is steam-volatile)

Benzene → m-dibromobenzene. Direct dibromination gives o/p-dibromobenzenes. The meta relationship needs a meta director first (NO2) and its conversion to Br through a diazonium salt.

HNO₃, H₂SO₄⟶ Br₂, FeBr₃⟶ Sn, HCl⟶ NaNO₂, HCl, 273 K⟶ CuBr, HBr⟶
Five steps: nitration, bromination (meta), reduction, diazotisation, Sandmeyer

Nitrobenzene → phenol.

Sn, HCl⟶ NaNO₂, HCl, 273 K⟶ H₂O, warm⟶
Reduce, diazotise, hydrolyse the diazonium salt

Toluene → aniline with one carbon fewer (Hofmann). The chain-shortening step is the Hofmann rearrangement of the amide.

KMnO₄; H⁺⟶ SOCl₂⟶ NH₃⟶ Br₂, NaOH⟶
The benzoic acid carbon is lost in the last step (as carbonate)

Benzene → n-propylbenzene. Direct alkylation with 1-chloropropane/AlCl3 gives isopropylbenzene (1,2-hydride shift). Acylation does not rearrange.

CH₃CH₂COCl, AlCl₃⟶ Zn–Hg, HCl⟶
Acylation then Clemmensen

Benzene → styrene.

CH₃COCl, AlCl₃⟶ NaBH₄; then H⁺⟶ H₂SO₄, Δ⟶
Acylation, reduction of C=O, dehydration (E1, benzylic cation)

Phenol → aspirin.

1. NaOH 2. CO₂, 400 K 3. H⁺⟶ (CH₃CO)₂O, H⁺⟶
Kolbe–Schmitt then acetylation of the phenolic OH
