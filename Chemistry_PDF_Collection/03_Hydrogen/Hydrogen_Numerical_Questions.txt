SECTION 1: H₂O₂ STRENGTH AND VOLUME PROBLEMS
===============================================

QUESTION 1: 20-Volume H₂O₂
----------------------------
A solution of H₂O₂ is labeled "20 volume."

(a) What volume of O₂ gas (at STP) will be released from 100 mL of this solution?

(b) How many grams of H₂O₂ are present in 500 mL of this solution?

(c) What is the molarity of the H₂O₂ solution?

ANSWERS:
  (a) 20 L
  (b) 2.94 g
  (c) 0.176 M

---

QUESTION 2: 30-Volume H₂O₂ Decomposition
------------------------------------------
A commercial H₂O₂ solution is "30 volume."

(a) If 50 mL of this solution decomposes completely: 2H₂O₂ → 2H₂O + O₂, 
    calculate moles of O₂ produced.

(b) What volume of O₂ (at STP) will be evolved?

(c) Considering 1 L of this solution, how many grams of pure H₂O₂ does it contain?

ANSWERS:
  (a) 0.067 mol
  (b) 1.5 L
  (c) 9.8 g

---

QUESTION 3: H₂O₂ Dilution
-----------------------------
An H₂O₂ solution labeled "10 volume" is diluted to make a "3 volume" solution.

(a) If the original volume was 200 mL, what volume of water must be added?

(b) In the original 200 mL solution, how many moles of H₂O₂ were present?

(c) After dilution, what is the mass percentage of H₂O₂ in the new solution 
    (assume density ≈ 1 g/mL)?

ANSWERS:
  (a) 467 mL
  (b) 0.118 mol
  (c) 0.29%

---

QUESTION 4: H₂O₂ Titration with KMnO₄
-----------------------------------------
A H₂O₂ sample is analyzed. 20 mL of this unknown strength H₂O₂ requires 16 mL 
of 0.1 M KMnO₄ in acidic medium.

Reaction: 2MnO₄⁻ + 5H₂O₂ + 6H⁺ → 2Mn²⁺ + 5O₂ + 8H₂O

(a) Calculate moles of KMnO₄ used.

(b) Calculate moles of H₂O₂ in 20 mL sample.

(c) Express the strength as "volume" and calculate molarity.

ANSWERS:
  (a) 0.0016 mol
  (b) 0.004 mol
  (c) 20 volume, 0.118 M

---

QUESTION 5: Concentrated H₂O₂ Solution
------------------------------------------
H₂O₂ (35% w/w, density = 1.11 g/mL) is used in an experiment.

(a) What is the molarity of 35% H₂O₂?

(b) Calculate the volume strength.

(c) How many mL of this concentrated solution is needed to prepare 500 mL 
    of 5-volume H₂O₂?

ANSWERS:
  (a) 11.4 M
  (b) 118 volume
  (c) 21.1 mL

QUESTION 6: Calcium Hardness and Softening
----------------------------------------------
Hard water contains 200 ppm of Ca²⁺ ions (atomic mass Ca = 40).

(a) Calculate moles of Ca²⁺ per liter.

(b) What is the hardness in terms of equivalent CaCO₃ (molecular mass = 100)?

(c) How many moles of Na₂CO₃ are required to soften 1 L of this water 
    by precipitation?

ANSWERS:
  (a) 0.005 mol
  (b) 500 ppm
  (c) 0.005 mol

QUESTION 7: Mixed Hardness (Ca²⁺ and Mg²⁺)
---------------------------------------------
A water sample has total hardness of 150 ppm (as CaCO₃). It contains both 
Ca²⁺ and Mg²⁺ in a 3:2 molar ratio.

(a) Calculate moles of total hardness per liter (in terms of CaCO₃).

(b) Find moles of Ca²⁺ and Mg²⁺ separately.

(c) What mass of lime (CaO, assuming 100% purity) is needed to soften 
    1000 L of this water?

ANSWERS:
  (a) 0.0015 mol
  (b) Ca²⁺: 0.0009 mol, Mg²⁺: 0.0006 mol
  (c) 67.2 kg

QUESTION 8: Temporary Hardness Removal by Boiling
-----------------------------------------------------
Temporary hardness in 500 mL of water is due to Ca(HCO₃)₂.

On boiling: Ca(HCO₃)₂ → CaCO₃↓ + H₂O + CO₂↑

(a) If 0.002 mol of Ca(HCO₃)₂ is present initially, what volume of CO₂ 
    (at STP) is released?

(b) What mass of CaCO₃ precipitate forms?

(c) What is the reduction in hardness (in ppm as CaCO₃)?

ANSWERS:
  (a) 0.045 L (45 mL)
  (b) 0.2 g
  (c) 400 ppm

---

QUESTION 9: Soda-Lime Softening Method
------------------------------------------
Hard water (400 ppm hardness as CaCO₃) is treated with soda lime 
(CaO + NaOH mixture) for softening.

(a) Using only CaO method: calculate mass of CaO needed per liter.

(b) If NaOH is used instead: Ca²⁺ + 2OH⁻ → Ca(OH)₂↓, how many grams of NaOH 
    (molecular mass = 40) are required per liter?

(c) Which method is more economical and why?

ANSWERS:
  (a) 56 g CaO (approx. 1 mol)
  (b) 7.2 g NaOH
  (c) CaO method (cheaper source)

---

QUESTION 10: Ion-Exchange Resin Softening
---------------------------------------------
A water softening plant receives hard water at 600 ppm (as CaCO₃).
The treatment uses ion-exchange resin with Na⁺ as the exchangeable ion.

(a) Calculate equivalent hardness in terms of moles of Ca²⁺/Mg²⁺ per liter 
    (assume all hardness is Ca²⁺).

(b) How many moles of Na⁺ are exchanged per liter of hard water treated?

(c) For 10,000 L of hard water daily, what mass of NaCl (to regenerate 
    the resin) is needed if 1 kg NaCl regenerates 20,000 L of softened water?

ANSWERS:
  (a) 0.006 mol Ca²⁺
  (b) 0.012 mol Na⁺
  (c) 0.5 kg NaCl

PROBLEM-SOLVING STRATEGY GUIDE
===============================

For H₂O₂ Strength (Volume) Problems:
------------------------------------
• "X-volume" means 1 mL solution yields X mL O₂ gas at STP
• Key reaction: 2H₂O₂ → 2H₂O + O₂
• Molar mass of H₂O₂ = 34 g/mol
• Molarity = (volume × density × %age) / (molar mass)
• Use dilution formula: M₁V₁ = M₂V₂ for dilution problems
• For titration: Use stoichiometric ratios from balanced equation

For Water Hardness Problems:
----------------------------
• Always express hardness as ppm CaCO₃ for standardization
• Molar mass CaCO₃ = 100 g/mol
• Molar mass CaO = 56 g/mol
• Molar mass NaOH = 40 g/mol
• Temporary hardness: HCO₃⁻ salts → removed by boiling
• Permanent hardness: Cl⁻, SO₄²⁻ → removed by chemical treatment
• Softening reagents:
    - CaO: for temporary hardness (most economical)
    - NaOH: for both types
    - Na₂CO₃: forms CaCO₃ precipitate
• Ion-exchange: Ca²⁺ + Na₂(resin) → CaResin + 2Na⁺

Key Conversions:
-----------------
1 ppm = 1 mg/L (for aqueous solutions with density ≈ 1 g/mL)
1 L water = 1000 mL = 1000 g
At STP: 1 mole of gas = 22.4 L
