Block J. Numerical problems

Practice 200 Numerical

10.6 g of benzaldehyde (M = 106) is converted by the Cannizzaro reaction with 100 % yield. Find the masses of benzoic acid (M = 122) and benzyl alcohol (M = 108) formed.

Show answer
  1. n = 10.6/106 = 0.100 mol; half is oxidised and half reduced. Acid: 0.050 × 122 = 6.1 g. Alcohol: 0.050 × 108 = 5.4 g.
Practice 201 Numerical

5.8 g of propanone (M = 58) reacts completely with I2/NaOH to give iodoform (M = 394). Find the mass of CHI3.

Show answer
  1. 5.8/58 = 0.100 mol acetone gives 0.100 mol CHI3 = 39.4 g.
Practice 202 Numerical

12.2 g of benzoic acid (M = 122) is converted to aniline (M = 93) in three steps (SOCl2; NH3; Br2/NaOH), each in 80 % yield. Find the mass of aniline.

Show answer
  1. n = 0.100 mol; overall yield 0.83 = 0.512; n(aniline) = 0.0512 mol; mass = 0.0512 × 93 = 4.76 g.
Practice 203 Numerical

0.92 g of glycerol (M = 92) is treated with excess CH3MgI. Find the volume of CH4 at STP (22.4 L mol−1).

Show answer
  1. n = 0.0100 mol; three OH groups: 0.0300 mol CH4 = 0.0300 × 22.4 = 0.672 L (672 mL).
Practice 204 Numerical

How many moles of CH3I are needed for exhaustive methylation of ethane-1,2-diamine, H2NCH2CH2NH2?

Show answer
  1. Each primary NH2 takes three CH3: 2 × 3 = 6 mol (giving the bis-trimethylammonium salt).
Practice 205 Numerical

How many moles of HIO4 are consumed, and what are the products, in the cleavage of (a) propane-1,2,3-triol, (b) glucose (open-chain form)?

Show answer
  1. (a) Two C–C bonds between oxygenated carbons: 2 mol HIO4, giving 2 HCHO + HCOOH. (b) Five C–C bonds: 5 mol HIO4, giving 5 HCOOH + 1 HCHO.
Practice 206 Numerical

Sodium ethanoate is electrolysed with a current of 9.65 A for 1000 s. Find the volume of ethane at STP formed at the anode.

Show answer
  1. Q = 9650 C = 0.100 F = 0.100 mol e−. Two carboxylates (2 e−) give one C2H6: n = 0.050 mol, V = 1.12 L. (CO2 at the anode: 0.100 mol = 2.24 L; H2 at the cathode: 0.050 mol = 1.12 L.)
Practice 207 Numerical

18.1 g of silver propanoate (M = 181) gives bromoethane (M = 109) in 70 % yield in the Hunsdiecker reaction. Find the mass of C2H5Br.

Show answer
  1. 0.100 mol × 0.70 = 0.0700 mol × 109 = 7.63 g.
Practice 208 Numerical

1.2 kg of cumene (M = 120) is converted to phenol (94) and acetone (58) via the hydroperoxide with 90 % overall yield. Find the mass of each.

Show answer
  1. n = 10.0 mol; 9.0 mol each. Phenol: 9.0 × 94 = 846 g; acetone: 9.0 × 58 = 522 g.
Practice 209 Numerical

13.8 g of salicylic acid (M = 138) is acetylated to aspirin (M = 180) in 75 % yield. Find the mass of aspirin.

Show answer
  1. 0.100 mol × 0.75 × 180 = 13.5 g.
Practice 210 Numerical

11.3 g of cyclohexanone oxime (M = 113) gives ε-caprolactam (M = 113) in 90 % yield by the Beckmann rearrangement. Find the mass.

Show answer
  1. The product is an isomer of the oxime (same M). 0.100 mol × 0.90 × 113 = 10.2 g.
Practice 211 Numerical

12.0 g of acetophenone (M = 120) gives benzoic acid (M = 122) in 80 % yield by the haloform reaction. Find the mass of acid.

Show answer
  1. 0.100 × 0.80 × 122 = 9.76 g (and 0.080 mol CHI3).
Practice 212 Numerical

The Robinson-annulation product of cyclohexanone and methyl vinyl ketone is C10H14O. Find its degree of unsaturation and the moles of H2 absorbed per mole over Pd/C at 1 atm.

Show answer
  1. DBE = (2 × 10 + 2 − 14)/2 = 4 (two rings, one C=C, one C=O). Over Pd/C at 1 atm only the C=C is hydrogenated: 1 mol H2.
Practice 213 Numerical

Compare the atom economy of the Diels–Alder reaction of butadiene (54) with maleic anhydride (98) and the Wittig reaction of cyclohexanone (98.1) with Ph3P=CH2 (276.3) giving methylenecyclohexane (96.2).

Show answer
  1. Diels–Alder: all atoms of both reactants appear in the product: 100 %. Wittig: 96.2/(98.1 + 276.3) = 25.7 % (Ph3P=O is the waste).
Practice 214 Numerical

9.3 g of aniline (M = 93) is diazotised and converted completely to chlorobenzene (M = 112.5) by the Sandmeyer reaction. Find the mass of C6H5Cl and the volume of N2 at STP.

Show answer
  1. n = 0.100 mol. PhCl: 0.100 × 112.5 = 11.25 g. N2: 0.100 mol = 2.24 L.
