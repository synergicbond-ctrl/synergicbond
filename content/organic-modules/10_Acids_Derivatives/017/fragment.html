17. Acetoacetic ester synthesis

Ethyl acetoacetate (EAA, ethyl 3-oxobutanoate) is made by the Claisen condensation of ethyl acetate. Its CH2 (between the C=O of the ketone and the ester) is acidic (pKa 10.7) so that NaOEt converts it quantitatively to the enolate, which is alkylated by primary or methyl halides (SN2). Hydrolysis and decarboxylation of the alkylated ester then give methyl ketones, or (under the other hydrolysis conditions) carboxylic acids.

17.1 The sequence

1. NaOEt 2. CH₃I⟶ 1. dil. NaOH, Δ 2. H₃O⁺, warm (− CO₂)⟶
The general pattern drawn for R = CH₃: butan-2-one
1. NaOEt 2. CH₃CH₂Br⟶ 1. dil. NaOH 2. H₃O⁺, Δ⟶
EAA + ethyl bromide → pentan-2-one
  • 1. EtO− removes the α-H between the carbonyls: enolate (stable, delocalised).
  • 2. SN2 on RX: monoalkyl EAA. (A second equivalent of base and R′X gives the dialkyl derivative R(R′)C(COCH3)COOEt.)
  • 3. Ketonic hydrolysis: warm dilute NaOH hydrolyses the ester to the β-keto carboxylate; acidification gives the β-keto acid, which loses CO2 on warming (cyclic six-membered transition state) to the methyl ketone CH3COCH2R (or CH3COCHRR′).
  • 3′. Acid (cleavage) hydrolysis: boiling with concentrated NaOH or KOH (or ethanolic KOH) cleaves the C–C bond next to the ketone C=O (retro-Claisen): products are CH3COO− and RCH2COO−; after acidification two acids (acetic acid and RCH2COOH) result.

Example 45 Main

Methyl ketones from EAA

Design syntheses from EAA of (a) hexan-2-one, (b) 3-methylbutan-2-one, (c) 4-phenylbutan-2-one.

Show solution
  1. (a) CH3COCH2CH2CH2CH3: R = n-propyl: EAA, NaOEt, n-C3H7Br; dil. NaOH; H3O+, Δ.
  2. (b) CH3COCH(CH3)2: two methyl groups on the same α-carbon: EAA, NaOEt, CH3I; NaOEt, CH3I again; ketonic hydrolysis. (Isopropyl bromide would eliminate.)
  3. (c) PhCH2CH2COCH3: R = benzyl (PhCH2): EAA, NaOEt, PhCH2Br; ketonic hydrolysis. The ketone CH3COCH2CH2Ph = 4-phenylbutan-2-one.
Example 46 Main

Acid cleavage products

Ethyl 2-methyl-2-propylacetoacetate is heated with concentrated KOH and then acidified. Name the products. What would dilute alkali then acid/heat give?

Show solution
  1. CH3CO–C(CH3)(C3H7)–COOEt. Acid cleavage: CH3COOH (ethanoic acid) + (CH3)(C3H7)CHCOOH = 2-methylpentanoic acid, plus EtOH.
  2. Ketonic hydrolysis: CH3COCH(CH3)CH2CH2CH3 = 3-methylhexan-2-one + CO2.
Example 47 Adv

Retrosynthesis choice: EAA or malonic ester?

Choose the better route: (a) 2-methylbutanoic acid; (b) hexane-2,5-dione; (c) 5-bromopentan-2-one. Explain by retro-analysis.

Show solution
  1. (a) An acid: malonic ester. 2-Methylbutanoic acid CH3CH2CH(CH3)COOH: disconnect to CH2(COOEt)2 + CH3I + C2H5Br (two alkylations, then hydrolysis, decarboxylation). EAA acid cleavage would give a mixture with acetic acid (wasteful), so malonic ester is the cleaner route.
  2. (b) CH3COCH2CH2COCH3 is a methyl ketone with an α-CH2CH2C(=O)CH3 chain: EAA + ClCH2COCH3 (the C-alkylation by an α-halo ketone) then ketonic hydrolysis.
  3. (c) A bromoalkyl ketone cannot survive the strongly basic hydrolysis conditions; use EAA + 1,2-dibromoethane for cyclopropyl methyl ketone, then ring-opening with HBr to 5-bromopentan-2-one (this is a recognised route: cyclopropyl ketone + HBr opens the three-membered ring to the γ-bromoketone).
Example 48 Adv

Product from a dihalide with two equivalents of EAA

Ethyl acetoacetate (2 mol) is treated with NaOEt (2 mol) and then 1,3-dibromopropane (1 mol), followed by ketonic hydrolysis and decarboxylation. What is formed? What happens instead with 1 mol EAA, 2 mol NaOEt and 1 mol of the dibromide?

Show solution
  1. Each enolate displaces one bromide, joining the two EAA units through the three-carbon bridge: CH3CO–CH(COOEt)–CH2CH2CH2–CH(COOEt)–COCH3. Ketonic hydrolysis and loss of two CO2: CH3CO–(CH2)5–COCH3, nonane-2,8-dione.
  2. With 1 mol EAA and 2 mol base, the second alkylation is intramolecular (4-exo ring closure) and gives, after hydrolysis and decarboxylation, cyclobutyl methyl ketone.
Trap Reaction of the EAA enolate with an alkyl halide may give O-alkylation (enol ether) in polar aprotic solvents with hard electrophiles; with alkyl halides in ethanol, C-alkylation dominates. Also: dialkylation is a competing reaction with the more reactive RX; use a slight excess of EAA enolate.
