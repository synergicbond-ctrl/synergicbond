7. The electromeric effect (E effect)

7.1 Definition

The electromeric effect is the complete and temporary transfer of a shared π-electron pair to one of the two atoms joined by a multiple bond, under the influence of an attacking reagent. It appears at the moment the reagent approaches and disappears when the reagent is removed. In effect it is the very first step of every polar addition to a double bond, drawn as a curved arrow.

  • +E effect: the π-electrons are transferred toward the atom to which the attacking reagent attaches — the attacking species is an electrophile (H⁺, Br⁺, NO₂⁺, ...) and a bond to the reagent is formed at that atom.
  • −E effect: the π-electrons are transferred away from the atom to which the reagent attaches — the attacking species is a nucleophile (CN⁻, OH⁻, R⁻, ...), the π-pair moves to the other atom.
+ +E⟶
+E: π electrons move to the carbon bonded to H⁺ (propene and H⁺ give the 2° cation)
+ −E⟶
−E: π pair of C=O moves to oxygen as CN⁻ bonds to carbon (cyanohydrin formation)
IMP Electromeric vs mesomeric vs inductive. Inductive: σ-bonds, permanent, partial. Mesomeric: π-bond or lone pair in conjugation, permanent, partial (shown by resonance structures). Electromeric: single multiple bond, temporary, complete transfer, needs a reagent. When both an electromeric effect and a mesomeric effect are possible, the mesomeric effect is the ground-state polarisation and the electromeric effect the extra push by the reagent; they usually act in the same direction.

Illustration 7.1 Main

Direction of the electromeric effect

In each case say whether the electromeric effect is +E or −E: (a) CH₂=CH₂ attacked by H⁺ (b) CH₃CHO attacked by CN⁻ (c) CH₃CH=CH₂ attacked by Br⁺ (d) acetone attacked by H⁺ on oxygen.

  1. (a) The reagent is an electrophile and the π-pair moves toward the carbon that bonds the electrophile: +E.
  2. (b) The nucleophile CN⁻ attacks carbon, the π-pair moves to oxygen, away from the attacked carbon: −E.
  3. (c) Br⁺ electrophile: +E (the π electrons move toward the carbon that bonds Br⁺; the other carbon carries the positive charge, preferably C-2).
  4. (d) An electrophile (H⁺) attaches to O and the π electrons move toward O: +E, making the carbonyl carbon strongly δ⁺ (the first step of acid-catalysed addition).
Illustration 7.2 Adv

Markovnikov addition explained with E and I effects

Use the +I (hyperconjugative) effect of CH₃ and the +E effect to explain why HBr adds to propene to give 2-bromopropane.

  1. In propene the +I and hyperconjugation of CH₃ pushes π-electron density toward C-1 (the CH₂ carbon), so C-1 is δ⁻ and C-2 is δ⁺ even before reaction.
  2. The electrophile H⁺ attacks the electron-rich C-1; under the +E effect the π electrons move to C-1 and the positive charge appears on C-2, giving the secondary cation CH₃–CH⁺–CH₃.
  3. Br⁻ then attaches to the cation: 2-bromopropane is the major product (Markovnikov addition).

Illustration 7.3 Adv

Which statement is true?

(A) Electromeric effect is a permanent effect. (B) The electromeric effect operates in the ground state. (C) Electromeric effect involves complete transfer of π-electrons. (D) The electromeric effect is observed in alkanes.

  1. (A) false (temporary); (B) false (only in the presence of the reagent); (C) true; (D) false (needs a multiple bond).
  2. Answer: C.
