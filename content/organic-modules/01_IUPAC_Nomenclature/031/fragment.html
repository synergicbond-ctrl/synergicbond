4.2 Alcohols and phenols

  • Drop the final e of the alkane and add -ol (-diol, -triol keep the e: propane-1,2-diol). Chain must contain the carbon bearing OH; number so that OH has the lowest locant.
  • The locant is written whenever more than one position is possible (propan-1-ol, propan-2-ol); it is omitted for methanol, ethanol and singly substituted rings such as cyclohexanol.
  • Phenol is the retained parent for hydroxybenzene. When a senior group is present, an –OH group is expressed by the prefix hydroxy.
Compare & Distinguish Alcohols are named on the chain that carries the OH, so the parent need not be the longest chain: in HOCH₂CH(CH₃)CH₂CH₂CH₃ the chain must include C–OH (2-methylpentan-1-ol), even if a longer chain existed elsewhere.

Example 19 Direct application

Name the alcohol.

Show solution
  1. Longest chain containing C–OH: five carbons.
  2. OH lowest: C2 from the right end (the left end would put it at C4).
  3. Methyl on C4.

4-Methylpentan-2-ol.

Example 20 Standard

Name the unsaturated alcohol.

Show solution
  1. Four-carbon chain containing both C–OH and C=C.
  2. Principal group first: from the CH₃ end OH is on C2; from the CH₂= end OH would be on C3. Take 2.
  3. The double bond is then C3=C4 → but-3-en-2-ol. (The double bond rule "lowest locant to C=C" applies only after the principal group has been placed.)

But-3-en-2-ol (not but-1-en-3-ol).

Example 21 Standard

Name the diol.

Show solution
  1. Longest chain: four carbons, with OH on the two inner carbons: butane-2,3-diol. The extra methyl is on the carbon that is C2 or C3 depending on direction.
  2. The OH locant set is {2,3} either way, so the next criterion is the prefix locant: number from the end that makes the quaternary carbon C-2, giving methyl = 2.

2-Methylbutane-2,3-diol.
