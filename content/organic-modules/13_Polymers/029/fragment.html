Q62 Application

Radical polymerisation statements

Select the correct statements. (A) The initiator is consumed and is therefore not a catalyst. (B) Each propagation step is exothermic. (C) Termination by coupling doubles the chain length relative to the kinetic chain length. (D) Head-to-head addition is the dominant mode of propagation.

Show answer

A, B, C. (A) The initiator fragments end up in the polymer as end groups. (B) A π bond is converted to a stronger σ bond; ΔH ≈ −85 to −95 kJ mol-1. (C) Two chains of length ν join: DP = 2ν. (D) False: head-to-tail dominates because attack on CH2 gives the more stable (substituted, delocalised) radical with less steric hindrance.

Q63 Application

Effect of [I]

In a free-radical polymerisation the initiator concentration is raised ninefold. Which are correct? (A) Rp rises threefold. (B) Kinetic chain length falls threefold. (C) Rp rises ninefold. (D) Kinetic chain length is unchanged.

Show answer

A, B. Rp ∝ [I]1/2 → √9 = 3. ν = Rp/Ri ∝ [I]1/2/[I] = [I]−1/2 → 1/3.

Q64 Application

Q73 Integration

Back-biting branch

In the radical polymerisation of ethene, which branch length is produced by 1,5-hydrogen back-biting and why?

Show answer

The chain-end radical (C-1) abstracts H from C-5 through a six-membered cyclic transition state (the lowest-strain size). The new radical on C-5 adds ethene, and C-1 to C-4 left behind become a butyl branch (C4H9). A 1,4-shift (five-membered TS) and 1,6-shift (seven-membered TS) are less favourable.

Q74 Integration

Disproportionation of PMMA radicals

Show what two growing PMMA chains give by disproportionation (use the models CH3–C•(CH3)COOMe).

+ disproportionation⟶ +
Saturated chain end (isobutyrate model) + chain end with a terminal C=C (methacrylate model).
Show answer

One radical abstracts a β-H (from the CH3 of the other chain end); the donor chain becomes a methacrylate-type end group with C=C, the acceptor becomes the saturated –CH(CH3)COOMe end. Net: two shorter chains, no change in the number of chains compared with two growing radicals, but the chain length is not doubled.

Q75 Integration

Regiochemistry

Explain with the radical stability argument why styrene polymerises head-to-tail.

Show answer

The radical adds to CH2, so the unpaired electron lands on the benzylic carbon and is delocalised over the ring (spin density at ortho and para carbons; SOMO mixes with ring π). Attack at the CHPh carbon would leave an unstabilised primary radical and suffer severe steric hindrance. The product is –CH2–CHPh–CH2–CHPh–.

Q76 Integration

Units from a diene

Isoprene polymerises with a stereo-specific catalyst. How many different repeat-unit types can result?

Show answer

Four: 1,2-addition (pendant isopropenyl group), 3,4-addition (pendant vinyl group on a quaternary carbon carrying CH3), cis-1,4 and trans-1,4. The 1,4-units differ only in C=C geometry; 1,2- and 3,4-units are different constitutions and each has a stereogenic carbon.

Q77 Integration

Units from butadiene

How many distinct repeat unit types arise from buta-1,3-diene (counting geometrical isomers)?

Show answer

Three: 1,2-vinyl, cis-1,4 and trans-1,4. (Butadiene is symmetrical, so 1,2 and 3,4 addition give the same unit.)

Q78 Integration

Ozonolysis of natural rubber

Ozonolysis followed by reductive work-up of natural rubber gives which product? Draw it. Why does that identify the head-to-tail structure?

1. O₃ 2. Zn/H₂O⟶
Levulinaldehyde (4-oxopentanal) from every isoprene unit.
Show answer

Every C=C is cleaved; the segment between two double bonds is –CH2–CH2– (from C-4 of one unit and C-1 of the next), giving CH3C(=O)CH2CH2CHO (4-oxopentanal, levulinaldehyde) from each unit. A single product proves a regular head-to-tail repeating unit; a mixture would mean head-to-head links.

Q85 Integration

Interfacial polymerisation

In the nylon rope trick, sebacoyl chloride in hexane is layered on aqueous hexamethylenediamine with NaOH. (a) Which polymer forms? (b) Why NaOH?

Show answer

(a) Nylon-6,10 at the interface. (b) The reaction releases HCl, which would protonate the amine into –NH3+, removing its lone pair (HOMO) and stopping attack at the acyl chloride. NaOH neutralises HCl, so the diamine stays nucleophilic and the reaction can run at room temperature.

Q86 Integration

Why does Carothers need 1:1?

A diamine is in 2 % molar excess over the diacid. Even at 100 % reaction of the acid groups, what limits Xn?

Show answer

r = NA/NB = 1/1.02 = 0.98039; Xn = (1 + r)/(1 − r) = 1.98039/0.01961 = 101. Every chain ends in –NH2 at both ends; no acid groups remain to continue growth.

Q87 Integration

Monofunctional impurity

In the manufacture of nylon-6,6, a little acetic acid is added. What does it do and why?

Show answer

It caps chain ends: the free –NH2 reacts with CH3COOH to give an unreactive –NHCOCH3 end. A chain with a blocked end cannot grow, so the final M is limited and the melt viscosity stays constant during spinning (no further polymerisation).

Q88 Integration

Nylon-6 mechanism role of water

Why is a little water added when caprolactam is polymerised?

Show answer

Water hydrolyses a small fraction of lactam to 6-aminohexanoic acid, whose free –NH2 then acts as the nucleophile that opens other lactam rings. Without a nucleophilic initiator the strain-free amide ring is slow to open.

Q89 Integration

Planar amide

Draw the two resonance forms of an amide and say how they explain why the peptide/amide bond is planar.

resonance⟶
Charge-separated form; the C–N bond has partial double-bond character (barrier ≈ 75 kJ mol⁻¹).
Show answer

The N lone pair (nN) overlaps with π*(C=O): the second form has a C=N⁺ double bond, so rotation about C–N is hindered and the six atoms (C, O, N, H, and the two attached carbons) lie in one plane. This gives stiff, flat links that hydrogen-bond into sheets: the basis of polyamide strength.

Q90 Integration

Kevlar vs Nomex

Why is Kevlar a high-strength fibre while Nomex is mainly flame-resistant?

Show answer

Kevlar (para) chains are straight and rigid, so they align in liquid-crystalline domains and form extended H-bonded sheets: very high tensile modulus. Nomex (meta) chains are kinked, so packing is less perfect and the fibre is less stiff; its aromatic backbone resists burning and gives flame resistance.

Q91 Integration

Bakelite structure

Why does the base-catalysed (resol) route lead to Bakelite on heating alone, but the novolac needs an added reagent?

Show answer

A resol contains –CH2OH groups, which can condense with ring C–H and with each other on heating. A novolac (acid, excess phenol) has no –CH2OH groups; it needs a methylene source, supplied by hexamethylenetetramine (HCHO and NH3 equivalents) to cross-link.

Q92 Integration

Bakelite functionality

How many reactive ring positions does phenol offer for HCHO, and what does this mean for the polymer?

Show answer

Three: two ortho and one para (activated by –OH/–O−). Trifunctional phenol + bifunctional HCHO → network (cross-linked) rather than linear chains.

Q93 Integration

Melamine

How many N–H bonds does melamine have, and why does it make a dense network?

Show answer

Six (three –NH2). Each can add HCHO to give –NH–CH2OH and then bridge to another N–H, so up to six links per melamine: a tight 3-D network, hard and heat-stable.

Q106 Integration

Show answer

Q107 Integration

Isotactic stereochemistry

In isotactic polypropene, how many stereogenic carbons per repeat unit, and what are the diad relationships?

Show answer

One stereogenic CH(CH3) per unit. In the isotactic chain neighbouring centres have the same spatial arrangement along the zig-zag (meso diads, m); in the syndiotactic chain they alternate (racemo diads, r). The centres are pseudo-asymmetric because the two chain arms are nearly identical, so the polymer is not optically active.

Q108 Integration

Copolymer composition

For r1 = 0.5 and r2 = 2.0 and an equimolar feed, find d[M1]/d[M2] (Mayo–Lewis).

Show answer

(r1[M1] + [M2])/(r2[M2] + [M1]) = (0.5 + 1)/(2.0 + 1) = 1.5/3.0 = 0.50: the polymer is richer in M2.

Q109 Integration

Alternating copolymer

Styrene (electron-rich) and maleic anhydride (electron-poor) copolymerise alternately. Give the frontier-orbital reason.

Show answer

A styryl radical is nucleophilic (high-lying SOMO); it interacts strongly with the low-lying π* of the electron-poor maleic anhydride. A maleic anhydride radical is electrophilic (low SOMO) and interacts best with the high-lying π of styrene. Each radical prefers the other monomer, so r1, r2 ≈ 0 and the sequence alternates. Maleic anhydride also cannot homopolymerise easily (steric and 1,2-disubstituted).

Q110 Integration

Number of stereocentres

The repeat unit of poly(propylene oxide) is –O–CH(CH3)–CH2–. How many stereogenic centres are in a chain of 10 units?

Show answer

10 (one CH(CH3) per unit); the chain can be isotactic, syndiotactic or atactic in arrangement.

Q111 Integration

PLA stereochemistry

Lactic acid made by fermentation is (S). Describe PLA from it.

Show answer

Each repeat unit –O–CH(CH3)–CO– keeps the (S)-configuration (no centre is inverted in esterification), giving isotactic, semicrystalline poly(L-lactide), m.p. ≈ 175 °C; a racemic mix gives atactic amorphous PLA.

Q112 Integration

Hydrolysis of a polylactide

Complete hydrolysis of a chain of 25 lactic acid residues, formed by condensation, uses how many water molecules?

Show answer

24. Twenty-five residues are joined by 24 ester links; each link takes up one H2O on hydrolysis, giving 25 lactic acid molecules.

Q113 Integration

Cross-linking and swelling

Why does vulcanised rubber swell but not dissolve in benzene?

Show answer

Solvent molecules enter between chains (weak forces) and swell the network, but covalent S–S bridges tether the chains to one another so they cannot separate and dissolve.

Q114 Integration

Thermal behaviour

Explain: rapid stretching of a rubber band warms it; a loaded rubber band shortens on heating.

Show answer

Rubber elasticity is entropic. Stretching lowers chain entropy; the work done is released as heat (adiabatic). On heating a loaded rubber, the restoring entropic force ∝ T rises, so the band contracts.

Q115 Integration

Saran

Why is Saran used as food wrap rather than polythene?

Show answer

PVDC–PVC copolymer packs tightly (polar C–Cl), has low gas permeability (O2, water vapour), adheres to itself and is transparent, keeping food fresh longer.

Q116 Integration

Brittle PHB and PHBV

Why is PHBV less brittle than PHB?

Show answer

The hydroxyvalerate unit has an ethyl side chain that disrupts crystal packing of the methyl-substituted PHB, lowering crystallinity, giving a tougher, more flexible copolymer.

Q118 Integration
