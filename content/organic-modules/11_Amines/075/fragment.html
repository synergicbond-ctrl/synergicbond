75. Level 3, set A — integer-type and numerical problems

Practice 220 L3 Integer

How many amines (all classes, constitutional isomers only) have the molecular formula C5H13N?

Show answer
  1. Primary: 8 (the eight pentylamines, Section 2). Secondary: R–NH–CH3 with R = n-butyl, sec-butyl, isobutyl or tert-butyl (4), plus R–NH–C2H5 with R = n-propyl or isopropyl (2): 6. Tertiary: N,N-dimethylpropan-1-amine, N,N-dimethylpropan-2-amine and N-ethyl-N-methylethanamine: 3.
  2. Total 8 + 6 + 3 = 17 (confirmed by exhaustive computer enumeration).

17.

Practice 221 L3 Integer

Of the 17 isomeric amines C5H13N, how many contain a stereogenic carbon (are chiral)?

Show answer
  1. Primary: pentan-2-amine, 2-methylbutan-1-amine, 3-methylbutan-2-amine (3). Secondary: N-methylbutan-2-amine (1). No tertiary isomer is chiral. Total 4 (so there are 17 + 4 = 21 stereoisomers counting enantiomers).

4.

Practice 222 L3 Integer

Of the 17 isomeric amines C5H13N, how many give a product that dissolves in aqueous KOH after reaction with benzenesulphonyl chloride, and how many give an alkali-insoluble sulphonamide?

Show answer
  1. Clear solution = 1° amine (acidic N–H in RNHSO2Ph): 8. Insoluble sulphonamide = 2° amines: 6. The 3 tertiary amines do not react.

8 dissolve; 6 give an insoluble sulphonamide.

Practice 223 L3 Integer

The total number of moles of CH3I consumed by exhaustive methylation of one mole each of methylamine, dimethylamine, trimethylamine and ethane-1,2-diamine is:

Show answer
  1. 3 (CH3NH2) + 2 ((CH3)2NH) + 1 ((CH3)3N) + 6 (H2NCH2CH2NH2: each NH2 takes 3) = 12.

12.

Practice 224 L3 Integer

Hexanediamide, H2NCO(CH2)4CONH2, is treated with excess Br2/KOH. Give the total number of moles of Br2 and KOH consumed per mole of diamide, and the number of carbon atoms in the diamine.

Show answer
  1. Each CONH2 group consumes 1 Br2 and 4 KOH and loses its carbonyl carbon as K2CO3. Two groups: 2 Br2 + 8 KOH. The six-carbon chain loses two C: H2N(CH2)4NH2.

Br2: 2; KOH: 8; total 10 moles; the product is butane-1,4-diamine with 4 carbons.

Practice 225 L3 Integer

How many of the following give a stable diazonium salt at 273 K with NaNO2/HCl: aniline, p-nitroaniline, benzylamine, cyclohexylamine, p-anisidine, 1-naphthylamine, N-methylaniline, 2-aminopyridine (2-aminopyridine gives an unstable diazonium; take it as not stable)?

Show answer
  1. Stable: aniline, p-nitroaniline, p-anisidine, 1-naphthylamine (primary aromatic amines). Benzylamine and cyclohexylamine (aliphatic, N2 lost at once), N-methylaniline (2°: nitrosamine) and 2-aminopyridine (unstable diazonium; hydrolyses at once) do not.

4.

Practice 226 L3 Integer

How many of the following compounds give a positive carbylamine test: aniline, benzylamine, tert-butylamine, N-methylaniline, acetanilide, glycine, trimethylamine, benzamide?

Show answer
  1. Free –NH2 on an amine: aniline, benzylamine, tert-butylamine, glycine (α-NH2). Amides (acetanilide, benzamide), 2° and 3° amines give no isocyanide.

4.

Practice 227 L3 Integer

How many of the compounds listed are more acidic than phenol (pKa 10): o-nitrophenol, m-nitrophenol, p-nitrophenol, p-cresol, p-methoxyphenol, picric acid, 2,4-dimethylphenol?

Show answer
  1. o-, m-, p-Nitrophenol and picric acid. The cresol, p-methoxyphenol and the dimethylphenol are weaker acids (electron-donating groups destabilise the phenoxide).

4.

Practice 228 L3 Integer

How many of these dissolve in cold dilute aqueous NaOH: phenol, nitromethane, aniline, acetamide, benzoic acid, 2-nitropropane, nitrobenzene, benzenesulphonamide (PhSO2NH2)?

Show answer
  1. Phenol (pKa 10), nitromethane (10.2) and 2-nitropropane (7.7) form soluble anions (nitronates) – the nitroalkanes dissolve slowly; benzoic acid (4.2); benzenesulphonamide (pKa 10.1) also dissolves. Aniline, acetamide and nitrobenzene do not. Count = phenol, nitromethane, benzoic acid, 2-nitropropane, benzenesulphonamide = 5.

5.

Practice 229 L3 Integer

The number of π bonds (in the Kekulé structure) in the benzenediazonium ion C6H5N2+ is:

Show answer
  1. Ring: 3 C=C π bonds; N≡N: 2 π bonds. Total 5.

5.

Practice 230 L3 Integer

How many resonance structures can be drawn for the guanidinium ion [C(NH2)3]+ with the positive charge on a nitrogen atom?

Show answer
  1. The C=N+H2 double bond can be placed on any of the three equivalent nitrogens.

3.

Practice 231 L3 Integer

How many moles of Sn and how many moles of HCl are consumed in reducing one mole of 1,4-dinitrobenzene to the dihydrochloride of benzene-1,4-diamine (Sn → SnCl2)? Give the sum.

Show answer
  1. Each NO2 → NH2 is a 6-electron reduction; two groups need 12 electrons; Sn → Sn2+ gives 2 e−: 6 Sn.
  2. Per nitro group: ArNO2 + 3Sn + 7HCl → ArNH3+Cl− + 3SnCl2 + 2H2O (7 HCl). Two groups: 14 HCl.

6 mol Sn and 14 mol HCl; sum 20.

Practice 232 L3 Integer

The number of different monobromo-substitution products possible when N-phenylacetamide (acetanilide) is mono-brominated on the ring is:

Show answer
  1. Positional isomers of the ring substitution: o-, m-, p-bromoacetanilide. (Ortho and para are favoured; meta is trace.)

3 (ortho, meta, para); the major one is para.

Practice 233 L3 Integer

N,N-Dimethylaniline is nitrosated with NaNO2/HCl. How many nitrogen atoms are present in one molecule of the product?

Show answer
  1. p-Nitroso-N,N-dimethylaniline, (CH3)2N–C6H4–N=O: one N of the NMe2 group and one N of the nitroso group.

2.

Practice 234 L3 Integer

How many nitrogen atoms are in methyl orange (as its sodium salt)? What is its molar mass (Na = 23, S = 32)?

Show answer
  1. C14H14N3NaO3S: (CH3)2N– (1 N) and –N=N– (2 N). M = 14×12.011 + 14×1.008 + 3×14.007 + 22.99 + 3×15.999 + 32.06 = 327.3.

3 nitrogen atoms; M = 327.3 g mol-1.

How many primary amines of formula C7H9N contain a benzene ring?

Practice 236 L3 Numerical

12.3 g of nitrobenzene (M = 123) is reduced with Fe/HCl to aniline (M = 93) in 85 % yield. What mass of aniline is obtained?

Show answer
  1. 12.3/123 = 0.100 mol → 0.100 mol aniline = 9.30 g theoretical; × 0.85 = 7.91 g.

7.9 g.

Practice 237 L3 Numerical

A sample (0.500 g) was analysed by the Kjeldahl method and the ammonia evolved neutralised 24.0 mL of 0.500 N H2SO4. Find % N.

Show answer
  1. % N = 1.4 × N × V/w = 1.4 × 0.500 × 24.0/0.500 = 33.6.

33.6 %.

Practice 238 L3 Numerical

Aniline (0.186 g, M = 93) is analysed by the Dumas method. What volume of N2 (at 300 K and 760 mm Hg, dry) is collected?

Show answer
  1. 0.186/93 = 2.00 × 10−3 mol aniline → 1.00 × 10−3 mol N2. At STP: 22.4 mL; at 300 K: 22.4 × 300/273 = 24.6 mL.

24.6 mL.

Practice 239 L3 Numerical

What mass of NaNO2 (M = 69) and of HCl (M = 36.5) are needed to diazotise 9.3 g of aniline completely, using the stoichiometric 2 equivalents of acid?

Show answer
  1. 9.3/93 = 0.100 mol aniline. NaNO2 = 0.100 mol × 69 = 6.9 g; HCl = 0.200 mol × 36.5 = 7.3 g.

NaNO2: 6.9 g; HCl: 7.3 g (a slight excess is used in practice).

Practice 240 L3 Numerical

0.0100 mol of benzenediazonium chloride couples with excess β-naphthol (C10H8O) to give 1-phenylazo-2-naphthol (C16H12N2O, M = 248). Find the mass at 70 % yield.

Show answer
  1. Theoretical 0.0100 mol × 248.3 = 2.48 g; 70 % → 1.74 g.

1.74 g.

Practice 241 L3 Numerical

1.000 g of the chloroplatinate of a monoacidic base B leaves 0.413 g of platinum on ignition (Pt = 195, Cl = 35.5). Find the molar mass of B and identify it if it is a gas at room temperature.

Show answer
  1. Salt (BH)2PtCl6: moles of salt = moles of Pt = 0.413/195 = 2.118 × 10−3. M(salt) = 1.000/2.118 × 10−3 = 472 g mol-1.
  2. 2(MB + 1) + 195 + 213 = 472 → MB = 31: CH3NH2, a gas (b.p. −6 °C).

MB ≈ 31; B = methylamine.

Practice 242 L3 Numerical

5.0 g of a mixture of ethylamine (M = 45) and dimethylamine (M = 45) – isomers – is treated with excess HNO2; 1.12 L of N2 at STP is evolved. Find the mass percentage of ethylamine in the mixture.

Show answer
  1. Only the 1° amine gives N2: 1.12 L/22.4 = 0.0500 mol → 0.0500 × 45 = 2.25 g ethylamine → 2.25/5.0 = 45 %.

45 %.

Practice 243 L3 Numerical

A solution contains 0.100 M aniline and 0.0500 M anilinium chloride. Find the pH. Then 1.0 mL of 0.50 M HCl is added to 10 mL of this buffer; find the new pH (neglect the volume change; pKa of anilinium = 4.62).

Show answer
  1. Buffer: pH = pKa + log([base]/[acid]) = 4.62 + log(0.100/0.0500) = 4.92.
  2. In 10 mL: aniline 1.00 mmol, anilinium 0.500 mmol. Added HCl: 1.0 mL × 0.50 M = 0.50 mmol, converting aniline to anilinium: aniline 0.50 mmol, anilinium 1.00 mmol. pH = 4.62 + log(0.50/1.00) = 4.62 − 0.30 = 4.32.

4.92; 4.32.
