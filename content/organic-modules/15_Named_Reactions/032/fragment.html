28. Kolbe–Schmitt reaction (carboxylation of phenoxide)

Reagents: sodium phenoxide (dry) + CO2 (≈ 4–7 atm), 400–410 K, then H3O+. Product: salicylic acid (2-hydroxybenzoic acid). Mechanism: phenoxide is an ambident nucleophile; its ortho carbon attacks CO2 (a weak electrophile), the sodium ion being chelated between the phenoxide O and the CO2 oxygen in a six-membered transition state; tautomerisation restores aromaticity. Regiochemistry: with sodium phenoxide at ≈ 400 K the product is ortho (chelation by the small Na+); with potassium phenoxide at higher temperature (≈ 500 K) the para isomer, 4-hydroxybenzoic acid, becomes the major product (the large K+ does not chelate and the thermodynamically more stable para isomer forms).
