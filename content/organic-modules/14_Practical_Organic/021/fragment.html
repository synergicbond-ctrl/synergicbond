7.1 Preparing the sodium fusion extract (SFE, Lassaigne's extract)

  • Cut a small piece (about the size of a pea) of sodium, dry it with filter paper and put it in a fusion (ignition) tube.
  • Heat the tube gently until the sodium melts to a shining globule, then add a small amount (about 50 mg) of the organic compound and heat strongly to red heat for about 2 minutes.
  • Plunge the hot tube into 10 to 15 mL of distilled water in a china dish (the tube breaks; keep the dish away from your face). Any unreacted sodium reacts with water.
  • Boil for a few minutes, cool and filter. The clear, colourless to pale yellow, alkaline filtrate is the sodium fusion extract.
Formula Na + C + N → NaCN 2Na + S → Na2S Na + X → NaX (X = Cl, Br, I) Na + C + N + S → NaSCN

The elements are detected as the ions CN−, S2−, X− (and SCN−). Sodium must be present in excess, and it must be fused completely, so that the carbon–nitrogen bond breaks.

Why It Happens Sodium (a very strong reductant) donates electrons to the heteroatoms, which are then released as stable, ionic, water-soluble anions (CN−, S2−, X−); the strong, covalent C–N, C–S and C–X bonds cannot be tested as such.
IMP Why sodium and not (say) iron? Sodium is a strong reducing agent that cleaves the C–N, C–S, C–X bonds, and it forms ionic salts that dissolve in water. Why not just heat in air? Burning oxidises N to N2 or NOx, S to SO2 and halogen to HX or X2: the information as ions is lost. Why boil with water? To destroy leftover sodium (Na + H2O → NaOH + ½H2) and bring the salts into solution; alcohol is sometimes added first to destroy Na safely. Why is the extract alkaline? NaOH from the excess sodium; it keeps cyanide from being lost as HCN.
