6.4 Kiliani cyanohydrin step and the Strecker synthesis

Step 1: NH3 adds⟶ Step 2: H+, − H2O⟶ Step 3: CN− adds⟶ Step 4: H3O+, Δ⟶
Strecker synthesis of alanine, mechanistic steps
  • Step 1. NH3 (nucleophile, lone pair HOMO) adds to the C=O π*: hemiaminal (carbinolamine).
  • Step 2. Acid protonates OH, water leaves; the N lone pair pushes: iminium ion. Why: the iminium carbon is a much better electrophile than the aldehyde (a positive charge lowers its π* LUMO).
  • Step 3. Cyanide, whose HOMO is the carbon sp lone pair, adds to the iminium π*. Why cyanide and not water: CN− is a good carbon nucleophile, and the product C–C bond is irreversible under these conditions; it is also the C-adding step in the Kiliani synthesis (CN− + C=O → cyanohydrin).
  • Step 4. Nitrile hydrolysis: protonation of N, water attack at the C≡N carbon, tautomerisation to amide, then a second hydrolysis (amide → acid + NH4+).
CN−, then H+⟶ H2, Pd/BaSO4; H2O⟶
Kiliani: cyanide adds to the aldehyde (Bürgi–Dunitz), then the nitrile is partially reduced to an imine and hydrolysed to the aldehyde (D-arabinose → D-glucose; the C-2 epimer D-mannose is formed alongside)
Shortcut In the Kiliani step the CN− can attack either face of the CHO of arabinose. The existing stereocentres (C-2 to C-4 of arabinose) make the two transition states diastereomeric, so the two epimers form in unequal amounts, but the new centre is the only one that varies.
