31.4 Paragraph-based questions

Paragraph 1. Four alcohols A, B, C and D have the formula C4H10O. A and D give a red colour in the Victor Meyer test and no turbidity with Lucas reagent at room temperature. B gives immediate turbidity with Lucas reagent. C is optically active and gives a blue colour in the Victor Meyer test. D has a branched chain.

Practice 266 L3 PAR1

(Paragraph 1) Identify B.

Show answer
  1. Immediate Lucas turbidity = tertiary: 2-methylpropan-2-ol.
Practice 267 L3 PAR1

(Paragraph 1) Identify C and give the oxidation product with CrO3.

Show answer
  1. Optically active and secondary (blue): butan-2-ol; oxidation gives butanone.
Practice 268 L3 PAR1

(Paragraph 1) A and D are dehydrated and the alkenes ozonised (Zn/H2O). Which carbonyl compounds does the major alkene of D give?

Show answer
  1. A = butan-1-ol, D = 2-methylpropan-1-ol (branched). Dehydration of D (hydride shift to the 3° cation) gives 2-methylpropene; ozonolysis gives acetone + methanal.

Paragraph 2. Compound P (C6H14O2) is made by the reduction of acetone with Mg/Hg followed by water. It reacts with H2SO4 to give Q (C6H12O), and with HIO4 to give R (two equivalents).

Practice 269 L3 PAR2

(Paragraph 2) Identify P and name the reaction type that converts P into Q.

Show answer
  1. P = pinacol (2,3-dimethylbutane-2,3-diol). P → Q is the pinacol rearrangement.
Practice 270 L3 PAR2

(Paragraph 2) Identify Q and say whether it gives the iodoform test.

Show answer
  1. Q = pinacolone, (CH3)3C–CO–CH3. It contains CH3CO– and gives a positive iodoform test.
Practice 271 L3 PAR2

(Paragraph 2) Identify R and the number of moles of HIO4 consumed per mole of P.

Show answer
  1. R = acetone (two molecules); 1 mol of HIO4.

Paragraph 3. In the cumene process cumene is oxidised by air to a hydroperoxide U; treatment with dilute acid gives phenol and acetone.

Practice 272 L3 PAR3

(Paragraph 3) Why is cumene selectively oxidised at the isopropyl C–H?

Show answer
  1. The tertiary benzylic C–H bond is the weakest C–H bond; its radical is stabilised by both the ring (resonance) and three alkyl groups.
Practice 273 L3 PAR3

(Paragraph 3) Which group migrates from carbon to oxygen in the acid-catalysed decomposition of U, and what is the cationic intermediate?

Show answer
  1. The phenyl group (better migratory aptitude than methyl) migrates to the electron-deficient oxygen as water leaves; the intermediate is the oxocarbenium ion PhO+=C(CH3)2.
Practice 274 L3 PAR3

(Paragraph 3) How many kilograms of acetone are co-produced with 1.88 tonnes of phenol (100 % yield)?

Show answer
  1. 1.88 t = 20 kmol phenol → 20 kmol acetone × 58 = 1160 kg.

Paragraph 4. Phenol (A) is treated with NaOH to give B; B is heated with CO2 under pressure and then acidified to give C; C is treated with acetic anhydride to give D.

Practice 275 L3 PAR4

(Paragraph 4) Name B and C and the reaction type linking B to C.

Show answer
  1. B = sodium phenoxide; C = salicylic acid; Kolbe–Schmitt reaction (electrophilic substitution by CO2).
Practice 276 L3 PAR4

(Paragraph 4) Which of A, C, D gives a violet colour with neutral FeCl3? Which gives CO2 with NaHCO3?

Show answer
  1. FeCl3: A (phenol) and C (salicylic acid); not D (phenolic OH acetylated). NaHCO3: C and D (both have COOH).
Practice 277 L3 PAR4

(Paragraph 4) Why is the OH group of C (as the carboxylate anion) so weakly acidic (pKa2 13.6)?

Show answer
  1. The phenoxide of the dianion lies close to the negatively charged carboxylate (charge repulsion), and the O–H···O− hydrogen bond to the carboxylate stabilises the monoanion; removing that proton is therefore difficult.

Paragraph 5. Allyl 2,6-dimethylphenyl ether is heated.

Practice 278 L3 PAR5

(Paragraph 5) Why can the first [3,3] shift not be followed by tautomerisation to a phenol?

Show answer
  1. The ortho carbon that receives the allyl group is quaternary (it bears CH3 as well), so there is no H to move; the dienone cannot aromatise.
Practice 279 L3 PAR5

(Paragraph 5) Give the final product and the number of [3,3] shifts involved.

Show answer
  1. 4-Allyl-2,6-dimethylphenol; two [3,3] shifts (Claisen, then Cope).
Practice 280 L3 PAR5

(Paragraph 5) Which carbon of the allyl group is bonded to the ring in the para product: the original O-bound carbon or the terminal carbon?

Show answer
  1. The original O-bound carbon (two inversions cancel).

Paragraph 6. 1-Methylcyclohexene is converted to its epoxide E.

Practice 281 L3 PAR6

(Paragraph 6) Give the product of E with CH3OH/H+ and describe its stereochemistry.

Show answer
  1. Methanol attacks the tertiary carbon with inversion: 2-methoxy-2-methylcyclohexan-1-ol, with OCH3 and OH trans (anti).
Practice 282 L3 PAR6

(Paragraph 6) Give the product of E with CH3ONa/CH3OH.

Show answer
  1. Methoxide attacks the secondary carbon: 2-methoxy-1-methylcyclohexan-1-ol, with OCH3 and OH trans.
Practice 283 L3 PAR6

(Paragraph 6) Give the product of E with LiAlH4 and state why it is formally a Markovnikov hydration product.

Show answer
  1. Hydride attacks the secondary carbon: 1-methylcyclohexan-1-ol (tertiary alcohol, OH on the more substituted carbon), without any carbocation.

Paragraph 7. An ether X (C8H10O) does not dissolve in NaOH or react with sodium. Heating with excess HI gives Y (C6H6O) and Z (C2H5I).

Practice 284 L3 PAR7

(Paragraph 7) Identify X, Y and Z.

Show answer
  1. X = ethoxybenzene (phenetole); Y = phenol; Z = ethyl iodide.
Practice 285 L3 PAR7

(Paragraph 7) Why is iodobenzene not formed?

Show answer
  1. The aryl C–O bond has partial double-bond character and the carbon is sp2: neither SN1 nor SN2 can occur there; I− attacks the ethyl carbon.
Practice 286 L3 PAR7

(Paragraph 7) Give the major product of mononitration of X.

Show answer
  1. OEt is an o,p-director: 4-nitroethoxybenzene (major) and 2-nitro isomer.

Paragraph 8. Glycerol is heated with KHSO4, treated with HNO3/H2SO4, and with excess HI in three separate reactions giving G1, G2 and G3.

Practice 287 L3 PAR8

(Paragraph 8) Name G1, G2 and G3.

Show answer
  1. G1 = acrolein (propenal); G2 = glyceryl trinitrate; G3 = 2-iodopropane.
Practice 288 L3 PAR8

(Paragraph 8) Which of G1, G2, G3 will reduce Tollens’ reagent?

Show answer
  1. G1 only (an aldehyde, an α,β-unsaturated one).
Practice 289 L3 PAR8

(Paragraph 8) Calculate the mass of G3 (M = 170) formed from 46 g of glycerol in 80 % yield.

Show answer
  1. 46 g = 0.50 mol; 0.50 × 0.80 = 0.40 mol × 170 = 68 g.
