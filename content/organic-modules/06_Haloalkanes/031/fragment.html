Practice 90 Main

Grignard preparation

Why must the preparation of a Grignard reagent be done in dry ether, and what happens with (a) water (b) ethanol (c) CO2 (d) O2?

Show answer
  1. Ether stabilises RMgX by coordination of the oxygen lone pairs to Mg; moisture destroys it. (a) RH + Mg(OH)X. (b) RH + Mg(OR)X. (c) RCOOMgX → RCOOH on acidification. (d) ROMgX → ROH after hydrolysis.

Practice 97 Main

Why inert

Explain why chlorobenzene does not react with NaOH at 25 °C but does react at 623 K and 300 atm.

Show answer
  1. The C–Cl bond is strong (sp2 carbon, resonance with the lone pair of Cl), the aryl cation is unstable, and back-side attack is blocked: very high activation energy. Under extreme conditions the addition–elimination (or, with strong base, elimination–addition) reaction becomes accessible: sodium phenoxide forms, giving phenol on acidification (Dow process).
Practice 98 Main

Activated aryl halides

Arrange in decreasing reactivity toward aqueous NaOH: (a) 2,4,6-trinitrochlorobenzene (b) 2,4-dinitrochlorobenzene (c) p-nitrochlorobenzene (d) chlorobenzene (e) m-nitrochlorobenzene.

Show answer
  1. Ortho/para nitro groups activate by stabilising the Meisenheimer complex; the more the better; meta gives only inductive help. (a) > (b) > (c) > (e) > (d).
Practice 99 Main

Mechanism

Write the two steps of the mechanism of the reaction of 2,4-dinitrochlorobenzene with hydroxide. Name the intermediate and say which step is rate-determining.

Show answer
  1. Step 1 (slow): OH− adds to the carbon bearing Cl, giving a resonance-stabilised cyclohexadienyl anion: the Meisenheimer complex (charge delocalised onto the two nitro groups). Step 2 (fast): loss of Cl− restores aromaticity to give 2,4-dinitrophenol. Rate-determining: step 1.
Practice 100 Main

Leaving group in SNAr

Which is the most reactive: 1-X-2,4-dinitrobenzene with X = F, Cl, Br, I toward piperidine? Explain the order.

Show answer
  1. F. The rate-determining step is the addition of the nucleophile; the highly electronegative F makes the ipso carbon most electrophilic and stabilises the anionic intermediate by induction. C–X bond breaking occurs after the slow step. Order: F ≫ Cl ≈ Br > I.
Practice 101 Main

Benzyne

What are the products when chlorobenzene is treated with KNH2 in liquid NH3? What does the 14C-labelling experiment show?

Show answer
  1. Aniline. With C-1 labelled chlorobenzene, aniline is produced with the label equally distributed between C-1 and C-2 (50:50), showing the symmetric benzyne intermediate in which the amide may attack either carbon.
Practice 102 Main

Benzyne product mixture

How many amines C7H9N are formed when p-chlorotoluene reacts with NaNH2/NH3? Name them.

Show answer
  1. Benzyne from loss of H ortho to Cl: C1–C2 triple bond with CH3 at C4. Amide adds to C1 (→ p-toluidine) or C2 (→ m-toluidine). 2 products: m-toluidine and p-toluidine, in nearly equal amounts.
Practice 103 Main

Electrophilic substitution

Why are halobenzenes less reactive than benzene in nitration but still give ortho and para products?

Show answer
  1. The halogen withdraws electron density by its –I effect (more electronegative than carbon) and so deactivates the ring (nitration slower than benzene). The lone pairs donate by +R (resonance) and stabilise the arenium ion formed by ortho or para attack (an extra resonance structure with positive charge on the carbon bearing X, delocalised onto halogen), hence o,p-orientation.
Practice 104 Main

Products

Write the products of nitration of chlorobenzene and say which one is the major product and why it can be separated.

Show answer
  1. o-Nitrochlorobenzene (≈ 30 %) and p-nitrochlorobenzene (≈ 70 %, major; less hindered). They are separated by fractional crystallisation because the para isomer (mp 83 °C) is much less soluble than the ortho (mp 32 °C), which stays in the mother liquor.
Practice 105 Main

DDT

How is DDT prepared? Name its constituents and state one harmful effect.

Show answer
  1. From two moles of chlorobenzene and one mole of chloral (trichloroacetaldehyde, CCl3CHO) in the presence of conc. H2SO4. Name: p,p′-dichlorodiphenyltrichloroethane (1,1,1-trichloro-2,2-bis(4-chlorophenyl)ethane). It is stable and fat-soluble, accumulates in food chains (thinning of bird egg shells) and is toxic to non-target organisms.
Practice 106 Main

Chloroform storage

Why is chloroform stored in dark bottles filled to the brim and with 1 % ethanol?

Show answer
  1. In the presence of air and sunlight CHCl3 slowly oxidises to carbonyl chloride (phosgene), highly poisonous. Dark bottles keep out light; filling to the brim excludes air; ethanol converts any phosgene to the harmless diethyl carbonate: COCl2 + 2 C2H5OH → (C2H5O)2CO + 2 HCl.

Carbylamine test

Which of the following gives the carbylamine test (foul smell with CHCl3/KOH): (A) aniline (B) N-methylaniline (C) trimethylamine (D) methylamine (E) acetamide? Write the reaction.

Show answer

Practice 109 Main

Freon

What is Freon-12, how is it prepared, and why has its use been restricted?

Show answer
  1. CCl2F2 (dichlorodifluoromethane); prepared from CCl4 + HF/SbF3 (Swarts reaction). Inert, non-toxic refrigerant and aerosol propellant, but in the stratosphere UV releases Cl• atoms that catalytically destroy ozone; banned under the Montreal Protocol.
Practice 110 Main

Carbon tetrachloride

Why is CCl4 not used as a fire extinguisher in closed rooms today? How is it made from CS2?

Show answer
  1. At high temperatures with moisture CCl4 forms phosgene (poisonous); it is also hepatotoxic and ozone-depleting. Preparation: CS2 + 3 Cl2 → CCl4 + S2Cl2 (Fe or AlCl3 catalyst); also from CH4 + Cl2 (excess).

Predict the product of aqueous NaOH with (a) C6H5CHCl2 (b) C6H5CCl3 (c) CH3CHCl2 (d) CH3CCl3.

Show answer

Practice 112 Main

Compare

Chlorobenzene has a lower dipole moment (1.69 D) than cyclohexyl chloride (2.31 D). Why?

Show answer
  1. (i) The sp2 carbon of the benzene ring is more electronegative than sp3 carbon, so the C–Cl bond is less polarised (smaller charge separation); (ii) the +R effect of Cl (lone pair into the ring) opposes the –I dipole; (iii) the C–Cl bond is shorter.
Practice 113 Main

Reagent

Which reagent distinguishes (a) chlorobenzene from benzyl chloride (b) CH3CH2CH2Br from (CH3)3CBr (c) chloroform from carbon tetrachloride?

Show answer
  1. (a) Alcoholic AgNO3: benzyl chloride gives AgCl at once; chlorobenzene none. (b) alcoholic AgNO3: tert-butyl bromide gives immediate AgBr, the primary bromide only on heating. (c) CHCl3 gives the carbylamine test (aniline + KOH, foul isocyanide smell); CCl4 does not.

Product of Reimer–Tiemann

Write the product of phenol + CHCl3/aq. NaOH (340 K), then H+. Name the reactive intermediate.

Show answer
  1. Salicylaldehyde (2-hydroxybenzaldehyde) mainly (some 4-hydroxybenzaldehyde). The electrophile is dichlorocarbene :CCl2, generated by α-elimination from CHCl3 and hydroxide.
