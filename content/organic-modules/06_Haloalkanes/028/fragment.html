27. Practice set, Level 1 (Main): structure, properties and preparation

Level 1 problems test single concepts at JEE Main depth (single correct, numerical, short answers). Try each before opening the answer. Answers are printed in full.

Practice 1 Main

Boiling points

Arrange in increasing order of boiling point: 1-chlorobutane, 2-chlorobutane, 2-chloro-2-methylpropane.

Show answer
  1. All three are C4H9Cl isomers; boiling point falls with branching (smaller surface area, weaker London forces).
  2. Straight chain 1-chlorobutane (78 °C) > 2-chlorobutane (68 °C) > tert-butyl chloride (51 °C).
  3. Increasing order: tert-butyl chloride < 2-chlorobutane < 1-chlorobutane.
Practice 2 Main

Dipole moment of isomers

Arrange o-, m- and p-dichlorobenzene in decreasing order of dipole moment, and in decreasing order of melting point.

Show answer
  1. The two C–Cl dipoles (each ≈ 1.7 D) are at 60° (ortho), 120° (meta) and 180° (para). The resultant is μ = 2μ0 cos(θ/2): ortho ≈ 2.5 D, meta ≈ 1.5–1.7 D, para = 0.
  2. Dipole moment: ortho > meta > para (= 0).
  3. Melting point depends on packing: the symmetrical para isomer packs best: para (53 °C) > ortho (−17 °C) > meta (−25 °C).
Practice 3 Main

Density

Which of these are denser than water? (A) CH3CH2Br (B) CH3CH2Cl (C) CHCl3 (D) C6H5Br (E) CH3(CH2)3Cl

Show answer
  1. Short-chain chlorides (ethyl chloride 0.90, butyl chloride 0.89 g mL−1) are lighter than water. Bromides, iodides and polychloro compounds are heavier: ethyl bromide 1.46, chloroform 1.49, bromobenzene 1.50 g mL−1.
  2. A, C, D.

How many structural isomers (without stereoisomers) are there for C4H9Cl, and how many including stereoisomers?

Practice 6 Main

Isomer count

How many structural isomers of C5H11Br exist, and how many of them are chiral?

Show answer

Practice 9 Main MCQ

Bond properties

Which statement is correct? (A) C–F is the weakest of the C–X bonds. (B) CH3Cl has a larger dipole moment than CH3F. (C) C–I bond is the shortest. (D) Alkyl fluorides are the most reactive halides in SN2.

Show answer
  1. C–F is the strongest and shortest bond; C–I the longest and weakest. Dipole moments: CH3Cl 1.87 D > CH3F 1.85 D. Alkyl iodides are the most reactive.
  2. (B).
Practice 10 Main

C–Cl bond length

Arrange in decreasing order of C–Cl bond length: CH3Cl, CH2=CHCl, C6H5Cl (vinyl and aryl as sp2 carbons). Give reasons.

Show answer
  1. The C–Cl bond of CH3Cl (sp3) is longest (≈ 1.78 Å). In vinyl chloride and chlorobenzene the carbon is sp2 (more s-character, shorter orbitals) and the Cl lone pair delocalises into the π-system, giving partial double-bond character: ≈ 1.72 Å and ≈ 1.70–1.74 Å.
  2. CH3Cl > CH2=CHCl ≈ C6H5Cl.
Practice 11 Main

Reagent for conversion

Which reagent is best for CH3CH2CH2OH → CH3CH2CH2Cl in the laboratory and why? (A) HCl (g) (B) PCl5 (C) SOCl2 (D) NaCl/H2SO4

Show answer
  1. SOCl2 gives the chloride together with SO2 and HCl, both gases that escape, so the product is obtained pure and in high yield (no purification from POCl3 as with PCl5, no equilibrium as with HCl, and no rearrangement). (C).
Practice 12 Main

Lucas test

Arrange in order of decreasing reactivity with Lucas reagent (conc. HCl/ZnCl2) at room temperature: (a) (CH3)3COH (b) (CH3)2CHOH (c) CH3CH2CH2OH (d) benzyl alcohol (C6H5CH2OH) (e) CH2=CH–CH2OH.

Show answer
  1. Reactivity follows carbocation stability (SN1): tertiary > benzylic ≈ allylic (resonance-stabilised) > secondary > primary.
  2. In practice (a) turbidity immediately; (d) and (e) are fast as well (benzylic/allylic react like 3°/2°); (b) takes ≈ 5 minutes; (c) no turbidity at room temperature.
  3. (a) > (d) ≈ (e) > (b) > (c).
Practice 13 Main

Rearrangement in preparation

Write the major product of 3-methylbutan-2-ol with concentrated HBr, and say why PBr3 would give a different product.

Show answer
  1. Protonation and loss of water gives the 2° cation CH3–CH+–CH(CH3)2, which rearranges by a 1,2-hydride shift to the 3° cation CH3CH2–C+(CH3)2. Br− captures it: 2-bromo-2-methylbutane (major).
  2. PBr3 acts by SN2 on the phosphite ester with no carbocation, so 2-bromo-3-methylbutane (unrearranged, with inversion) is the main product.
HBr⟶
Rearranged product with HBr (secondary alcohol, tertiary bromide).
Practice 14 Main

Markovnikov and anti-Markovnikov

Give the product of propene with (a) HBr (b) HBr in the presence of benzoyl peroxide (c) HCl in the presence of peroxide. Explain (c).

Show answer
  1. (a) 2-Bromopropane (Markovnikov; more stable 2° cation). (b) 1-Bromopropane (anti-Markovnikov; radical chain, Br• adds to give the more stable 2° radical). (c) 2-Chloropropane: no peroxide effect with HCl (the H-abstraction by the carbon radical from HCl is endothermic, since H–Cl bond is strong), so ionic addition occurs.
HBr⟶
Markovnikov (no peroxide).
HBr, ROOR⟶
anti-Markovnikov (peroxide effect, HBr only).
Practice 15 Main

NBS

Write the product(s) formed when (a) cyclohexene (b) toluene (c) propene is treated with N-bromosuccinimide in CCl4 with light.

Show answer
  1. Allylic or benzylic C–H is replaced by C–Br through the stabilised radical; NBS keeps Br2 concentration low so there is no addition across C=C.
  2. (a) 3-bromocyclohexene. (b) benzyl bromide. (c) 3-bromoprop-1-ene (allyl bromide).
Practice 16 Main

Free-radical chlorination

Calculate the percentage of 1-chlorobutane and 2-chlorobutane formed on monochlorination of butane at 25 °C (relative reactivity per H: 1° : 2° = 1 : 3.8).

Show answer
  1. Butane has 6 primary and 4 secondary hydrogens. 1-chloro: 6 × 1 = 6. 2-chloro: 4 × 3.8 = 15.2. Total 21.2.
  2. 1-Chlorobutane = 6/21.2 = 28 %; 2-chlorobutane = 15.2/21.2 = 72 %.
Practice 17 Main

Free-radical chlorination

2,2-Dimethylbutane is monochlorinated by Cl2/hν at 25 °C (relative rate per H: 1° : 2° : 3° = 1 : 3.8 : 5). How many constitutional isomers form, and what is the percentage of the secondary chloride?

Show answer
  1. (CH3)3C–CH2–CH3: three types of H: nine equivalent CH3 H on the tert-butyl group (1°), two CH2 H (2°), three terminal CH3 H (1°). Constitutional isomers: 1-chloro-2,2-dimethylbutane, 3-chloro-2,2-dimethylbutane, 1-chloro-3,3-dimethylbutane: 3.
  2. Amounts: 9 × 1 = 9; 2 × 3.8 = 7.6; 3 × 1 = 3. Total 19.6. Secondary chloride (3-chloro-2,2-dimethylbutane) = 7.6/19.6 = 39 %.
Practice 18 Main

Selectivity

Why is bromination of an alkane far more selective than chlorination?

Show answer
  1. Hydrogen abstraction by Cl• is exothermic with an early transition state that resembles the reactants, so there is little differentiation between 1°, 2°, 3° C–H bonds. Abstraction by Br• is endothermic: the transition state is late and resembles the alkyl radical, so the stability of the radical (3° > 2° > 1°) strongly influences the rate (reactivity ratio 1 : 82 : 1640 versus 1 : 3.8 : 5.0 for chlorine). (Hammond postulate.)
Practice 19 Main

Finkelstein

What happens when n-butyl bromide is warmed with NaI in dry acetone? Why is acetone used? Would NaCl/acetone convert butyl iodide back?

Show answer
  1. n-Butyl iodide forms and NaBr precipitates. NaI is soluble in acetone, while NaBr and NaCl are not, so the precipitation drives the equilibrium (Le Chatelier). The reverse (with NaCl in acetone) does not proceed: NaCl is insoluble, so there is essentially no chloride in solution.
Practice 20 Main

Swarts reaction

Write the product of (a) CH3CH2Br + AgF (b) CCl4 + SbF3 (with SbCl5 catalyst, excess SbF3) (c) CH3Cl + KF in DMF. What is common to these?

Show answer
  1. (a) CH3CH2F. (b) CCl2F2 (dichlorodifluoromethane, Freon-12) and CCl3F as a partial product. (c) CH3F. All are halogen-exchange reactions (SN2 at carbon with fluoride from a metal fluoride): direct fluorination with F2 is too violent.

Give the product of (a) silver butanoate + Br2/CCl4 (b) silver benzoate + Br2/CCl4. Mention the by-products.

Show answer
  1. The silver salt of an acid loses CO2 and is converted into a bromide with one carbon fewer. (a) 1-bromopropane (+ CO2, AgBr). (b) bromobenzene (+ CO2 + AgBr).

Practice 22 Main

Aryl halide preparation

How would you prepare (a) iodobenzene (b) fluorobenzene (c) chlorobenzene (d) p-bromotoluene starting from the appropriate arylamine or arene?

Show answer
  1. (a) Aniline → diazotise (NaNO2/HCl, 0–5 °C) → KI: C6H5I + N2 (no Cu needed). (b) Aniline → diazonium → HBF4 → ArN2+BF4−, heat (Balz–Schiemann) → C6H5F. (c) Benzene + Cl2/FeCl3, or Sandmeyer (CuCl/HCl). (d) Toluene + Br2/FeBr3 gives o- and p-bromotoluene (separate); or p-toluidine → diazotise → CuBr/HBr gives pure p-bromotoluene.
Practice 23 Main

Iodination

Why can benzene not be iodinated directly with I2 alone, and how is this solved?

Show answer
  1. The reaction C6H6 + I2 ⇌ C6H5I + HI is reversible and unfavourable; HI is a strong reducing agent and converts iodobenzene back to benzene. Addition of an oxidising agent (HNO3, HIO3, HgO, Cu2+) removes HI by oxidising it to I2, driving the reaction forward.
Practice 24 Main

Toluene and chlorine

Write the products of toluene with Cl2 under (a) FeCl3, dark, room temperature (b) light, boiling, in excess.

Show answer
  1. (a) Electrophilic substitution on the ring: o-chlorotoluene + p-chlorotoluene. (b) Radical side-chain substitution: benzyl chloride, then benzal chloride (PhCHCl2), then benzotrichloride (PhCCl3) with excess Cl2.
Practice 25 Main

Choose the method

Which is the better route to chloroethane: (A) ethane + Cl2/hν (B) ethene + HCl? Explain in one line.

Show answer
  1. (B). Radical chlorination of ethane gives a mixture of mono-, di- and polychloroethanes (and HCl), whereas addition of HCl to ethene is clean and gives only chloroethane.
Practice 26 Main

Phenol to chlorobenzene

Why does phenol not give chlorobenzene with PCl5 or SOCl2 in good yield, although ethanol gives ethyl chloride?

Show answer
  1. In phenol the C–O bond has partial double-bond character (the oxygen lone pair is delocalised into the ring, the carbon is sp2) and is not easily broken. The intermediate aryl chlorophosphate/chlorosulfite cannot be attacked by Cl− at the sp2 carbon (no SN1/SN2). Only triphenyl phosphate and a trace of chlorobenzene are formed.

Practice 28 Main

Ethers and HI

(a) What is formed when ethyl methyl ether is heated with excess HI? (b) With one equivalent of cold HI, which iodide forms from methyl tert-butyl ether?

Show answer
  1. (a) CH3I + C2H5I (both alkyl groups converted into iodides when HI is in excess; also water). (b) tert-Butyl iodide + methanol: the 3° alkyl–O bond cleaves by SN1 (the stable cation), not the methyl group by SN2.
Practice 29 Main

Hell–Volhard–Zelinsky

Give the product and conditions to prepare 2-bromopropanoic acid from propanoic acid.

Show answer
  1. Br2 with a catalytic amount of red phosphorus (PBr3), followed by water (H2O work-up): CH3CH2COOH → CH3CHBrCOOH. (The acyl bromide enolises and brominates at the α-carbon.)

How many monochloro products (structural and stereoisomers) are possible from 2-methylbutane in radical chlorination?

Practice 31 Main

Ratio

Bromination of 2-methylpropane with Br2/hν at 125 °C (relative rates 1° : 3° = 1 : 1640): what is the ratio of tert-butyl bromide to isobutyl bromide?

Show answer
  1. Nine primary H and one tertiary H. Rate of formation of isobutyl bromide ∝ 9 × 1 = 9; of tert-butyl bromide ∝ 1 × 1640 = 1640. Ratio = 1640 : 9 ≈ 182 : 1 (99.5 % tert-butyl bromide).

Anti-Markovnikov

Which alkene–reagent pair gives an anti-Markovnikov hydrohalogenation product? (A) CH3CH=CH2/HCl/peroxide (B) CH3CH=CH2/HBr/peroxide (C) CH3CH=CH2/HI/peroxide (D) (CH3)2C=CH2/HBr (no peroxide)

Practice 34 Main

Side reaction

During chlorination of methane, the by-product ethane (traces) is found. Explain.

Show answer
  1. Chain termination by combination of two methyl radicals: CH3• + CH3• → CH3CH3. Also CH2Cl2, CHCl3, CCl4 form by further substitution if chlorine is not kept in deficiency.
