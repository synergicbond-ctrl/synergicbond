4. Preparation of phenols

Industrially the dominant route is the cumene process. Benzene is alkylated with propene in presence of an acid catalyst to give cumene (isopropylbenzene). Air oxidation at the tertiary benzylic C–H, which is weak because the radical formed is benzylic and tertiary, gives cumene hydroperoxide. Treatment with dilute acid then rearranges the hydroperoxide to phenol and acetone.

Reactant:

Reagent: (i) O2 (air); (ii) dilute H2SO4, warm

Product:

Cumene → cumene hydroperoxide → phenol + acetone. One mole of each product forms per mole of cumene.

  1. The hydroperoxide oxygen is protonated and water leaves, giving an electron-deficient oxygen.
  2. The phenyl group migrates from carbon to this oxygen (a Baeyer–Villiger-like shift), producing an oxocarbenium ion, Ph–O–C+(CH3)2.
  3. Water adds to the cation, giving a hemiketal that collapses to phenol and acetone.

Laboratory and other industrial routes follow different logic. A primary aromatic amine is converted through its diazonium salt: diazotisation at 273–278 K and then warming the aqueous diazonium solution replaces N2+ by OH with loss of nitrogen. An arenesulfonic acid (as its sodium salt) fused with solid NaOH at high temperature (about 570 K) gives the sodium phenoxide, acidified to phenol.

Reactant:

Reagent: (i) NaNO2, HCl, 273–278 K; (ii) H2O, warm

Product:

p-Toluidine → p-cresol via the diazonium salt.

Reactant:

Reagent: (i) NaOH (solid), fusion at about 570 K; (ii) H+

Product:

Alkali fusion of sodium benzenesulfonate.

Chlorobenzene is inert to ordinary nucleophiles, but with aqueous NaOH at very high temperature and pressure (about 620 K, 300 atm) it gives sodium phenoxide (Dow process). Nucleophilic aromatic substitution becomes easy when strong electron-withdrawing groups sit ortho/para to the halogen, as in 2,4-dinitrochlorobenzene, which is hydrolysed by warm aqueous alkali. Under very strong base (NaNH2 in liquid ammonia) elimination gives benzyne, and the nucleophile adds to either carbon of the triple bond, so a 1-labelled chlorobenzene gives a product labelled equally at C-1 and C-2.

Key idea. Choose the mechanism from the conditions: unactivated aryl halide + very strong base → benzyne (elimination–addition); activated aryl halide (o/p NO2) + mild base → addition–elimination through a Meisenheimer complex; diazonium salt + water → aryl cation-like loss of N2; cumene hydroperoxide + acid → migration to oxygen.
