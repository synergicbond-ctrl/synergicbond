51. Mechanism: nitrosation of 2° and 3° amines; the fate of aliphatic diazonium ions

  • 2° amine (R2NH). nN → NO+ gives R2N+H–N=O; loss of H+ leaves the neutral N-nitrosamine R2N–N=O. It cannot tautomerise to a diazohydroxide (no second N–H), so the reaction stops there (yellow oil).
  • 3° aliphatic amine. It only gets protonated (an ammonium nitrite salt); the N–NO adduct R3N+–N=O has no H to lose and reverts. (Slow dealkylation with HNO2 can occur on heating – not examined.)
  • 3° aromatic amine (PhNMe2). The ring (HOMO high because of N) is the nucleophile: NO+ attacks para: σ-complex → p-nitroso-N,N-dimethylaniline (green).
  • 1° aliphatic amine. Steps 1–5 of diazotisation give R–N2+; the alkyl diazonium ion loses N2 at once (σ(C–N) → N2 very exergonic): primary carbocation (or concerted with 1,2-shift) → mixture of alcohols, alkenes, alkyl chlorides.
