2.6 Peptide synthesis

Making a defined dipeptide, say Phe–Ala, from Phe and Ala is not simply "mix and heat": that gives all four dipeptides (Phe–Phe, Ala–Ala, Phe–Ala, Ala–Phe) plus polymers. A directed synthesis needs (1) protection of groups that must not react, (2) activation of the carboxyl group, (3) coupling, and (4) deprotection.

Key idea A protecting group must be (i) installed in high yield, (ii) stable to the coupling conditions, (iii) removed selectively without damaging the peptide bonds. The Boc/Cbz pair is orthogonal: TFA removes Boc but not Cbz; hydrogenolysis removes Cbz but not Boc.
<h4>Coupling reagents</h4>

DCC (N,N′-dicyclohexylcarbodiimide) activates the carboxyl: the acid adds to the C=N to give an O-acylisourea (a good acylating agent, like an anhydride); the amino group of the second residue attacks the carbonyl to give the amide and dicyclohexylurea (DCU), which is insoluble and filtered off.

Trap Strong activation of the carboxyl group of an N-acyl amino acid (not carbamate) can form an oxazolone (azlactone), which racemises the α-carbon. Carbamate protecting groups (Boc, Cbz, Fmoc) suppress this, which is why they are preferred. Additives (HOBt) further reduce racemisation.
<h4>Solid-phase synthesis (Merrifield, Nobel Prize 1984)</h4>
  • 1. Attach the C-terminal Boc-(or Fmoc-)amino acid to chloromethylpolystyrene beads through an ester bond.
  • 2. Remove the N-protecting group (TFA for Boc; piperidine for Fmoc).
  • 3. Couple the next protected amino acid with DCC; wash away excess reagents and by-products (just filter and rinse the beads).
  • 4. Repeat steps 2–3 for each residue: the chain grows from C to N.
  • 5. Cleave from the resin (HF or TFA) and remove side-chain protection.

Advantages: automation, no intermediate purification, large excess of reagents drives each coupling; Merrifield made ribonuclease A (124 residues) this way. Disadvantage: errors accumulate: with 99 % yield per cycle a 50-mer is only 0.9950 ≈ 60 % pure.

Shortcut In a "protect, couple, deprotect" question, check which residue ends up N-terminal: the residue whose NH2 is free in the dipeptide had its carboxyl protected; the C-terminal residue had its amine free. For Phe–Ala: protect NH2 of Phe (Boc-Phe), protect COOH of Ala (Ala-OMe).
Example 48 Main

Reagents for a dipeptide

Which pair of reactants gives the dipeptide Phe–Ala by DCC coupling? (i) Boc-Ala-OH (ii) H-Phe-OMe (iii) Boc-Phe-OH (iv) H-Ala-OMe.

Show solution
  1. N-terminal residue Phe must carry the free carboxyl (to be activated) and a protected N: Boc-Phe-OH (iii). C-terminal Ala must carry the free amine and a protected carboxyl: H-Ala-OMe (iv). (iii) + (iv).
Example 49 Adv

Which dipeptides?

Equal moles of Gly and Ala are heated together without protecting groups. List all the dipeptides that can form.

Show solution
  1. N-terminal Gly or Ala; C-terminal Gly or Ala: Gly–Gly, Ala–Ala, Gly–Ala, Ala–Gly = 4 (and also higher peptides and diketopiperazines).
Example 50 Adv

Yield of solid-phase synthesis

Each coupling+deprotection cycle in a Merrifield synthesis is 98 % efficient. What fraction of the chains has the correct sequence after assembling a 20-residue peptide (19 couplings)? And for a 100-residue chain?

Show solution
  1. 20-mer: 0.9819 = 0.681 → 68 %.
  2. 100-mer: 0.9899 = 0.135 → 13.5 % (this is why chemical synthesis is limited to ≈ 50–100 residues; larger proteins are made by gene expression or by ligation of fragments).
