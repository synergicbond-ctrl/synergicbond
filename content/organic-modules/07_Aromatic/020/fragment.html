18. Diazonium salts: a synthetic hub

Primary aromatic amines react with nitrous acid (NaNO2 + HCl, 273–278 K) to give arenediazonium salts, ArN2+X−, which are stable in cold aqueous solution (unlike aliphatic diazonium ions, which decompose at once). The N2 group is replaced by many nucleophiles. Because the amine itself comes from nitration followed by reduction, diazonium chemistry lets us place groups that cannot be introduced by EAS (F, I, CN, OH, H) and in positions that violate directing rules.

Diazonium routes

Prepare from aniline: (a) fluorobenzene (b) iodobenzene (c) benzonitrile → benzoic acid (d) phenol (e) benzene.

Show solution
  1. All start with NaNO2/HCl at 273–278 K → C6H5N2+Cl−.
  2. (a) HBF4 → ArN2+BF4− (precipitates), heat → fluorobenzene + N2 + BF3.
  3. (b) KI(aq) → iodobenzene (no catalyst).
  4. (c) CuCN/KCN → benzonitrile; H3O+, Δ → benzoic acid.
  5. (d) H2O, warm → phenol (add acid to avoid coupling with product).
  6. (e) H3PO2 → benzene.
