47. Mechanisms of nitrile and isocyanide reactions

47.1 Base hydrolysis of a nitrile
  • Step 1 – attack. OH− adds to the nitrile carbon (HOMO of OH− into π*C≡N, the lobe on carbon). The electrons go onto N: imidate anion R–C(OH)=N−.
  • Step 2 – protonation. The N is protonated by water: R–C(OH)=NH (imidic acid).
  • Step 3 – tautomerisation. Imidic acid → amide RCONH2 (the C=O form is far more stable).
  • Step 4 – amide hydrolysis. Further OH− attack (tetrahedral intermediate, loss of NH2− assisted by proton transfer from water) gives RCOO− + NH3.

Hydrolysis of acetonitrile: amide intermediate then carboxylate + NH₃
47.2 Grignard addition to a nitrile
  • Step 1 – coordination. Mg2+ binds the nitrile N lone pair (Lewis acid activation, lowering the π*C≡N LUMO).
  • Step 2 – C–C bond formation. The carbanion-like R' of R'MgX attacks the nitrile carbon; π electrons go to N: the imine anion R–C(R')=N–MgX.
  • Step 3 – no second addition. The N-magnesio-imine is anionic; the C=N is not electrophilic enough for another R'MgX. Why ketones differ: a ketone would be attacked again.
  • Step 4 – hydrolysis. H3O+ protonates N: iminium → water adds → carbinolamine → loss of NH3 → ketone.
47.3 LiAlH4 reduction of a nitrile (two hydride transfers)
  • Step 1: H− from AlH4− attacks the nitrile carbon (σ(Al–H) → π*C≡N): imine anion R–CH=N− bound to Al. Step 2: the Al–N complex is still electrophilic at carbon; a second H− adds giving R–CH2–N2−(Al). Step 3: aqueous work-up protonates N: RCH2NH2.
47.4 Acid hydrolysis of an isocyanide
  • Step 1 – protonation at carbon. The isocyanide carbon carries a lone pair (the σ HOMO, carbene-like) and is the basic/nucleophilic site: H+ adds to C giving the nitrilium ion R–N+≡CH.
  • Step 2 – water attacks the nitrilium carbon (π* LUMO; strongly electrophilic because of the positive charge): R–NH–CH=O+H → R–NH–CHO (N-alkylformamide) after loss of H+.
  • Step 3 – amide hydrolysis (acid): R–NH–CHO + H2O/H+ → RNH3+ + HCOOH.
  • Why stable to base: the unprotonated isocyanide carbon is electron-rich (carbene-like lone pair) and is not attacked by OH−; it becomes electrophilic only after protonation to the nitrilium ion.

Acid hydrolysis of methyl isocyanide: nitrilium ion → N-methylformamide → methylammonium + formic acid
47.5 Isocyanide → nitrile isomerisation

At about 523 K the R group migrates from N to C in a 1,2-shift through a three-centre transition state; the thermodynamically favoured product has the C≡N triple bond with the carbon attached to R (stronger C–C bond, less charge separation). For simple alkyl groups the reaction is first order and intramolecular (not a dissociation–recombination of R• and CN•).
