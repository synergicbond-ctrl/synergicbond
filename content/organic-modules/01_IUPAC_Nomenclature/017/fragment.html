2.5 The alkane isomers of C₄ to C₇, drawn and named

The numbers of alkane isomers are C₄ 2, C₅ 3, C₆ 5, C₇ 9, C₈ 18, C₉ 35, C₁₀ 75. To generate them systematically, start with the unbranched chain, shorten the main chain by one carbon, place the removed carbon on every inequivalent position, and repeat.

Exam Trap Two drawings that look different may be the same molecule: "4-methylhexane" is 3-methylhexane numbered from the other end. When counting isomers, name every candidate; duplicates then appear as identical names. An ethyl group cannot sit on C-2 of a chain (the chain would be longer) and a methyl group cannot sit on C-1.
Think Further

Show that no alkane isomer of C₇H₁₆ has a "3-propyl" or a "4-ethyl" substituent on a six-carbon chain.

Show solution

A propyl group on C-3 of a hexane chain would lie in a longer chain through the propyl group (the chain C–C–C(–)–C–C–C extended by the propyl carbons gives at least seven carbons in a row), so the compound would be named as a heptane with fewer branches. A "4-ethyl" on hexane is equivalent, after renumbering, to a 3-ethyl; and an ethyl on the main chain of a hexane would give a total of eight carbons, not seven.
