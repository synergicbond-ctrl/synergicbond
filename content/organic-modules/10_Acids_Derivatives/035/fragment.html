Quantities that depend on measured constants (pKa values, boiling points, melting points, Kc = 4 for the ethanol/acetic acid esterification, σ and ρ values in the Hammett problem) are literature values rounded to the precision at which exams use them.

What the verification script checked

  • Every structure in the text and in the figures parses and every molecular formula and molar mass quoted for a named compound was recomputed (aspirin, ethyl acetoacetate, the Dieckmann product, lactide, benzocaine, tristearin, triolein, oleic and linoleic acids, barbital, barbituric acid, coumarin, phenolphthalein, benzilic acid, urea nitrate, and others).
  • CIP labels were recomputed for the three tartaric acid stereoisomers, (S)-2-methylbutanoic acid and its Hofmann/Curtius product, 2-phenylpropanoic acid and its amine, the E/Z geometry of crotonic, isocrotonic, cinnamic and α-methyl-cinnamic acids and of acetophenone oxime, and the dibromosuccinic acid enantiomer.
  • Stereoisomer counts (tartaric 3, dibromosuccinic 3, lactide 3, lactic 2, citric achiral, sec-butyl methanoate 2) and constitutional isomer counts (C4H8O2: 2 acids + 4 esters; C5H10O2: 4 acids, 9 esters) were enumerated by program.
  • All numerical answers (pH, buffer, α, Kc and yields, titrations, saponification and iodine values, Kolbe volumes, kinetic constants, Hammett factors, polymer masses, silver-salt and colligative analyses) were recalculated.

Not verified here

  • The pKa, boiling-point, and melting-point values themselves are recalled from standard tables (spot-checked only for ethanol and acetic acid); treat them as ±0.1 pKa unit and a few °C.
  • Qualitative statements about reaction conditions (temperatures, yields, catalysts) come from standard textbook practice and were not experimentally checked.
  • The mapping of these topics onto particular past JEE Advanced papers was not done paper by paper in this chapter.
