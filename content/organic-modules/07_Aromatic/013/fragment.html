11. Disubstituted benzenes: what happens when two groups compete

When a ring already carries two groups, the position of the third (the incoming E+) is decided by a hierarchy of rules. Work through them in order; stop as soon as one rule gives a clear answer.

The decision procedure
StepQuestionRule
1Do the two groups reinforce each other?If both direct to the same carbon, that carbon is substituted (unless it is hindered, step 4)
2If they oppose, which is the stronger activator?The more strongly activating (or less deactivating) group controls the orientation
3Is one group o/p-directing and the other m-directing?The o/p-director normally wins (it stabilises the cation; the m-director merely destabilises)
4Steric screeningThe carbon between two meta-disposed groups (position 2 of a 1,3-disubstituted ring) is almost never substituted; a bulky group suppresses its adjacent (ortho) positions
5Electrophile sizeLarge electrophiles (SO3H, acyl, t-alkyl, Br2FeBr3) prefer the less hindered position and para
Key idea Activating ranking to use in step 2: –O− > –NR2 > –NH2 ≈ –OH > –OR > –NHCOR > –OCOR > –R > –Ph > –H > halogens (o/p, weak deactivators) > –CHO, –COR, –COOR, –COOH, –CN, –SO3H > –NO2, –CF3, –NR3+. The first five are the strong activators; the order within each family follows the donor ability (how freely the lone pair is available).

11.1 Both groups reinforce each other

  • p-Nitrotoluene: CH3 directs to its ortho positions (C2, C6); NO2 directs to *its* meta positions, which are also C2 and C6 (relative to CH3 at C1). Substitution occurs at C2 (ortho to CH3, meta to NO2): nitration gives 2,4-dinitrotoluene.
  • m-Dinitrobenzene (both meta-directors): both direct to C5 (meta to both); C4/C6 are ortho/para to one NO2. Substitution at C5 is possible but extremely slow (1,3,5-trinitrobenzene).
  • o-Nitrophenol / p-nitrophenol: OH directs to the free ortho/para positions; NO2 directs meta to itself, which in these isomers are the same positions: p-nitrophenol + Br2 → 2-bromo-4-nitrophenol (2,6-dibromo-4-nitrophenol with excess).
  • m-Chlorotoluene / m-xylene: positions 4 and 6 are ortho to one methyl and para to the other: both groups reinforce. m-Xylene is nitrated at C4 (giving 4-nitro-m-xylene), almost never at C2 (hindered, between the two methyls) and not at C5 (meta to both).

Example 22 Main

Predict the major product of mononitration

(a) o-nitrotoluene (b) m-nitrotoluene (c) p-chlorotoluene (d) p-nitrophenol (e) p-toluic acid (4-methylbenzoic acid) (f) o-xylene (g) m-dibromobenzene.

Show solution
  1. (a) CH3 at C1, NO2 at C2. CH3 directs to C4 and C6; NO2 directs meta to itself, also C4 and C6. Both groups reinforce: 2,4-dinitrotoluene (major, para to CH3) with 2,6-dinitrotoluene (minor).
  2. (b) CH3 at C1, NO2 at C3. CH3 directs to C2, C4, C6; NO2 directs to C5. The groups conflict, so CH3 (activator) wins; C2 is sandwiched; the attack goes to C4 and C6 (both are ortho/para to the NO2, which costs rate): 3,4-dinitrotoluene and 2,5-dinitrotoluene. The reaction is slow and the mixture is the typical result.
  3. (c) CH3 is the stronger activator than Cl: 4-chloro-2-nitrotoluene (nitro ortho to CH3).
  4. (d) OH controls, NO2 is already at para: 2,4-dinitrophenol (ortho to OH, meta to the existing NO2: the groups reinforce).
  5. (e) CH3 directs ortho (C3); COOH directs meta (C3): reinforce, 4-methyl-3-nitrobenzoic acid.
  6. (f) o-Xylene: 4-nitro-o-xylene (1,2-dimethyl-4-nitrobenzene) is the main product; 3-nitro is a substantial minor product (adjacent to a CH3).
  7. (g) Br at C1 and C3: C4 and C6 are ortho to one Br and para to the other: 1,3-dibromo-4-nitrobenzene (2,4-dibromonitrobenzene); C2 is sandwiched.
Example 23 Adv

Mono- or poly-substitution?

Nitrate p-xylene, m-xylene, mesitylene and hemimellitene (1,2,3-trimethylbenzene) once. How many mononitro products does each give?

Show solution
  1. p-Xylene: all four ring H equivalent → 1 (2-nitro-1,4-dimethylbenzene).
  2. m-Xylene: three different ring H (C2, C4=C6, C5), so 3 are possible; C4 is strongly favoured (ortho to one CH3, para to the other), C2 is hindered, C5 is meta to both: one major product (4-nitro).
  3. Mesitylene: all three ring H equivalent → 1 (2-nitromesitylene).
  4. 1,2,3-Trimethylbenzene: ring H at C4=C6 and at C5: 2 possible; C4 (ortho to C3-CH3, para to C1-CH3) is activated twice and is the major product, 4-nitro-1,2,3-trimethylbenzene.

The ortho fraction falls as the group enlarges: steric hindrance to the incoming NO2+ at the ortho carbon. Notice also that m rises slightly for t-butyl (the o/p carbons are hindered and the inductive effect is not much changed). In acylation, which has a larger electrophile, ortho disappears (≈ 1 % for toluene).

Trap The para product is not always dominant: toluene gives ortho > para in nitration and in chlorination, and phenol gives roughly equal amounts. Dominant para is expected with bulky groups (t-Bu) or bulky electrophiles (acylation, sulphonation, bromination with FeBr3 in solvent) and in halobenzenes.

11.4 Sandwiched positions

When two groups are meta to each other, the carbon between them (C2) is usually unreactive: it is sterically shielded by both. A typical case: m-xylene is nitrated at C4, not C2, although C2 is ortho to both methyls. 1,2,3-Trisubstituted products are therefore seldom made by EAS of an m-disubstituted benzene: use a different strategy (blocking group, a diazonium route, or start from the 1,2,3-pattern).

Example 24 Adv

A meta director and an o/p director that are meta to each other

Predict the position of the next nitro group in (a) 3-nitroacetanilide (b) 3-nitrobenzoic acid (c) 1,3-dinitrobenzene.

Show solution
  1. (a) NHCOCH3 at C1, NO2 at C3. NHCOCH3 directs to C2, C4, C6; C2 is sandwiched. C4 (para to N, ortho to NO2) and C6 (ortho to N, para to NO2) both receive activation from N but deactivation from NO2: a mixture of 3,4-dinitro- and 2,5-dinitroacetanilide, formed slowly. This is not a good preparative route: a better plan is to nitrate acetanilide to the 2,4-dinitro compound.
  2. (b) Both groups are meta directors and both direct to C5: 3,5-dinitrobenzoic acid (fuming HNO3, H2SO4, heating).
  3. (c) 1,3-Dinitrobenzene → 1,3,5-trinitrobenzene (C5, meta to both), but only with very forcing conditions and in poor yield (the standard route to TNB is from TNT by oxidation to trinitrobenzoic acid then decarboxylation).
