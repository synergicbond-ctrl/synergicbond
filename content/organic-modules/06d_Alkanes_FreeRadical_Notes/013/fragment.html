Practice 1 L1 Foundation

The hybridisation of carbon in methane and the H–C–H bond angle are respectively

(A) sp2, 120°

(B) sp3, 109.5°

(C) sp3, 90°

(D) sp, 180°

Show answer
  1. Carbon in CH4 forms four σ bonds using four equivalent hybrid orbitals made from one 2s and three 2p orbitals: sp3.
  2. Four equivalent bonds point to the corners of a regular tetrahedron, giving H–C–H = 109.5°. Hence (B).

Answer: B

Practice 2 L1 Foundation

Which of the following has the highest boiling point?

(A) 2,2-Dimethylpropane

(B) 2-Methylbutane

(C) Pentane

(D) Butane

Show answer
  1. Boiling point is governed by London dispersion forces, which increase with molecular surface area. Pentane is unbranched and has five carbons.
  2. Branching lowers the boiling point (pentane 36 °C > 2-methylbutane 28 °C > 2,2-dimethylpropane 9.5 °C), and butane (−0.5 °C) has one carbon fewer. Hence (C).

Answer: C

Practice 3 L1 Foundation

Among the isomers of C5H12 the one with the highest melting point is

(A) Pentane

(B) 2-Methylbutane

(C) 2,2-Dimethylpropane

Show answer
  1. Melting point depends on the efficiency of crystal packing, which is best for the most symmetrical molecule.
  2. 2,2-Dimethylpropane is almost spherical and melts at about −17 °C, compared with −130 °C for pentane and −160 °C for 2-methylbutane. Hence (C).

Answer: C

Practice 4 L1 Foundation

How many σ bonds are present in one molecule of 2-methylbutane?

Show answer
  1. 2-Methylbutane is C5H12: C–C bonds = 5 − 1 = 4 and C–H bonds = 12.
  2. Total σ bonds = 4 + 12 = 16 (= 3n + 1 for n = 5). There are no π bonds.

Answer: 16

Practice 5 L1 Foundation

Methyl bromide and ethyl bromide are heated with sodium in dry ether (Wurtz reaction). How many different alkanes can form?

Show answer
  1. Possible couplings: CH3–CH3, CH3–C2H5 and C2H5–C2H5.
  2. These are ethane, propane and butane: n(n+1)/2 = 2 × 3/2 = 3.

Answer: 3

Practice 6 L1 Foundation

A mixture of CH3Cl, C2H5Cl and (CH3)2CHCl is treated with sodium in dry ether. How many different alkanes can be obtained?

Show answer
  1. For n different halides the number of R–R' combinations, including R = R', is n(n+1)/2 = 3 × 4/2 = 6.
  2. Here all six are different compounds: ethane (Me–Me), propane (Me–Et), 2-methylpropane (Me–iPr), butane (Et–Et), 2-methylbutane (Et–iPr) and 2,3-dimethylbutane (iPr–iPr). Count = 6.

Answer: 6

Practice 7 L1 Foundation

Electrolysis of aqueous sodium propanoate (Kolbe reaction) gives, at the anode, the hydrocarbon

(A) Ethane

(B) Propane

(C) Butane

(D) Pentane

Show answer
  1. At the anode CH3CH2COO− loses e− and CO2 to give the ethyl radical CH3CH2•.
  2. Two ethyl radicals couple: CH3CH2–CH2CH3, i.e. butane (twice the alkyl chain). Hence (C).

Answer: C

Practice 8 L1 Foundation

Sodium butanoate, CH3CH2CH2COONa, is heated with soda lime. The alkane formed is

(A) Methane

(B) Ethane

(C) Propane

(D) Butane

Show answer

  2. The residue R = CH3CH2CH2–, so the product is propane, C3H8 (one carbon fewer than the acid). Hence (C).

Practice 10 L1 Foundation

Write the initiation step of the light-induced chlorination of methane.

Show answer
  1. The weakest bond in the mixture is Cl–Cl (243 kJ mol−1, compared with C–H 439), so photons of suitable energy cleave it.
  2. Homolysis gives two chlorine atoms, each with an unpaired electron: Cl–Cl → 2 Cl•. No radical is consumed in this step.

Answer: Cl–Cl → 2 Cl• (homolysis, hν)

Practice 11 L1 Foundation

How many propagation steps are there in the chain mechanism of monochlorination of methane?

Show answer
  1. Propagation 1: CH4 + Cl• → CH3• + HCl.
  2. Propagation 2: CH3• + Cl2 → CH3Cl + Cl•. These two steps repeat, so the answer is 2.

Answer: 2

Practice 12 L1 Foundation

Which of the following is NOT a chain-termination step in the chlorination of methane?

(A) Cl• + Cl• → Cl2

(B) CH3• + Cl• → CH3Cl

(C) CH3• + Cl2 → CH3Cl + Cl•

(D) CH3• + CH3• → C2H6

Show answer
  1. A termination step removes two radicals and produces no radical. (A), (B) and (D) each combine two radicals into a molecule.
  2. In (C) a radical (CH3•) reacts with a molecule (Cl2) and a new radical Cl• is formed, so the chain continues: this is propagation 2. Hence (C).

Answer: C

Practice 13 L1 Foundation

Which halogen reacts with alkanes most violently, even in the dark?

(A) Fluorine

(B) Chlorine

(C) Bromine

(D) Iodine

Show answer
  1. Both propagation steps with F• and F2 are strongly exothermic (about −129 and −293 kJ mol−1 for methane) with almost no barrier.
  2. Chlorine needs light or heat, bromine is slower and iodine does not react. The order is F2 > Cl2 > Br2 > I2. Hence (A).

Answer: A

Practice 14 L1 Foundation

The correct order of stability of alkyl radicals is

(A) CH3• > 1° > 2° > 3°

(B) 3° > 2° > 1° > CH3•

(C) 2° > 3° > 1° > CH3•

(D) 1° > 2° > 3° > CH3•

Show answer
  1. Alkyl radicals are stabilised by hyperconjugation with α C–H and C–C bonds; more alkyl substituents mean more such interactions.
  2. The tert-butyl radical has 9 α-hydrogens, isopropyl 6, ethyl 3, methyl 0. So 3° > 2° > 1° > CH3•. Hence (B).

Answer: B

Practice 18 L1 Foundation

The reagent used for allylic or benzylic bromination without addition to the C=C bond is

(A) Br2 in the dark

(B) HBr

(C) N-Bromosuccinimide, light or a peroxide, CCl4

(D) Br2, FeBr3

Show answer
  1. NBS releases a very low, steady concentration of Br2 (from NBS + HBr), which favours radical hydrogen abstraction at the weak allylic C–H bond.
  2. Br2 in the dark would add across C=C, HBr is not a brominating agent for C–H, and Br2/FeBr3 brominates the aromatic ring. Hence (C).

Answer: C

Practice 19 L1 Foundation

The most stable conformation of ethane is

(A) Eclipsed, with torsional strain of 12 kJ mol−1 relative to staggered

(B) Staggered

(C) Both are equally stable because rotation is free

(D) A skew-boat form

Show answer
  1. In the staggered form each C–H bond on one carbon bisects the H–C–H angle of the other, so there are no H/H eclipsing repulsions.
  2. The eclipsed form lies 12 kJ mol−1 higher, so staggered is the minimum (rotation is rapid at room temperature but the molecule spends most time near staggered). Hence (B).

Answer: B

Practice 20 L1 Foundation

In the eclipsed conformation of ethane there are three H/H eclipsing interactions, each contributing about 4.0 kJ mol−1. What is the energy barrier (kJ mol−1) to rotation about the C–C bond?

Show answer
  1. The staggered conformer has no eclipsing and is the reference zero.
  2. The eclipsed conformer has 3 × 4.0 = 12 kJ mol−1 of torsional strain, equal to the barrier.

Answer: 12

Practice 21 L1 Foundation

How many moles of O2 are needed for the complete combustion of 2 mol of hexane?

Show answer
  1. Hexane C6H14 + (3n+1)/2 O2 with n = 6: C6H14 + 9.5 O2 → 6 CO2 + 7 H2O (O balance: 12 + 7 = 19 O atoms = 9.5 O2).
  2. For 2 mol hexane: 2 × 9.5 = 19 mol O2.

Practice 22 L1 Foundation

Heating a higher alkane at high temperature in the absence of air so that it breaks into smaller alkanes and alkenes is called

(A) Isomerisation

(B) Cracking (pyrolysis)

(C) Aromatisation

(D) Hydrogenolysis

Show answer
  1. Thermal fragmentation of C–C bonds in the absence of oxygen is pyrolysis, and in the petroleum industry it is called cracking.
  2. Isomerisation changes the skeleton without changing the formula, aromatisation forms benzene rings, and hydrogenolysis uses H2. Hence (B).

How many moles of Cl2 are consumed in converting 1 mol of ethane completely into hexachloroethane, C2Cl6?

Practice 27 L1 Foundation

Ethylmagnesium bromide reacts with water to give mainly

(A) Ethane

(B) Butane

(C) Ethanol

(D) Ethene

Practice 40 L2 Main

Free-radical chlorination of butane at C-2 yields 2-chlorobutane. Which statements are correct?

(A) The intermediate 2-butyl radical is planar (sp2) at the radical carbon

(B) The product is a racemic mixture

(C) The product is optically active because a new stereocentre forms

(D) The product 2-chlorobutane is formed with inversion of configuration

Show answer

Practice 41 L2 Main

Which statements about alkyl radicals are correct?

(A) They are paramagnetic

(B) The methyl radical is trigonal planar

Show answer

Practice 50 L3 Advanced

Which statements about the halogenation of propane are consistent with the Hammond postulate?

(A) Abstraction of a hydrogen by Br• is endothermic and its transition state resembles the alkyl radical

(B) Abstraction by Cl• is exothermic and its transition state is early

(C) Br• is more selective than Cl•

(D) Cl• is more selective than Br• because Cl• is more stable

Show answer

  2. The endothermic step has a late, radical-like transition state that feels the full 3° > 2° > 1° stability difference, so Br• is the more selective reagent; Cl• has an early transition state and low selectivity. (D) is wrong in its conclusion. Answer A, B, C.

Practice 51 L2 Main

Given bond dissociation enthalpies (kJ mol−1): C–H (CH4) 439, H–Br 366. Calculate ΔH (kJ mol−1) for the step CH4 + Br• → CH3• + HBr.

Show answer
  1. ΔH = energy of bonds broken − energy of bonds formed = D(C–H) − D(H–Br).
  2. ΔH = 439 − 366 = +73 kJ mol−1 (endothermic, so the step is slow and highly selective).

Answer: 73
