26. Retention of N2: reduction and coupling

Reduction. ArN2+Cl− + SnCl2/HCl (or Na2SO3) → phenylhydrazine ArNHNH2. Phenylhydrazine reacts with aldehydes/ketones to give phenylhydrazones and with excess sugar (osazone formation).

26.1 Azo coupling

An arenediazonium ion is a weak electrophile (the positive charge is delocalised over the diazo group). It attacks highly activated aromatic rings (phenols as phenoxide ions, aromatic amines, naphthols) in an electrophilic aromatic substitution to give an azo compound Ar–N=N–Ar', normally coloured (a dye). Substitution takes place at para to the activating group, or ortho if para is blocked.

Coupling with phenol: 4-hydroxyazobenzene (p-hydroxyazobenzene, yellow-orange)

Coupling with aniline: 4-aminoazobenzene (aniline yellow)
+ NaOH, 273 K⟶
Coupling with β-naphthol at C-1: 1-(phenylazo)-2-naphthol (Sudan I, orange-red)

The pH rules (important):

  • Phenols: weakly basic medium, pH 9–10. The phenoxide ion is a far stronger donor than phenol itself, so coupling is fast; at pH > 10 the diazonium ion is converted to the unreactive diazotate Ar–N=N–O− (the electrophile is lost).
  • Aromatic amines: weakly acidic medium, pH 4–5. Enough free amine must be present to couple; in strongly acidic solution the amine is fully protonated (ArNH3+, no donor), and at higher pH the diazonium is converted to the diazotate.
  • Never strongly acidic (pH < 3) for amines; never strongly basic (pH > 11) for phenols.
  • If both ortho and para are free, coupling occurs at para. If para is blocked, at ortho (slower).
  • Coupling with a 1° or 2° amine can occur at N (N-coupling, giving a diazoamino compound/triazene, Ar–N=N–NHAr'), which rearranges to the C-coupled p-aminoazo compound on warming with the amine hydrochloride. Tertiary amines (N,N-dimethylaniline) couple at C-para only.
  • The azo group –N=N– is the chromophore (it, with the conjugated rings, absorbs visible light); groups such as –OH, –NMe2, –SO3Na that intensify colour or confer solubility are auxochromes.
+ pH 4–5⟶
Coupling with N,N-dimethylaniline: 4-(dimethylamino)azobenzene (butter yellow)
Example 26 Main

Reagents

What are the conditions for coupling benzenediazonium chloride with (a) phenol and (b) aniline, and why do they differ?

Show solution
  1. (a) Phenol: mildly alkaline medium, pH 9–10. The phenoxide ion is a much stronger nucleophile than phenol.
  2. (b) Aniline: mildly acidic medium, pH 4–5. In strong acid aniline is protonated (no lone pair); in alkali the diazonium ion is converted into diazotate.
