Aniline: diazotisation then coupling: the red azo dye proves a primary aromatic amine
IMP Hinsberg in words: primary amine → RNHSO2Ph, which still has an N–H and is acidic (pKa ≈ 10-11), dissolves in aqueous NaOH. Secondary amine → R2NSO2Ph, no N–H, insoluble in alkali. Tertiary amine → no sulphonamide; remains as insoluble amine, dissolves in dilute HCl.
Exam Trap Aniline + HNO2 at 273 K gives a diazonium salt, not nitrogen gas, whereas ethylamine gives N2 at once, because aliphatic diazonium ions are unstable. If the temperature rises above 283 K aryl diazonium salts decompose to phenol + N2. N-methylaniline gives a yellow oil (N-nitroso), not a gas. The carbylamine test is *not* given by 2°/3° amines, amides or nitro compounds.
