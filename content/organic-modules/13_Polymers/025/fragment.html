21. Molecular mass of polymers

A synthetic polymer is a mixture of chains of different length, so no single M exists. We describe it by averages and a distribution.

21.1 The averages

Let Ni = number of molecules of mass Mi, wi = mass fraction of those molecules.

Formula Number-average Mn = ΣNiMi / ΣNi = total mass / total molecules = 1 / Σ(wi/Mi)
Formula Weight-average Mw = ΣNiMi2 / ΣNiMi = Σ wiMi
Formula Z-average Mz = ΣNiMi3 / ΣNiMi2 Viscosity-average Mv = ( ΣNiMi1+a / ΣNiMi )1/a (a = Mark–Houwink exponent, 0.5–0.8)
Formula Polydispersity index PDI (Đ) = Mw/Mn ≥ 1. Order: Mn ≤ Mv ≤ Mw ≤ Mz
  • Mn counts molecules equally; small chains pull it down. It is the average that colligative properties (osmotic pressure, ebullioscopy, cryoscopy, vapour-pressure lowering) and end-group analysis measure.
  • Mw weights each chain by its mass; large chains pull it up. Measured by light scattering (Rayleigh) and ultracentrifugation.
  • Mv from intrinsic viscosity [η] = K·Mva (Mark–Houwink–Sakurada), with Mv close to Mw.
  • PDI = 1 only for a monodisperse sample (proteins, living anionic polymer). Typical: living anionic 1.01–1.1; metallocene 2; free-radical 1.5–2 (2 for disproportionation, 1.5 for pure combination); step-growth at high conversion 2 (Flory: PDI = 1 + p); Ziegler–Natta PE 4–8; LDPE up to 20+.

A single smooth, skewed distribution has Mn on the low side of the peak and Mw on the high side, because the long tail of heavy chains pulls Mw up.

21.2 Proof that Mw ≥ Mn

Mw/Mn = (ΣNiMi2)(ΣNi) / (ΣNiMi)2. By the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, (Σ NiMi)2 = (Σ √Ni · √NiMi)2 ≤ (ΣNi)(ΣNiMi2), so the ratio is ≥ 1, with equality only if all Mi are equal. Equivalent statement: Mw − Mn = (variance of M over the number distribution)/Mn ≥ 0.

21.3 Degree of polymerisation

Formula DP = Mpolymer / Mrepeat unit (for A–A + B–B polymers the number of monomer molecules per chain is 2 × DP)
Numbers to remember: repeat-unit masses: PE 28.05, PP 42.08, PVC 62.50, PS 104.15, PAN 53.06, PTFE 100.02, PMMA 100.12, PVAc 86.09, nylon-6 113.16, nylon-6,6 226.32, PET 192.17, isoprene unit 68.12.
Validity Check The number-average formula Mn = ΣNiMi/ΣNi is exact for any list of species. Osmotic-pressure results are reliable only in dilute solution; strictly one plots π/c against c and extrapolates c → 0, because the polymer solution deviates from ideal behaviour. End-group analysis works only while the number of end groups is large enough to titrate (Mn below about 2.5 × 104) and only for linear chains with one end group of the type analysed per chain.
Example 15 Think Further

Does adding a trace of high-mass polymer raise Mn much?

A sample of 1.00 g of mass 10 000 has 0.010 g of mass 1 000 000 added. Estimate the change in Mn and in Mw.

Show solution
  1. Before: monodisperse, Mn = Mw = 10 000.
  2. After: total mass 1.010 g; moles = 1.00/10 000 + 0.010/106 = 1.0000 × 10-4 + 1.0 × 10-8 = 1.0001 × 10-4. Mn = 1.010/1.0001 × 10-4 = 10 099 (+1.0 %).
  3. Mw = (1.00 × 10 000 + 0.010 × 106)/1.010 = 20 000/1.010 = 19 802 (+98 %).
  4. Lesson: Mw weights by mass and is very sensitive to a small high-mass tail; Mn counts molecules and barely moves.

21.4 Worked examples (Level: Main to Advanced)

Example 16 Main

DP of PVC and PS

A PVC sample has average mass 1.25 × 105 g mol-1; a polystyrene has 2.08 × 105. Find DP of each.

Show solution
  1. PVC repeat unit CH2CHCl = 62.5, so DP = 125 000/62.5 = 2000.
  2. PS repeat unit C8H8 = 104.15 (≈ 104), so DP = 208 000/104 = 2000.
Example 17 Main

Mn and Mw for a small sample

A sample contains 3 mol of chains of mass 10 000 g mol-1 and 2 mol of chains of mass 30 000 g mol-1. Find Mn, Mw, PDI.

Show solution
  1. Mn = (3 × 104 + 2 × 3 × 104)/5 = 9 × 104/5 = 18 000.
  2. ΣNM² = 3 × 108 + 2 × 9 × 108 = 2.1 × 109; ΣNM = 9 × 104. Mw = 2.1 × 109/9 × 104 = 23 333.
  3. PDI = 23 333/18 000 = 1.30.
Example 18 Adv

Mixing by weight

40 g of a monodisperse polymer of mass 20 000 is blended with 60 g of one of mass 80 000. Find Mn, Mw and PDI.

Show solution
  1. Use mass fractions: w1 = 0.40, w2 = 0.60.
  2. Mw = 0.40 × 20 000 + 0.60 × 80 000 = 56 000.
  3. Mn = 1/(0.40/20 000 + 0.60/80 000) = 1/(2.0 × 10−5 + 0.75 × 10−5) = 1/(2.75 × 10−5) = 36 364.
  4. PDI = 56 000/36 364 = 1.54.
  5. Check by moles: 2.0 × 10−3 mol + 0.75 × 10−3 mol = 2.75 × 10−3 mol total; Mn = 100/2.75 × 10−3 = 36 364 ✓.
Example 19 Adv

Osmometry gives Mn

Osmotic pressure of a solution of 5.00 g polymer in 1.00 L of solvent at 300 K is 2.46 × 10−3 atm. Find Mn. (R = 0.0821 L atm K-1 mol-1)

Show solution
  1. π = (c/Mn)RT (dilute solution, c in g L-1). So Mn = cRT/π = 5.00 × 0.0821 × 300/2.46 × 10−3 = 123.15/2.46 × 10−3 = 5.0 × 104 g mol-1.
  2. Why osmotic pressure and not freezing-point depression: for M = 50 000 the freezing-point depression of a 5 g L-1 solution in water is ~ 10−4 K (too small to measure), while osmotic pressure of 2.46 × 10−3 atm equals ≈ 2.5 cm of water... large enough with a sensitive membrane osmometer. Colligative properties count molecules, so the result is Mn.
Example 20 Adv

End-group analysis

2.50 g of a linear nylon is dissolved and titrated; 2.0 mL of 0.100 M HCl neutralises the terminal –NH2 groups. Each chain has one –NH2. Find Mn.

Show solution
  1. Moles –NH2 = 0.100 × 2.0 × 10−3 = 2.0 × 10−4 mol = moles of chains.
  2. Mn = 2.50 g/2.0 × 10−4 mol = 12 500 g mol-1. (Only valid for low masses; at M > 25 000 the end-group count is too small to titrate accurately.)
Example 21 Adv

Viscosity–average mass

[η] = K Ma with K = 1.0 × 10−2 mL g-1, a = 0.70 for a polymer of M = 1.0 × 105. Find [η]. By what factor does [η] change if M is doubled?

Show solution
  1. [η] = 0.010 × (105)0.70 = 0.010 × 103.5 = 0.010 × 3162 = 31.6 mL g-1.
  2. Doubling M multiplies [η] by 20.70 = 1.62.
Example 22 Adv

How much conversion for a given nylon mass?

Nylon-6,6 of Mn = 20 000 is wanted from stoichiometric adipic acid and hexamethylenediamine. What extent of reaction p is required? (Mean mass per monomer unit = 226.32/2 = 113.16.)

Show solution
  1. Xn = 20 000/113.16 = 176.7 monomer residues per chain.
  2. Xn = 1/(1 − p) → 1 − p = 1/176.7 = 0.00566 → p = 0.9943 (99.43 % of the functional groups must have reacted).
Example 23 Adv

Mass balance of nylon-6,6

How much adipic acid (146.14) and hexamethylenediamine (116.21) are needed for 1.000 kg of high-mass nylon-6,6 (repeat unit 226.32), and how much water forms?

Show solution
  1. Repeat unit forms from 1 acid + 1 amine − 2 H2O: 146.14 + 116.21 − 36.03 = 226.32 ✓.
  2. Moles of repeat units = 1000/226.32 = 4.419 mol.
  3. Adipic acid = 4.419 × 146.14 = 645.7 g; amine = 4.419 × 116.21 = 513.5 g; water = 4.419 × 36.03 = 159.2 g. (645.7 + 513.5 − 159.2 = 1000.0 ✓.)
Example 24 Adv

Composition of Buna-S

A Buna-S contains 3 butadiene molecules for each styrene. Find the mass percentage of styrene and the elemental C : H atom ratio.

Show solution
  1. Butadiene C4H6 = 54.09; styrene C8H8 = 104.15. Per 3:1 block: mass = 3 × 54.09 + 104.15 = 266.42.
  2. Styrene % = 104.15/266.42 = 39.1 % (commercial SBR has ≈ 23 % by mass, i.e. about 1 styrene per 6 butadiene).
  3. C = 3 × 4 + 8 = 20, H = 3 × 6 + 8 = 26. C : H = 20 : 26 = 10 : 13.
Example 25 Main

Elemental percentages

Calculate the %Cl in PVC and in neoprene, and %N in PAN.

Show solution
  1. PVC (C2H3Cl, 62.50): Cl = 35.45/62.50 = 56.7 %.
  2. Neoprene (C4H5Cl, 88.54): Cl = 35.45/88.54 = 40.0 %.
  3. PAN (C3H3N, 53.06): N = 14.01/53.06 = 26.4 %.
Example 26 Adv

Sulphur in vulcanised rubber

A vulcanised rubber contains 2.0 % S by mass (rest isoprene units, C5H8 = 68.12). How many isoprene units are there per sulphur atom? If each cross-link uses two S atoms, per how many units is there one cross-link?

Show solution
  1. In 100 g: S = 2.0/32.06 = 0.0624 mol; isoprene = 98.0/68.12 = 1.439 mol.
  2. Units per S atom = 1.439/0.0624 = 23.
  3. One cross-link = 2 S → 46 isoprene units per cross-link (assuming all S forms –S–S– bridges).
Concept Check C17 Checkpoint

Averages

A blend contains 1 mol of mass 20 000 and 1 mol of mass 60 000. Find Mn and Mw.

Show answer

Mn = (20 000 + 60 000)/2 = 40 000. Mw = (20 000² + 60 000²)/(80 000) = (4 × 108 + 36 × 108)/80 000 = 50 000.
