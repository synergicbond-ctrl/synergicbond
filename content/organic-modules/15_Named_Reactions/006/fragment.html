4. Kolbe electrolysis (anodic decarboxylative dimerisation)

Conditions: concentrated aqueous solution of the sodium or potassium salt of a carboxylic acid, electrolysed with inert (Pt) electrodes. Anode (oxidation): 2 RCOO− → 2 RCOO• → 2 R• + 2 CO2 → R–R. Cathode (reduction): 2 H2O + 2 e− → H2 + 2 OH−, so the solution turns alkaline and H2 is liberated at the cathode. The carbon count of the alkane is 2(n − 1) for a salt RCOONa having n carbons. Mixed salts give a statistical mixture, exactly like the mixed Wurtz.

+ electrolysis, anode⟶ + +
Sodium ethanoate → ethane (2 CO2 at the anode, H2 at the cathode)
electrolysis⟶ + +
Succinate (a dicarboxylate) → ethene
electrolysis⟶ + +
Maleate or fumarate → ethyne
Key idea Counting question: "How many moles of CO2 per mole of alkane?" Always 2, from the two carboxylate groups. Gases from sodium propanoate: at the anode butane and CO2; at the cathode H2.
Trap Kolbe fails for salts whose radical is unstable to further oxidation, for strongly electron-withdrawing α-groups, and its yield drops for branched acids (a competing "non-Kolbe" path gives the carbocation, then alkene or alcohol). With a sodium alkanoate in methanol/NaOH the radical dimerises; with a Pt anode in water alkenes and esters can also form.
Example 4 Main

Kolbe with a mixture

A 1 : 1 mixture of sodium acetate and sodium propanoate is electrolysed. Give the alkanes formed and the number of moles of CO2 evolved per mole of total alkane.

Show solution
  1. Radicals: CH3• and C2H5•. Couplings: ethane, propane, butane (statistical 1 : 2 : 1).
  2. Each C–C bond formed consumes two carboxylate groups and releases 2 CO2, whatever the alkane: 2 mol CO2 per mol of alkane.
Example 5 Adv

Kolbe sequence

Which sodium salt on Kolbe electrolysis gives (i) 2,3-dimethylbutane, (ii) but-2-ene?

Show solution
  1. (i) 2,3-Dimethylbutane = (CH3)2CH–CH(CH3)2: the radical is (CH3)2CH•, from sodium 2-methylpropanoate.
  2. (ii) A dicarboxylate R–CH(COO−)–CH(COO−)–R′ loses both carboxylates as CO2 and forms the C=C bond: R–CH=CH–R′. For but-2-ene R = R′ = CH3, so the salt is disodium 2,3-dimethylbutanedioate (2,3-dimethylsuccinate). The E/Z ratio is not controlled by the reaction, so Kolbe is not used for stereo-defined alkenes.
