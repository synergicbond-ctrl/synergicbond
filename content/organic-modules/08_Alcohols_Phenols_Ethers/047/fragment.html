8.3 The iodoform (haloform) test

A compound that contains the CH3–CH(OH)– group (or CH3–CO–) gives yellow iodoform, CHI3 (mp 119 °C, antiseptic smell), when warmed with I2 and NaOH (or NaOI). The reagent first oxidises a CH3CH(OH)R alcohol to the methyl ketone, so the alcohol group counts for the test; then the three α-hydrogens of CH3CO– are replaced by iodine (each substitution makes the next one faster by the −I effect of I on the enolate), and hydroxide cleaves the CI3–CO bond (the CI3− group is a good leaving group) to give CHI3 and the carboxylate.

I₂, NaOH⟶ +
Propan-2-ol → iodoform + sodium ethanoate (via propanone)

Positive: ethanol (the only primary alcohol), ethanal (the only aldehyde), propan-2-ol, butan-2-ol, propanone, butanone, pentan-2-one, acetophenone, 1-phenylethanol, ethyl acetoacetate (it has a CH3CO– group). Negative: methanol, propan-1-ol, butan-1-ol, pentan-3-ol, pentan-3-one, 2-methylpropan-2-ol, methanal, benzaldehyde, benzyl alcohol, ethanoic acid and its esters (the CH3CO– is part of an acid derivative, not a ketone).

Trap Only the CH3–C(=O)–R or CH3–CH(OH)–R unit counts, where R is any carbon or H. Pentan-3-one has no methyl ketone; 2-methylpropan-2-ol has no α-H on the carbinol carbon so cannot be oxidised to a ketone. Acetic acid and its esters do not give the test. Ethanol is the only primary alcohol and ethanal the only aldehyde that are positive.
Example 24 L2 Main

Iodoform candidates

Which of the following give yellow precipitate with I2/NaOH: ethanol, methanol, butan-2-ol, pentan-3-ol, 1-phenylethanol, acetone, diethyl ketone, acetophenone, benzyl alcohol, propan-1-ol?

Show solution
  1. Need CH3CH(OH)– or CH3CO–.
  2. Ethanol ✓ (CH3CH2OH has CH3CH(OH)–: the carbinol carbon is attached to CH3 and H). Butan-2-ol ✓. 1-Phenylethanol ✓. Acetone ✓. Acetophenone ✓.
  3. Methanol ✗, pentan-3-ol ✗ (carbinol attached to two ethyls), diethyl ketone ✗, benzyl alcohol ✗, propan-1-ol ✗.
  4. Answer: ethanol, butan-2-ol, 1-phenylethanol, acetone, acetophenone (five).
Example 25 L1 Foundation

Distinguishing pairs

Name one chemical test to distinguish (a) ethanol from methanol; (b) propan-1-ol from propan-2-ol; (c) butan-2-ol from 2-methylpropan-2-ol; (d) ethanol from propan-2-ol.

Show solution
  1. (a) Iodoform test: ethanol positive, methanol negative.
  2. (b) Iodoform test: propan-2-ol positive, propan-1-ol negative (also Lucas: 2° turbid in 5 min; Victor Meyer: blue vs red).
  3. (c) Lucas reagent (3° immediately, 2° slowly) or Cu/573 K (2° → ketone; 3° → alkene, decolourises Br2 water).
  4. (d) Both are iodoform-positive. Use oxidation: ethanol → ethanal/acetic acid; propan-2-ol → propanone (the aldehyde reduces Tollens’ reagent, the ketone does not); or Lucas (2° turbid, 1° not).
