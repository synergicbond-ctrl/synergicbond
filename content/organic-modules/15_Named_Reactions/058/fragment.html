52. Wagner–Meerwein rearrangement and carbocation rearrangements

Any carbocation that has an adjacent C–H or C–C bond giving a more stable cation will rearrange (1,2-hydride or 1,2-alkyl/aryl shift). It explains: products of E1/SN1; hydration or HX addition to alkenes with a quaternary neighbour; Friedel–Crafts alkylation; dehydration of neopentyl-type alcohols; and the bicyclic terpene examples (camphene ⇌ isobornyl chloride). The driving force is the increase in carbocation stability, so the sequence 1° → 2° → 3° (and allylic or benzylic best) is the direction; it is faster than most other steps, so the rearranged product often dominates.

HBr⟶
Neopentyl alcohol → 2-bromo-2-methylbutane: protonation, loss of water is concerted with CH3 shift (1° → 3° cation) and Br− captures the 3° cation
+ HBr (Markovnikov, then 1,2-CH₃ shift)⟶
3,3-Dimethylbut-1-ene + HBr gives mainly 2-bromo-2,3-dimethylbutane (not 2-bromo-3,3-dimethylbutane): H+ adds to CH2, the 2° cation takes CH3 from the quaternary carbon and becomes 3°
Trap "Does rearrangement occur?" A carbocation rearranges only if it gives a more stable one. 2-Methylbutan-2-yl (3°) cation does not rearrange; the 3-methylbutan-2-yl (2°) cation does (hydride shift to the 3° cation). A 1° cation is never an intermediate: the shift is concerted with leaving-group loss. Radical, carbanion and SN2 paths do not rearrange this way.
