5.2 Choosing the chain: the principal groups come first

Example 28 Standard

Name the diol (a longer chain with one OH is available).

Show solution
  1. Two candidate chains: (i) HOCH₂–CH–CH₂–CH₃ is four carbons long but carries only one OH (the other CH₂OH would be a hydroxymethyl branch); (ii) HOCH₂–CH–CH₂OH is three carbons long and carries two OH groups.
  2. Rule 3(a): maximum number of principal groups in the parent chain → choose (ii): propane-1,3-diol.
  3. The ethyl group is a substituent on C2.

2-Ethylpropane-1,3-diol, not 2-(hydroxymethyl)butan-1-ol.

Example 29 Standard

Name citric acid: HOOC–CH₂–C(OH)(COOH)–CH₂–COOH.

Show solution
  1. A five-carbon chain through the two terminal COOH groups (pentanedioic acid, containing two principal groups) is available.
  2. But a three-carbon chain C–C–C with three COOH groups attached as suffix groups also exists. Maximum number of principal groups wins (three > two).
  3. Because the COOH carbons are not in the chain, the suffix is -carboxylic acid on propane: propane-1,2,3-tricarboxylic acid; the OH is on C2.

2-Hydroxypropane-1,2,3-tricarboxylic acid (the 2013 PIN name uses the same logic). The pentanedioic acid name, 3-carboxy-3-hydroxypentanedioic acid, is incorrect because it expresses only two of the three acid groups as suffix.

Example 30 Standard

Name the compound; one chain has the double bond but is shorter.

Show solution
  1. Longest chain through the vinyl group: CH₂=CH–CH–CH₂–CH₃ = five carbons including the C=C. The ethyl branch is the other CH₂CH₃.
  2. A five-carbon chain without C=C also exists (CH₃CH₂–CH–CH₂CH₃) but it would turn the double bond into an ethenyl substituent: criterion 3(b) forbids that.
  3. Numbering: double bond at 1 (lowest); ethyl at 3.

3-Ethylpent-1-ene.
