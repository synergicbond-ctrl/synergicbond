8. SNi and neighbouring-group participation: retention of configuration

Most substitutions at a stereogenic sp3 carbon invert (SN2) or racemise (SN1). Retention is observed in a few well-defined cases. In the SNi (internal nucleophilic substitution) reaction an alcohol reacts with thionyl chloride in the absence of base to give a chlorosulfite ester ROS(O)Cl, which collapses in an ion pair or tight cyclic arrangement in which the chloride is delivered from the same face as the departing group. The result is an alkyl chloride with retention. If pyridine is added, chloride is freed from the chlorosulfite as an external nucleophile and attacks from the back, so inversion (SN2) results.

Reactant:

Reagent: SOCl2, no base (SNi)

Product:

(S)-Butan-2-ol → (S)-2-chlorobutane with retention. With SOCl2/pyridine the product is (R)-2-chlorobutane.

Neighbouring-group participation (anchimeric assistance) also gives retention. A group with a lone pair, positioned to attack the reacting carbon, displaces the leaving group intramolecularly (first inversion) and is then itself displaced by the external nucleophile (second inversion). Two inversions give net retention, and the intramolecular first step is fast, so the reaction is faster than expected and its rate does not depend on the external nucleophile. Typical participating groups: –COO–, –OR, –SR, –NR2, halogen and arene.

Reactant:

Reagent: Ag2O, H2O: double inversion through an α-lactone

Product:

(S)-2-Bromopropanoate gives (S)-lactate with retention. In concentrated hydroxide the direct SN2 reaction dominates and (R)-lactate (inversion) is formed.

Key idea. Retention ⇒ either SNi (ROH/SOCl2, no base) or two successive inversions (neighbouring-group participation); both are recognised by an unexpectedly retained configuration.
