9. Nitrenes

9.1 Structure and formation

A nitrene is the nitrogen analogue of a carbene. The nitrogen has one bond and two lone pairs, six electrons in all, and is highly electrophilic. Like carbenes, nitrenes can be singlets or triplets. They are generated by loss of a small stable molecule: N2 from an azide (the usual route), HX from an N-halo compound with base.

9.2 The Curtius, Hofmann and related rearrangements

The reactions below are written with an acyl nitrene as intermediate. In each the group R migrates from the carbonyl carbon to the electron-deficient nitrogen (a Wolff-like 1,2-shift) and gives an isocyanate R–N=C=O, which is converted by water into a carbamic acid and then into an amine with loss of CO2, or by an alcohol into a carbamate. The amine has one carbon fewer than the starting acid derivative. The migrating group keeps its configuration, as in any 1,2-shift to an electron-deficient atom.

Hofmann reaction: ethanamide gives methylamine (one carbon fewer)
  • Hydroxide removes an N–H proton and the amide anion attacks Br2 to give the N-bromoamide RCONHBr.
  • A second deprotonation gives RCON−Br.
  • R migrates to nitrogen as bromide leaves, giving the isocyanate R–N=C=O.
  • Hydroxide adds to the isocyanate carbon to give the carbamate anion RNHCOO−, which loses CO2 to give RNH2.

For the thermal Curtius rearrangement it is often argued that the loss of N2 and the migration of R are concerted, so that no free nitrene intervenes; the photochemical reaction does give nitrenes, which also add to C=C bonds (aziridines) and insert into C–H bonds. Either description leads to the same products.

Example 33 Main

Counting carbon atoms

Benzamide, 3-phenylpropanamide and cyclohexanecarboxamide are each treated with Br2 and NaOH. Name the amines.

Show solution
  1. Benzamide gives aniline (C7 → C6). 3-Phenylpropanamide, PhCH2CH2CONH2, gives 2-phenylethanamine (C9 → C8). Cyclohexanecarboxamide gives cyclohexanamine (C7 → C6).
  2. The carbonyl carbon is lost as carbonate (CO32− in base); the nitrogen ends up attached to the carbon that was bonded to the carbonyl group.
Example 34 Adv

Stereochemistry of the migration

(S)-2-Phenylpropanamide, CH3CH(Ph)CONH2, undergoes Hofmann rearrangement. Give the product and its configuration.

Show solution
  1. The migrating carbon moves with its bonding pair and does not break any of its own bonds, so its configuration is retained.
  2. The priorities at the stereocentre are CONH2 > Ph > CH3 > H in the amide and NH2 > Ph > CH3 > H in the amine, with the new group in the same place as the old. The priority order is unchanged, so the descriptor stays (S): the product is (S)-1-phenylethanamine.

Think Further Show that Hofmann degradation of an optically active amide R*CONH2 gives an amine with the same spatial arrangement, and say how the CIP descriptor may change.
Show solution
The migrating carbon moves with its bonding electron pair to nitrogen without any of its own bonds breaking, so the arrangement of its four substituents is unchanged (retention). The descriptor (R or S) changes only if the replacement of C(=O)NH2 by NH2 changes the priority order; for (S)-2-phenylpropanamide the order stays the same and the amine is (S)-1-phenylethanamine.
