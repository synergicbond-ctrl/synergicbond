43. Additions from the cross-check against textbooks

Reading the substitution/elimination chapters of standard textbooks and one problem collection (see the appendix) showed a few topics that deserve explicit mention here, plus several problem types worth practising.

43.1 Why benzyne attack is sensitive to sterics

The extra bond of benzyne lies in the plane of the ring, so the nucleophile must approach in the plane, right next to any ortho substituent. Two effects therefore control the regiochemistry: (i) the carbanion formed should be next to an inductively electron-withdrawing group (OCH3, CF3), and (ii) the nucleophile prefers to attack the end of the triple bond away from a bulky neighbour. Both push o-chloroanisole and m-bromoanisole towards m-anisidine. With a para-alkyl substituent neither effect operates and a ≈ 1:1 mixture of meta and para amines results. (Contrast ordinary SNAr and electrophilic substitution, where the attack is on the p-orbital perpendicular to the ring and steric hindrance from ortho groups matters little.)

43.2 SN2 dealkylation of esters and ethers

Lithium iodide (or LiCl, NaCN) in DMF, DMSO or pyridine cleaves methyl esters: the iodide attacks the methyl carbon (SN2, the best electrophilic carbon with a carboxylate leaving group), giving CH3I and the lithium carboxylate. The acyl carbon is not touched, so the reaction works on esters that would be hydrolysed or epimerised by aqueous base. It is a use of the "naked anion in a dipolar aprotic solvent" idea of section 5.4.

43.3 Oxygen versus carbon alkylation of a phenoxide

A phenoxide (or naphthoxide) is an ambident nucleophile: O attack gives an aryl ether (Williamson), C attack at the ortho/para ring position gives an alkylated phenol. Polar aprotic solvents (DMF, DMSO) leave the oxygen anion "naked" and favour O-alkylation; protic solvents (water, trifluoroethanol) hydrogen-bond to oxygen, blocking it and pushing the reaction towards C-alkylation.

2-Naphthoxide + benzyl bromide in DMF: the 2-naphthyl benzyl ether (O-alkylation).

43.4 Substituent effects on the solvolysis of benzyl halides

Electron-donating groups on the ring (p-OCH3, p-CH3) strongly accelerate SN1 solvolysis of benzyl chlorides by stabilising the cation (resonance for OCH3); electron-withdrawing groups (p-NO2, p-CF3) retard it. The same substituents have small effects on the SN2 rate, which is why a p-methoxybenzyl chloride reacts by SN1 in water while p-nitrobenzyl chloride needs a good nucleophile.

43.5 Kinetic isotope effect in radical chlorination

Replacing H by D in the substrate slows hydrogen abstraction when the C–H bond is broken in the rate-determining step: CH4 is chlorinated by Cl• about 12 times faster than CD4. Like the E2 isotope effect (kH/kD ≈ 3–8), a large primary effect tells you that the C–H bond is broken in the slow step.

Practice 320 Adv

Dealkylation

Methyl 3-phenylpropanoate is heated with LiI in DMF and then acidified. Name the organic products and say which carbon is attacked by iodide.

Show answer
  1. Iodide attacks the methyl carbon (SN2; the carboxylate is the leaving group): CH3I + lithium 3-phenylpropanoate; acidification gives 3-phenylpropanoic acid. The acyl carbon is not attacked. Ethyl or isopropyl esters react more slowly; tert-butyl esters would not go by SN2.
Practice 321 Adv

Dealkylation

Why does LiI/DMF cleave a methyl ester much faster than the corresponding isopropyl ester?

Show answer
  1. The reaction is SN2 at the alkyl carbon of the ester: methyl ≫ primary > secondary because of steric hindrance to the back-side attack (the isopropyl ester reacts about 102 times more slowly).
Practice 322 Adv

Ambident phenoxide

2-Naphthol is treated with NaOH and then benzyl bromide, (i) in DMF (ii) in trifluoroethanol. Which type of product predominates in each?

Show answer
  1. (i) DMF: unsolvated oxygen anion: O-alkylation, the 2-benzyloxynaphthalene. (ii) Trifluoroethanol: hydrogen-bonding solvent shields the oxygen; the ring carbon C-1 (the more nucleophilic carbon of the naphthoxide) attacks: 1-benzyl-2-naphthol (C-alkylation, as the keto tautomer which re-aromatises).
Practice 323 Adv

Substituent effect

Arrange in decreasing rate of solvolysis in aqueous acetone (SN1): benzyl chloride, 4-methoxybenzyl chloride, 4-nitrobenzyl chloride, 4-methylbenzyl chloride.

Show answer
  1. Rate follows cation stability: the p-OCH3 cation has an extra oxocarbenium resonance form; CH3 stabilises by hyperconjugation/+I; NO2 destabilises.
  2. 4-methoxy > 4-methyl > H > 4-nitro.
Practice 324 Adv

Weak base

1-Bromo-1-methylcyclohexane is warmed with triethylamine in an aprotic solvent. Predict the main product and the mechanism type; why does the amine not give a substitution (quaternary ammonium) product?

Show answer
  1. The substrate is tertiary: SN2 by the bulky triethylamine is impossible. The amine can only act as a base (or the substrate ionises, E1): the major product is 1-methylcyclohexene (Zaitsev) with a small amount of methylenecyclohexane. Tertiary halides give elimination with amines; quaternary salts form only from unhindered primary halides.
Practice 325 Adv

Secondary cyclic halide

Predict the product from chlorocyclohexane with (i) NaOCH3 in methanol (ii) NaOC(CH3)3 in tert-butyl alcohol.

Show answer
  1. Secondary halide with a strong base: E2 in both cases, cyclohexene (with a minor amount of methyl cyclohexyl ether from SN2 in (i); essentially none in (ii) because tert-butoxide is too hindered a nucleophile).
Practice 326 Adv

Isotope effect

Methane is chlorinated 12 times faster than CD4. What does this indicate about the rate-determining step, and what range of kH/kD would you expect for the E1 elimination of tert-butyl chloride?

Show answer
  1. A large primary isotope effect shows that the C–H bond is broken in the rate-determining step (hydrogen abstraction by Cl•). For E1 the C–H bond is broken only after the slow ionisation, so kH/kD ≈ 1 (no primary effect; a small secondary effect may be seen).
Practice 327 Adv

Benzyne regiochemistry

2-Chloroanisole reacts with NaNH2/NH3(l) to give a single amine. Name it and give the two reasons for the absence of the ortho isomer.

Show answer
  1. 3-Methoxyaniline (m-anisidine). With Cl at C1 and OCH3 at C2, the only ortho hydrogen is at C6, so the aryne is C1≡C6 with OCH3 next to the C1 end. NH2− adds at C6 (the end remote from OCH3), leaving the carbanion at C1 beside the C–OCH3 carbon. (i) Electronic: that carbanion is stabilised by the inductive effect of oxygen. (ii) Steric: in-plane attack at C1 would be hindered by the neighbouring OCH3. The amino group ends up meta to OCH3.
