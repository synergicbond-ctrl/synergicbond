Fischer, wedge-dash, sawhorse and Newman converter

1. Fischer projection

Horizontal bonds toward you, vertical bonds away.

2. Wedge-dash (zigzag)

Chain in the plane, anti conformation.

3. Sawhorse

Oblique view along the chosen C–C bond.

4. Newman

Looking down the chosen C–C bond, front carbon in the centre.

Rules that make the conversions work

  • In a Fischer projection the horizontal groups point toward you and the vertical chain points away, so each stereocentre is a small bent chain, not a flat cross.
  • Rotating a Fischer projection by 180° in the plane keeps the molecule the same. Rotating by 90° gives its mirror image.
  • Swapping any two groups on a centre inverts it (R becomes S). Swapping twice restores it.
  • The Fischer arrangement is an eclipsed conformation. Rotating the rear carbon by 180° about the bond turns it into the staggered anti conformation, which is the zigzag chain. Rotation about a bond never changes R or S.
  • In a Newman projection, read the front carbon as: bottom = chain above, upper-left = Fischer right group, upper-right = Fischer left group.
How the 26 examples were checked

Structures were taken from PubChem (PUG REST) by name. The CIP descriptors RDKit computes from each structure were matched to the descriptors in PubChem's IUPAC name, and the structure's InChIKey was matched to the key NCI CACTUS returns when it parses that same IUPAC name. Every Fischer, zigzag and Newman assignment shown here was derived from the 3D geometry and then re-checked by rebuilding each centre in 3D from the drawing and recomputing R or S with RDKit (Newman at six rear-carbon rotations). The sawhorse uses the same angular assignment as the Newman view. Drawing conventions follow standard textbook practice (see IUPAC, Pure Appl. Chem. 1996, 68, 2193); that paper was not re-read for this page.
