9. Optical activity

9.1 Plane-polarised light and the polarimeter

Ordinary light vibrates in all planes perpendicular to its direction of travel. A Nicol prism or a polarising filter passes only one plane. When plane-polarised light traverses a solution of a chiral substance, the plane of polarisation is rotated by an angle α. A substance that rotates the plane is optically active.

  • Dextrorotatory (+) or d: rotates clockwise as seen by the observer looking toward the light source.
  • Levorotatory (−) or l: rotates anticlockwise.
  • Enantiomers rotate by equal magnitudes and opposite signs.
  • The sign is determined only by experiment. It has no simple relation to R/S or D/L.
Formula Specific rotation [α]λT = αobs / (l × c) (l in dm, c in g mL-1; units: deg dm-1 g-1 mL)
Formula If c is in g/100 mL: [α] = 100 αobs/(l × c)
The standard sodium D-line (589 nm) and 20 or 25 °C are conventional; solvent and concentration are quoted because [α] depends on them.
Example 17 Main

Specific rotation

A solution of 1.50 g of a chiral compound in 25.0 mL of ethanol in a 10.0 cm tube shows αobs = +1.65°. Find [α].

Show solution
  1. c = 1.50 g / 25.0 mL = 0.0600 g mL-1; l = 10.0 cm = 1.00 dm.
  2. [α] = 1.65 / (1.00 × 0.0600) = +27.5°.
Example 18 Main

Reading from a different tube

The compound in the previous example has [α] = +27.5°. What rotation is seen for a 0.020 g/mL solution in a 20 cm tube?

Show solution
  1. α = [α] l c = 27.5 × 2.0 dm × 0.020 = +1.10°.

9.2 Racemic mixture, enantiomeric excess, optical purity

A racemic mixture (±), racemate contains equal amounts of the two enantiomers. It has α = 0 (the rotations cancel, "external compensation"; contrast meso compounds, where cancelation occurs inside the molecule, "internal compensation"). A racemate can be a conglomerate (a physical mixture of separate crystals of each enantiomer), a racemic compound (both enantiomers in one crystal lattice, with different melting point and solubility) or, rarely, a solid solution.

Formula Enantiomeric excess ee = |R − S|/(R + S) × 100 % = (major − minor)/(major + minor) × 100 %
Formula Optical purity = [α]mixture / [α]pure enantiomer × 100 % (equal to ee when [α] is linear in composition)
Formula If the major enantiomer is x % and minor (100 − x) %: ee = 2x − 100
Example 19 Main

Composition from rotation

A mixture of (+)-2-butanol and (−)-2-butanol shows [α] = +4.5°. The pure (+)-enantiomer has [α] = +13.5°. Find the ee and the percentage of each enantiomer.

Show solution
  1. ee = 4.5/13.5 × 100 = 33.3 %.
  2. (+) − (−) = 33.3 %, (+) + (−) = 100 % → (+) = 66.7 %, (−) = 33.3 %.
Example 20 Adv

Mixing racemate with enantiomer

2.0 g of pure (R)-X ([α] = +40°) is mixed with 3.0 g of racemic X. What is the specific rotation of the mixture, and its ee?

Show solution
  1. In the racemate, 1.5 g is R and 1.5 g is S. Total R = 2.0 + 1.5 = 3.5 g; total S = 1.5 g; total 5.0 g.
  2. ee = (3.5 − 1.5)/5.0 = 40 %, so [α] = 0.40 × 40° = +16°; mixture is 70 % R, 30 % S.
Example 21 Adv

Mixing two enantiomer-rich samples

Sample A: 60 % ee of (+); sample B: 20 % ee of (−). Equal masses are mixed. Find the ee of the mixture.

Show solution
  1. Net excess of (+): 0.5 × 60 − 0.5 × 20 = 20 percentage points of the total.
  2. ee = 20 % (+).
Trap ee is a percentage excess, not the percentage of the major enantiomer. 90 % ee = 95 % major + 5 % minor. 100 % ee means one pure enantiomer; 0 % ee is racemic.

9.3 Optical activity facts for exams

  • Enantiomers: identical melting point, boiling point, density, refractive index, solubility in an achiral solvent, IR, NMR (in achiral medium). They differ in the sign of rotation and in their reactions with other chiral reagents or biological receptors (smell of (R)- and (S)-carvone; taste of asparagine; thalidomide).
  • Diastereomers: different physical properties (m.p., b.p., solubility, [α]).
  • Racemate: different m.p. and solubility from the pure enantiomer in many cases: (+)-tartaric acid m.p. ≈ 170 °C, racemic acid ≈ 206 °C, meso-tartaric acid ≈ 140 °C. (These are standard values.)
  • Optical activity of a mixture of diastereomers or different compounds: sums of contributions.
  • A compound can be optically inactive because: (i) it is achiral; (ii) it is a racemic mixture (external compensation); (iii) it is meso (internal compensation); (iv) its enantiomers interconvert rapidly (simple amines, conformationally mobile biaryls).
Shortcut Optical rotation of an enantiomer is (+) or (−) independent of the R/S label: (R)-lactic acid is (−), (S)-lactic acid is (+), while (R)-2-butanol is (−), (S)-2-butanol is (+) and (R)-glyceraldehyde is (+). Never infer sign from R/S.
(R)-(−)-lactic acid (S)-(+)-lactic acid (R)-(−)-2-butanol (S)-(+)-2-butanol
Here R happens to be (−) in both cases, but this is coincidence: (R)-glyceraldehyde is (+)

9.4 Mutarotation and invert sugar (numerical favourites)

α-D-Glucopyranose has [α] = +112.2°; β-D-glucopyranose +18.7°. In water they interconvert through the open-chain form until the equilibrium value +52.7° is reached. This slow change in rotation is mutarotation.

Example 22 Adv

Mutarotation equilibrium

Using [α](α-glucose) = +112.2°, [α](β-glucose) = +18.7°, equilibrium [α] = +52.7°, find the percentage of α-anomer at equilibrium (ignore the open-chain form).

Show solution
  1. Let x = fraction of α: 112.2x + 18.7(1 − x) = 52.7 → 93.5x = 34.0 → x = 0.364.
  2. 36.4 % α and 63.6 % β.
Example 23 Adv

Invert sugar

Sucrose hydrolyses (inversion) to equimolar glucose ([α] = +52.7°) and fructose ([α] = −92.4°). What is the specific rotation of the equimolar mixture (treating [α] as additive)?

Show solution
  1. Average = (52.7 − 92.4)/2 = −19.85° (about −20°). The sign of rotation changes from the dextrorotatory sucrose (+66.5°) to levorotatory: the inversion of sugar.

9.5 Summary sheet: chirality and optical activity

  • Chiral ⇔ no σ, i, Sn. One stereocentre ⇒ chiral. Meso ⇒ achiral. Allenes, biaryls, spiranes can be chiral without stereocentres.
  • Pyramidal-inversion amines are not resolvable; sulfoxides, phosphines, quaternary ammonium salts are.
  • [α] = α/(l c); ee = |R−S|/(R+S); optical purity = [α]obs/[α]max.
  • The sign (+/−) is experimental: no link to R/S or D/L.
  • Enantiomers differ only in rotation sign and chiral interactions; diastereomers differ in all physical properties.
