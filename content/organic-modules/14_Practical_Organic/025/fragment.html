7.5 Test for halogens

The extract contains NaCN and Na2S if N or S is present; they would give precipitates with AgNO3 (AgCN white, Ag2S black) and be mistaken for halides. So the extract is first boiled with dilute (or conc.) HNO3 to expel them as HCN and H2S (in a fume cupboard):

Formula NaCN + HNO3 → NaNO3 + HCN↑ Na2S + 2HNO3 → 2NaNO3 + H2S↑

Cool, then add AgNO3 solution:

Br− → Br2: orange/brown organic layer. I− → I2: violet organic layer. Cl−: layer colourless (Cl2 cannot oxidise Cl−). 2X− + Cl2 → X2 + 2Cl−.
Exam Trap With excess chlorine water the violet iodine colour disappears: I2 + 5Cl2 + 6H2O → 2HIO3 + 10HCl (iodate is colourless). Add chlorine water dropwise. Also, the AgNO3 test never detects fluorine (AgF is soluble), and boiling with HNO3 is skipped if only halogen (no N or S) is present, though it is harmless.
Example 11 Adv

Why does AgCl dissolve in ammonia but AgI not?

Calculate approximately the solubility (mol L-1) of AgCl, AgBr and AgI in 1 M NH3 using Ksp(AgCl) = 1.8 × 10-10, Ksp(AgBr) = 5.0 × 10-13, Ksp(AgI) = 8.5 × 10-17 and Kf[Ag(NH3)2]+ = 1.7 × 107.

Show solution
  1. AgX(s) + 2NH3 ⇌ [Ag(NH3)2]+ + X−; K = Ksp × Kf. With solubility s: K = s2/(1 − 2s)2 so s/(1 − 2s) = √K.
  2. AgCl: K = 1.8 × 10-10 × 1.7 × 107 = 3.06 × 10-3, √K = 0.0553, s = 0.0553/(1 + 2 × 0.0553) = 0.050 M (dissolves freely in 1 M NH3).
  3. AgBr: K = 8.5 × 10-6, √K = 2.9 × 10-3, s = 2.9 × 10-3 M (sparingly soluble; ppt dissolves only in concentrated ammonia).
  4. AgI: K = 1.45 × 10-9, √K = 3.8 × 10-5, s = 3.8 × 10-5 M (insoluble for all practical purposes).
  5. The solubilities are in the ratio ≈ 1300 : 76 : 1, matching the qualitative statement in the chapter.
