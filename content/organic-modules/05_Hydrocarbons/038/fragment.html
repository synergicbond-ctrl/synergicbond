37. Dienes: classification and structure

Dienes CnH2n−2 contain two C=C bonds. By the relative position of the double bonds:

buta-1,2-diene (cumulated, allene) buta-1,3-diene (conjugated) penta-1,4-diene (isolated) cyclopenta-1,3-diene (conjugated, cyclic)
Cumulated, conjugated and isolated dienes
Types of dienes
TypePatternFeatures
Cumulated (allenes)C=C=Ccentral carbon sp (180°); the two π bonds are perpendicular; the substituents at the two ends lie in perpendicular planes; axial chirality if each end carries two different groups; less stable than conjugated
ConjugatedC=C–C=Cdelocalised π system; C2–C3 shortened (148 pm vs 154 pm in alkane, because sp2–sp2); extra stability ≈ 15 kJ (heat of hydrogenation 239 vs 254 kJ for two isolated C=C)
IsolatedC=C–(CH2)n–C=C (n ≥ 1)behave as two independent alkenes

Why conjugated dienes are stabilised: (i) overlap of the four p orbitals gives delocalisation (butadiene MOs ψ1–ψ4; the four π electrons occupy ψ1 and ψ2); (ii) the central σ bond is sp2–sp2, stronger and shorter than sp3–sp3. Resonance: CH2=CH–CH=CH2 ↔ −CH2–CH=CH–CH2+, minor contributors; they explain why the central bond has some double-bond character and the 1,4-addition.

37.1 Allenes and their chirality

In allene H2C=C=CH2 the two terminal CH2 planes are at 90° to each other. A 1,3-disubstituted allene abC=C=Cab with a ≠ b on each end has no plane of symmetry and is chiral (axial chirality) even though no stereocentre exists; e.g. penta-2,3-diene CH3CH=C=CHCH3 is chiral (two enantiomers), but 3-methylbuta-1,2-diene (CH3)2C=C=CH2 is achiral (two identical groups on one end).

Trap Axial chirality (allenes, biaryls, spiranes) gives optical activity without a stereocentre. A cumulene with an even number of C=C (allene, 2) has perpendicular end planes (axial chirality); one with an odd number (butatriene, 3) has coplanar ends and shows cis/trans isomerism.

Classify and count

Classify each as cumulated, conjugated or isolated: (a) CH2=C=CH–CH3 (b) CH3CH=CH–CH=CH2 (c) CH2=CH–CH2–CH=CH2 (d) cyclohexa-1,4-diene. Which are optically active? How many geometrical isomers does (b) have?

Show solution
  1. (a) cumulated; (b) conjugated; (c) isolated; (d) isolated.
  2. Optical activity requires a chiral axis or centre: (a) buta-1,2-diene has a CH2 end: achiral. None are optically active.
  3. (b) Penta-1,3-diene has one double bond (C3=C4) that can show E/Z (the C1=C2 end is CH2): 2 geometrical isomers (E and Z).
