Racemisation kinetics

Optically active 2-iodooctane is shaken with 0.10 M radioiodide in acetone. The second-order rate constant for isotopic exchange is 5.0 × 10−4 L mol−1 s−1. Calculate the half-life for loss of optical activity.

Show answer
  1. Each exchange inverts one molecule, so the rate constant for racemisation is twice that for exchange: krac = 2 × 5.0 × 10−4 = 1.0 × 10−3 L mol−1 s−1. With [I−] constant (0.10 M): pseudo-first-order k′ = 1.0 × 10−3 × 0.10 = 1.0 × 10−4 s−1.
  2. t½ = 0.693/1.0 × 10−4 = 6.9 × 103 s (≈ 1.9 h).

Ether by SN2 from a chiral halide

(R)-2-Bromobutane is treated with sodium ethoxide in ethanol. Name the substitution product and the elimination products.

Show answer
  1. SN2 with inversion: the priorities OEt > C2H5 > CH3 > H, same order as Br > C2H5 > CH3 > H, so (S)-2-ethoxybutane. Practice 191 Adv

Solvent polarity and reaction type

Predict the effect of increasing solvent polarity on the rate: (a) (CH3)3CCl → ion pair (b) CH3CH2Br + OH− (SN2) (c) (C2H5)3N + C2H5I → (C2H5)4N+I− (d) (CH3)3S+ + OH− → (CH3)2S + CH3OH.

Show answer
  1. The Hughes–Ingold rule: compare the charge density of the reactants and the transition state. More charge separation or localisation in the TS than in the reactants → faster in polar solvent; charge dispersal or destruction → slower.
  2. (a) Neutral → charge-separated TS: large increase. (b) Anion → TS with charge dispersed over Nu and LG: small decrease. (c) Neutral + neutral → ion pair (Menshutkin): large increase. (d) Cation + anion → neutral products (charge destroyed in the TS): decrease.

Halogen exchange equilibria

The Finkelstein reaction R–Cl + NaI ⇌ R–I + NaCl is run in acetone but the reverse RI + NaCl is not. Use solubility data to explain, and say what happens in methanol.

Show answer
  1. In acetone NaI dissolves; NaCl and NaBr are nearly insoluble: precipitation removes chloride from the equilibrium, driving it to the right (Le Chatelier). In methanol both sodium halides are soluble, so no precipitate drives the reaction and the equilibrium constant is close to 1; the reaction proceeds toward a mixture.

Show answer

Practice 202 Adv

Compare dipole moments

Order C6H5Cl (1.69 D), C6H5CH2Cl (≈ 1.85 D), cyclohexyl chloride (2.31 D) and p-dichlorobenzene (0 D), and give reasons for the extremes.

Show answer
  1. Cyclohexyl chloride has the largest dipole: sp3 carbon and no opposing resonance. In chlorobenzene the dipole is reduced by the more electronegative sp2 carbon (smaller charge difference) and by +R donation of Cl into the ring. p-Dichlorobenzene is symmetric: the two C–Cl dipoles cancel (0 D).
  2. Decreasing: cyclohexyl chloride (2.31) > benzyl chloride (≈ 1.85) > chlorobenzene (1.69) > p-dichlorobenzene (0).

Practice 204 Adv

Why SN2 at sp3 but not sp2

Give three distinct reasons why vinyl chloride does not undergo SN2 with hydroxide, comparing with ethyl chloride.

Show answer
  1. (1) The back side of the sp2 carbon is shielded by the π-electron cloud; the nucleophile is repelled. (2) The C–Cl bond is stronger and shorter (sp2 carbon, and partial double-bond character from n(Cl) → π* conjugation), so it breaks with difficulty. (3) The planar sp2 geometry would require inversion through an sp-hybridised transition state, highly strained, while the alternative in-plane approach is sterically impossible.
