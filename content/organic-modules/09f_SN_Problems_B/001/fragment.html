Practice 1 L1 Foundation

Four chlorides, drawn below, are solvolysed in 80% aqueous ethanol under identical conditions: (a) 1-chloropropane, (b) 2-chloropropane, (c) 2-chloro-2-methylpropane, (d) 2-chloro-2-phenylpropane. The correct order of increasing SN1 reactivity is

(A) d < c < b < a

(B) b < a < c < d

(C) a < b < c < d

(D) a < b < d < c

Show answer
  1. The rate-determining step of SN1 is ionisation to a carbocation, so the order follows carbocation stability.
  2. (a) would have to give a primary cation, which is so unstable that 1-chloropropane does not ionise at all; whatever slow reaction it shows is SN2 by the solvent. (b) gives a secondary cation, (c) a tertiary cation.
  3. (d) gives PhC+(CH3)2, a tertiary cation that is in addition delocalised into the benzene ring, so it is more stable than the tert-butyl cation.
  4. Order: a < b < c < d.

Answer: C

Practice 2 Worked example

The bromides (P) 5-bromocyclopenta-1,3-diene, (Q) bromocyclopentane and (R) 7-bromocyclohepta-1,3,5-triene are drawn below. Their reactivity in an SN1 solvolysis (aqueous acetone, same temperature) increases in the order

(A) Q < P < R

(B) R < Q < P

(C) P < R < Q

(D) P < Q < R

Show answer
  1. Write the cation each bromide would give in the slow step. (P) would give the cyclopentadienyl cation, a planar cyclic conjugated system with 4 π electrons (4n, n = 1): antiaromatic and strongly destabilised.
  2. (Q) gives the ordinary secondary cyclopentyl cation, which is neither stabilised nor destabilised by cyclic conjugation.
  3. (R) gives the cycloheptatrienyl (tropylium) cation, planar, cyclic, fully conjugated, with 6 π electrons (4n + 2, n = 1): aromatic and exceptionally stable.
  4. Because the transition state of ionisation resembles the cation, the rates follow the stability order antiaromatic < ordinary secondary < aromatic.
  5. So P < Q < R. (The antiaromatic bromide P ionises with extreme difficulty, while R ionises exceptionally readily.)

Answer: D

Practice 3 L1 Foundation

Which of the chlorides drawn below reacts slowest in an SN1 solvolysis in aqueous ethanol?

(A) Chlorocyclopentane

(B) Chlorocyclopropane

(C) 2-Chloro-2-methylbutane

(D) 2-Chloropropane

Show answer
  1. A carbocation prefers a planar geometry with 120° angles. The cyclopropyl cation would need 120° at a ring carbon whose ring angle is fixed near 60°, so it suffers very large angle strain.
  2. The cyclopentyl cation is only slightly strained (ring angle about 108°), and the acyclic secondary and tertiary cations from (C) and (D) are unstrained.
  3. Hence chlorocyclopropane ionises least readily: option B.

Answer: B

Practice 4 L1 Foundation

Select all of the halides drawn below that are essentially inert to both SN1 and SN2 substitution under ordinary laboratory conditions.

(A) Chlorobenzene

(B) 1-Bromocyclohexene

(C) Benzyl bromide

(D) Bromocyclohexane

(E) 1-Bromobicyclo[2.2.1]heptane

Show answer
  1. Aryl and vinylic halides: the carbon is sp2, the C–X bond is short and strong, an aryl or vinyl cation would be extremely unstable (SN1 fails) and the rear of the C–X bond is blocked by the π cloud or ring (SN2 fails). So A and B are inert.
  2. E is a bridgehead halide: the rigid cage prevents the planar cation (SN1 fails) and backside attack is impossible because the cage blocks the rear of the C–Br bond (SN2 fails). It is inert as well.
  3. C (benzyl bromide) reacts by both mechanisms and D (bromocyclohexane) is an ordinary secondary halide. Therefore A, B and E.

Answer: A,B,E

Practice 5 L2 Main

Chloromethyl methyl ether, CH3OCH2Cl, is a primary halide, yet it solvolyses in methanol by an SN1 pathway far faster than 1-chloropropane. The best explanation is that

(A) the C–Cl bond is weaker in CH3OCH2Cl because chlorine is bonded to a carbon that also carries oxygen

(B) the primary carbon is less hindered, so backside attack by methanol is easier

(C) the oxygen lone pair delocalises into the developing cation, giving the resonance-stabilised CH3O+=CH2

(D) oxygen is electronegative and so its inductive effect stabilises a positive charge on the neighbouring carbon

Show answer
  1. An inductive electron withdrawal by oxygen would destabilise, not stabilise, a carbocation, so D is wrong. Option B is wrong because SN1 ionisation does not depend on backside attack, and option A gives no reason for the bond to be weaker.
  2. Ionisation gives CH3O–CH2+, which is not a localised primary cation: an oxygen lone pair is donated into the empty p orbital to give the oxocarbenium ion CH3O+=CH2 in which every atom has an octet.
  3. This large resonance stabilisation lowers the energy of the ionisation transition state, which explains the high SN1 rate. Option C.

Answer: C

Practice 8 L3 Advanced

A very crowded tertiary chloride in which all three groups on the carbon bearing chlorine are bulky solvolyses in aqueous ethanol far faster than tert-butyl chloride, much faster than electron release by the alkyl groups alone would predict. The best explanation is

(A) a hydride shift converts the cation into a primary cation

(B) relief of steric compression (B-strain) when the tetrahedral carbon (about 109.5°) becomes the planar cation (120°)

(C) the large groups make the C–Cl bond stronger, so the reaction goes by SN2

(D) bulky groups prevent solvation of the cation, which speeds up ionisation

Show answer
  1. Alkyl groups stabilise a cation by +I and hyperconjugation, but this alone cannot account for a rate enhancement of this size.
  2. In the tetrahedral reactant the three bulky groups are pushed close together (steric strain, B-strain). On ionisation the carbon becomes trigonal planar and the groups move further apart (about 120°), so part of the strain is released in the transition state.
  3. Release of ground-state strain lowers the activation energy: this is steric acceleration. Option B. Bulky groups hinder, not help, SN2, and a hydride shift to a primary cation is never favourable.

Answer: B

Practice 9 L2 Main

The substrates tert-butyl bromide (X), 1-bromobicyclo[2.2.2]octane (Y) and 1-bromobicyclo[2.2.1]heptane (Z) are drawn below. Their SN1 reactivity in 80% ethanol decreases in the order

(A) Y > X > Z

(B) Z > Y > X

(C) X > Y > Z

(D) X > Z > Y

Show answer
  1. X ionises to the planar tert-butyl cation, which is stabilised by hyperconjugation.
  2. Y and Z are tertiary bridgehead bromides. The bridgehead carbon is held in a pyramidal geometry by the cage and cannot flatten to the planar cation that is stabilised by hyperconjugation. Both are therefore very much less reactive than X.
  3. The [2.2.1] cage is more rigid and holds the bridgehead further from planarity than the [2.2.2] cage, so Z ionises even less readily than Y.
  4. X > Y > Z.

Answer: C

Practice 10 L2 Main

The dibromide drawn below, 1-bromo-4-(2-bromopropan-2-yl)bicyclo[2.2.2]octane, is warmed in aqueous ethanol (no strong nucleophile). Which statement is correct?

(A) The bridgehead bromine is replaced; the other bromine remains

(B) Neither bromine is replaced because the molecule is too hindered

(C) Both bromines are replaced at comparable rates

(D) The bromine on the isopropyl-type carbon is replaced; the bridgehead bromine remains

Show answer
  1. Compare the cations that each C–Br ionisation would produce.
  2. Loss of the exocyclic bromide gives an open tertiary cation that can adopt the planar geometry and is stabilised by hyperconjugation.
  3. Loss of the bridgehead bromide would give a bridgehead cation, which cannot become planar because of the cage and is therefore strongly destabilised; it is formed only with great difficulty.
  4. So only the exocyclic tertiary bromide ionises under SN1 conditions: option D.

Answer: D

Practice 11 Worked example

The positively charged tert-butyldimethylsulfonium ion, (CH3)3C–S+(CH3)2, undergoes SN1 solvolysis in ethanol–water mixtures, giving (CH3)3C+ and (CH3)2S. When the proportion of water in the solvent is increased, the rate is expected to

(A) increase, because water is a better nucleophile than ethanol

(B) decrease slightly, because the full charge of the reactant is stabilised by the polar solvent more than the dispersed charge of the transition state

(C) remain exactly constant because the nucleophile is not involved

(D) increase greatly, as for tert-butyl chloride, because ionisation is favoured by polar solvents

Show answer
  1. For a neutral substrate (RCl) the transition state has partial charges that are larger than in the reactant, so a more polar solvent lowers the activation energy and speeds the reaction.
  2. Here the reactant already carries a full + charge localised on sulfur. In the transition state that charge is spread over sulfur and the developing tert-butyl carbon, i.e. it is more dispersed.
  3. A more polar solvent stabilises the concentrated charge of the reactant more than the dispersed charge of the transition state, so the activation energy rises slightly and the rate falls slightly. Option B.
  4. Summary: the effect of solvent polarity depends on whether the charge is more concentrated in the reactant or in the transition state; for neutral RX it is the transition state, so polar solvents speed up SN1, whereas for a cationic substrate the opposite (small) trend is found.

Answer: B

Practice 12 L2 Main

Select all the media in which the SN1 ionisation of a neutral alkyl halide would be extremely slow.

(A) The gas phase

(B) Liquid hexane

(C) 80% water–20% ethanol

(D) Formic acid

Show answer
  1. SN1 ionisation produces a cation and an anion from a neutral molecule. It is feasible only if the solvent can stabilise both ions by solvation (cations by lone-pair donation, anions by hydrogen bonding or ion–dipole interactions).
  2. In the gas phase there is no solvent, and in hexane there are no polar groups to solvate ions; the charge-separated transition state is not stabilised, so the reaction is extremely slow in both (A and B).
  3. Aqueous ethanol and formic acid are highly ionising protic media and promote SN1 (C and D are not correct).

Answer: A,B

Practice 13 L2 Main

tert-Butyl chloride undergoes solvolysis in 80% ethanol–20% water. When 0.5 M sodium azide is added to the medium, which outcome is expected?

(A) The rate becomes proportional to [N3–] and tert-butyl azide is the only product

(B) The rate falls because azide is a base and removes the proton from the cation

(C) The rate of disappearance of tert-butyl chloride is nearly unchanged, but some tert-butyl azide appears among the products

(D) No azide product forms because azide is too weakly nucleophilic to react

Show answer
  1. In the SN1 mechanism the slow step is ionisation of the chloride, which does not involve the nucleophile; hence the rate is essentially independent of [N3–] (apart from a small ionic-strength salt effect).
  2. After the slow step the tert-butyl cation is captured by whatever nucleophile is nearby. Azide is a much better nucleophile than water or ethanol, so a share of the cation is trapped as (CH3)3CN3 alongside the alcohol and ether.
  3. So the rate is unchanged and the product composition changes: option C.

Answer: C

Practice 14 L2 Main

Select all the correct statements about added salts and additives in nucleophilic substitution.

(A) A non-common-ion salt such as LiClO4 modestly raises the rate of an SN1 solvolysis.

(B) Silver(I) ion accelerates the ionisation of an alkyl bromide in aqueous ethanol.

(C) Adding azide increases the rate of an SN1 solvolysis in direct proportion to [N3–].

(D) A large rate increase on adding NaN3 to the reaction of a secondary halide indicates a bimolecular component.

Show answer
  1. A is true: raising the ionic strength stabilises the polar transition state of ionisation (salt effect).
  2. B is true: Ag+ binds the departing halide as insoluble AgBr, pulling the C–Br bond apart (electrophilic catalysis).
  3. C is false: the nucleophile is not in the rate-determining step of SN1, so the rate is not proportional to [N3–].
  4. D is true: azide is a strong nucleophile, so a large acceleration signals a direct SN2 attack. Correct set: A, B, D.

Practice 15 L2 Main

tert-Butyl fluoride is almost unreactive in neutral aqueous ethanol, but it solvolyses smoothly when a strong acid is present. The role of the acid is to

(A) convert the solvent into a stronger nucleophile

(B) protonate the tert-butyl cation to make it more stable

(C) remove a proton from the tert-butyl group and produce an alkene intermediate

(D) hydrogen-bond to (protonate) fluorine so that the leaving group is neutral HF rather than the strongly basic F–

Show answer
  1. Fluoride is the strongest base of the halide ions and the C–F bond is the strongest carbon–halogen bond, so F– is a very poor leaving group.
  2. In strong acid, fluorine (the most electronegative halogen, with a high affinity for hydrogen bonding) is hydrogen-bonded or protonated. The group leaving is then the neutral, weakly basic HF molecule, which is a far better leaving group.
  3. This is electrophilic catalysis of the SN1 ionisation. Option D.

Answer: D

(R)-1-Bromo-1-phenylethane is stirred in glacial acetic acid with silver acetate. Give the substitution product (indicate both stereoisomers formed).

Practice 18 Worked example

Optically pure (R)-1-chloro-1-phenylethane, drawn below, is hydrolysed in aqueous acetone. The product alcohol is almost, but not completely, racemic, with a small excess of the inverted (S) alcohol. The excess of inversion is larger in solvents richer in water. The best explanation is

(A) water, being a poorer nucleophile than acetone, selects the more hindered face

(B) the departing chloride shields the front face of the cation for a short time; in water-rich solvent the cation is captured by water before the ion pair separates, so attack from the back is slightly preferred

(C) in water-rich solvent the reaction changes to SN2 at a larger fraction of the molecules

(D) the benzylic cation is non-planar and so is attacked only on one face

Show answer
  1. If a free planar cation were formed, water would attack the two faces equally and the alcohol would be exactly racemic.
  2. The cation is born next to the chloride ion it has just lost: they form an intimate ion pair. For a short time the chloride occupies the front face (the side from which it departed), so water can reach the cation more easily from the rear. This gives a small excess of inversion.
  3. In water-rich solvent the concentration of nucleophile is high, so many cations are captured while still in the ion pair, giving more inversion. When the medium contains less water, more ion pairs separate before capture, the cation becomes fully free and the product approaches 50:50.
  4. Hence option B. (A partial SN2 contribution or a non-planar cation would not explain the trend with water content.)

Answer: B

Practice 19 L2 Main

Two secondary substrates are each labelled with deuterium in place of hydrogen on the carbon that carries the leaving group (α-deuterium). The solvolysis of substrate X shows kH/kD = 1.18 and that of substrate Y shows kH/kD = 1.01. Which conclusion is justified?

(A) Both react by SN2

(B) X reacts by SN2 and Y by SN1

(C) Both react by SN1

(D) X reacts by SN1 and Y by SN2

Show answer
  1. An α-secondary isotope effect arises from the change in C–H(D) out-of-plane bending as the carbon rehybridises. On going from sp3 to the planar sp2 cation-like transition state the bending becomes freer, and the lighter H gains more than D: kH/kD is about 1.1–1.25.
  2. In an SN2 transition state the carbon stays essentially tetrahedral-like (five-coordinate, with bending changing little), so the effect is close to 1.
  3. X (1.18) is therefore SN1-like and Y (1.01) is SN2-like.

Answer: D

Practice 20 L2 Main

The following isotopically labelled forms of 2-chloro-2-methylbutane are solvolysed under identical SN1 conditions in 80% ethanol: (A) the unlabelled compound; (B) (CD3)2CClCH2CH3; (C) (CH3)2CClCD2CH3; (D) (CH3)2CClCH2CD3. Which solvolyses most slowly?

Practice 22 Worked example

(R)-1-Phenylethanol, drawn below, is gently warmed in water enriched in 18O (H218O) containing a little sulfuric acid. After some time, the alcohol that is recovered is

(A) racemic but contains no 18O

(B) a single enantiomer (S) containing 18O

(C) racemic and contains 18O

(D) still (R) and contains no 18O

Practice 24 L2 Main

Practice 25 Worked example

tert-Butyl acetate and ethyl acetate are hydrolysed separately in dilute sulfuric acid dissolved in H218O. In which products does the 18O label end up?

(A) tert-butyl acetate: in the acetic acid; ethyl acetate: in the alcohol

(B) both esters: in the alcohol

(C) both esters: in the acetic acid

(D) tert-butyl acetate: in the alcohol; ethyl acetate: in the acetic acid

Practice 28 Worked example

3,3-Dimethylbutan-2-ol is shaken with concentrated hydrobromic acid at room temperature. Give the main alkyl bromide formed.

Show answer
