19.1 Vapour density and Victor Meyer's method

Molecular mass of a volatile compound: M = 2 × V.D. (V.D. relative to H2). In the Victor Meyer method a weighed volatile liquid is vaporised in a heated tube; the vapour displaces an equal volume of air, which is collected over water and measured (corrected for aqueous tension).

Formula M = w R T/(P V) with P = Patm − paq (pressure of the dry displaced air), V = volume of air displaced, T = temperature of the measuring burette
Example 38 Adv

Victor Meyer

0.200 g of a volatile liquid displaced 40.0 mL of air collected over water at 300 K. The barometric pressure was 750 mm Hg and the aqueous tension at 300 K is 27 mm Hg. Find the molecular mass (R = 0.0821 L atm K-1 mol-1).

Show solution
  1. P = 750 − 27 = 723 mm Hg = 723/760 = 0.9513 atm.
  2. n = PV/RT = 0.9513 × 0.0400/(0.0821 × 300) = 1.545 × 10-3 mol.
  3. M = 0.200/1.545 × 10-3 = 129 g mol-1.
