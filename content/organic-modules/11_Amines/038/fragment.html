38. Mechanism: the Gabriel synthesis

(a) Making the nucleophile. Phthalimide N–H is flanked by two C=O. KOH removes H+: the conjugate base has the negative charge delocalised onto both oxygens (three-centre π system O–C–N–C–O). Why it is acidic (pKa 8.3): the negative charge is shared by two carbonyl groups (two π* acceptors for nN).

  • Step 1 – SN2. The anion's N lone pair (the HOMO) attacks the 1° alkyl halide from the back (σ*C–X). Why N, not O: the N is the larger-coefficient nucleophilic site, and the product (N-alkylimide) is the more stable. Why only once: the product has no N–H and the lone pair is delocalised into two C=O – it cannot react again.
  • Step 2 – hydrazinolysis (Ing–Manske). N2H4 is a strong α-effect nucleophile. Its NH2 attacks one C=O of the N-alkylphthalimide (tetrahedral intermediate), the C–N(imide) bond breaks (the leaving group is the amide anion stabilised by the remaining C=O), then the second N of hydrazine attacks the other C=O and closes the six-membered phthalhydrazide ring; the amine RNH2 is expelled.
+ 1. SN2: n(N) → σ*(C–Br)⟶ 2. N₂H₄: two acyl substitutions⟶
Gabriel: SN2 alkylation then hydrazinolysis releases the amine and the stable cyclic phthalhydrazide

Driving force of step 2: formation of the very stable, aromatic-fused cyclic hydrazide; the amine is liberated as a free base.
