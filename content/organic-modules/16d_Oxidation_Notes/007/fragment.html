8. Wacker oxidation, allylic oxidation with SeO2 and hydroboration–oxidation

The Wacker oxidation converts a terminal alkene into a methyl ketone (and ethene into ethanal) using PdCl2 as catalyst, CuCl2 as co-catalyst and O2 as the terminal oxidant, in water–DMF or water–dioxane. Water attacks the Pd(II)-complexed alkene at the more substituted carbon (Markovnikov), and β-hydride elimination gives the enol and Pd(0), which tautomerises to the ketone. Pd(0) is reoxidised to Pd(II) by 2 CuCl2 (giving 2 CuCl), and the CuCl is reoxidised by O2 and HCl. The net change is RCH=CH2 + ½ O2 → RCOCH3; palladium is only a catalyst.

Reactant:

Reagent: PdCl2 (cat.), CuCl2 (cat.), O2, H2O/DMF

Product:

Hex-1-ene gives hexan-2-one.

Selenium dioxide oxidises an allylic C–H to an allylic alcohol (and, with excess, onwards to the enone). The mechanism is an ene reaction of SeO2 followed by a [2,3]-sigmatropic rearrangement of the allylseleninic acid and hydrolysis. SeO2 also oxidises the CH2 (or CH3) next to a carbonyl group to a second carbonyl group (Riley oxidation): ketone → 1,2-diketone, acetophenone → phenylglyoxal.

Reactant:

Reagent: SeO2 (cat.), tBuOOH, CH2Cl2

Product:

Allylic oxidation; the C=C bond is not attacked.

Reactant:

Reagent: SeO2, dioxane–H2O, heat

Product:

Riley oxidation of the α-methyl group.

Hydroboration–oxidation also deserves a place in the oxidation chapter. BH3·THF adds syn across C=C with boron on the less hindered, less substituted carbon. In the second step alkaline H2O2 oxidises the C–B bond: HOO− attacks boron, an alkyl group migrates from boron to oxygen with retention of configuration and loss of hydroxide, and hydrolysis releases the alcohol. The net result is anti-Markovnikov, syn addition of H and OH.

Reactant:

Reagent: (i) BH3·THF; (ii) H2O2, NaOH

Product:

1-Methylcyclopentene gives trans-2-methylcyclopentanol: H (on C1) and B (on C2) add to the same face, boron is replaced by OH with retention, so OH and CH3 are trans (racemate; one enantiomer drawn).

Key idea. Hydroboration–oxidation: oxidation of the C–B bond to C–O occurs with retention. Wacker: Markovnikov, the carbonyl lands on the internal carbon.
Trap. In the Wacker process oxygen is not the oxidant that reacts with the alkene; Pd(II) is, and O2 reoxidises copper. Do not write the product of a terminal alkene as an aldehyde.
