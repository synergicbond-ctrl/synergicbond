11. Meerwein–Ponndorf–Verley reduction, transfer hydrogenation and diimide

In the MPV reduction a ketone or aldehyde is reduced by aluminium isopropoxide in excess propan-2-ol. The ketone coordinates to Al; a hydride from the isopropoxide C–H is transferred through a six-membered cyclic transition state; the oxidised partner leaves as acetone. The reaction is an equilibrium, so it is driven by a large excess of propan-2-ol and removal of acetone by distillation. The reverse process with excess acetone is the Oppenauer oxidation.

Reactant:

Reagent: Al(OiPr)3, iPrOH, heat

Product:

C=O is reduced; the C=C bond is untouched.

MPV is highly chemoselective: C=C bonds, halogens and ester groups are not affected, and α,β-unsaturated ketones give allylic alcohols. Transfer hydrogenation uses a hydrogen donor (cyclohexene, 1,4-cyclohexadiene, ammonium formate, formic acid/triethylamine) with Pd/C or a Ru catalyst instead of gaseous H2.

Diimide, HN=NH, is generated in situ (hydrazine with H2O2/Cu2+, or by heating a sulfonyl hydrazide). It transfers two H atoms in a concerted syn manner to non-polar C=C and C≡C bonds, giving N2. Symmetrical, less polar multiple bonds react best, and C=O, benzylic C–O and aryl halide bonds are left unchanged.

Reactant:

Reagent: N2H4, H2O2, Cu2+ (diimide)

Product:

The benzyl ether survives; Pd/C and H2 would cleave it.

Trap. MPV reverses easily. A secondary alcohol is a poor MPV product if acetone is allowed to accumulate; conversely a substrate alcohol dissolved in acetone with Al(OiPr)3 is oxidised.
