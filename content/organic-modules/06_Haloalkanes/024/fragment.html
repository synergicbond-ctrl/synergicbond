23. Other reactions of aryl halides

23.1 Electrophilic substitution

Halogens deactivate the ring (–I outweighs +R for rate) but are ortho, para-directing (the +R lone-pair donation stabilises the arenium ion from o/p attack; meta attack gets no such help). The para isomer is usually major (steric hindrance at ortho, and less –I effect at para).

23.2 Coupling and metal reactions

  • Fittig: 2 ArX + 2 Na (dry ether) → Ar–Ar. Wurtz–Fittig: ArX + RX + 2 Na → Ar–R.
  • Ullmann: 2 ArI + 2 Cu (heat, ≈ 200 °C) → Ar–Ar + 2 CuI; works best with o-nitro iodobenzene (EWG).
  • Grignard: ArBr + Mg/ether (ArCl needs THF) → ArMgBr.
  • Rosenmund–von Braun: ArBr + CuCN (DMF, heat) → ArCN.
  • Hydrogenolysis: ArX + H2/Pd, base → ArH (also Na/EtOH, Zn/NaOH).

23.3 DDT and BHC

DDT (p,p′-dichlorodiphenyltrichloroethane; 1,1,1-trichloro-2,2-bis(4-chlorophenyl)ethane) is made by electrophilic substitution of two moles of chlorobenzene on chloral (CCl3CHO) in conc. H2SO4. BHC (gammexane, lindane, hexachlorocyclohexane) forms by radical addition of three Cl2 to benzene in sunlight (addition, not substitution; no catalyst).

+ + conc. H2SO4⟶
DDT: two chlorobenzene + chloral (the electrophile is the protonated aldehyde; para attack).
3 Cl2, sunlight⟶
Benzene hexachloride C6H6Cl6: the γ-isomer is the insecticide lindane.
