16. Mannich reaction

Reagents: a compound with an enolisable C–H (ketone, aldehyde, nitroalkane, phenol, terminal alkyne) + formaldehyde + a primary or secondary amine (as hydrochloride), in ethanol, mildly acidic. Product: a β-aminocarbonyl compound (Mannich base). Mechanism: amine + HCHO → iminium ion CH2=N+R2 (the actual electrophile); the enol of the ketone attacks it through its α-carbon. The final C–C bond is between the α-carbon and the former HCHO carbon.

+ + HCl, EtOH⟶
Acetone + methanal + dimethylamine → 4-(dimethylamino)butan-2-one
+ + H⁺⟶
Cyclohexanone + HCHO + piperidine → 2-(piperidin-1-ylmethyl)cyclohexanone
Trap Mannich bases lose the amine on heating or by quaternisation (elimination of R2NH to give an α,β-unsaturated ketone, here CH3COCH=CH2): they are masked vinyl ketones. A primary amine would give a secondary amine which reacts again (double Mannich); with ammonia or a primary amine the second N–H can also react. Use secondary amines for clean mono-Mannich products.
