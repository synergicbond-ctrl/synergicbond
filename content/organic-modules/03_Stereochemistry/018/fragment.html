15. Chirality without a stereocentre

Three kinds of molecules are chiral although no single carbon carries four different groups: those with a chiral axis (allenes, spiranes, hindered biaryls, alkylidene-cycloalkanes), a chiral plane (trans-cyclooctene, paracyclophanes), or a chiral helix (helicenes, twisted shapes). JEE Advanced regularly tests the first group.

15.1 Allenes (1,2-dienes)

In an allene X(a)(b)C=C=C(c)(d), the central carbon is sp, the two terminal carbons are sp2. The two π-bonds are at 90° to each other, so the substituents on one end lie in a plane perpendicular to the plane of the substituents on the other end. The molecule looks like a twisted "propeller" along the C=C=C axis.

Formula Allene X(a)(b)C=C=C(c)(d) is chiral (axial chirality) if and only if a ≠ b AND c ≠ d.
penta-2,3-diene (chiral) 1,3-dichloroallene (chiral) 1,1-dimethylallene (achiral) propadiene (achiral) hexa-2,3-diene (chiral)
Trap The test needs different groups on each end carbon, not four different groups overall. Penta-2,3-diene is chiral although it contains only H and CH3. Buta-1,2-diene (CH2=C=CH–CH3) is not chiral: one end carbon carries two H.
Example 46 Main

Allenes

Which of the following allenes are optically active: (a) CH3CH=C=CHCH3; (b) (CH3)2C=C=CH2; (c) CH3CH=C=CHCl; (d) C6H5CH=C=CHC6H5; (e) (CH3)2C=C=C(CH3)2?

Show solution
  1. Apply a ≠ b and c ≠ d at each end. (a) both ends H/CH3: chiral. (b) one end has two CH3: achiral. (c) both ends H/other: chiral. (d) chiral (1,3-diphenylallene). (e) both ends identical groups: achiral.
  2. Optically active: a, c, d.

Even versus odd cumulenes. Allene has 2 cumulated double bonds (even): axial chirality. Butatriene has 3 (odd): planar, shows cis/trans. Hexapentaene (5) is planar again, pentatetraene (4) is chiral. The rule: an even number of cumulated double bonds gives a chiral axis; an odd number gives geometric isomerism (the end groups lie in one plane). This is standard theory; the toolkit does not model it. Naming (aR/aS or P/M). Look along the axis from either end. Rank the two near substituents 1 and 2, then the two far substituents 3 and 4 (near groups outrank far groups). Trace 1 → 2 → 3: clockwise is aR, anticlockwise is aS. (P/M: helicity of the 1 → 3 torsion.) This is a convention described here for reading, not machine-checked.

15.2 Spiranes and alkylidenecycloalkanes

A spirane has two rings sharing one atom. The two rings lie in perpendicular planes, so the geometry is the same as in an allene: the substituents on the two sides of each ring play the role of a, b and c, d.

spiro[3.3]heptane-2,6-dicarboxylic acid (chiral) spiro[3.3]heptane (achiral) (4-methylcyclohexylidene)acetic acid (chiral axis)
Key idea Spiro[3.3]heptane-2,6-dicarboxylic acid is a textbook example (often called Fecht's acid). Each ring plane is perpendicular to the other; the H and COOH on C-2 lie in one plane, those on C-6 in the other; so a ≠ b and c ≠ d.
Alkylidenecycloalkanes: in (4-methylcyclohexylidene)acetic acid the ring end carries two ring arms that are constitutionally identical but face the methyl differently (one cis, one trans), the other end carries H and COOH: the molecule is chiral with an axis through C-1 and the exocyclic carbon.

15.3 Biphenyls and atropisomerism

Rotation about the single bond joining two rings can be so hindered by bulky ortho substituents that the two ring planes sit nearly perpendicular and the rotamers (atropisomers) can be separated. Three conditions are needed:

  • 1. At least three (usually four) ortho substituents, large enough to block passage through the planar transition state (the energy barrier must exceed about 80–100 kJ mol-1 so that the rotamers live long enough to separate).
  • 2. Each ring must be unsymmetrical with respect to the axis: on each ring the two ortho positions must carry different groups (a ≠ b, c ≠ d), exactly the allene criterion.
  • 3. The molecule must not be able to flatten: ring substituents must be big (I, Br, CH3, NO2, COOH, ...) and in the ortho positions of both rings.
6,6'-dinitrobiphenyl-2,2'-dicarboxylic acid (chiral) 2,2',6,6'-tetramethylbiphenyl (axis, achiral: a = b) biphenyl (achiral, free rotation) BINOL (axially chiral, 1,1'-bi-2-naphthol)
Trap Substituents must be ortho. Meta or para substituents do not hinder rotation. Biphenyls with only one ortho substituent on each ring (2,2'-disubstituted) are chiral in principle but racemise rapidly unless the substituents are very large (e.g. 2,2'-diiodobiphenyl type); with F, OCH3, OH they are not resolvable. 2,2',6,6'-tetrasubstituted biphenyls with a = b on each ring (such as tetramethyl) are achiral even though rotation is blocked, because each ring has a mirror plane containing the axis.
Shortcut BINOL and BINAP: chiral because each naphthalene ring is "unsymmetrical" about the axis (C-2 carries OH or PPh2, the other ortho position (C-8a) is a ring-fusion carbon). The rotation barrier is about 150 kJ mol-1, so the enantiomers are stable at room temperature; they are used as ligands/catalysts for asymmetric synthesis (Noyori's Nobel Prize 2001).
Example 47 Adv

Which biphenyls are resolvable?

Predict resolvability: (a) 2,2'-dimethylbiphenyl; (b) 2,2',6,6'-tetramethylbiphenyl; (c) 2,6-dimethyl-2',6'-dinitrobiphenyl; (d) 6,6'-dinitrobiphenyl-2,2'-dicarboxylic acid; (e) 4,4'-dinitrobiphenyl.

Show solution
  1. (a) a ≠ b on each ring (CH3 and H) so chiral in principle, but two small ortho groups give a low barrier: not resolvable at room temperature. (b) Four ortho groups, but a = b on each ring: achiral (the ring planes are perpendicular, each ring has a mirror plane through the axis). (c) Four ortho groups but each ring symmetric (2,6-dimethyl on one ring, 2',6'-dinitro on the other): a = b and c = d: achiral. (d) a ≠ b on both rings (COOH ≠ NO2), four bulky ortho groups: chiral and resolvable (this is the classical Christie–Kenner experiment). (e) para substituents: free rotation: achiral.

Biaryl chirality in practice: many drugs and ligands rely on atropisomerism; vancomycin, BINAP, BINOL, and many hindered biaryl amides. The three-orthosubstituent rule is for exams; real barriers depend on ring size, buttressing and temperature.

15.4 Planar chirality and helicity

  • trans-Cyclooctene is chiral: the ring cannot flip through itself, so the two twisted forms are enantiomers. (trans-cycloheptene exists only transiently; trans-cyclooctene is the smallest stable trans-cycloalkene.)
  • [n]Paracyclophanes with a substituent on the benzene ring: the short bridge locks the ring so it cannot rotate: a chiral plane.
  • Helicenes: ortho-fused benzene rings that cannot all be coplanar (hexahelicene) form a screw. Left-handed (M) and right-handed (P) helices are enantiomers and show enormous specific rotations (of the order of thousands of degrees for hexahelicene).
hexahelicene (screw-shaped, chiral) trans-cyclooctene (chiral)
Chirality from shape: no stereocentre

Optical activity, therefore, depends on the whole molecule's symmetry and not on atoms alone. Always run the σ / i / Sn test.

15.5 Bridged and fused ring systems: fewer stereoisomers than 2n

For rigid polycyclic compounds, the formula 2n fails, because the bridge forces certain combinations. Example: camphor has two stereocentres (C-1 and C-4, the bridgeheads) but only two stereoisomers ((1R,4R) and (1S,4S)); the combinations (1R,4S) and (1S,4R) would require an impossibly twisted one-carbon bridge. Counting with a three-dimensional embedding filter confirms this:

Key idea In norbornane/camphor type compounds, the bridgehead configurations are not independent. Count the stereoisomers by the allowed 3-D combinations, not 2n. The same holds for 2-substituted bicyclo[2.2.1]heptanes: exo/endo × enantiomer.
Example 48 Adv

Camphor

How many stereoisomers of camphor (1,7,7-trimethylbicyclo[2.2.1]heptan-2-one) exist? Which are optically active?

Show solution
  1. C-1 and C-4 are both stereocentres (C-4 is a bridgehead carrying H). Naively 22 = 4, but the bridge forces the configurations to be related. Only (1R,4R) and (1S,4S) exist: 2 stereoisomers, an enantiomeric pair, both optically active ((+)-camphor from the camphor tree is (1R,4R)).
Example 49 Adv

How many stereoisomers?

Count the stereoisomers of (a) 1,2-dimethylcyclohexane; (b) decalin; (c) norbornan-2-ol.

Show solution
  1. (a) cis (meso) + trans (pair) = 3; (b) cis- and trans-decalin: 2; (c) norbornan-2-ol: exo-OH and endo-OH, each chiral: 4 stereoisomers (two pairs).

15.6 Summary sheet: unusual chirality

  • Chirality ⇔ no σ, i, Sn; this may be caused by a stereocentre, an axis, a plane, or a helix.
  • Allene (even cumulene): chiral iff a ≠ b and c ≠ d on the two ends.
  • Biaryl: chiral iff ≥ 3 bulky ortho groups and a ≠ b on each ring. Tetra-ortho with a = b on each ring: achiral.
  • Spiranes behave like allenes (rings in perpendicular planes).
  • Bridged/fused rings: count by allowed 3-D combinations (camphor: 2, not 4).
  • trans-Cyclooctene and helicenes: chiral by shape.
