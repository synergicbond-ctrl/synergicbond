2. Nomenclature

2.1 IUPAC rules for aldehydes

  • The longest chain including the carbonyl carbon is chosen; the suffix -al replaces the final -e of the alkane. The aldehyde carbon is always C-1 and needs no locant: CH3CH2CHO is propanal, not propan-1-al.
  • Where the CHO group is attached to a ring or to a chain that cannot contain it, the suffix is -carbaldehyde: cyclohexanecarbaldehyde, benzenecarbaldehyde (benzaldehyde).
  • On a substituent the group is named formyl (–CHO) or oxo (=O on a chain).
  • Unsaturation is cited as in alkenes: CH2=CH–CHO is prop-2-enal (acrolein); CH3CH=CH–CHO is but-2-enal (crotonaldehyde). When the double bond is in the chain, the locants are fixed by the aldehyde at C-1 first.
methanal (formaldehyde) ethanal (acetaldehyde) propanal 2-methylpropanal 2,2-dimethylpropanal (pivaldehyde) prop-2-enal (acrolein) but-2-enal (crotonaldehyde) cyclohexanecarbaldehyde
Aldehydes: C-1 is always the CHO carbon

2.2 IUPAC rules for ketones

  • The suffix is -one; number the chain so the carbonyl carbon gets the lowest locant. CH3COCH2CH3 is butan-2-one (commonly written 2-butanone).
  • For rings the carbonyl carbon is C-1: cyclohexanone, 2-methylcyclohexan-1-one.
  • When a ketone ranks below another functional group, it is named as oxo (a prefix): CH3COCH2COOH is 3-oxobutanoic acid.
  • For C=O attached to two rings or to an aryl group, the ketone is named as a substituted methanone or ethanone: PhCOCH3 is 1-phenylethan-1-one; PhCOPh is diphenylmethanone; PhCOCH2CH3 is 1-phenylpropan-1-one.
propanone (acetone) butanone pentan-2-one pentan-3-one 3-methylbutan-2-one cyclohexanone 2-methylcyclohexan-1-one 1-phenylethan-1-one (acetophenone)
Ketones: the lowest locant goes to the carbonyl
butane-2,3-dione (biacetyl) pentane-2,4-dione (acetylacetone) ethanedial (glyoxal) hexane-2,5-dione cyclohex-2-en-1-one 4-methylpent-3-en-2-one (mesityl oxide) 1,7,7-trimethylbicyclo[2.2.1]heptan-2-one (camphor) 4-hydroxy-4-methylpentan-2-one (diacetone alcohol)

2.3 Priority and the "oxo" prefix When several functional groups are present, the principal group is chosen by the order carboxylic acid > anhydride > ester > acid chloride > amide > nitrile > aldehyde > ketone > alcohol > amine > alkene/alkyne. Lower-ranked groups are cited as prefixes. For carbonyls this gives:

  • Aldehyde beats ketone: CH3COCH2CHO is 3-oxobutanal (the ketone becomes oxo).
  • Ketone beats alcohol: CH3COCH2CH(OH)CH3 is 4-hydroxypentan-2-one.
  • Acid beats ketone: CH3COCH2CH2COOH is 4-oxopentanoic acid (levulinic acid).
  • Ester beats ketone: CH3COCH2COOC2H5 is ethyl 3-oxobutanoate (ethyl acetoacetate).
3-oxobutanal 4-hydroxypentan-2-one 4-oxopentanoic acid (levulinic acid) ethyl 3-oxobutanoate (ethyl acetoacetate) 2-hydroxybenzaldehyde (salicylaldehyde) 4-hydroxy-3-methoxybenzaldehyde (vanillin) 3-phenylprop-2-enal (cinnamaldehyde) 2,2,2-trichloroethanal (chloral)
The same rules applied to polyfunctional molecules
Trap A ketone group is "oxo" only when a higher-priority group is present. In 4-oxopentanoic acid the chain is numbered from COOH, so the oxo group has locant 4; do not number from the other end to give "oxo" a lower locant.
2.4 Greek-letter positions
The carbon next to C=O is α, then β, γ, δ. The hydrogens on the α-carbon are the α-hydrogens (α-H). In an enone C=C–C=O, the carbon next to C=O is the α-carbon and the other alkene carbon is the β-carbon: this is where the term "α,β-unsaturated carbonyl" comes from. Number of α-hydrogens is the first thing to count in any problem in this chapter.
count α-H⟶
In butan-2-one: C-1 (CH₃) has 3 α-H and C-3 (CH₂) has 2 α-H: five α-H on two different α-carbons

2.5 Isomerism of C=O compounds Aldehydes and ketones of the same carbon number are functional isomers (propanal and propanone, C3H6O). Ring-chain isomers (cyclopropanol, oxetane, allyl alcohol) are also possible for the formula C3H6O but are not carbonyls. A useful counting skill is to list all carbonyl isomers of C5H10O:

pentanal 2-methylbutanal (chiral) 3-methylbutanal 2,2-dimethylpropanal pentan-2-one pentan-3-one 3-methylbutan-2-one
All seven constitutional carbonyl isomers of C₅H₁₀O: four aldehydes, three ketones. With stereoisomers there are eight, because 2-methylbutanal has an enantiomer.
Example 1 Main

Counting carbonyl isomers

How many aldehydes and ketones (structural isomers, stereoisomers ignored) have the formula C4H8O? How many of them are optically active?

Show solution
  1. The degree of unsaturation is 1, used by the C=O. Aldehydes: butanal, 2-methylpropanal (two). Ketone: butanone (one). Total 3.
  2. None has a stereocentre: butanal is CH3CH2CH2CHO, 2-methylpropanal has two equal methyls on C-2, butanone has two H on C-3. Optically active: 0.
butanal 2-methylpropanal butanone
Example 2 Main

Name the compounds

Give IUPAC names: (a) CH3CH(CH3)CH2COCH3 (b) OHC–CH2–CH(CH3)–CHO (c) CH3COCH=C(CH3)2 (d) OHC–C6H4–COOH (para).

Show solution
  1. (a) Longest chain containing C=O has five carbons: CH3–CO–CH2–CH(CH3)–CH3. C=O at C-2 from the right end. 4-Methylpentan-2-one (isobutyl methyl ketone, "MIBK").
  2. (b) Two CHO groups. The chain is OHC–CH2–CH(CH3)–CHO: four carbons. 2-Methylbutanedial.
  3. (c) The enone: C=O is C-2 of CH3–CO–CH=C(CH3)–CH3; chain numbered from the methyl on the carbonyl side: 4-methylpent-3-en-2-one (mesityl oxide).
  4. (d) Acid outranks aldehyde: 4-formylbenzoic acid.
Example 3 Main

Priority

Name CH3COCH2CH2CHO and CH3CH(OH)CH2COCH3.

Show solution
  1. First: aldehyde outranks ketone; chain OHC–CH2–CH2–CO–CH3; the carbonyl on C-4 is the oxo prefix: 4-oxopentanal.
  2. Second: ketone outranks alcohol; numbered from the right end to give C=O the lowest locant (2): CH3–CO–CH2–CH(OH)–CH3 so the OH is on C-4: 4-hydroxypentan-2-one.
