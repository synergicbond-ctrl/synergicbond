2.2 Sublimation

Some solids pass directly from the solid to the vapour state on heating and back to the solid on cooling, without melting. This works for solids whose vapour pressure reaches the atmospheric pressure before they melt (or which have an appreciable vapour pressure well below the m.p.), when the impurity is non-volatile. The vapours condense on a cold surface (a water-cooled finger, or an inverted funnel with cotton plug).

Formula Typical sublimable solids: naphthalene, anthracene, camphor, benzoic acid, iodine, ammonium chloride, mercuric chloride (corrosive sublimate), dry ice (solid CO2), caffeine

Mixtures separated by sublimation: naphthalene + sodium chloride; camphor + charcoal or sand; benzoic acid + sand; I2 + NaCl; NH4Cl + NaCl. In each pair only the first sublimes.

Exam Trap Sublimation separates a sublimable solid from a non-sublimable one only. A mixture of NH4Cl and I2 (or naphthalene and camphor) sublimes together, so sublimation cannot separate two sublimable solids.
Validity Check A solid sublimes (rather than melts) at 1 atm when the pressure of its triple point is above 1 atm (dry ice: 5.1 atm), or when, as with naphthalene or iodine, the vapour pressure is high enough well below the melting point that vapour is collected before melting is significant. Check the property before choosing sublimation: it fails when both components sublime.
Concept check

Why can camphor + sand be separated by sublimation but camphor + naphthalene cannot?

Show answer

In the first pair only camphor is volatile as a solid; in the second both sublime and pass together.
