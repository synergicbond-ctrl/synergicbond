46. Problem bank, Level 1: alkenes

Practice 56 L1 Structure

Degrees of unsaturation

A hydrocarbon C5H8 takes up 2 mol H2 to give pentane. List the possible classes of structure.

Show answer

DoU = 2, and the product pentane is acyclic, so there is no ring: either two C=C (pentadienes) or one C≡C (pentynes).

Practice 57 L1 Structure

E/Z designation

Assign E or Z to the compound shown.

Show answer

On C-2: Cl > F; on C-1: CH3 > H. CH3 is on the same side as Cl, so the higher-priority groups are cis: Z.

Practice 58 L1 Structure

E/Z designation

Assign E or Z.

Show answer

C-2: Br > CH3; C-3: Cl > CH3. In the drawing Br and Cl are on opposite sides: E.

Practice 59 L1 Structure

Geometrical isomers

How many geometrical isomers: (a) hex-2-ene (b) hexa-2,4-diene (c) hex-1-ene (d) 2-methylbut-2-ene?

Show answer

(a) 2. (b) 3 (E,E; Z,Z; E,Z, since the molecule is symmetrical). (c) 1 (no isomerism, =CH2). (d) 1 (two identical CH3 on C-2).

Practice 60 L1 Stability

Stability order

Arrange in decreasing stability: 1-butene, cis-2-butene, trans-2-butene, 2-methylpropene.

Show answer

trans-2-Butene > cis-2-butene ≈ 2-methylpropene > 1-butene (heats of hydrogenation 115, 120, 119, 127 kJ mol-1).

Practice 61 L1 Stability

Which is most stable?

Which is most stable: 1-pentene, (E)-2-pentene, 2-methyl-2-butene, 2-methyl-1-butene?

Show answer

2-Methyl-2-butene (trisubstituted).

Practice 62 L1 Prep

Dehydration

Predict the major alkene when butan-2-ol is heated with conc. H2SO4. Name the minor products.

Show answer

Practice 63 L1 Prep

Ease of dehydration

Arrange in decreasing ease of dehydration: ethanol, propan-2-ol, 2-methylpropan-2-ol.

Practice 72 L1 Addition

Acid-catalysed hydration

Product of propene + H2O/H+, and of 3,3-dimethylbut-1-ene + H2O/H+.

Show answer

Practice 73 L1 Addition

Rearrangement

Why does 3-methylbut-1-ene + HBr give mainly 2-bromo-2-methylbutane?

Show answer

The initial 2° cation (CH3)2CH–CH+–CH3 undergoes a 1,2-hydride shift to the more stable 3° cation (CH3)2C+–CH2CH3, trapped by Br−.

Practice 74 L1 Addition

Hydroboration

Product of 1-pentene with (i) BH3·THF (ii) H2O2/NaOH. Compare with oxymercuration–demercuration.

Show answer

Pentan-1-ol (anti-Markovnikov). Oxymercuration–demercuration gives pentan-2-ol.

Practice 75 L1 Addition

Hydroboration of a trisubstituted alkene

Product of 2-methylbut-2-ene with BH3 then H2O2/NaOH?

Show answer

B goes to C-3 (the less substituted alkene carbon): 3-methylbutan-2-ol, (CH3)2CH–CH(OH)–CH3.

Practice 76 L1 Addition

What is the product of cyclopentene + Br2/CCl4? Is it optically active?

Show answer

Practice 77 L1 Addition

Halohydrin

Product of propene + Br2/H2O.

Practice 92 L1 Addition

Chiral products

Does the addition of HBr to but-1-ene give a chiral product? Is it optically active?

Show answer

Practice 93 L1 Addition

Carbocation stability

Arrange: CH3CH2+, (CH3)2CH+, (CH3)3C+, CH2=CH–CH2+.

Practice 96 L1 Mechanism

Peroxide effect with HCl

Does HCl show the peroxide effect? Explain.

Show answer

No. The step R• + HCl → RH + Cl• is endothermic (+5 kcal/mol), so the chain does not propagate efficiently. (HI also fails: I• addition to the alkene is endothermic and reversible.)

Practice 97 L1 Industrial

Ethene

How is ethene converted industrially to (a) ethanol (b) ethylene oxide (c) polythene?

Show answer

(a) H3PO4/SiO2, 300 °C, 60 atm, steam. (b) O2, Ag, 250 °C. (c) high pressure with trace O2 (LDPE) or Ziegler–Natta (HDPE).

Practice 98 L1 Reasoning

H2 uptake

A hydrocarbon C6H10 absorbs 1 mol H2 (Pt, RT) to give C6H12. Is it an alkyne? Give possibilities.

Show answer

One H2 absorbed ⇒ one C=C; DoU 2 ⇒ one more ring: a cyclohexene or alkylcyclopentene/cyclobutene etc. (a cycloalkene), not an alkyne.

Practice 99 L1 Reasoning

Which does not exhibit geometrical isomerism?

(a) but-2-ene (b) 1-chloropropene (c) 2-methylbut-2-ene (d) pent-2-ene.

Show answer

(c): C-2 carries two CH3 groups.

Practice 100 L1 Mechanism

Markovnikov rationale

Explain the regiochemistry of HBr addition to 2-methylpropene in terms of intermediates.

Show answer

Protonation at CH2 gives the 3° cation (CH3)3C+; protonation at C-2 would give the unstable 1° cation (CH3)2CH–CH2+. Br− traps the 3° cation: tert-butyl bromide.

Practice 101 L1 Mechanism

Bromonium

What is the evidence that bromination proceeds through a bromonium ion rather than an open cation?

Show answer

Exclusive anti addition (cyclohexene → trans only) and the absence of rearrangement; NaCl additions give bromochlorides, showing attack on the bridged ion.

Practice 102 L1 Mechanism

syn or anti

Classify as syn or anti: (a) H2/Pt (b) Br2 (c) OsO4 (d) BH3 (e) mCPBA (f) Hg(OAc)2/H2O.

Show answer

Practice 103 L1 Mechanism

Hydroboration regiochemistry

Why does boron attach to the less substituted carbon?

Show answer

Less steric hindrance for the bulky BH3, and in the polar TS the more substituted carbon bears more positive character and receives hydride-like H; no cation forms.

Practice 104 L1 Mechanism

E2 requirement

What geometric requirement governs E2?

Show answer

The leaving group and the β-hydrogen must be anti-periplanar (dihedral 180°).

Practice 105 L1 Prep

Alkenes from alkanes

Outline the conversion of propane to propene (via bromination).

Show answer

Propane + Br2/hν → 2-bromopropane (96 %); alcoholic KOH, Δ → propene.

Practice 106 L1 Prep

t-BuOK

Why does t-BuOK in t-BuOH favour the Hofmann alkene?

Show answer

The bulky base removes the sterically most accessible (least hindered) hydrogen, which is on the less substituted β carbon.

Practice 107 L1 Reasoning

Which gives acetone on ozonolysis?

Choose all: (a) 2-methylpropene (b) 2,3-dimethylbut-2-ene (c) propene (d) 2-methylbut-2-ene.

Show answer

(a), (b), (d). (a) gives acetone + methanal; (b) gives 2 acetone; (d) gives acetone + ethanal.
