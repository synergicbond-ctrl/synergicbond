2. Wurtz–Fittig and Fittig reactions (aryl–alkyl and aryl–aryl)

Wurtz–Fittig: ArX + R′X + 2 Na, dry ether → Ar–R′ + 2 NaX. Fittig: 2 ArX + 2 Na → Ar–Ar. The aryl halide is converted to an aryl sodium (or aryl radical), which reacts with the alkyl halide by SN2. Because Ar–X does not undergo SN2 itself, the mixed product Ar–R is the major one (unlike the all-alkyl Wurtz), together with some R–R and Ar–Ar.

Wurtz–Fittig: bromobenzene + bromoethane → ethylbenzene

Fittig: bromobenzene → biphenyl
Shortcut Compare with Friedel–Crafts: Wurtz–Fittig puts the alkyl group exactly where the halogen was (no rearrangement, no polyalkylation, no o/p mixture), so it is the route to a regiochemically pure alkylarene such as m-xylene from m-bromotoluene + CH3Br, which Friedel–Crafts cannot give directly.
Example 3 Main

Regiospecific alkylbenzene

Prepare m-ethyltoluene (1-ethyl-3-methylbenzene) from a single bromo-compound and one other halide.

Show solution
  1. Friedel–Crafts on toluene gives o/p products, so use a halide whose position is already fixed.
  2. m-Bromotoluene + C2H5Br + 2 Na, dry ether (Wurtz–Fittig) → m-ethyltoluene; the ethyl group takes the place of Br at C-3.
