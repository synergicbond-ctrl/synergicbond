20. Kinetic and thermodynamic enolates (regioselective enolate alkylation)

An unsymmetrical ketone has two different α-carbons, so it can form two different enolates. Which one forms depends on conditions, and which one you alkylate decides which constitutional isomer is made.

Choice of enolate from 2-methylcyclohexanone

Key idea LDA, −78 °C = kinetic = less substituted carbon. NaOEt/NaH, higher temperature, equilibrium = thermodynamic = more substituted carbon. The alkylating agent must be a primary, allylic or benzylic halide (SN2). A tertiary halide eliminates.
Example 29 Adv

Which enolate

Give the major product of 3-methylbutan-2-one, (CH3)2CH–CO–CH3, with (a) LDA at −78 °C then CH3I, (b) NaOEt/EtOH then CH3I.

Show solution
  1. The two α-carbons: the CH3 (primary, less substituted, three H) and the CH of the isopropyl group (tertiary, one H).
  2. (a) Kinetic: LDA removes an H from CH3: the enolate CH2=C(O−)–CH(CH3)2 reacts with CH3I: (CH3)2CH–CO–CH2CH3 = 2-methylpentan-3-one.
  3. (b) Thermodynamic: the more substituted enolate (CH3)2C=C(O−)CH3; methylation: (CH3)3C–CO–CH3 = 3,3-dimethylbutan-2-one (pinacolone).
