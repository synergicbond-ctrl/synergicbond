4. Oxidation of aldehydes and ketones: Tollens, Fehling and haloform

Aldehydes are oxidised easily because the carbonyl carbon carries a hydrogen; ketones are not, because oxidation would require C–C cleavage. Tollens reagent is an ammoniacal silver nitrate solution containing [Ag(NH3)2]+ in alkaline medium. It oxidises an aldehyde to the carboxylate and deposits metallic silver as a mirror: RCHO + 2[Ag(NH3)2]+ + 3OH− → RCOO− + 2Ag + 4NH3 + 2H2O. Two moles of silver form per mole of a simple aldehyde; methanal, which can be oxidised through methanoic acid onwards to carbonate, is usually quoted as reducing up to four Ag+ when Tollens reagent is in excess (the exact amount depends on conditions).

Fehling solution is a deep-blue alkaline copper(II) tartrate complex and Benedict solution is the citrate analogue. Aliphatic aldehydes reduce Cu(II) to red-brown Cu2O; aromatic aldehydes such as benzaldehyde do not respond, while they do respond to Tollens reagent. Ketones are negative to both, with the exception of α-hydroxy ketones (for example fructose), which isomerise in base to an enediol and then behave as reducing agents. Methanoic acid, an aldehyde as well as an acid, is positive to Tollens reagent.

Reactant:

Reagent: [Ag(NH3)2]+, OH−; then H3O+

Product:

A silver mirror forms; benzaldehyde is Tollens-positive but Fehling-negative.

The haloform reaction oxidises and cleaves methyl ketones and secondary methyl carbinols with hypohalite (X2 + NaOH, i.e. NaOX). The three α-hydrogens of the CH3CO group are replaced one after another because each halogenation makes the next enolisation easier; the CX3C(=O)R ketone is then attacked by hydroxide and CX3− leaves, giving the carboxylate with one carbon fewer than the ketone and the haloform CHX3. With iodine, yellow CHI3 precipitates (iodoform test).

Reactant:

Reagent: I2, NaOH (NaOI), then H3O+

Product:

Acetophenone gives benzoic acid (one carbon fewer) and iodoform.

Key idea. Iodoform-positive structures contain CH3–CO–C or CH3–CH(OH)–C: ethanal, ethanol and all methyl ketones and 2-ols. Methanol, propan-1-ol and pentan-3-one are negative.
Trap. The haloform reaction is not the same as Tollens oxidation: it shortens the chain. Iodoform formation is also negative for esters and amides of acetic acid, because they lack the CH3–CO–C ketone pattern.
