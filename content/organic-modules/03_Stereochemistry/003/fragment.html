0. How to use this chapter

This chapter is written as a course, not a summary. Each section teaches one rule, shows it on drawn structures, lists the exceptions, and ends with worked examples. The last parts are graded practice sets with full solutions: Level 1 (direct application), Level 2 (JEE Advanced single-correct and multi-correct), and Level 3 (integer, matrix-match and paragraph problems). Every R/S label, every E/Z label, every isomer count and every cis/trans statement printed here was checked by computer (RDKit's CIP labeller and a stereoisomer enumerator) while the notes were being built. Where a statement is a convention or an experimental number (for example an A-value), it is marked as such.

Key idea The order of attack for any isomerism question: (1) get the molecular formula and degrees of unsaturation; (2) decide constitutional isomerism first (connectivity); (3) only then ask the stereo questions: double bonds and rings (cis/trans), stereocentres, symmetry (meso), axes and planes of chirality; (4) count with symmetry in mind.
Shortcut Habit that wins marks: before counting, write down the symmetry of the skeleton (is the molecule R–X–R, R–X–R′, a ring with equivalent positions?). Almost every wrong count in this chapter comes from ignoring symmetry.
