11. Kinetics: rate laws, orders and the Arrhenius equation

The order in each reactant is found by changing one concentration at a time and observing the response of the initial rate. If doubling [RX] doubles the rate, the reaction is first order in RX; if doubling [Nu] has no effect, the order in Nu is zero (typical for SN1); if doubling [Nu] doubles the rate, it is first order in Nu (typical for SN2). When both mechanisms operate in parallel the observed first-order constant for a large excess of nucleophile is kobs = k1 + k2[Nu]; a plot of kobs against [Nu] has slope k2 and intercept k1.

Formulae. First order: ln[A] = ln[A]0 – kt; t½ = 0.693/k.
Pseudo-first order (excess Nu): k′ = k2[Nu].
Arrhenius: k = A e–Ea/RT; ln(k2/k1) = (Ea/R)(1/T1 – 1/T2), with R = 8.314 J mol–1 K–1.

Worked example. A substitution has Ea = 83.1 kJ mol–1. Between 300 K and 310 K, ln(k2/k1) = (83100/8.314)(1/300 – 1/310) = 9995 × 1.075 × 10–4 = 1.075, so k2/k1 = 2.93: close to the rule of thumb that a 10 K rise roughly doubles to triples a rate. Conversely, when the rate constant doubles from 298 K to 308 K, Ea = R ln 2 /(1/298 – 1/308) = 52.9 kJ mol–1.

  • Rate = k[RX] and no dependence on [Nu]: SN1 (or solvolysis).
  • Rate = k[RX][Nu]: SN2.
  • Rate = k1[RX] + k2[RX][Nu]: borderline, both pathways.
  • Remember that k for a second-order reaction has units L mol–1 s–1; for first order, s–1.
Trap. A rate that is first order in the nucleophile does not prove SN2 unless the stereochemistry or the substrate structure agrees; always use two lines of evidence.
