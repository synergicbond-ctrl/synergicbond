20. Water-forming additions: oxymercuration and hydroboration

20.1 Oxymercuration–demercuration (Markovnikov, no rearrangement)

Step 1 (oxymercuration): alkene + Hg(OAc)2 (or Hg(O2CCF3)2) in aqueous THF → β-hydroxyalkylmercury acetate. Step 2 (demercuration): NaBH4/NaOH replaces –HgOAc with –H. Mechanism of step 1: Hg(OAc)+ (formed by dissociation of the salt) is the electrophile; it adds to the alkene to give a bridged mercurinium ion (a three-membered ring of C, C, Hg2+), an ion that does not rearrange. Water attacks the more substituted carbon (which bears the larger share of positive charge) from the side opposite to Hg: anti addition of Hg and OH. Demercuration is a radical process and is not stereospecific.

1. Hg(OAc)₂, H₂O/THF 2. NaBH₄, OH⁻⟶
1-Methylcyclohexene → 1-methylcyclohexan-1-ol
1. Hg(OAc)₂, H₂O 2. NaBH₄⟶
3,3-Dimethylbut-1-ene → 3,3-dimethylbutan-2-ol, with no methyl shift (compare acid hydration)
  • With an alcohol solvent (ROH) the product is an ether (alkoxymercuration–demercuration): CH3CH=CH2 + Hg(OAc)2/CH3OH then NaBH4 → CH3CH(OCH3)CH3.

20.2 Hydroboration–oxidation (anti-Markovnikov, syn)

Borane (BH3, as BH3·THF or from NaBH4 + BF3, existing as the dimer B2H6) is a Lewis acid with an empty p orbital: an electrophile. Step 1, hydroboration: the alkene adds across B–H in one concerted, four-centre step: B bonds to one carbon and H to the other on the same face (syn). B bonds to the less substituted, less hindered carbon (steric factor); in the polar four-centre TS the more substituted carbon carries more of the positive character, so it receives the hydride-like H (electronic factor). No free carbocation forms, so there is no rearrangement. BH3 + 3 RCH=CH2 → (RCH2CH2)3B (trialkylborane; all three B–H react if the alkene is not hindered). Step 2, oxidation: H2O2/NaOH: HOO− attacks B, an alkyl group migrates from B to O (with retention of configuration), then hydrolysis: R3B → 3 ROH + B(OH)3 (borate).

Propene → propan-1-ol

Example 32 Advanced

Which reagent?

Starting from 3-methylbut-1-ene, how would you prepare (a) 3-methylbutan-2-ol (b) 3-methylbutan-1-ol (c) 2-methylbutan-2-ol?

Show solution
  1. (a) Markovnikov without rearrangement: 1. Hg(OAc)2, H2O; 2. NaBH4 (3-methylbutan-2-ol).
  2. (b) Anti-Markovnikov: 1. BH3·THF; 2. H2O2, NaOH (3-methylbutan-1-ol).
  3. (c) Rearranged Markovnikov: dilute H2SO4/H2O (H shift to the 3° cation) gives 2-methylbutan-2-ol.
Example 33 Advanced

Stereochemistry of hydroboration

1-Methylcyclopentene is treated with (i) BH3·THF; (ii) H2O2/NaOH. Give the product with stereochemistry and the number of stereoisomers formed. Repeat with D2-labelled B–D adding (i.e. BD3).

Show solution
  1. B adds to C-2, H to C-1, on the same face (syn). OH replaces B with retention: H (C-1) and OH (C-2) are cis, so CH3 (C-1) and OH (C-2) are trans: trans-2-methylcyclopentan-1-ol as a racemate (the two enantiomers form by attack on the two faces).
  2. Number of stereoisomers formed: one diastereomer (trans), two enantiomers = 2 (a racemic pair); no cis product.
  3. With BD3: D replaces H at C-1; the product is trans-2-methylcyclopentanol-1-d with D and OH cis (syn addition), still as a racemate.

Comparison table prediction

Give the products (constitution) of 1-methylcyclohexene with (a) H3O+, (b) Hg(OAc)2/H2O then NaBH4, (c) BH3, then H2O2/OH−, (d) HBr/ROOR, (e) HBr.

Show solution
  1. (a) and (b): 1-methylcyclohexan-1-ol (3° alcohol). (c): 2-methylcyclohexan-1-ol (trans). (d): 1-bromo-2-methylcyclohexane (cis/trans mixture; Br on C-2 via the 3° radical). (e): 1-bromo-1-methylcyclohexane.
