39. Electrophilic addition to conjugated dienes: 1,2 vs 1,4

HX or X2 adds to buta-1,3-diene to give a mixture of the 1,2-adduct and the 1,4-adduct, because the intermediate allylic cation is delocalised over two carbons.

  • Step 1: H+ adds to C-1 (the terminal carbon) to give the allylic cation CH3–CH+–CH=CH2 ↔ CH3–CH=CH–CH2+ (protonation at C-2 would give a non-stabilised primary cation).
  • Step 2: Br− attacks C-2 (giving the 1,2-adduct, 3-bromobut-1-ene) or C-4 (the 1,4-adduct, 1-bromobut-2-ene).
HBr, −80 °C (kinetic)⟶
1,2-Addition: 3-bromobut-1-ene (≈ 80 %): formed faster
HBr, 40 °C (thermodynamic)⟶
1,4-Addition: 1-bromobut-2-ene (E, ≈ 80 %): more stable
Kinetic vs thermodynamic control: the 1,2 pathway has the lower TS (positive charge is greater on the secondary carbon C-2, so Br⁻ attacks there); the 1,4 product has the more substituted internal C=C and is lower in energy
  • Low temperature, short time (kinetic control): the product that forms faster (1,2) predominates. The reaction is essentially irreversible.
  • High temperature (thermodynamic control): the 1,2-adduct reionises (allylic bromide ionises easily) and the mixture equilibrates, so the more stable 1,4-adduct (disubstituted internal alkene) predominates.
  • Heating the pure 1,2-adduct at 40 °C converts it to the equilibrium mixture rich in the 1,4-adduct: that proves the common intermediate.
Key idea Kinetic = 1,2 (lower Ea, proximity effect and greater positive charge at C-2); thermodynamic = 1,4 (more stable product). Also seen with Br2: −15 °C 54 : 46 (1,2 : 1,4); 60 °C 10 : 90.
Example 53 Advanced

Substituted dienes

Predict the products of HBr addition to (a) penta-1,3-diene (b) 2-methylbuta-1,3-diene (isoprene) (c) cyclohexa-1,3-diene. Which carbon is protonated?

Show solution
  1. (a) CH2=CH–CH=CH–CH3: protonation at C-1 gives the allylic cation CH3–CH+–CH=CH–CH3 ↔ CH3–CH=CH–CH+–CH3. Both ends are secondary and identical, so attack at either end gives the same constitution, 4-bromopent-2-ene (CH3CHBr–CH=CH–CH3), as E and Z forms and a stereocentre at C-4.
  2. (b) Isoprene CH2=C(CH3)–CH=CH2: protonation at C-1 gives the tertiary allylic cation (CH3)(CH3)C+–CH=CH2 ↔ (CH3)(CH3)C=CH–CH2+: attack at the tertiary end: 3-bromo-3-methylbut-1-ene (1,2); at the CH2 end: 1-bromo-3-methylbut-2-ene (1,4, major thermodynamic).
  3. (c) Cyclohexa-1,3-diene + HBr: the allylic cation is symmetric; 1,2- and 1,4-addition give 3-bromocyclohexene (the same compound for both modes).
