9. Gabriel phthalimide synthesis

Pure 1° aliphatic amines (free of 2° and 3°) are made in three steps.

  • Phthalimide (N–H pKa ≈ 8.3, acidic because the anion is stabilised by two C=O groups) is deprotonated by KOH to potassium phthalimide.
  • The phthalimide anion undergoes SN2 with a 1° (or unhindered 2°) alkyl halide to give the N-alkylphthalimide. The N is now fully substituted, so alkylation stops after one step.
  • The N-alkylphthalimide is cleaved by aqueous NaOH (or acid) giving phthalate and RNH2, or, more cleanly, by hydrazine N2H4 (Ing–Manske), giving RNH2 and phthalhydrazide.
KOH⟶ CH₃CH₂Br⟶ NaOH(aq) or N₂H₄⟶
Gabriel synthesis of ethylamine; phthalimide is a protected form of NH₃

Limitations (always examined):

  • Works only for 1° (and some 2°) alkyl halides. Aryl halides cannot be used (no SN2), so aniline cannot be made; 3° halides give elimination, so tert-butylamine cannot be made.
  • The product is always primary. Benzylic and allylic halides work well; neopentyl halides are too hindered.
Example 10 Main

Gabriel limits

Which of these can be prepared by Gabriel's synthesis? (a) benzylamine, (b) aniline, (c) tert-butylamine, (d) 2-methylpropan-1-amine.

Show solution
  1. Gabriel needs SN2 at the halide carbon. Benzyl bromide (a) and isobutyl bromide (d) are 1° halides and work.
  2. Aniline (b) needs an aryl halide: no SN2. tert-Butylamine (c) needs a 3° halide: elimination.
  3. Answer: (a) and (d).
Example 11 Adv

Build the halide first

Starting from benzene and using the phthalimide route, prepare benzylamine.

Show solution
  1. The phthalimide step needs an sp3 C–X bond. Benzene → CH3Cl/AlCl3 → toluene → Cl2/hν (or NBS) → benzyl chloride.
  2. Benzyl chloride + potassium phthalimide → N-benzylphthalimide → N2H4 → benzylamine.
