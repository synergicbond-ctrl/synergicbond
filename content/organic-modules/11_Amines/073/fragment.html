Practice 181 L2 Reactivity

Arrange the following diazonium ions in decreasing reactivity toward coupling with phenoxide in dilute NaOH: (I) p-O2NC6H4N2+, (II) C6H5N2+, (III) p-CH3C6H4N2+, (IV) p-CH3OC6H4N2+, (V) p-(CH3)2NC6H4N2+.

Show answer
  1. The diazonium ion is the electrophile: electron-withdrawing groups (NO2) increase the positive character of the terminal N (lower the LUMO). Donors (CH3, OCH3, N(CH3)2) delocalise their lone pairs/hyperconjugation into the N≡N+ group (quinoid forms) and reduce electrophilicity; the order of donor strength is CH3 < OCH3 < N(CH3)2.

I > II > III > IV > V.

Practice 182 L2 Triazene

Benzenediazonium chloride is added to p-toluidine in neutral solution. A yellow solid X (C13H13N3) separates. (a) Identify X. (b) What happens when X is warmed with p-toluidine hydrochloride? (c) What are the products on boiling X with dilute H2SO4?

X: 1-phenyl-3-(4-methylphenyl)triazene (b) 2-amino-5-methylazobenzene
Show answer
  1. N-coupling is kinetically preferred (neutral solution: the amine N is the best nucleophile) and reversible in acid; C-coupling is irreversible and thermodynamically favoured.

(a) The diazoamino compound (triazene) C6H5–N=N–NH–C6H4CH3-p. (b) It rearranges to the C-coupled aminoazo compound; the para position of the toluidine is blocked by CH3, so coupling occurs ortho to NH2: 2-amino-5-methylazobenzene. (c) The triazene is cleaved by acid back to the diazonium ion (which then gives phenol + N2) and p-toluidinium sulphate: products phenol, N2 and p-toluidine (as its salt).

Practice 183 L2 Dye

Benzenediazonium chloride is coupled with N,N-dimethylaniline at pH 4–5. Give the product, its common name and the reason for the position of coupling.

butter yellow
Show answer

4-(Dimethylamino)azobenzene ("butter yellow"). Coupling is at the para position of the tertiary amine; it is the position of the largest HOMO coefficient and the least hindered; the tertiary N cannot undergo N-coupling.

Practice 184 L2 Differentiate

Name two reagents each that distinguish aniline from cyclohexylamine and explain the observations.

Show answer

(a) Bromine water: aniline gives a white precipitate of 2,4,6-tribromoaniline; cyclohexylamine only forms the N–Br/ammonium bromide with no precipitate. (b) NaNO2/HCl at 273 K and then β-naphthol: aniline gives a red azo dye; cyclohexylamine evolves N2. (c) Basicity: 0.1 M cyclohexylamine has pH ≈ 11.8; aniline ≈ 8.8 – a simple pH-paper test.

Practice 188 L2 Basicity-nucleophile

Why is N,N-dimethylaniline nitrosated on the ring (para) while N-methylaniline gives an N-nitroso compound?

Show answer

N-Methylaniline has an N–H: NO+ attacks N and the proton is lost to give the stable N-nitrosamine. N,N-Dimethylaniline has no N–H; an N-nitroso adduct cannot lose a proton and reverts, so the ring (activated by NMe2, HOMO large at para) is nitrosated instead.

Practice 189 L2 Acylation vs alkylation

A 1° amine reacts once with an acid chloride but repeatedly with an alkyl halide. Explain using the nature of the products.

Show answer

The amide product is much less nucleophilic than the amine (lone pair conjugated with C=O), so acylation stops. The alkylation product is a more electron-rich (more nucleophilic) amine, so reaction continues to the quaternary salt.

Practice 190 L2 Hinsberg quantitative

1.00 g of a mixture of ethylamine (M = 45) and diethylamine (M = 73) gives 3.40 g of total sulphonamides with excess benzenesulphonyl chloride. Find the mass of ethylamine in the mixture (M of C6H5SO2NHC2H5 = 185; M of C6H5SO2N(C2H5)2 = 213).

Show answer
  1. Let x g be ethylamine: moles = x/45 → sulphonamide mass 185x/45 = 4.111x; diethylamine (1 − x) g → 213(1 − x)/73 = 2.918(1 − x).
  2. 4.111x + 2.918(1 − x) = 3.40 → 1.193x = 0.482 → x = 0.404 g ≈ 0.40 g (40 % by mass).

0.40 g ethylamine (40 %).
