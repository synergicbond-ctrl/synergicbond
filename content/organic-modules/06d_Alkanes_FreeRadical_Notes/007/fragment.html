8. Stereochemistry of radical halogenation

An alkyl radical is trigonal planar (sp2) or a very shallow pyramid that inverts rapidly. The half-filled p orbital is equally accessible to X2 from both faces. Consequently:

  • When halogenation creates a new stereocentre in an achiral alkane, the two enantiomers form in equal amounts, a racemic mixture that is optically inactive.
  • When substitution occurs at an existing stereocentre, the configuration is lost and the product is racemic, even if the reactant was optically pure.
  • When substitution occurs at a different carbon of a chiral molecule and creates a second stereocentre, the new centre is formed on either face of the radical but the faces are now diastereotopic, so diastereomers form in unequal amounts.

Reagent: Cl2, hν

Product:

2-Chlorobutane forms as a 1 : 1 mixture of R and S. 1-Chlorobutane (achiral) is the other monochloride.

Worked example. (R)-2-chlorobutane is chlorinated further. At C-1 gives 1,2-dichlorobutane (the original centre retained); at C-2 gives 2,2-dichlorobutane (centre destroyed, achiral); at C-3 gives 2,3-dichlorobutane as a meso compound plus a chiral (R,R) isomer, in unequal amounts; at C-4 gives 1,3-dichlorobutane (centre retained). That is 5 stereochemically distinct products.

2,3-Dichlorobutane: the first drawing is the meso isomer (achiral, internal mirror plane); the second is one chiral enantiomer, whose mirror image is the third stereoisomer.

Key idea. Counting rule for 'including stereoisomers': a chiral product counts twice (R and S), a meso compound once, an E/Z pair twice. For 'structural isomers only' count each constitution once.
Trap. A racemic mixture is not 'one product': when the question says to count all stereoisomers, both enantiomers are counted. A product mixture of enantiomers shows no optical rotation, but a mixture of diastereomers in unequal amounts may.
