63. Estimation of nitrogen — numerical methods

63.1 Dumas method

The compound is heated with CuO in a CO2 atmosphere; the nitrogen oxides are reduced over hot Cu gauze to N2, collected over KOH solution (which absorbs CO2). The volume is corrected to S.T.P.

Formula % N = (28 × V0) / (22400 × w) × 100 , V0 in mL at STP; w in g
Correction for aqueous tension: Pdry gas = Patm − aqueous tension.
63.2 Kjeldahl method

The compound is heated with conc. H2SO4 (K2SO4, CuSO4 or Hg catalyst) → (NH4)2SO4; excess NaOH liberates NH3, which is absorbed in a known volume of standard H2SO4; the unused acid is back-titrated.

Formula % N = 1.4 × N × V / w (N = normality of acid, V = mL of acid consumed by NH3, w in g)
Not applicable to: nitro compounds, azo compounds and ring-nitrogen compounds such as pyridine (the nitrogen is not quantitatively converted to ammonium sulphate unless special reduction is used).
Example 39 Main

Kjeldahl

1.00 g of an organic compound was treated by Kjeldahl's method and the NH3 passed into 50.0 mL of 0.50 M H2SO4. The excess acid required 30.0 mL of 1.00 M NaOH. Find % N.

Show solution
  1. 50.0 mL of 0.50 M H2SO4 = 50.0 mL of 1.0 N = 50.0 meq. NaOH used = 30.0 meq, so acid left = 30.0 meq; acid consumed by NH3 = 20.0 meq.
  2. % N = 1.4 × 20.0 / 1.00 = 28 %. (Check: 20 meq NH3 = 20 × 14 mg = 280 mg N in 1.00 g.)
Example 40 Main

Dumas

0.30 g of an organic compound gave 43.5 mL of N2 collected over water at 300 K and 715 mm Hg (aqueous tension at 300 K = 15 mm). Find % N.

Show solution
  1. P(N2) = 715 − 15 = 700 mm. V0 = 43.5 × (700/760) × (273/300) = 36.46 mL at STP.
  2. Mass N = 28 × 36.46/22400 = 0.04557 g; % N = 0.04557/0.30 × 100 = 15.2 %.
63.3 Molar mass of an amine from its chloroplatinate

Amines (and ammonia) give yellow-orange insoluble salts with chloroplatinic acid: (RNH3)2PtCl6. On ignition only Pt remains. For a monoacidic base B: salt = (BH)2PtCl6, M(salt) = 2(MB + 1) + 195 + 6 × 35.5.

Example 41 Adv

Chloroplatinate

0.500 g of the chloroplatinate of a monoacidic base leaves 0.1636 g of platinum on ignition (Pt = 195, Cl = 35.5). Find the molar mass of the base and identify it if it is a primary aromatic amine.

Show solution
  1. Moles Pt = 0.1636/195 = 8.39×10-4 mol = moles of salt. M(salt) = 0.500/8.39×10-4 = 596 g mol-1.
  2. 2(MB + 1) + 195 + 213 = 596 → MB = 93. A primary aromatic amine of M = 93: aniline (C6H7N).
Example 42 Adv

Titration of an amine

0.465 g of a monoacidic amine requires 50.0 mL of 0.100 M HCl for complete neutralisation. Find its molar mass, and the formula if it is a saturated 1° amine.

Show solution
  1. Moles HCl = 5.00×10-3 = moles amine, so M = 0.465/5.00×10-3 = 93 g mol-1.
  2. A saturated 1° aliphatic amine has M = 14n + 17: n = 5.43 – not an integer, so the amine is not saturated aliphatic; aniline (C6H5NH2, M = 93) fits.
Example 43 Adv

Mass of tribromoaniline

How much 2,4,6-tribromoaniline (M = 330) is precipitated when excess bromine water is added to a solution containing 4.65 g of aniline? What mass of Br2 is consumed (Br = 80)?

Show solution
  1. 4.65 g / 93 = 0.0500 mol aniline → 0.0500 mol tribromoaniline = 0.0500 × 330 = 16.5 g.
  2. Each mole consumes 3 Br2: 0.150 mol × 160 = 24.0 g Br2 (the other Br of each Br2 leaves as HBr).
