Part E. Rearrangements

A rearrangement changes the carbon (or heteroatom) skeleton: a group migrates from one atom to a neighbouring electron-deficient atom (a 1,2-shift), or bonds shift through a cyclic transition state (sigmatropic). Sort every rearrangement by the electron-deficient centre that receives the migrating group:

Migratory aptitude is the question behind most problems. For carbocation (pinacol-type) shifts and for peracid (Baeyer–Villiger) shifts the group that best stabilises positive charge migrates better: roughly H or aryl > tertiary alkyl > secondary alkyl > primary alkyl > methyl. The order of H and aryl changes with the reaction; the exact orders are given in each section.
