2. Vacancy and Interstitial Defects

  • Vacancy defect: a normally occupied site is empty; mass per crystal volume generally decreases, so density decreases.
  • Interstitial defect: an extra constituent occupies an interstitial site; for a simple added-particle model density increases.
  • Thermal defect concentration increases strongly with temperature.

3. Schottky Defect

◆Concept

In an ionic solid, equal stoichiometric numbers of cations and anions are missing so electrical neutrality and overall stoichiometry are preserved.
  • Favoured when cation and anion sizes are comparable and coordination is relatively high.
  • Density decreases.
  • Typical textbook examples: NaCl, KCl, CsCl; AgBr can show both Schottky and Frenkel character.

∑Formula

Dilute pair concentration: n/N ≈ exp[−E_s/(2k_BT)].

⚠Correction guardrail

A Schottky defect by itself does not automatically mean “p-type electronic semiconductor”. Keep ionic vacancy formation separate from electronic p/n-type classification.

4. Frenkel Defect

◆Concept

A smaller ion, usually a cation, leaves its normal site and occupies an interstitial site. A vacancy–interstitial pair is created without loss of material.
  • Density is essentially unchanged.
  • Favoured when cation and anion sizes differ greatly and the smaller ion can fit interstitially.
  • Examples: ZnS, AgCl, AgBr, AgI.

∑Formula

Dilute Frenkel count: n ≈ √(NN_i) exp[−E_f/(2k_BT)].

5. Thermodynamic View

◆Concept

Defect creation usually has positive enthalpy but also increases configurational entropy. At finite temperature the free-energy minimum therefore contains a non-zero equilibrium defect population.

∑Formula

ΔG = ΔH − TΔS.

◆Concept

Hence equilibrium defect concentration rises rapidly with temperature.

6. Metal Excess — Anion Vacancy / F-Centre

◆Concept

When an alkali halide is heated in metal vapour, anion vacancies can be created and electrons become trapped at those vacant anion sites. Such electron-containing anion vacancies are F-centres.
  • Colour develops because trapped electrons absorb visible light.
  • Electronic conductivity can increase.
  • Unpaired trapped electrons can contribute paramagnetism.
  • The crystal remains electrically neutral overall.

7. Metal Excess — Interstitial-Cation Model / ZnO

◆Concept

On heating ZnO, oxygen can be lost and the solid becomes zinc-rich/non-stoichiometric. The conventional school model represents excess Zn²⁺ in interstitial positions accompanied by electrons, producing n-type behaviour and yellow colour on heating.

⚠Density caution

Do not memorize a universal density sign from the phrase “metal excess”. Actual density depends on which atoms/ions are removed or added and on volume change.

8. Metal Deficiency — Fe₁₋ₓO

◆Concept

Some cation sites are vacant and charge neutrality is maintained by oxidizing some remaining lower-valent cations to a higher oxidation state.
  • FeO commonly exists as Fe₁₋ₓO rather than exact FeO.
  • Fe²⁺ vacancies are compensated by formation of Fe³⁺.
  • Density generally decreases; hole-type/p-type electronic behaviour can arise.

9. Impurity Defects and Charge Compensation

Doping situationCharge-compensation result
NaCl doped with CaCl₂ or SrCl₂Each M²⁺ replacing Na⁺ requires one Na-site vacancy per M²⁺ in the 2Na⁺ → M²⁺ + vacancy bookkeeping.
NaCl doped with AlCl₃One Al³⁺ on a Na⁺ site requires two Na⁺ vacancies per Al³⁺.
NaCl doped with LiClLi⁺ is isovalent with Na⁺, so no charge-compensating cation vacancy is required purely by valence.
NiO doped with Li₂OLi⁺ substitution for Ni²⁺ is compensated by oxidation of some Ni²⁺ to Ni³⁺ / hole creation, giving p-type behaviour.

⚠JEE Advanced traps & edge cases

  • Schottky lowers density; Frenkel leaves density essentially unchanged.
  • AgBr is a standard exception that can show both Schottky and Frenkel defects.
  • F-centre means an electron trapped in an anion vacancy, not simply any vacancy.
  • Always enforce electrical neutrality in impurity-defect counting.

▶Solved examples

Worked example 1

Example 1 — Doping stoichiometry

NaCl contains 10⁶ Na⁺ sites and 1000 Ca²⁺ ions substitute into Na sites. How many Na-site vacancies are required for charge compensation?

  1. 2Na⁺ sites are replaced by one Ca²⁺ plus one vacancy.
  2. Thus each Ca²⁺ substitution requires one Na-site vacancy in the bookkeeping.

1000 Na-site vacancies.

Worked example 2

Example 2 — Defect identification

A cation leaves its normal site and occupies an interstitial without changing composition. Identify defect and density effect.

  1. Vacancy + interstitial pair of same species → Frenkel.
  2. No particle is lost from crystal.

Frenkel defect; density essentially unchanged.
