68. Hofmann exhaustive methylation and the Cope elimination

Hofmann exhaustive methylation: amine + excess CH3I → quaternary ammonium iodide (SN2 on CH3I; each N–H is replaced by N–CH3) → Ag2O/H2O (AgI precipitates, leaving the hydroxide) → heat (400–450 K): E2 elimination gives the alkene (Hofmann rule: the least substituted alkene) + tertiary amine + water. The number of CH3I molecules consumed equals the number of replaceable N-H hydrogens plus one for the final quaternisation: a primary amine consumes 3 CH3I, a secondary 2, a tertiary 1. This gives the class of the amine, and repeating the process shows the number of C–N bonds to carbon.

Ag₂O, H₂O; Δ (≈ 420 K)⟶ +
sec-Butyltrimethylammonium hydroxide → but-1-ene (Hofmann) + trimethylamine (a little but-2-ene)

Cope elimination: a tertiary amine N-oxide (R3N+–O−, from R3N + H2O2 or peracid) on heating to 150–200 °C undergoes a syn elimination through a five-membered cyclic transition state (the N-oxide oxygen removes a β-hydrogen: an Ei reaction), giving an alkene + N,N-dialkylhydroxylamine. No external base is needed; the alkene is Hofmann-type; the syn stereochemistry is a favourite question.

Δ, 423 K (Cope elimination)⟶ +
N,N-Dimethylethanamine N-oxide → ethene + N,N-dimethylhydroxylamine
Trap Name clash: the Cope rearrangement (1,5-diene) and the Cope elimination (amine oxide) are different reactions. Compare the three "pyrolytic" syn eliminations: Cope (amine oxide, ≈ 400–450 K, gives R2NOH), Chugaev (xanthate, ≈ 450–500 K), ester pyrolysis (acetate, ≈ 770 K). All are intramolecular (Ei), syn, and do not rearrange the carbon skeleton, unlike acid-catalysed dehydration.
Example 60 Main

Exhaustive methylation

An amine of formula C3H9N consumes 3 mol of CH3I and, after Ag2O and heat, gives propene. Identify the amine and the final nitrogen-containing product.

Show solution
  1. Three equivalents of CH3I show –NH2 (primary amine). C3H9N primary: propan-1-amine or propan-2-amine. The elimination product is propene from either: Hofmann elimination of CH3CH2CH2N+(CH3)3 (β-H on CH2) or of (CH3)2CHN+(CH3)3 (β-H on CH3) gives propene + N(CH3)3. The nitrogen product is trimethylamine in both cases. Either isomer fits the data, so the alkene alone does not distinguish them.
