9. Acyl chlorides

9.1 Preparation and character

Made from the acid with SOCl2 (best), PCl5 or PCl3 (Section 7.2), or from the acid and oxalyl chloride. Acetyl chloride is a colourless, pungent liquid (bp 51 °C) that fumes in moist air (HCl). The C–Cl bond is not a normal alkyl–Cl: Cl− is only weakly able to donate by resonance (3p–2p overlap is poor), so the carbonyl carbon is highly electrophilic. Aroyl chlorides (PhCOCl) are less reactive than aliphatic ones because the ring conjugates with the C=O; the acyl C–Cl bond is still far more labile than any aryl C–Cl bond.

Example 20 Main

Acylation versus alkylation

Prepare n-propylbenzene from benzene. Why will propyl chloride/AlCl3 not work?

Show solution
  1. PrCl/AlCl3: the n-propyl cation (or polarised complex) undergoes a hydride shift to the isopropyl cation, giving mainly isopropylbenzene (cumene).
  2. Instead: benzene + CH3CH2COCl/AlCl3 → propiophenone (PhCOCH2CH3); then Zn–Hg/HCl (Clemmensen) or N2H4/KOH, glycol, Δ (Wolff–Kishner) → PhCH2CH2CH3.

9.3 Aromatic acid chlorides and carbamoyl compounds

  • Benzoyl chloride reacts with phenols and alcohols in aqueous NaOH (Schotten–Baumann benzoylation); a solid benzoate or benzamide is obtained, used for identifying amines and alcohols.
  • The Hinsberg-type test uses sulfonyl chlorides, not acyl chlorides (Amines chapter); but acetylation and benzoylation also distinguish 1°/2° from 3° amines, which cannot be acylated.
  • Phosgene COCl2 (carbonyl chloride) behaves as the acyl chloride of carbonic acid: with NH3 gives urea; with ROH gives carbonates and chloroformates (ClCOOR); with ArNH2 gives isocyanates ArNCO.
Trap Acyl chlorides react with AgNO3(aq) at once to give a white AgCl precipitate (because they hydrolyse to Cl−), whereas alkyl chlorides do not, and aryl chlorides and vinyl chlorides never do. A common test question compares CH3COCl, C2H5Cl, C6H5Cl, C6H5COCl and C6H5CH2Cl with alcoholic AgNO3.
