5. Electrophilic substitution of phenol: halogenation, nitration, sulfonation, Friedel–Crafts

The OH group is a powerful activator and directs o/p, so phenol reacts with milder reagents and under milder conditions than benzene. A Lewis acid is not needed for halogenation. In aqueous medium, phenol is partly present as the even more reactive phenoxide ion and all three activated positions react at once.

Reactant:

Reagent: excess bromine water

Product:

White precipitate of 2,4,6-tribromophenol; 3 mol HBr are formed per mole of phenol.

In a non-polar, non-ionising solvent (CS2 or CHCl3) at low temperature, ionisation is suppressed and the reaction can be stopped at the monobromo stage, giving mainly p-bromophenol with some ortho isomer.

Reactant:

Reagent: dilute HNO3, about 298 K

Product:

Mixture of o- and p-nitrophenol. Concentrated HNO3 tends to oxidise phenol to tars and quinones; the mixture is separated by steam distillation, because only the ortho isomer (intramolecular H-bond) is steam volatile.

Sulfonation is reversible, so temperature controls the product. At about room temperature the faster-forming ortho isomer predominates (kinetic control); at about 373 K the more stable, less crowded para isomer predominates (thermodynamic control).

Reactant:

Reagent: conc. H2SO4, 373 K

Product:

Thermodynamic product, p-hydroxybenzenesulfonic acid.

Picric acid is made industrially by first forming the phenol-2,4-disulfonic acid (the sulfonic acid groups protect the ring against oxidation) and then treating with concentrated nitric acid, which replaces both –SO3H groups and adds a third nitro group.

Reactant:

Reagent: (i) conc. H2SO4; (ii) conc. HNO3

Product:

2,4,6-Trinitrophenol (picric acid).

Trap. Friedel–Crafts reactions of phenol are poor. The oxygen lone pair coordinates to AlCl3 and deactivates the ring, and the O–AlCl2 species is a poor nucleophile. Acylation of phenol with AlCl3 mostly gives the phenolic ester (O-acylation); the ring ketone arises only after Fries rearrangement. Alkylation is usually done with an alcohol, alkene or carbonyl compound under acid catalysis, e.g. phenol + acetone gives bisphenol A.
