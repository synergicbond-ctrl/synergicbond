18. Identification of unknown amines — a working scheme

  • Step 1 – solubility. Dissolves in dilute HCl → an amine (or other base). Not dissolved in acid but contains N → amide, nitro, nitrile or weakly basic arylamines (di/triaryl).
  • Step 2 – Hinsberg. Separates 1° (dissolves in KOH), 2° (insoluble), 3° (no reaction).
  • Step 3 – HNO2. 1° aliphatic: N2; 1° aromatic: azo-dye with β-naphthol; 2°: yellow oil (nitrosamine); 3° aromatic: green nitroso colour; 3° aliphatic: nothing.
  • Step 4 – carbylamine / mustard oil confirms 1°.
  • Step 5 – elimination/degradation. Hofmann exhaustive methylation (number of CH3I) and the alkene formed fix the carbon skeleton.
  • Step 6 – derivatives: benzamide, acetamide, picrate, N-benzenesulphonamide with definite melting points.
Example 21 Adv

Identification chain

An organic compound A (C3H9N) is treated with benzenesulphonyl chloride in the presence of aqueous KOH: a clear solution is formed that gives a precipitate on acidification. A reacts with HNO2 at 273 K to give a gas and an alcohol B (C3H8O) that on oxidation gives acetone. Identify A and B.

Show solution
  1. Clear solution in KOH, precipitate with acid → 1° amine. N2 evolution → aliphatic 1°.
  2. B is oxidised to acetone, so B is propan-2-ol and A = propan-2-amine (isopropylamine). (Propan-1-amine would give a mixture of alcohols and could not give a single clean product.)
Example 22 Adv

Hinsberg numerical

0.58 g of a saturated 1° amine on treatment with benzenesulphonyl chloride gives 1.38 g of the sulphonamide. Find the molar mass of the amine and its formula. (C = 12, H = 1, N = 14, O = 16, S = 32)

Show solution
  1. RNH2 + PhSO2Cl → RNHSO2Ph + HCl. The amine mass increases by the group C6H5SO2 − H = 141 − 1 = 140 g per mole.
  2. Moles of amine = 0.80/140 = 5.71×10-3 mol. Molar mass = 0.58/5.71×10-3 ≈ 101 g mol-1.
  3. A saturated 1° amine CnH2n+1NH2 has M = 14n + 17 = 101 → n = 6: C6H13NH2 (C6H15N, a hexylamine isomer). Check: sulphonamide C12H19NO2S has M = 241; 5.71×10-3 × 241 = 1.38 g ✓.
