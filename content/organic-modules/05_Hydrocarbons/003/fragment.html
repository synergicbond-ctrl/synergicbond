Degree of unsaturation (DoU) = (2C + 2 + N − H − X)/2. Each ring and each π bond counts one. It tells you how many rings plus π bonds a formula must contain; ozonolysis and hydrogenation data then split the two.

Example 1 Foundation

Degrees of unsaturation

A hydrocarbon C7H10 absorbs 2 mol of H2 to give a saturated compound C7H14 (that is a cycloalkane formula). How many rings and π bonds can it have?

Show solution
  1. DoU = (2×7 + 2 − 10)/2 = 3.
  2. Two moles of H2 are taken up, so there are two π bonds (a ring is not hydrogenated under mild conditions). The remaining DoU is one ring.
  3. So C7H10 = one ring + two C=C (or one C≡C) — e.g. methylcyclohexa-1,3-diene or a cycloalkyne-containing structure; hydrogenation product C7H14 is a cycloalkane.
