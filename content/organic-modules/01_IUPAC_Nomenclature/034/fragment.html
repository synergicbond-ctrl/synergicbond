4.5 Carboxylic acids and their derivatives

Definition and rules. Acid: suffix -oic acid; the COOH carbon is C-1 (no locant). Diacids: -dioic acid (hexanedioic acid, adipic). On a ring: -carboxylic acid (cyclohexanecarboxylic acid); on benzene: benzoic acid. Prefix forms: carboxy.

Ester R′–CO–O–R: two words, alkyl (from the O side) + alkanoate (from the acyl side). "Propyl ethanoate" — the group on oxygen comes first.

Acid chloride: -oyl chloride (ring: -carbonyl chloride; benzoyl chloride).

Anhydride: replace "acid" by "anhydride": ethanoic anhydride; mixed anhydrides cite the two acids alphabetically: ethanoic propanoic anhydride.

Amide: -amide (ring: -carboxamide). On nitrogen: N- locants (N-methylethanamide, N,N-dimethylmethanamide). Prefix: carbamoyl (–CONH₂).

Nitrile: -nitrile; the nitrile carbon is counted in the chain (CH₃CN = ethanenitrile, 2 carbons); on a ring: -carbonitrile; prefix cyano.

Special Case On a ring or on benzene, the carbon of –COOH, –CHO, –C≡N, –COCl or –CONH₂ cannot be part of the parent, so the suffix names the group and its carbon: cyclohexanecarboxylic acid, cyclohexanecarbaldehyde, cyclohexanecarbonitrile. The same group on a chain is counted in the chain: butanoic acid, butanal, butanenitrile.

Example 26 Standard

Count carbons: how many carbon atoms are in 2-methylpropanenitrile and in cyclopentanecarbonitrile? Why are they different in kind?

a b
Show solution
  1. 2-Methylpropanenitrile: propane chain of 3 carbons includes the nitrile carbon, plus a methyl: 4 carbons total (C₄H₇N).
  2. Cyclopentanecarbonitrile: the ring has 5 carbons; the nitrile carbon is not part of the ring name, so the total is 6 (C₆H₉N).
  3. Rule: on a chain the group carbon is counted in the root (-nitrile, -al, -oic acid); on a ring it is added by the suffix (-carbonitrile, -carbaldehyde, -carboxylic acid).
