1.3 Anatomy of a name

5-chloro-3-methylhex-2-enoic acid
The skeleton behind the name analysed below

Take 5-chloro-3-methylhex-2-enoic acid and read it from the right, the order in which it was built:

  • -oic acid: the principal group is –COOH; the chain must contain that carbon, and it is numbered 1.
  • hex-: six carbons in the chain, counted from the –COOH carbon.
  • -2-en-: one C=C between C2 and C3. The locant is the lower-numbered carbon of the double bond, and the ending –en– replaces the –an– of the saturated name (hexanoic → hexenoic).
  • 3-methyl, 5-chloro: prefixes, each with the locant of the carbon that carries it, in alphabetical order (c before m).
Formula NAME = [prefixes with locants, alphabetical] + [root] + [an / en / yn with locants] + [suffix of the principal group]
Punctuation. Numbers are separated from numbers by commas and from words by hyphens; a one-word name contains no spaces. The final e of –ane, –ene, –yne is dropped before a suffix that begins with a vowel (butanol, propanoic acid) and kept before a consonant (butanediol, propanenitrile).
