Nature of Solids, Crystal Lattices and Unit Cells

10-part continuous textbook chapter

1.1 The solid state and structural order

A solid has a definite volume and resists shear because its constituent particles occupy positions around which only limited thermal motion is possible. The word constituent is deliberately broad: the repeating entities may be atoms, ions, molecules or extended covalent fragments. Solid-state chemistry therefore begins with the spatial order of these entities, not merely with the chemical formula.

In a crystalline solid, equivalent environments recur by translation through macroscopic distances. In an amorphous solid, the nearest-neighbour arrangement may be reproducible over a few interatomic distances, but no translation reproduces the entire structure over long range.

Macroscopic behaviour follows from microscopic order.

Do not confuse rigidity with crystallinity

Glass is rigid on an ordinary time scale but lacks long-range translational periodicity. Calling glass a ‘supercooled liquid’ is a historical simplification; for examination purposes, classify it as an amorphous solid.

1.2 Anisotropy, isotropy and polycrystalline materials

A physical property is anisotropic when its measured value depends on crystallographic direction. Electrical conductivity, refractive index, thermal expansion and mechanical strength may differ along different axes because the arrangement and bonding of particles differ with direction.

P[uvw]≠P[u′v′w′]P_{[uvw]}\neq P_{[u'v'w']}P[uvw]​=P[u′v′w′]​

A directional property PPP can have different values along two crystal directions.

A single crystal can display anisotropy clearly. A polycrystalline specimen contains many small crystallites or grains. If those grains are randomly oriented, their directional responses average out and the bulk specimen may appear isotropic. If processing produces preferred orientation, or texture, the bulk material can remain anisotropic.

Worked example

Recognising polycrystalline averaging

A metal foil consists of many cubic crystallites with random orientations. One crystallite is elastically anisotropic. Must the foil show the same anisotropy?

  1. The response of each grain depends on its orientation.
  2. Random orientations sample all directions statistically.
  3. The measured bulk response is therefore an orientational average, unless the foil has texture.
No. A random polycrystal may be macroscopically isotropic even though every grain is anisotropic.

Worked example

Bonding model from properties

A solid is soft, melts near room temperature and does not conduct in either the solid or liquid state. Which broad class is most likely?

  1. Low melting and softness exclude a three-dimensional ionic or covalent network.
  2. Failure to conduct in the melt argues against a simple ionic solid.
  3. Weak intermolecular attractions are consistent with a molecular crystal.
A molecular solid is the most likely classification.

1.4 Lattice, basis and crystal structure

A space lattice is an infinite periodic array of equivalent geometrical points. Every lattice point has the same surroundings as every other lattice point. A lattice point is not automatically an atom: it may represent an ion, a molecule, or the reference point of a multi-particle motif.

crystal structure=lattice+basis\text{crystal structure}=\text{lattice}+\text{basis}crystal structure=lattice+basis

Attach the same basis, in the same orientation, to every lattice point.

The basis or motif is the atom or group of atoms associated with each lattice point. Two crystals can possess the same Bravais lattice but different bases and therefore different structures. Conversely, a drawing that contains a point at the body centre is not necessarily a body-centred Bravais lattice; the corner and centre sites must be translationally equivalent.

The CsCl test

In CsCl, a Cs+^++ ion and a Cl−^-− ion occupy corner and body-centre positions. Because the two positions are chemically different, translation by (12,12,12)(\tfrac12,\tfrac12,\tfrac12)(21​,21​,21​) does not reproduce an equivalent point. CsCl is therefore not a BCC Bravais lattice.

1.5 Unit cell and lattice parameters

A unit cell is a finite region whose translations generate the entire lattice without gaps or overlaps. A primitive cell contains exactly one lattice point in total. A conventional cell may contain more than one lattice point because it is chosen to display symmetry more clearly.

Worked example

Primitive cell versus one atom

A primitive cell of a molecular crystal contains one lattice point. The basis at each point is a molecule containing 12 atoms. How many atoms are associated with the primitive cell?

  1. A primitive cell contains one lattice point, not necessarily one atom.
  2. Each point carries one 12-atom molecular basis.
The primitive cell contains one molecule, hence 12 atoms.
