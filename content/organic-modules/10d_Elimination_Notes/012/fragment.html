13. Effect of the leaving group and of the solvent

The leaving group appears in the rate-determining step of E1 and E2, so a better leaving group (weaker conjugate base) increases the rate. For halides the order of reactivity toward E2 is R–I > R–Br > R–Cl >> R–F, following the C–X bond strength and the stability of X−. Sulfonates (tosylate, mesylate, triflate) are better than halides, and neutral leaving groups such as H2O (from protonated alcohols) or amines (from quaternary ammonium salts) can also be used.

The leaving group also changes the regiochemistry. A poor leaving group such as fluoride makes the transition state resemble a carbanion (E1cB-like), so the more acidic, less hindered hydrogen is removed and the Hofmann alkene predominates. With good leaving groups the transition state is more alkene-like and the Zaitsev product is formed.

  • Solvent for E2 with an anionic base: polar aprotic solvents (DMSO, DMF) leave the anion poorly solvated and therefore more reactive, so E2 is faster than in alcohol solvents. In protic solvents (ethanol, water) the base is hydrogen bonded and slightly weaker.
  • Solvent for E1/SN1: polar protic solvents with a high dielectric constant (water, alcohols, formic acid) stabilise the carbocation and the leaving group, so ionization is faster.
  • Concentration of base: high [base] favours E2 over E1; low or no base with a polar protic solvent allows E1.

The bromide gives mostly Zaitsev (but-2-ene); the fluoride gives a much larger share of the Hofmann alkene (but-1-ene) because of the carbanion-like transition state.

Key idea. Better leaving group: faster and more Zaitsev. Poorer or bulkier leaving group: slower and more Hofmann. Polar aprotic: faster E2; polar protic: favours ionization (E1/SN1).
