11. Counting alkenes from a substrate

Counting problems appear often. Use a fixed routine to avoid missing or double-counting products.

  1. Locate the carbon bearing the leaving group (the α-carbon).
  2. List every neighbouring carbon (β-carbon) that has at least one hydrogen. Each such carbon gives one constitutional alkene (a new C=C between α and that β-carbon). Equivalent β-carbons give the same alkene and are counted once.
  3. For each alkene, check whether each sp2 carbon has two different substituents. If so, the alkene has E and Z forms, and both are counted when the question asks for stereoisomers.
  4. Add the totals. Report constitutional isomers and stereoisomers separately as the question requires.

Reactant:

Reagent: NaOEt, EtOH, heat

Product:

3-Bromo-3-methylhexane has three different β-carbons (CH3, C2 and C4). The CH3 route gives one alkene without E/Z isomerism; C2 and C4 each give an E/Z pair. Result: 3 constitutional alkenes, 5 including stereoisomers.

Reactant:

Reagent: NaOEt, EtOH

Product:

3-Chloropentane has two equivalent β-carbons, so only one constitutional alkene (pent-2-ene), but it exists as E and Z: 1 constitutional, 2 with stereoisomers.

Trap. Do not count enantiomers of chiral alkenes as extra products unless the question says so. Also, a β-carbon that has no hydrogen (quaternary) gives no alkene. A double bond inside a small ring (six-membered or smaller) can only be cis, so ring alkenes of that size have no E/Z pair.
