6.5 The peptide bond: resonance and a three-orbital picture

The six atoms Cα–C(=O)–N(H)–Cα′ are coplanar because the N lone pair is delocalised.

  • VB picture: O=C–N: ↔ −O–C=N+. The second form contributes substantially, so the C–N bond is intermediate between single and double (1.32 Å).
  • MO picture: three p orbitals (on O, C, N) combine into three π MOs. ψ1 (bonding, all in phase, lowest) holds two electrons; ψ2 (non-bonding, node at C, mostly O and N) holds two; ψ3 (antibonding) is empty. Four electrons fill ψ1 and ψ2. ψ1 has bonding character across both C–O and C–N, which is the partial double bond and the ≈ 80 kJ mol−1 rotation barrier: rotating about C–N moves the N p orbital out of overlap with the carbonyl π system and loses that stabilisation.
  • Consequences: (i) planar and usually trans; (ii) the amide N is non-basic (its lone pair is in ψ1/ψ2); (iii) the carbonyl is less electrophilic than that of a ketone (higher-energy π* because of the filled ψ2 from N) so amide bonds survive in water for years; (iv) protonation of an amide occurs on O, not N (O-protonation keeps the delocalisation).
  • Hydrolysis (acid): O-protonation lowers the LUMO of the carbonyl, water attacks (HOMO → π*), a tetrahedral intermediate forms, N is protonated (so that it is a leaving group), and the C–N bond breaks. (Base: HO− attacks; the amide −NHR is a poor leaving group, so base hydrolysis needs heat.)
