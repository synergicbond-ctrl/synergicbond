8. Aldol addition and condensation

Reagents: dilute NaOH (or aq. base, or acid) at low temperature gives the aldol (β-hydroxy aldehyde or ketone); heating (or acid) dehydrates it to the α,β-unsaturated aldol condensation product. Needs at least one α-hydrogen on the enolisable partner. Mechanism (base): (1) OH− removes an α-H to give the resonance-stabilised enolate; (2) the enolate α-carbon attacks the C=O carbon of a second molecule to give an alkoxide; (3) the alkoxide takes H+ from water to give the β-hydroxy carbonyl; (4) on heating, E1cB elimination: the new α-H is removed, then OH− leaves, giving a conjugated enone. The dehydration is irreversible because the product is conjugated, which is why heating pushes the equilibrium to the condensation product.

Key idea Product pattern: the new C–C bond is between the α-carbon of one molecule and the carbonyl carbon of the other. The aldol product has the carbonyl carbon (C-1), the old α-carbon (C-2) and the new carbinol carbon (C-3). Carbon count of the aldol from RCH2CHO is 2n; of a ketone dimer 2n as well.

Example 14 Adv

Intramolecular aldol, ring size

Hexane-2,5-dione and heptane-2,6-dione are each treated with dilute NaOH and heated. Give the product and explain ring size.

Show solution
  1. Hexane-2,5-dione: enolate of C-1 attacks C-5; ring = C-1, C-2, C-3, C-4, C-5 = five-membered; dehydration gives 3-methylcyclopent-2-en-1-one (conjugated).
  2. Heptane-2,6-dione: enolate at C-1 attacks C-6, ring C-1…C-6 = six-membered; 3-methylcyclohex-2-en-1-one.
  3. Other ring closures would give 3- or 4-membered (strained) or 7-membered (slow, entropically disfavoured) rings, so intramolecular aldol gives 5- and 6-membered rings only.
NaOH, Δ⟶
Intramolecular aldol condensation: hexane-2,5-dione → 3-methylcyclopent-2-enone

Crossed aldol (two different carbonyl compounds). If both partners have α-hydrogens, four products form (two self, two crossed), which is useless synthetically. Make it selective in one of three ways.

  • One partner has no α-H (HCHO, ArCHO, (CH3)3CCHO, R3C–CHO, ethyl formate, ethyl oxalate): this partner can only be the electrophile. Add the enolisable compound slowly to the mixture of the non-enolisable one and base. This is the Claisen–Schmidt condensation.
  • Preformed enolate: convert the nucleophilic partner completely into its enolate with LDA at −78 °C, then add the other carbonyl compound (directed aldol).
  • Different electrophilicity: an aldehyde is a much better electrophile than a ketone, so a ketone enolate adds to an aldehyde (not to another ketone).
Trap Dehydration needs an H on the α-carbon of the aldol. If that carbon is quaternary the β-hydroxy carbonyl is isolated and cannot dehydrate by E1cB: isobutanal (CH3)2CHCHO gives (CH3)2CH–CH(OH)–C(CH3)2–CHO (3-hydroxy-2,2,4-trimethylpentanal), which has no α-H and does not lose water under base.
Example 15 Adv

Counting products of a mixed aldol

How many different aldol (addition) products, ignoring stereoisomers, are possible when ethanal and propanal are mixed with dilute NaOH? Give the count when the partner without α-H is used instead.

Show solution
  1. Let A = CH3CHO and B = CH3CH2CHO; either can give an enolate and either can be attacked, so there are 2 × 2 = 4 aldol products:
  2. A-enolate + A → CH3CH(OH)CH2CHO (3-hydroxybutanal); A-enolate + B → CH3CH2CH(OH)CH2CHO (3-hydroxypentanal); B-enolate + A → CH3CH(OH)CH(CH3)CHO (3-hydroxy-2-methylbutanal); B-enolate + B → CH3CH2CH(OH)CH(CH3)CHO (3-hydroxy-2-methylpentanal).
  3. Replace B by benzaldehyde (no α-H): only the ethanal enolate exists, and with PhCHO in excess the crossed product dominates (cinnamaldehyde after dehydration). Hence only 2 products in principle (self-aldol of ethanal and the crossed product), and 1 in practice.
