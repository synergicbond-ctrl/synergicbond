7.1 Intramolecular dehydration (to alkenes)

Reagents: conc. H2SO4 or H3PO4, Δ; Al2O3 at 350–400 °C (vapour phase); POCl3/pyridine; P2O5; I2 (catalytic); anhydrous ZnCl2. Conditions are milder the more substituted the alcohol: 1° alcohols need about 95 % H2SO4 at 170–180 °C; 2° alcohols about 75 % acid at 100–140 °C; 3° alcohols dehydrate in 20–50 % acid at 25–80 °C (approximate textbook values). This is the same order as carbocation stability, because the rate-determining step is loss of water from the protonated alcohol. Mechanism (E1 for 2° and 3°): (1) protonation; (2) loss of H2O → carbocation (rate-determining); (3) a base (H2O or HSO4−) removes a β-proton. For 1° alcohols a concerted E2-like path avoids a high-energy primary cation (the protonated ethanol loses water as a base removes the β-H).

H₂SO₄ (dil.), warm⟶ +
2-Methylbutan-2-ol: Zaitsev product 2-methylbut-2-ene (about 85 %) and 2-methylbut-1-ene (about 15 %)

Zaitsev (Saytzeff) rule: the more substituted (more stable) alkene predominates. Alkene stability: tetrasubstituted > trisubstituted > disubstituted > monosubstituted, and trans > cis. Reasons: hyperconjugation and stronger sp2–sp3 C–C bonds. E1 gives mainly the trans isomer when both are possible (the transition state resembles the alkene). Rearrangement: since a carbocation is formed, the 2° cation of a 2° alcohol rearranges to a 3° cation by hydride or methyl shift if that is possible, and the dehydration product comes from the more stable cation.

1-Cyclobutylethanol: the secondary cation expands the strained four-membered ring to a five-membered ring (relief of ring strain), then a hydride shift gives the 1-methylcyclopentyl cation → 1-methylcyclopentene
Key idea Ring expansion (cyclobutylcarbinyl → cyclopentyl cation) and hydride/methyl shifts are driven by moving the + charge to a more substituted carbon or relieving strain. Ring expansion is favourable for 4 → 5 (relief of strain); for an unstrained cyclohexylcarbinyl system a hydride shift to the tertiary 1-methylcyclohexyl cation is preferred instead of expansion to a seven-membered ring.
Stereoelectronic effects in E2 dehydration (POCl3/pyridine). Phosphorus oxychloride converts OH to OPOCl2, a good leaving group, with pyridine as the base; no carbocation forms and there is no rearrangement. E2 requires the leaving group and the β-H to be anti-periplanar (both axial in cyclohexane). So cis-2-methylcyclohexanol (OH axial in the conformer with equatorial CH3) has an axial H on C-2 anti to OH and gives the Zaitsev product 1-methylcyclohexene; trans-2-methylcyclohexanol has OH axial only in the diaxial conformer where C-2 H is equatorial, so it eliminates only towards C-6 to give 3-methylcyclohexene (Hofmann-like).
