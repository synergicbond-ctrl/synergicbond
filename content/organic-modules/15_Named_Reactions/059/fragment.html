53. Dienone–phenol rearrangement

A 4,4-disubstituted cyclohexa-2,5-dienone with acid rearranges to a 3,4-disubstituted phenol: the protonated C=O makes C-4 part of a cation delocalised through the ring; a group on C-4 (alkyl or aryl) migrates to the adjacent C-3, and loss of H+ then aromatises the ring. Aromatisation is the driving force.

H₂SO₄, (CH₃CO)₂O⟶
4,4-Dimethylcyclohexa-2,5-dienone → 3,4-dimethylphenol (one CH3 shifts C-4 → C-3)
Key idea No H remains on C-4 to be lost, so aromatisation cannot happen by simple deprotonation: the 1,2-shift is what lets the ring aromatise. This is the logic behind products when both ortho positions of a phenol are blocked (para-substituted dienones in Reimer–Tiemann with p-cresol).
