1.1 IUPAC nomenclature of alcohols

  • Choose the longest continuous carbon chain that contains the carbon bearing OH (even if a longer chain exists elsewhere).
  • Number from the end that gives OH the lowest locant (the suffix gets the lowest number before double bonds, substituents, etc.).
  • Replace the final “e” of the alkane by “ol”: butane → butan-ol; add “di”, “tri” before the suffix for several OH (keep the “e”: propane-1,2-diol).
  • Unsaturation: the OH gets priority for the low locant, then the multiple bond: CH2=CH–CH2OH is prop-2-en-1-ol, CH3CH=CHCH2OH is but-2-en-1-ol.
  • Cyclic alcohols: the carbon bearing OH is C-1 and need not be numbered: cyclohexanol, 2-methylcyclohexan-1-ol.
  • If a group of higher priority is present the OH is expressed as the prefix hydroxy-.

Priority order of the principal functional groups (descending): carboxylic acids > sulphonic acids > anhydrides > esters > acid halides > amides > nitriles > aldehydes > ketones > alcohols > amines > alkenes > alkynes. Halo, nitro, alkoxy, alkyl are never principal groups.

Common (trivial) names to know: methyl alcohol (methanol, wood spirit), ethyl alcohol (ethanol, grain alcohol), isopropyl alcohol (propan-2-ol, rubbing alcohol), n-butyl, isobutyl (2-methylpropan-1-ol), sec-butyl (butan-2-ol), tert-butyl (2-methylpropan-2-ol), allyl alcohol, benzyl alcohol, ethylene glycol, glycerol (glycerine), pinacol, neopentyl alcohol.
Example 1 L1 Foundation

Naming and classification

Give the IUPAC name and the class (1°, 2°, 3°) of (a) (CH3)2CH–CH2–CH2OH, (b) CH3CH2CH(OH)CH(CH3)2, (c) (CH3)3C–CH(OH)CH3.

Show solution
  1. (a) The chain containing the C–OH is four carbons with a methyl at C-3 (numbering from the OH end): 3-methylbutan-1-ol, primary.
  2. (b) The longest chain that contains the carbinol carbon is five carbons (ethyl on one side, the CH(CH3)–CH3 unit on the other). OH sits on C-3 whichever end we number from, so the tie is broken by the substituent: numbering from the isopropyl end puts the methyl on C-2 (the other direction would give C-4). Name: 2-methylpentan-3-ol, secondary.
  3. (c) Chain: CH3–CH(OH)–C(CH3)3: butane chain with OH at C-2 and two methyls at C-3: 3,3-dimethylbutan-2-ol, secondary.
Example 2 L1 Foundation

Priority of groups

Name CH3–CH(OH)–CH2–CHO and HO–CH2–CH=CH–CH2–COOH.

Show solution
  1. First compound: aldehyde outranks alcohol, so the chain is numbered from CHO as C-1 and OH becomes a prefix: 3-hydroxybutanal.
  2. Second compound: the acid is the principal group, numbering from COOH: C-1 COOH, C-2 CH2, C-3 =CH, C-4 =CH, C-5 CH2OH: 5-hydroxypent-3-enoic acid. The double bond is between C-3 and C-4. Its geometry would be indicated as (E) or (Z) if known.
