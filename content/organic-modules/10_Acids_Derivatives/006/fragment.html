6. Preparation of carboxylic acids

Every route either (i) oxidises a carbon that is already bonded to O or N, (ii) adds a carbon (CO2 or CN−), or (iii) hydrolyses a derivative. Learn each route with its carbon bookkeeping (+1, 0, −1) because that is how problems are set.

6.1 Oxidation methods

  • Primary alcohol → aldehyde → acid. Strong aqueous oxidants cannot stop at the aldehyde (the hydrate is oxidised quickly), so the acid is the product. PCC or Swern stop at the aldehyde instead.
  • Alkylbenzenes are oxidised at the benzylic carbon whatever the chain length: toluene, ethylbenzene, isopropylbenzene (cumene) all give benzoic acid. A benzylic hydrogen is mandatory because the first step is benzylic C–H abstraction: tert-butylbenzene does not react. Two alkyl groups give a diacid: o-xylene → phthalic acid, p-xylene → terephthalic acid (the raw material of PET).
  • Alkenes cleave: CH3CH=CHCH3 → 2 CH3COOH; CH3CH=CH2 → CH3COOH + CO2; cyclohexene → adipic acid HOOC(CH2)4COOH; cyclohexanone with HNO3 → adipic acid. Alkynes cleave into two acids; a terminal alkyne gives CO2 as the second product.
  • Haloform reaction: a methyl ketone (or ethanol, acetaldehyde, a methyl carbinol) with X2/NaOH gives CHX3 and a carboxylate with one carbon fewer than the ketone. It is also a clean way to prepare benzoic acid from acetophenone and to tell methyl ketones apart.

<h4>6.2 Grignard reagent + carbon dioxide</h4>

RMgX adds to the C=O of CO2 (a weak but real electrophile) to give a magnesium carboxylate, protonated on acid work-up. The sequence adds one carbon.

1. CO₂ (dry ice) 2. H₃O⁺⟶
Ethylmagnesium bromide → propanoic acid
1. Mg, ether 2. CO₂ 3. H₃O⁺⟶
Bromobenzene → benzoic acid (aryl halides can be used here, unlike with CN⁻)
cyclohexanecarboxylic acid from cyclohexyl bromide (1. Mg 2. CO₂ 3. H₃O⁺)

Limitations: the R group cannot contain O–H, N–H, C=O, NO2, CN, or other groups that react with RMgX; the solvent must be dry ether (water would destroy the reagent).

Shortcut Carbon-counting shortcut. R–Br → RCOOH by Grignard/CO2 or by CN−/H3O+: both add one carbon. When the halide is aryl, vinyl or 3°, use the Grignard route; when the molecule has a C=O or O–H, use the nitrile route (CN− is tolerated, Mg is not).
<h4>6.3 Nitrile hydrolysis</h4>

RX + KCN (SN2) → RCN; RCN + H2O/H+ (or OH−) → RCOOH + NH4+ (or NH3). Complete hydrolysis passes through the amide. Other sources of nitriles: dehydration of amides (P2O5), cyanohydrins (→ α-hydroxy acids), the Sandmeyer reaction of ArN2+ with CuCN (→ ArCN → ArCOOH), and Rosenmund–von Braun (ArBr + CuCN).

Trap Alkyl halide + KCN gives RCN (cyanide attacks through C); AgCN gives RNC (isocyanide) because the covalent Ag–CN bond leaves the N lone pair free. Only the nitrile hydrolyses to an acid.
<h4>6.4 Kolbe–Schmitt reaction</h4>

Sodium phenoxide is carboxylated by CO2 under pressure at about 125 °C; acidification gives salicylic acid (2-hydroxybenzoic acid). The sodium ion chelates the phenoxide oxygen and the incoming CO2, so attack goes ortho. Potassium phenoxide, with a larger ion that chelates poorly, gives mainly p-hydroxybenzoic acid at higher temperature (about 200 °C).

1. CO₂, 125 °C, 5 atm 2. H₃O⁺⟶
Sodium phenoxide → salicylic acid
(CH₃CO)₂O, H⁺ cat.⟶
Salicylic acid → aspirin (acetylsalicylic acid)

6.5 Arndt–Eistert homologation (adds one CH2)

RCOOH → RCOCl (SOCl2); RCOCl + 2 CH2N2 → RCOCHN2 (a diazoketone) + CH3Cl + N2; Ag2O, H2O (or ROH, or NH3) → Wolff rearrangement to the ketene R–CH=C=O, which adds the nucleophile to give RCH2COOH (or ester or amide). The migrating group R keeps its configuration (retention).

1. SOCl₂ 2. CH₂N₂ (2 equiv.) 3. Ag₂O, H₂O⟶
Benzoic acid → phenylacetic acid
Key idea Why two equivalents of diazomethane: the first acylates CH2N2 to give the diazonium-ketone salt RCOCH2N2+ Cl−; the second removes HCl as CH3Cl. Without the second equivalent the α-chloromethyl ketone RCOCH2Cl is the product.
<h4>6.6 Other routes worth knowing</h4>
  • Industrial acetic acid: methanol carbonylation (CH3OH + CO, Rh/I−) and oxidation of acetaldehyde (Mn2+ or Co2+, air). Formic acid: CO + NaOH, 120–150 °C, 6–10 atm → HCOONa → H2SO4 → HCOOH; or by heating oxalic acid with glycerol at 110 °C.
  • Benzoic acid: industrially from toluene by air oxidation (Co catalyst), or by hydrolysis of benzotrichloride C6H5CCl3 with water.
  • Cannizzaro: 2 ArCHO + OH− → ArCOO− + ArCH2OH (and HCHO as hydride donor in crossed Cannizzaro).
  • Benzilic acid rearrangement: PhCOCOPh + KOH → Ph2C(OH)COO− K+ (1,2-aryl shift in the tetrahedral intermediate). It is a route to α-hydroxy acids from 1,2-diketones.
  • Hydrolysis of esters, amides, anhydrides, acyl chlorides (see the mechanism chapters below): the standard laboratory ending to many syntheses.
Example 7 Main

Multi-step carbon counting

Convert (a) ethanol to propanoic acid, (b) bromobenzene to phenylacetic acid, (c) propene to butanoic acid.

Show solution
  1. (a) C2H5OH → C2H5Br (PBr3) → C2H5CN (KCN) → C2H5COOH (H3O+, Δ). Alternatively C2H5Br → Mg → CO2.
  2. (b) C6H5Br → C6H5MgBr → C6H5COOH (CO2) is one carbon; one more needed: Arndt–Eistert (SOCl2; CH2N2; Ag2O/H2O) → C6H5CH2COOH. A shorter route: toluene → benzyl bromide (NBS) → C6H5CH2CN → hydrolysis.
  3. (c) CH3CH=CH2 + HBr (peroxide, anti-Markovnikov) → CH3CH2CH2Br → CH3CH2CH2MgBr → CO2, then H3O+ → CH3CH2CH2COOH. The peroxide effect is essential: ordinary HBr would give the 2° bromide, whose Grignard would give 2-methylpropanoic acid (CH3)2CHCOOH instead.
Example 8 Adv

Products of oxidation

Predict the organic product(s) of hot, acidic KMnO4 on (i) 1,2-dimethylbenzene, (ii) PhCH2CH2CH3, (iii) PhC(CH3)3, (iv) cyclohexene, (v) CH3CH=CHCH2CH3.

Show solution
  1. (i) Phthalic acid (both methyls oxidised).
  2. (ii) Benzoic acid (the whole n-propyl chain is cleaved beyond the benzylic carbon; fragments are lost as CO2/acetic acid-level pieces).
  3. (iii) No reaction: no benzylic hydrogen.
  4. (iv) Adipic acid, HOOC(CH2)4COOH.
  5. (v) Ethanoic acid + propanoic acid (cleavage at the double bond, each carbon becomes COOH).
Example 9 Adv

Haloform scope

Which of the following give an acid on treatment with I2/NaOH, followed by acidification? (A) CH3COCH2CH3 (B) C6H5COCH3 (C) CH3CH(OH)CH2CH3 (D) C6H5COCH2CH3 (E) CH3CH2OH (F) HCHO

Show solution
  1. The iodoform test needs CH3–C(=O)– or CH3–CH(OH)– (which is oxidised first to the methyl ketone), or ethanal/ethanol.
  2. (A) gives propanoic acid; (B) gives benzoic acid; (C) gives propanoic acid (after oxidation to butan-2-one, the ketone is cleaved); (E) gives formic acid (via CH3CHO → CHI3 + HCOO−).
  3. (D) has no methyl ketone unit (C6H5CO–CH2CH3): no. (F): no.
  4. Answer: A, B, C, E.
