1. Oxidation numbers and counting electrons

Oxidation of an organic molecule means loss of electron density from carbon, that is, a rise in the oxidation number of one or more carbon atoms. The working rule is: oxidation number of a carbon = (number of bonds to O, N or halogen, a double bond counting twice) − (number of bonds to H). Bonds to other carbon atoms count zero. Along the one-carbon ladder the values are CH4 −4, CH3OH −2, HCHO 0, HCOOH +2 and CO2 +4. In ethanol the CH3 carbon is −3 and the CH2OH carbon is −1; in ethanal the carbonyl carbon is +1; in ethanoic acid the carboxyl carbon is +3. The methyl carbon is −3 throughout, so only the functional carbon needs to be followed.

The number of electrons removed is the change in oxidation number of the carbons that change. Ethanol → ethanal removes 2 e− (−1 → +1), ethanal → ethanoic acid removes 2 e− more (+1 → +3), so ethanol → ethanoic acid is a 4 e− oxidation: CH3CH2OH + H2O → CH3COOH + 4H+ + 4e−. A fast shortcut is that removal of H2 (alcohol → carbonyl) or replacement of one C–H by C–O is a 2 e− step.

Reactant:

Reagent: K2Cr2O7, dil. H2SO4, heat under reflux (excess oxidant)

Product:

Four electrons are lost per molecule: carbon changes from −1 to +3.

The n-factor of an oxidant is the number of electrons it accepts per formula unit. Cr(VI) → Cr(III) accepts 3 e− per Cr, hence 6 per Cr2O72−. MnO4− accepts 5 e− in acid (→ Mn2+), 3 e− in neutral or weakly alkaline solution (→ MnO2) and 1 e− in strongly alkaline solution (→ MnO42−). Hydrogen peroxide, peracids, HIO4 and Pb(OAc)4 accept 2 e− each. Equating electrons lost to electrons gained gives every stoichiometric ratio used later in this chapter.

  1. Balance for ethanol and dichromate: 4 e− are lost per ethanol and 6 e− are gained per Cr2O72−.
  2. Least common multiple of 4 and 6 is 12, so 3 ethanol react with 2 Cr2O72−.
  3. Complete the balance with H+ and H2O: 3CH3CH2OH + 2Cr2O72− + 16H+ → 3CH3COOH + 4Cr3+ + 11H2O (atoms and charge balance: charge +12 on both sides).
Formula. Carbon oxidation number = (bonds to O/N/X) − (bonds to H). Electrons transferred per molecule = Σ change in oxidation number of the carbons that change.
Trap. Do not average the oxidation numbers over the whole molecule when counting electrons for a reaction that touches only one carbon. In the oxidation of toluene to benzoic acid only the CH3 carbon changes (−3 → +3, six electrons); the ring carbons are unchanged.
