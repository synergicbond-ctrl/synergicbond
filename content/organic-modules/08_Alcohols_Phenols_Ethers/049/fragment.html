9. Diols and glycols

Dihydric alcohols are named diols (IUPAC) or glycols (common). The two OH groups may be on adjacent carbons (1,2-diol, vicinal), 1,3 (as in propane-1,3-diol), or further apart; two OH on the same carbon is a geminal diol (unstable, equivalent to a carbonyl hydrate). Ethylene glycol (ethane-1,2-diol), HOCH2CH2OH.

  • Preparation: (i) hydration of ethylene oxide (aq. acid at 100 °C, or water at 200 °C, 15 atm) — industrial; (ii) cold dilute alkaline KMnO4 (Baeyer’s reagent) with ethene (syn dihydroxylation; the purple colour is discharged and brown MnO2 forms: Baeyer’s test for unsaturation); (iii) ClCH2CH2Cl + aq. Na2CO3; (iv) OsO4 then H2S or NaHSO3.
  • Properties: colourless, sweet, viscous, bp 197 °C (two H-bonding OH groups), miscible with water; poisonous (oxidised to oxalic acid in the body, which damages the kidneys).
  • Uses: antifreeze (a 1:1 volume mixture with water freezes at about −35 °C), terylene/PET (condensation with terephthalic acid), plasticiser, brake fluid.
