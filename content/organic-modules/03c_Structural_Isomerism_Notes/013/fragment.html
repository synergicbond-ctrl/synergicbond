14. Properties of isomers: boiling point and acidity

Boiling point and branching. For isomers of one class, the straight-chain compound boils highest and the more branched compound boils lower, because branching reduces the surface area available for London dispersion forces. The pentanes show it clearly: pentane 36 °C, 2-methylbutane 28 °C, 2,2-dimethylpropane 10 °C. For butanols: butan-1-ol 118 °C, butan-2-ol 100 °C, 2-methylpropan-2-ol 83 °C.

Butan-1-ol (118 °C), butan-2-ol (100 °C), 2-methylpropan-2-ol (83 °C).

Functional class. Hydrogen bonding sets the order between classes: roughly, carboxylic acids (dimers) > alcohols > aldehydes, ketones and esters (dipole-dipole, no O-H) > ethers > alkanes, for compounds of comparable molar mass. Ethanoic acid (118 °C) is far above methyl methanoate (32 °C), and ethanol (78 °C) far above dimethyl ether (-24 °C). Symmetry affects melting point more than boiling point: p-dichlorobenzene packs well and melts at 53 °C, much higher than the ortho isomer, which melts below 0 °C.

Acidity. The acidic functional isomer is easy to identify: of the C7H8O isomers o-cresol (pKa about 10) is a weak acid dissolving in NaOH and giving a violet colour with FeCl3; benzyl alcohol (pKa about 15) is a neutral alcohol; anisole is not acidic at all. Of ethanoic acid (pKa 4.76), methyl methanoate and hydroxyethanal, only ethanoic acid is acidic enough to liberate CO2 from carbonates. Carboxylate resonance explains why acids beat phenols, and phenoxide delocalisation explains why phenols beat alcohols.

Intramolecular hydrogen bonding. o-Nitrophenol (intramolecular H-bond) is steam-volatile and has a lower boiling point than p-nitrophenol, which is associated by intermolecular bonds.

o-Nitrophenol (chelated) and p-nitrophenol.

Key idea. Same class: less branching means a higher boiling point. Different class: stronger intermolecular forces (hydrogen bonds) mean a higher boiling point. Acidity order: carboxylic acid > phenol > alcohol.
Trap. Branching raises the melting point when it makes the molecule more symmetrical (neopentane melts higher than pentane), even though it lowers the boiling point.
