30. Preparation

30.1 Aliphatic nitro compounds
  • Alkyl halide + AgNO2 (ethanol): R–X + AgNO2 → R–NO2 + AgX. The nitrite ion is ambident, able to react through N or O. With the covalent AgNO2 the lone pair on N attacks (the O's are tied up with Ag): nitroalkane as the main product. With ionic NaNO2/KNO2 in the usual textbook conditions the more electronegative O reacts more readily and the alkyl nitrite R–ONO is the major product, with some nitro compound. (In dipolar aprotic solvents, DMF or DMSO, nitro compounds can predominate – the Kornblum modification – but JEE uses the AgNO2/KNO2 contrast.)
AgNO₂⟶
Silver nitrite gives the nitroalkane (N-attack)
NaNO₂ or KNO₂⟶
Ionic nitrite gives mainly the alkyl nitrite (O-attack)
  • Vapour-phase nitration of alkanes (HNO3, 673–773 K): free-radical process that also cleaves C–C bonds, giving mixtures (CH3NO2, C2H5NO2, 1-NP and 2-NP from propane). Industrially the route to nitromethane/nitroethane/nitropropanes.
  • Oxidation of 1° amines, oximes or hydroxylamines with peroxy-acids (CF3CO3H).
  • Victor Meyer route (halide → AgNO2); nitrite ion with α-halo acids: ClCH2COONa + NaNO2 → NO2CH2COONa → heat → CH3NO2 (decarboxylation).
30.2 Aromatic nitro compounds

Nitration: HNO3 + H2SO4 (nitrating mixture) at 323–333 K. Mechanism: H2SO4 protonates HNO3 to H2NO3+, which loses water to give the nitronium ion NO2+ (linear, O=N+=O, detected by Raman); this electrophile attacks benzene to give the σ-complex (arenium ion), which loses H+.

conc. HNO₃ + conc. H₂SO₄, 323–333 K⟶ fuming HNO₃, H₂SO₄, 373 K⟶
Benzene → nitrobenzene → m-dinitrobenzene (nitro is meta-directing and deactivating)

Facts: (i) the nitro group deactivates the ring, so the second nitration needs fuming acid and heat, and the third is very difficult (1,3,5-trinitrobenzene is made indirectly); (ii) nitrobenzene cannot be used as a substrate for Friedel–Crafts, and is therefore a suitable solvent for Friedel–Crafts reactions of other arenes; (iii) toluene is nitrated much faster than benzene (CH3 activates), giving o- (58 %) and p- (38 %) nitrotoluenes and a little meta; further nitration gives TNT (2,4,6-trinitrotoluene).

HNO₃/H₂SO₄, stepwise, 3 stages⟶
Toluene → TNT: each nitration is harder than the one before, so conditions are intensified

Picric acid (2,4,6-trinitrophenol): phenol is first sulphonated (H2SO4) and then nitrated with conc. HNO3 (direct nitration of phenol would oxidise it); the sulphonic acid groups are displaced. pKa ≈ 0.4: a strong organic acid (three nitro groups, two of them o/p to OH).
