How to use this chapter

This chapter is written as a course, not as a list of facts. Every reaction is introduced with its mechanism, the reason for its regiochemistry and stereochemistry, the exceptions that examiners like, and then worked examples. At the end there is a graded problem set of more than four hundred questions at three levels, all with full solutions.

  • Part I – Alcohols (sections 1–11): nomenclature, hydrogen bonding, every preparation, acidity, substitution with HX/Lucas, dehydration and carbocation rearrangement, oxidation reagents, glycols, the pinacol rearrangement, glycerol.
  • Part II – Phenols (sections 12–19): preparation, acidity and substituent effects, electrophilic substitution, Reimer–Tiemann, Kolbe–Schmitt, Fries, Claisen, tests and important phenols.
  • Part III – Ethers, epoxides, crown ethers and thiols (sections 20–27): Williamson synthesis and its limits, cleavage by HX, epoxide opening under acidic and basic conditions with stereochemistry, crown ethers, thiols.
  • Part IV – Problems (sections 28–31): Level 1 (foundation, single-concept), Level 2 (JEE Main / early Advanced, multi-step), Level 3 (JEE Advanced: multiple correct, integer type, matrix match, paragraph).
Shortcut Study order that works: read a section, redraw every mechanism on paper without looking, then do the worked examples of that section before opening the solution. Only after finishing a Part, attempt the matching problems of Part IV. Each problem is tagged with its level and topic.
Key idea Three questions solve almost every problem in this chapter: (1) what is the nucleophile and what is the leaving group (is it made good by protonation?); (2) is a carbocation involved (rearrangement, SN1, E1) or is the step concerted (SN2, E2, [3,3])? (3) what does the stereochemistry demand (inversion, retention, anti, syn)?
