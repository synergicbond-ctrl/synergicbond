39. Hinsberg test and carbylamine (isocyanide) reaction

Hinsberg: amine + benzenesulfonyl chloride (PhSO2Cl) in aqueous NaOH. 1° amine: the sulfonamide PhSO2NHR still has an acidic N–H (pKa ≈ 10–11), so it dissolves in NaOH as a sodium salt (soluble), and acidification re-precipitates it. 2° amine: the sulfonamide PhSO2NR2 has no N–H and is insoluble in NaOH. 3° amine: no stable sulfonamide forms; the amine remains (insoluble, oily layer; goes into solution only on acidification).

+ aq. NaOH⟶
1° amine: N-methylbenzenesulfonamide, soluble in alkali as its sodium salt
+ aq. NaOH⟶
2° amine: insoluble product

Carbylamine reaction (Hofmann isocyanide test): a primary amine (aliphatic or aromatic) + CHCl3 + alcoholic KOH, warm → isocyanide R–N+≡C− (foul smell). Mechanism: dichlorocarbene :CCl2 (from CHCl3 + OH−) adds to RNH2, then two HCl are eliminated. Secondary and tertiary amines do not give it.

+ alc. KOH, Δ⟶
Ethylamine → ethyl isocyanide
Shortcut The tests discriminate: carbylamine = "is there an –NH2 on carbon?" (smell); Hinsberg = "1°, 2° or 3°?" (solubility); nitrous acid = aliphatic 1° gives N2 gas + alcohol, aromatic 1° gives diazonium, 2° gives yellow oily N-nitrosamine, 3° aliphatic gives just a salt, 3° aromatic gives C-nitroso green solid (Liebermann).
