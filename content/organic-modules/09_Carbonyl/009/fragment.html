6. Family 1: oxidation methods

6.1 Oxidation of primary alcohols to aldehydes A primary alcohol is easily over-oxidised to the carboxylic acid because the aldehyde in water forms a hydrate R–CH(OH)2, which is oxidised further like an alcohol. The cure is to use an anhydrous oxidant that stops at the aldehyde.

  • PCC (pyridinium chlorochromate, C5H5NH+CrO3Cl−) in anhydrous CH2Cl2: stops at aldehyde; also oxidises 2° alcohols to ketones. Without water there is no hydrate.
  • PDC (pyridinium dichromate): similar; in DMF it can go to the acid.
  • Collins reagent: CrO3·2 pyridine in CH2Cl2.
  • Swern oxidation: DMSO, (COCl)2, −78 °C, then Et3N. Metal-free; 1° → aldehyde, 2° → ketone.
  • Dess–Martin periodinane (DMP): hypervalent iodine reagent; mild, neutral.
  • Cu, 573 K (catalytic dehydrogenation): 1° alcohol → aldehyde + H2; 2° alcohol → ketone + H2; 3° alcohol → alkene (dehydration, no C–H on the carbinol carbon to lose).
  • Oppenauer oxidation: Al(OtBu)3 with acetone as hydride acceptor (the reverse of MPV); selective for 2° alcohols to ketones; leaves C=C untouched.
  • MnO2: selectively oxidises allylic and benzylic alcohols to aldehydes or ketones; saturated alcohols are untouched.

6.2 Oxidation of secondary alcohols to ketones
Any of the above works; K2Cr2O7/H2SO4 or Jones reagent (no over-oxidation problem for ketones, which resist further oxidation under normal conditions) is the cheapest.

Cyclohexanol → cyclohexanone

Vigorous oxidation (hot KMnO4, conc. HNO3) breaks a C–C bond next to the C=O of a ketone and gives a mixture of carboxylic acids. For an unsymmetrical ketone, Popoff's rule says that the C=O group stays with the smaller alkyl group, i.e. the bond broken is on the side of the larger alkyl (butan-2-one gives mainly ethanoic acid, with some propanoic acid and methanoic acid). Ketones therefore resist mild oxidants but not forcing ones. 6.3 Ozonolysis of alkenes Ozone adds to C=C to give a molozonide, which rearranges to the ozonide; reductive work-up (Zn/H2O or Me2S) gives aldehydes or ketones, oxidative work-up (H2O2) turns the aldehydes into acids. The sum of fragments is a map of the alkene: each C=C is replaced by two C=O.

Shortcut To work back from ozonolysis products to the alkene: connect the two carbonyl carbons with a double bond (remove the O's). For a cyclic alkene, one product molecule contains two C=O groups.
6.4 Wacker oxidation
Ethene is oxidised to ethanal by air in aqueous PdCl2/CuCl2: CH2=CH2 + ½O2 → CH3CHO. Terminal alkenes give methyl ketones (Markovnikov): propene → propanone. Mechanism: Pd2+-alkene π-complex, water attacks the more substituted carbon, β-hydride elimination gives the enol, then the carbonyl; Pd0 is reoxidised by Cu2+, which is regenerated by O2.

6.5 Hydroformylation (oxo process) Alkene + CO + H2 under Co or Rh catalysis gives an aldehyde with one more carbon: RCH=CH2 → RCH2CH2CHO (and some branched isomer). It is an industrial route to butanal from propene.

CO, H₂, Co₂(CO)₈⟶ +
Propene → butanal (major) + 2-methylpropanal

6.6 Oxidative cleavage of 1,2-diols Periodic acid (HIO4) / NaIO4 (Malaprade) and lead tetraacetate Pb(OAc)4 (Criegee) cleave the C–C bond of a vicinal diol to give two carbonyl compounds. A diol with three or more adjacent OH groups also loses the central carbon as formic acid. 1,2-Diols are made from alkenes by cold dilute KMnO4/OsO4 (syn) or by epoxide opening (anti). The total process is equivalent to ozonolysis, but sometimes more convenient.

NaIO₄⟶ +
Butane-2,3-diol → two ethanal
Pb(OAc)₄⟶
cis- or trans-Cyclohexane-1,2-diol → hexanedial (the cis diol reacts faster because the cyclic ester forms easily)

6.7 Dehydrogenation / oxidation of an alkane side chain The benzylic CH3 of toluene can be taken to the aldehyde level selectively:

  • Etard reaction: toluene + CrO2Cl2 (chromyl chloride) in CS2 or CCl4 gives an addition complex (brown), hydrolysed by water to benzaldehyde.
  • Toluene + CrO3/Ac2O gives benzylidene diacetate PhCH(OCOCH3)2, which is hydrolysed to benzaldehyde (the acetic anhydride protects the aldehyde from over-oxidation).
  • Toluene + Cl2/hν (excess, at the boiling point) gives benzal chloride PhCHCl2, which is hydrolysed by water (or aqueous alkali) to benzaldehyde.
