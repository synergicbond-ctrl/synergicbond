26. Meerwein–Ponndorf–Verley reduction and Oppenauer oxidation

MPV: aldehyde or ketone + excess isopropanol, Al(O-i-Pr)3 (catalyst), heat, acetone distilled out → secondary/primary alcohol. Oppenauer: the reverse: secondary alcohol + excess acetone (or cyclohexanone), Al(O-t-Bu)3 → ketone. Both are the same reversible hydride transfer through a six-membered cyclic transition state (Al coordinates both oxygens, the H moves from the carbinol C–H of the donor alcohol to the carbonyl carbon). Direction is set by Le Chatelier: remove acetone to push MPV; use excess acetone to push Oppenauer.

+ Al(O-i-Pr)₃ (MPV)⟶ +
MPV: cyclohexanone → cyclohexanol, isopropanol → acetone
Key idea Chemoselectivity is why these reactions are used: MPV reduces only C=O (leaves C=C, NO2, C–X, esters untouched), e.g. crotonaldehyde → crotyl alcohol, whereas H2/Ni would saturate C=C and NaBH4 might attack other groups. Oppenauer oxidises secondary alcohols without touching C=C or over-oxidising (no acids), e.g. cholesterol → cholest-4-en-3-one with the double bond migrating into conjugation.
Example 35 Main

MPV selectivity

Crotonaldehyde, CH3CH=CHCHO, is reduced by (a) Al(O-i-Pr)3/i-PrOH, (b) H2/Ni, (c) NaBH4 followed by work-up (in methanol). Give the main product in each.

Show solution
  1. (a) Crotyl alcohol, CH3CH=CHCH2OH (only C=O reduced). (b) Butan-1-ol (both C=O and C=C reduced). (c) NaBH4 gives mainly crotyl alcohol (1,2-reduction) with some butan-1-ol from conjugate reduction, so it is less clean than MPV.
