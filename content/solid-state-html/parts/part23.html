◆Concept

This part closes with magnetic susceptibility, permanent and induced moments, Curie behaviour, cooperative ferro/antiferromagnetic ordering and magnetic properties of superconductors. Ferrimagnetism is included as the JEE-standard companion classification.

1. Magnetization and Susceptibility

∑Formula

M = χH.

∑Formula

Molar susceptibility: χ_m = χV_m.
BehaviourSign / microscopic origin
Diamagneticχ<0; all electrons paired in the simple picture; induced response opposes applied field.
Paramagneticχ>0; one or more unpaired electrons provide permanent magnetic moments that partially align in a field.

2. Spin Magnetic Moment

◆Concept

For total spin S, the spin magnetic moment magnitude is proportional to √[S(S+1)]. For many JEE problems using n unpaired electrons, the spin-only effective moment is:

∑Formula

μ_eff = √[n(n+2)] μ_B.

◆Concept

The source gives the more general spin form m = g_e√[S(S+1)] μ_B.

3. Curie Law

∑Formula

χ_m = C/T.

◆Concept

For an ideal paramagnet, increasing temperature randomizes spin orientations, so susceptibility decreases approximately as 1/T.

4. Cooperative Magnetic Ordering

TypeSpin arrangementNet magnetization
FerromagneticParallel alignment within domainsLarge non-zero spontaneous magnetization below Curie temperature
AntiferromagneticEqual antiparallel sublatticesMoments cancel ideally; ordering below Néel temperature
FerrimagneticAntiparallel but unequal sublattice momentsNon-zero net magnetization

5. Curie and Néel Temperatures

  • Ferromagnetic order is lost above the Curie temperature T_C.
  • Antiferromagnetic order is lost above the Néel temperature T_N.
  • Above the ordering temperature, many such materials revert to paramagnetic-like behaviour.

6. Temperature-Independent Contributions

◆Concept

All materials possess an induced diamagnetic contribution. The source also notes temperature-independent paramagnetism in special cases where low-lying excited electronic states contribute orbital response. This is enrichment beyond standard JEE calculations.

7. Magnetic Behaviour of Superconductors

◆Concept

Type-I superconductors expel magnetic flux below the critical field: the Meissner effect. The source gives an empirical critical-field relation:

∑Formula

H_c(T) = H_c(0)[1 − (T/T_c)²].

◆Concept

Type-II superconductors allow partial flux penetration in a mixed state between two critical fields; high-T_c cuprates are Type II.

8. Measurement Context

◆Concept

The source mentions the Gouy balance and SQUID magnetometry. For JEE, the essential takeaway is the sign/magnitude of susceptibility and the spin arrangement; instrumental details are usually qualitative.

⚠JEE Advanced traps & edge cases

  • Diamagnetic does not mean “no magnetic response”; it means the induced susceptibility is negative.
  • Paramagnetic materials need not retain magnetization after removing the field.
  • Antiferromagnetic and ferrimagnetic both involve antiparallel sublattices, but only ferrimagnetic order has unequal opposing moments and a net magnetization.
  • Meissner effect is magnetic-flux expulsion, not merely zero resistance.

▶Solved examples

Worked example 1

Example 1 — Magnetic moment

An ion has 3 unpaired electrons. Find spin-only magnetic moment.

  1. Use μ_eff=√[n(n+2)] μ_B.
  2. For n=3: √(3×5)=√15.

μ_eff = √15 ≈ 3.87 μ_B.

Worked example 2

Example 2 — Classification

A solid has two antiparallel magnetic sublattices of unequal moment. What magnetic order is expected?

  1. Antiparallel alignment rules out ferromagnetic.
  2. Unequal opposing moments do not cancel completely.

Ferrimagnetic order.
