17. Estimation of nitrogen: the Kjeldahl method

The weighed compound is heated (digested) with concentrated H2SO4, with some K2SO4 (raises the boiling point) and a little CuSO4 or Hg (catalyst). Nitrogen is converted to ammonium sulphate. The solution is cooled, diluted and heated with excess NaOH; NH3 is distilled into a known volume of standard acid (H2SO4 or HCl) of measured excess. The unreacted acid is back-titrated with standard NaOH.

Formula Organic N + H2SO4 → (NH4)2SO4 (NH4)2SO4 + 2NaOH → Na2SO4 + 2NH3↑ + 2H2O 2NH3 + H2SO4 → (NH4)2SO4
Formula meq of acid used by NH3 = (meq of acid taken) − (meq of NaOH in back titration). %N = 14 × (meq of NH3)/(10 × w) = 1.4 × N × V / w (N = normality of acid, V = mL of acid used, w = g of sample)

Limitations. Kjeldahl does not convert nitrogen of nitro (–NO2), azo (–N=N–), azoxy groups and nitrogen present in rings (pyridine, quinoline) to ammonium sulphate quantitatively; the nitrogen is partly lost as N2 or NOx. The method is, however, simple, accurate and cheap for amines, amides and proteins, and is the standard method for food analysis.

HYF Protein content of foods = %N × 6.25 (the factor assumes ~16 % N in protein; 6.38 for milk). If N is expressed as normality, remember that 1 M H2SO4 = 2 N.
Example 29 Main

Kjeldahl with back titration

0.40 g of an organic compound was digested and the ammonia evolved was absorbed in 50.0 mL of 0.20 M H2SO4. The excess acid required 20.0 mL of 0.50 M NaOH for neutralisation. Find %N.

Show solution
  1. meq of acid taken = 50.0 × (0.20 × 2) = 20.0.
  2. meq of NaOH used = 20.0 × 0.50 = 10.0 (monoacidic base), which neutralised the excess acid.
  3. meq of acid used by NH3 = 20.0 − 10.0 = 10.0 ⇒ NH3 = 10.0 mmol, N = 10.0 × 14 = 140 mg.
  4. %N = 0.140/0.40 × 100 = 35.0 %. (Using the formula: 1.4 × 10.0/0.40 = 35.0.)
Example 30 Adv

Protein in food

A 1.00 g sample of dried meat powder was analysed by Kjeldahl. The ammonia distilled neutralised 25.0 mL of 0.100 M H2SO4 fully (no excess titration needed to change). Estimate the percentage of protein (factor 6.25).

Show solution
  1. meq = 25.0 × 0.200 = 5.00 (0.100 M H2SO4 is 0.200 N); mass of N = 5.00 × 14 = 70 mg = 0.070 g.
  2. %N = 7.0 %. Protein = 7.0 × 6.25 = 43.75 %.
