48. Mechanism: imine formation, reductive amination and the pH profile

  • Step 1 – addition. The amine lone pair attacks C=O (HOMO nN → LUMO π*C=O) giving a zwitterionic tetrahedral intermediate; a proton shift (O− ← N–H+) gives the neutral carbinolamine R'CH(OH)–NHR.
  • Step 2 – acid-catalysed dehydration. The OH is protonated (making H2O a leaving group) and the N lone pair pushes out water (iminium ion, C=N+).
  • Step 3 – deprotonation. Loss of H+ from N gives the imine R'CH=NR.
  • The pH bell curve: the rate is maximal near pH 4–5. At lower pH the amine is protonated (no lone pair): step 1 is too slow. At higher pH there is too little acid to protonate the OH in step 2 (the rate-determining step above pH ≈ 5).
  • Reductive amination. At pH 6–7 the iminium ion (a much better electrophile than the C=O group because its π* is lower in energy) is attacked by H− from NaBH3CN (the CN group makes the hydride a weaker, more selective donor), giving the amine.
