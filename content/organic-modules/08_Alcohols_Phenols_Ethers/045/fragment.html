8.1 Chromium(VI) reagents and their mechanism

In acid, Cr(VI) (CrO3, H2CrO4, Cr2O72−) first forms a chromate ester with the alcohol: ROH + H2CrO4 → RO–CrO3H + H2O. A base (water) then removes the α-hydrogen in an E2-like step while the O–Cr bond breaks; the carbonyl C=O forms and chromium is reduced from Cr(VI) (orange) to Cr(IV), which disproportionates or is reduced further to Cr(III) (green). The colour change from orange to green is the basis of the breathalyser test and of a laboratory test for 1° and 2° alcohols (tertiary alcohols give no change).

Why PCC stops at the aldehyde. In the Jones reagent water is present, so the aldehyde forms the hydrate RCH(OH)2, a new alcohol that is oxidised by the same mechanism. In anhydrous CH2Cl2 there is no hydrate, and aldehydes are oxidised far more slowly by Cr(VI) than hydrates are.
