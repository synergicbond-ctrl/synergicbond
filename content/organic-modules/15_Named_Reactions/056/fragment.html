50. Pinacol–pinacolone rearrangement

Reagents: a vicinal (1,2-)diol + acid (H2SO4, H3PO4, Al2O3, heat). Product: a ketone (or aldehyde), usually with a quaternary carbon. Mechanism: (1) protonation of one OH, (2) loss of water gives the more stable carbocation (tertiary > secondary; benzylic/doubly benzylic best), (3) 1,2-shift of a group from the neighbouring carbinol carbon to the cationic carbon gives an oxocarbenium (a cation stabilised by the lone pair on O: this is the driving force), (4) loss of H+ from O gives the carbonyl.

Pinacol (2,3-dimethylbutane-2,3-diol) → pinacolone (3,3-dimethylbutan-2-one): one CH3 migrates
H⁺, Δ⟶
Benzopinacol → 2,2,2-triphenylacetophenone (phenyl migrates)
H⁺⟶
Unsymmetrical diol: the OH leaves from the carbon bearing two phenyls (diphenylcarbocation); CH3 (not Ph) migrates from the other carbon → 3,3-diphenylbutan-2-one
H⁺⟶
Cyclic diol: ring expansion (cyclopentyl → cyclohexanone) gives the spiro ketone spiro[4.5]decan-6-one
Key idea Decide in two questions. (1) Which OH leaves? The one that gives the more stable carbocation. (2) Which group migrates? A group on the *other* carbon, in the order aryl ≈ H > tertiary alkyl > secondary alkyl > primary alkyl > methyl (a ring bond migrating means ring expansion or contraction). The C=O forms on the carbon that kept its OH, i.e. the carbon the group migrated away from.
Trap In 2-methylpropane-1,2-diol, (CH3)2C(OH)–CH2OH, the tertiary OH leaves (tertiary cation), and H migrates from CH2OH, giving (CH3)2CH–CHO, 2-methylpropanal (an aldehyde), not a ketone. In cyclic cis/trans diols the group anti to the leaving OH is the one that migrates, so stereochemistry matters (trans-diol on a ring may ring-contract while cis may not).
Example 46 Main

Predict the pinacolone-type product

Give the product of acid-catalysed rearrangement of (a) 2,3-dimethylbutane-2,3-diol, (b) 1,1-diphenylethane-1,2-diol, Ph2C(OH)CH2OH, (c) 1-methylcyclohexane-1,2-diol.

Show solution
  1. (a) Pinacolone, CH3COC(CH3)3.
  2. (b) The OH on the diphenyl carbon leaves (diphenylmethyl-type cation). The neighbouring carbon, CH2OH, carries only H as a migrating group, so H shifts and the CH2OH carbon becomes CHO: diphenylacetaldehyde, Ph2CH–CHO.
  3. (c) The tertiary OH leaves. H migrates from C-2 to C-1, and C-2 becomes C=O: 2-methylcyclohexanone. (If the ring C–C bond anti to the leaving group migrates instead, ring contraction gives 1-methylcyclopentanecarbaldehyde; the H-shift product is the usual answer.)
Example 47 Adv

Aryl migration

Hydrobenzoin, PhCH(OH)–CH(OH)Ph, is heated with dilute H2SO4. Give the product and show which group moves.

Show solution
  1. One benzylic OH is protonated and lost, giving a secondary benzylic cation at C-1. The group that migrates to C-1 from C-2 is phenyl (aryl shows a higher migratory aptitude than H here, through a bridged phenonium ion). C-2 then bears H and the OH and becomes the aldehyde carbon: diphenylacetaldehyde, Ph2CH–CHO (C14H14O2 → C14H12O + H2O).
