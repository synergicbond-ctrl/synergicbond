13.1 The cumene (Hock) process — the industrial route

Benzene + propene (H3PO4 on silica, about 523 K, or AlCl3/HCl) → cumene (isopropylbenzene). Cumene is oxidised with air at about 373–393 K by a radical chain to cumene hydroperoxide. This is selective because the benzylic tertiary C–H bond is the weakest C–H bond in the molecule. Treatment with dilute H2SO4 (323–333 K) splits the hydroperoxide into phenol and acetone.

Mechanism of the Hock rearrangement (analogous to the pinacol and Baeyer–Villiger rearrangements, with migration of a group to an electron-deficient oxygen):

  • The terminal OH of the hydroperoxide is protonated.
  • Water leaves while the phenyl group migrates from carbon to oxygen (1,2-shift to electron-poor O). The product is a resonance-stabilised oxocarbenium ion, PhO+=C(CH3)2.
  • Water adds to the carbocation, giving a hemiketal, PhO–C(CH3)2–OH.
  • The hemiketal collapses (like any acetal/hemiketal in acid) to acetone and phenol.

Key idea The cumene process is the answer to “both phenol and acetone are formed from one starting material”. It makes about 95 % of the world’s phenol. Cumene hydroperoxide is explosive if concentrated: it is the reason the process is run in dilute acid.
Example 36 L1 Foundation

Cumene process arithmetic

How much phenol and acetone are produced from 240 kg of cumene if the overall yield is 80 %?

Show solution
  1. C6H5CH(CH3)2 (120 g mol−1) → C6H5OH (94) + CH3COCH3 (58). The reaction is 1:1:1.
  2. 240 kg cumene = 2000 mol. At 80 % yield: 1600 mol of each product.
  3. Phenol: 1600 × 94 g = 150.4 kg. Acetone: 1600 × 58 g = 92.8 kg.
