58. Mechanism: amine addition to an epoxide and to an α,β-unsaturated nitrile

  • Epoxide. nN attacks the less hindered ring carbon from the back (SN2; σ*C–O is the LUMO; ring strain ≈ 110 kJ mol-1 is released). The alkoxide takes a proton: β-amino alcohol. The product amine is still nucleophilic, so excess epoxide gives di- and tri-substituted products (ethanolamines).
  • Acrylonitrile (cyanoethylation). nN adds to the β-carbon of CH2=CH–C≡N (Michael-type: the LUMO is the π* of the conjugated C=C–C≡N with the largest coefficient on the β carbon) to give a stabilised carbanion at C–α, which is protonated: R2N–CH2CH2CN.
