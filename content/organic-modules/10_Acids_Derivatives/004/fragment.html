4. Structure of the carboxyl group and of the carboxylate ion

In the carboxyl group the carbonyl carbon is sp2, the hydroxyl oxygen is also effectively sp2 because its lone pair is in conjugation with the C=O π bond. Resonance in the neutral acid places a small positive charge on the –OH oxygen, which makes the O–H bond more polar (more acidic) than in an alcohol. Loss of H+ gives the carboxylate ion, where two equivalent resonance forms put the negative charge equally on both oxygens; both C–O bonds are identical (≈ 127 pm, intermediate between C=O ≈ 122 pm and C–O ≈ 134 pm). The carboxylate anion is stabilised by delocalisation over two electronegative atoms, which is the principal reason why acids are about 1010–1011 times stronger than alcohols.
