26. Mechanism clinic: how to attack a "predict the product" question

A reliable five-question routine:

  • Q1. What is the electrophilic carbon? sp3 (methyl/1°/2°/3°/allylic/benzylic) or sp2 (vinyl/aryl)?
  • Q2. What is the reagent: strong base, good Nu/weak base, or weak/neutral?
  • Q3. What is the solvent? protic or aprotic; "alcoholic" or "aqueous"; temperature (heat?).
  • Q4. Does the structure allow a rearrangement (a carbocation next to a quaternary carbon or a tertiary C–H)?
  • Q5. What stereochemistry is required? Inversion (SN2), racemisation (SN1), anti elimination (E2), retention (SNi, NGP).

Practice the routine on the three cases below before the problem set.

Example 41 Adv

Mechanism clinic 1

Predict the products (and mechanism) when (S)-2-bromobutane is treated separately with (i) NaOH in 90 % acetone/10 % water, (ii) NaI in acetone, (iii) hot concentrated ethanolic KOH, (iv) boiling water.

Show solution
  1. (i) Strong Nu, moderate solvent polarity, 2° substrate: mainly SN2, with E2 competing: (R)-butan-2-ol (inversion) plus some butenes.
  2. (ii) SN2 with iodide (good Nu, weak base): (R)-2-iodobutane; subsequent halide exchange gradually racemises it (each exchange inverts).
  3. (iii) E2: but-2-ene (trans > cis) major, but-1-ene minor.
  4. (iv) weak Nu, protic: SN1 (secondary, slow) giving racemic butan-2-ol (partial inversion) and traces of alkenes.
Example 42 Adv

Mechanism clinic 2

Predict the product of 3-bromo-2,2-dimethylbutane in (i) boiling ethanol, (ii) NaOEt/EtOH, heat.

Show solution
  1. The substrate is (CH3)3C–CHBr–CH3: secondary with a quaternary neighbour.
  2. (i) Boiling ethanol: ionisation, 1,2-methyl shift gives the tertiary cation (CH3)2C+–CH(CH3)2; capture by ethanol: 2-ethoxy-2,3-dimethylbutane (and 2,3-dimethylbut-2-ene, 2,3-dimethylbut-1-ene).
  3. (ii) With ethoxide, E2 prevails: the only β-hydrogens are on the CH3 (C4); the quaternary carbon has none. Product: 3,3-dimethylbut-1-ene (no rearrangement).
Example 43 Adv

Mechanism clinic 3

Predict the products of the reaction of benzyl bromide and of bromobenzene with (i) CH3ONa/CH3OH, (ii) NaNH2/NH3(l).

Show solution
  1. Benzyl bromide: (i) SN2: benzyl methyl ether; (ii) amide attacks the CH2Br by SN2 as well (benzylamine, with overalkylation), since the sp3 carbon is far more reactive.
  2. Bromobenzene: (i) no reaction (no EWG; no SN2/SN1; methoxide is too weak a base for benzyne); (ii) benzyne → aniline.
