8. Claisen rearrangement of allyl aryl ethers

Heating an allyl aryl ether to about 200 °C converts it into an ortho-allylphenol. The reaction is a concerted [3,3]-sigmatropic rearrangement: six atoms (C=C–C–O–C=C) reorganise through a cyclic six-membered, chair-like transition state, with the O–CH2 bond breaking as a new C–C bond forms between the allyl terminus and the ortho carbon.

Reactant:

Reagent: heat, about 200 °C

Product:

Allyl phenyl ether to 2-allylphenol.

  1. The [3,3] shift moves the allyl group to the ortho carbon and gives a non-aromatic cyclohexa-2,4-dienone.
  2. The dienone has an sp3 ring carbon bearing an H and the allyl group. Tautomerisation (enolisation) restores the aromatic ring and gives the phenol.
  3. The allyl group is attached through the opposite terminus: the carbon bonded to oxygen before the reaction is the terminal =CH2 afterwards (allylic inversion). A crotyl ether therefore gives a branched side chain.

Reactant:

Reagent: heat

Product:

Crotyl phenyl ether gives 2-(but-3-en-2-yl)phenol.

If both ortho positions are blocked, the dienone cannot tautomerise. It undergoes a second [3,3] shift (a Cope rearrangement) that moves the allyl group to the para position, and tautomerisation then gives the para-allylphenol. Free-radical or ionic intermediates are not involved, which is why the reaction is intramolecular and first order.

Reactant:

Reagent: heat

Product:

2,6-Dimethylphenyl allyl ether: para-Claisen product, 4-allyl-2,6-dimethylphenol.

Key idea. The aliphatic version (allyl vinyl ether, CH2=CH–O–CH2CH=CH2) gives a γ,δ-unsaturated aldehyde, pent-4-enal; the driving force is formation of the strong C=O bond.
