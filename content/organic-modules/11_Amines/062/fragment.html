62. Frequent errors and traps in this chapter

  • 1. Writing 3° > 2° > 1° for aqueous basicity of alkylamines. (2° is strongest in water; 3° is strongest only in the gas phase.)
  • 2. Treating benzylamine as an aromatic amine: it is an aliphatic 1° amine – evolves N2 with HNO2.
  • 3. Using Gabriel's phthalimide synthesis for aniline or tert-butylamine.
  • 4. Counting carbons in Hofmann degradation (loses one carbon) and in the nitrile route (gains one).
  • 5. Forgetting that Sandmeyer needs the same halide in CuX as in HX; HBF4 → F; KI → I; no Cu for I and OH.
  • 6. Coupling pH: phenol 9–10, aniline 4–5.
  • 7. Direct nitration of aniline: gives m-nitroaniline (47 %), not only the para isomer.
  • 8. Friedel–Crafts with aniline or nitrobenzene as substrate: aniline fails (AlCl3 complex); nitrobenzene is inert (solvent).
  • 9. Hinsberg: the sulphonamide of the 1° amine dissolves in KOH; 2° is insoluble; 3° does not react.
  • 10. Hofmann elimination: least substituted alkene, except when a β-H is acidified by Ph or C=O.
  • 11. Carbylamine is positive for aniline and also for tertiary-alkyl 1° amines; never for 2° or 3° amines.
  • 12. The diazonium salt must be kept at 273–278 K; do not warm until the replacement step.
  • 13. Pyrrole is not basic although it has N; pyridine is a weak base (sp2 N); aniline is weaker than NH3; guanidine is the strongest base in the usual lists.
  • 14. In SNAr, F is the best leaving group; o/p nitro groups activate, m-nitro does not.
  • 15. AgCN gives the isocyanide; KCN gives the nitrile; AgNO2 gives nitro; KNO2 gives mainly the nitrite.
