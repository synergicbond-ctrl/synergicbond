Reduction reactions: reagents and what they do

In organic chemistry, reduction usually means adding hydrogen (or hydride, or electrons) or removing oxygen. The choice of reagent decides which group is reduced.

Hydride reagents. NaBH₄ reduces aldehydes and ketones only (not esters, acids, amides or nitriles). LiAlH₄ reduces aldehydes, ketones, esters, acids, amides, nitriles and epoxides; it reacts violently with water. DIBAL-H at −78 °C stops at the aldehyde (from esters and nitriles). LiAlH(OtBu)₃ reduces acyl chlorides to aldehydes.
Catalytic hydrogenation (H₂ with Pd, Pt or Ni) adds H₂ to C=C and C≡C by syn addition. A Lindlar catalyst stops an alkyne at the cis alkene; Na/NH₃(l) gives the trans alkene. Birch reduction (Na, NH₃, ROH) of benzene gives 1,4-cyclohexadiene.
C=O to CH₂. Clemmensen (Zn(Hg), HCl) works in acid; Wolff–Kishner (NH₂NH₂, KOH, heat) works in base. Nitro to amine: Sn/HCl or Fe/HCl or H₂/Pd; Zn/NH₄Cl stops at the hydroxylamine.
