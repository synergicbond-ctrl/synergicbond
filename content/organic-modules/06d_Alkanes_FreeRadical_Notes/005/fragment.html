6. Energetics, bond energies and the Hammond postulate

Whether a halogen reacts with an alkane is decided by the enthalpy of each propagation step. For R–H + X• → R• + HX, ΔH = D(R–H) − D(H–X); for R• + X2 → R–X + X•, ΔH = D(X–X) − D(R–X). The approximate bond dissociation enthalpies (kJ mol−1) for methane chemistry are: C–H in CH4 439; H–F 568, H–Cl 431, H–Br 366, H–I 298; F–F 159, Cl–Cl 243, Br–Br 193, I–I 151; C–F 452, C–Cl 351, C–Br 293, C–I 234.

  • F: step 1 = 439 − 568 = −129; step 2 = 159 − 452 = −293; overall −422 kJ mol−1.
  • Cl: step 1 = +8; step 2 = −108; overall −100.
  • Br: step 1 = +73; step 2 = −100; overall −27.
  • I: step 1 = +141; step 2 = −83; overall +58 (unfavourable).

Fluorine reacts explosively: both propagation steps are strongly exothermic with almost no barrier, and the large heat released can break C–C bonds. Iodine does not halogenate alkanes: step 1 is 141 kJ mol−1 uphill and the overall reaction is endothermic; moreover the HI formed reduces any alkyl iodide back to the alkane. Chlorine and bromine are practicable, bromine being slow and chlorine fast. The order of reactivity is F2 > Cl2 > Br2 > I2.

Hammond postulate. The transition state of a step resembles the species closer to it in energy. For Cl• abstracting H the step is exothermic (or nearly thermoneutral), the transition state is early and resembles the reactants, so little of the radical stability difference is felt: low selectivity. For Br• the step is endothermic by roughly 40–75 kJ mol−1, the transition state is late and resembles the alkyl radical, so the 3° > 2° > 1° stability order is expressed almost fully: high selectivity.

Key idea. Reactive reagent, low selectivity (Cl•); less reactive reagent, high selectivity (Br•). The rate-determining, selectivity-determining step is hydrogen abstraction, not the reaction of R• with X2.
