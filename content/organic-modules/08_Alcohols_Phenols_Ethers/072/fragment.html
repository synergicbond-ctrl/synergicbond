Why does phenol not undergo the SN reactions of alcohols? (i) The C–O bond is stronger: it is shortened by resonance (partial C=O+ character). (ii) Substitution at an sp2 carbon by SN1 would need an aryl cation (very unstable) and by SN2 would need backside attack, which the ring blocks. So phenols do not react with HX, and the Lucas test is negative. Oxidation to quinone. Phenol, air-sensitive, slowly gives red/brown quinones. Dihydric phenols are oxidised much more easily: hydroquinone ⇌ p-benzoquinone is the reversible redox pair of the quinhydrone electrode; catechol → o-benzoquinone. Hydroquinone is a photographic developer (reduces Ag+ to Ag), and an antioxidant that scavenges radicals (the same function as vitamin E, BHT, and butylated hydroxyanisole).

Example 50 L1 Foundation

Behaviour towards reagents

For each reagent state whether phenol reacts and give the product: (a) NaOH; (b) NaHCO3; (c) HBr; (d) acetic anhydride; (e) neutral FeCl3; (f) Zn dust, heat.

Show solution
  1. (a) Yes: sodium phenoxide. (b) No: phenol is weaker than carbonic acid. (c) No: the C–O bond is strong and cannot undergo SN. (d) Yes: phenyl acetate (acylation, accelerated by base or acid). (e) Yes: violet colour (complex [Fe(OC6H5)6]3−). (f) Yes: benzene.
Example 51 L2 Main

Williamson and phenols

Why can anisole be made from sodium phenoxide and methyl iodide but not from sodium methoxide and bromobenzene? What would form from phenoxide and tert-butyl bromide?

Show solution
  1. Phenoxide is a good nucleophile and CH3I a good SN2 substrate: anisole forms.
  2. Bromobenzene cannot undergo SN2 (sp2 carbon, backside blocked by the ring) or SN1 (aryl cation); under forcing conditions it would react by benzyne, and the product would be a mixture of anisole and others.
  3. Phenoxide with tert-butyl bromide acts as a base (E2): isobutene + phenol. (With some C-alkylation, giving p-tert-butylphenol, under SN1 conditions.)
