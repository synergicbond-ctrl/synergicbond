9. Dehydration of alcohols; syn eliminations (Chugaev, Cope, ester pyrolysis)

Alcohols lose water in hot concentrated H2SO4 or H3PO4. The OH group is first protonated to give a good leaving group (H2O). Tertiary and secondary alcohols then ionize to a carbocation (E1), which loses a proton; primary alcohols usually go through a concerted E2-like step on the protonated alcohol. The ease of dehydration is 3° > 2° > 1°, the Zaitsev alkene predominates, and rearrangements occur through the carbocation.

Reactant:

Reagent: conc. H₂SO₄, heat

Product:

3,3-Dimethylbutan-2-ol: the secondary cation undergoes a methyl shift to the tertiary cation, and loss of a proton gives the tetrasubstituted 2,3-dimethylbut-2-ene (major).

Reactant:

Reagent: conc. H₂SO₄, 170 °C

Product:

At the lower temperature of about 140 °C, diethyl ether (SN2) forms instead.

A different family of eliminations is thermal and syn. In a syn elimination the departing hydrogen and the leaving group are on the same face; a cyclic transition state delivers the hydrogen to the leaving group. No external base is needed and no carbocation forms, so there is no rearrangement.

  • Ester pyrolysis: an acetate heated at high temperature gives the alkene and acetic acid through a six-membered cyclic transition state.
  • Chugaev elimination: an alcohol is converted into a methyl xanthate, which on heating gives the alkene, COS and methanethiol (six-membered transition state).
  • Cope elimination: a tertiary amine N-oxide on heating gives an alkene and N,N-dialkylhydroxylamine through a five-membered cyclic transition state.

Reactant:

Reagent: heat (Cope elimination)

Product:

Cyclohexene and N,N-dimethylhydroxylamine.

Trap. Syn elimination needs a hydrogen on the same face as the leaving group. In a ring, an adjacent carbon whose H is trans to the leaving group cannot take part; the stereochemical rule is the opposite of the anti E2 rule.
