7. Selectivity and calculation of product distribution

Alkane hydrogens differ in reactivity because the radical formed has different stability. The usual approach is to quote a relative rate per hydrogen. Typical approximate values for photochemical halogenation near room temperature (primary = 1.0) are: chlorination 1° : 2° : 3° = 1 : 3.8 : 5; bromination 1° : 2° : 3° = 1 : 82 : 1600. Such values are given in a question; you are never expected to recall them exactly.

Formula. Amount of a product ∝ (number of equivalent H) × (relative rate per H). Percentage of one product = [N(H) × r] ÷ Σ[N(H) × r] × 100.
  1. Draw the alkane and group the hydrogens into sets that give the same product (symmetry equivalent).
  2. Multiply the number of hydrogens in each set by the relative rate of its type (1°, 2° or 3°).
  3. Add all the products; divide each by the total to get the percentage.

Worked example, propane. It has 6 primary and 2 secondary hydrogens. Chlorination: 6 × 1 = 6 for 1-chloropropane and 2 × 3.8 = 7.6 for 2-chloropropane, total 13.6, giving 44% and 56%. Bromination: 6 × 1 = 6 and 2 × 82 = 164, total 170, giving 3.5% 1-bromopropane and 96.5% 2-bromopropane. Bromination is almost selective, chlorination is not.

Reactant:

Reagent: Br2, hν

Product:

Major product (about 96%); 1-bromopropane is the minor product.

Worked example, 2-methylbutane. Sets: 6 H (two CH3 on C-2), 1 H (C-2, tertiary), 2 H (C-3, secondary), 3 H (C-4). Chlorination: 6, 5, 7.6, 3 = 21.6 total. The tertiary chloride is only 5/21.6 = 23%, and the largest product is the secondary chloride (35%).

Trap. Count hydrogens, not carbons: the two CH3 groups on C-2 of 2-methylbutane together contribute 6 equivalent primary hydrogens. Also, higher temperature reduces selectivity because every step has sufficient energy and the differences in activation energy matter less.
