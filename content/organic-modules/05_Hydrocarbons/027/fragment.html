26. Carbenes and cyclopropanation

Carbenes (:CR2) are neutral divalent carbon species with a sextet (6 electrons): electrophilic. Singlet carbene (paired electrons, sp2, empty p orbital) adds to alkenes in a concerted, stereospecific syn manner (geometry of alkene retained). Triplet carbene (two unpaired electrons) adds stepwise through a diradical, so stereochemistry is lost.

Example 46 Advanced

Carbene chemistry

(a) What does CHCl3/t-BuOK do to cis-but-2-ene? (b) How many stereoisomers of 1,1-dichloro-2,3-dimethylcyclopropane form from (E)-but-2-ene? (c) Why is Simmons–Smith preferred to CH2N2/hν?

Show solution
  1. (a) :CCl2 adds syn to give cis-1,1-dichloro-2,3-dimethylcyclopropane (the two CH3 cis; achiral/meso because the two ring carbons C-2/C-3 are mirror related).
  2. (b) trans alkene → trans-2,3-dimethyl product with C-1 bearing two Cl: C-2 and C-3 are stereocentres with trans CH3: a racemic pair of enantiomers (2 stereoisomers).
  3. (c) The carbenoid reacts selectively with C=C, cleanly and stereospecifically; free CH2 also inserts into C–H bonds and gives mixtures.
