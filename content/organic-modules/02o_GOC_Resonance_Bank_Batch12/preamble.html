AC-054 [Application] · Akshay topic Q54 · Status: Partially verified (isotope-effect size is the standard qualitative argument; no opened source gives a number)

[Akshay Chaudhary topic, master-sheet page 21] Compare (I) CD₃–C⁺–CD₃ and (II) CH₃–C⁺–CH₃. Which statement is correct? (a) I more stable than II (b) II more stable than I (c) both equally stable (d) criterion cannot be applied

Full solution
  1. Both are the isopropyl cation with the same α-carbon framework.
  2. Deuteration changes C–H hyperconjugation only slightly; the isotope effect on carbocation stability is negligible in the usual treatment.
  3. Option (c).

Answer: (c)

AC-057 [Application] · Akshay topic Q57 · Status: Verified (Ref. [2], p. 344; alkyl substitution order)

[Akshay Chaudhary topic, page 21] Alkenes ranked lowest to highest stability: (i) tetrasubstituted (iii) trisubstituted (ii) disubstituted (iv) terminal. Options: (a) iv<ii<iii<i (b) iv<iii<ii<i (c) i<ii<iii<iv (d) iv<i=iii<i

Full solution
  1. Alkene stability rises with alkyl substitution on the C=C: more α-H, more hyperconjugation.
  2. Terminal (iv) least stable; disubstituted (ii) next; trisubstituted (iii); tetrasubstituted (i) most stable.
  3. Option (a).

Answer: (a)
