72. Level 2, set C — diazonium chemistry, aromatic substitution, nitro, cyanide, identification

Practice 157 L2 Synthesis

Prepare m-bromophenol from benzene. Give reagents for each step and explain the order.

Show answer
  1. The meta relationship needs NO2 (a meta director) as the first group; bromine goes meta. NO2 is later converted to NH2 → N2+ → OH.

Benzene → HNO3/H2SO4 → nitrobenzene → Br2/FeBr3 → m-bromonitrobenzene → Sn/HCl (then NaOH) → m-bromoaniline → NaNO2/HCl, 273 K → diazonium → H2O, warm (H2SO4, Δ) → m-bromophenol.

Practice 158 L2 Synthesis

Prepare p-bromobenzoic acid from toluene. Does the order of bromination and oxidation matter?

Show answer
  1. Brominate before oxidising: CH3 is an o/p director, whereas COOH (formed by oxidation) is a meta director and would give m-bromobenzoic acid.

Toluene → Br2/FeBr3 → p-bromotoluene (separated from the ortho isomer) → KMnO4, OH−, heat; then H3O+ → p-bromobenzoic acid.

Practice 159 L2 Synthesis

Convert aniline into (a) 2,4,6-tribromophenol, (b) iodobenzene, (c) p-iodotoluene from p-toluidine, (d) 2,4,6-tribromobenzoic acid.

Show answer
  1. Order: introduce Br with the strongly activating NH2 present; then replace the NH2 through the diazonium salt.

(a) Aniline → NaNO2/HCl, 273 K → H2O/Δ → phenol → Br2/H2O → 2,4,6-tribromophenol. (b) diazotise, then KI. (c) p-toluidine → diazotise, KI → p-iodotoluene. (d) Aniline → Br2/H2O → 2,4,6-tribromoaniline → diazotise → CuCN → 2,4,6-tribromobenzonitrile → H3O+ → 2,4,6-tribromobenzoic acid.

Practice 160 L2 Synthesis

Prepare 3,5-dibromotoluene from p-toluidine and explain why the sequence acetylation → bromination → hydrolysis → deamination is used.

p-toluidine 3,5-dibromotoluene
Show answer
  1. NHAc is a stronger director than CH3; with its para position blocked by CH3, bromine enters the two ortho positions. Deamination then removes the nitrogen, leaving Br at 3 and 5 relative to CH3.

p-Toluidine → Ac2O → N-(4-methylphenyl)acetamide → 2 Br2/AcOH (both Br enter ortho to NHAc, i.e. meta to CH3) → 2,6-dibromo-4-methylacetanilide → H3O+ → 2,6-dibromo-4-methylaniline → NaNO2/HCl → H3PO2 → 3,5-dibromotoluene.

Practice 162 L2 Reactivity

Arrange in decreasing rate of coupling with phenoxide: (a) p-nitrobenzenediazonium, (b) benzenediazonium, (c) p-methoxybenzenediazonium.

Show answer
  1. A diazonium ion is a weak electrophile: an electron-withdrawing group (NO2) increases the positive character of the terminal N (lowers the LUMO), while OCH3 (+M) donates into N≡N+ and reduces electrophilicity.

(a) > (b) > (c).

Practice 163 L2 Reasoning

Explain why a diazonium salt can be coupled with phenol at pH 9–10 but not at pH 13.

Show answer

At pH 13 hydroxide adds to the terminal N of ArN2+ giving ArN=N–OH and then the diazotate ion ArN=N–O−, which is not an electrophile; the diazonium ion is thus consumed before it can couple.

Practice 164 L2 Reasoning

Why are diazonium salts prepared and used immediately at 0–5 °C, and why is NaNO2 added slowly with excess mineral acid?

Show answer
  1. Slow addition keeps nitrous acid concentration low, avoiding NOx loss and local heating (the reaction is exothermic).

At higher temperature ArN2+ reacts with water (phenol + N2) and HNO2 decomposes; excess acid (i) generates NO+, (ii) prevents the diazonium ion from coupling with unreacted amine (giving a diazoamino compound), (iii) suppresses diazotate formation.

Practice 165 L2 Identification

An aromatic compound A (C7H9N) is insoluble in NaOH, dissolves in HCl, and with benzenesulphonyl chloride/KOH gives a clear solution that precipitates on acidification. Diazotisation at 273 K, then CuCN, then acid hydrolysis, gives B (C8H8O2); B on oxidation with KMnO4 gives terephthalic acid. Identify A and B.

Show answer
  1. Hinsberg behaviour → 1° amine; diazotisation works (stable diazonium) → arylamine. CuCN then hydrolysis converts N2+ to COOH: B = CH3C6H4COOH (C8H8O2). Oxidation of the CH3 to COOH gives terephthalic acid, proving the 1,4 relationship.

A = p-toluidine; B = p-methylbenzoic acid (p-toluic acid).

Practice 166 L2 Identification

Compound X (C6H5NO2) is reduced by Sn/HCl to Y (C6H7N). Y gives a red dye with β-naphthol after NaNO2/HCl at 273 K, a foul smell with CHCl3/KOH, and a white precipitate (M = 330) with bromine water. Identify X and Y.

  1. The white precipitate with M = 330 is 2,4,6-tribromoaniline (C6H4Br3N); the other tests confirm a 1° aromatic amine; its precursor is nitrobenzene.

Practice 167 L2 Identification

Compound A (C2H3N) is hydrolysed by acid to B (C2H4O2), whereas an isomer C (C2H3N) with a foul smell is hydrolysed by acid to D (CH5N) and E (CH2O2). Identify A–E.

Show answer
  1. Nitrile hydrolysis keeps the carbon attached to carbon: CH3–COOH. Isocyanide hydrolysis converts the isocyanide carbon into HCOOH and leaves CH3NH2.

A = acetonitrile; B = acetic acid (+ NH4+); C = methyl isocyanide; D = methylamine; E = formic acid.

Practice 171 L2 Nitro

Why does 1-chloro-2,4-dinitrobenzene react with aqueous Na2CO3 at moderate temperature but chlorobenzene needs 623 K and 300 atm?

Show answer

The two nitro groups (o and p) stabilise the Meisenheimer intermediate by delocalising the negative charge onto the O atoms; chlorobenzene has no such stabilisation and the intermediate (or benzyne pathway at high T) is of much higher energy.

Practice 172 L2 Nitro

Which is the better SNAr substrate toward CH3O−: 1-fluoro-4-nitrobenzene or 1-chloro-4-nitrobenzene? Why does the answer differ from an SN2 comparison of CH3F and CH3Cl?

Show answer

1-Fluoro-4-nitrobenzene reacts faster. In SNAr the rate-determining step is nucleophilic addition, accelerated by the highly electronegative F; C–X breaking occurs later. In SN2 C–X bond breaking is part of the rate-determining step, so CH3Cl > CH3F.

Practice 173 L2 Nitro

Predict the product when nitromethane reacts with (a) one equivalent of PhCHO and NaOH, then dilute H2SO4, warm; (b) NaOH, then cold dilute H2SO4 (Nef).

Show answer

(a) PhCH=CH–NO2 via PhCH(OH)CH2NO2 (Henry reaction then dehydration); (b) formaldehyde (CH2=O) and N2O – the Nef reaction converts a 1° nitroalkane to an aldehyde.

Practice 174 L2 Nitro

Why is the nitrous acid test for nitroalkanes given only by nitroalkanes with α-hydrogen? Describe what is seen for CH3CH2NO2 and (CH3)2CHNO2.

Show answer

The reaction requires the α-H to be removed (aci form) so that the nitronate attacks NO+. CH3CH2NO2 → nitrolic acid → red with NaOH; (CH3)2CHNO2 → pseudonitrole → blue; (CH3)3CNO2 → no reaction.

Practice 175 L2 Cyanide

Explain why cyanide gives CH3CN with KCN but CH3NC with AgCN from CH3I. Draw the ambident ion.

Show answer

CN− has nucleophilic sites C and N. In KCN (ionic) the free ion attacks through the carbon (the better nucleophile, larger HOMO coefficient). In AgCN (covalent Ag–C) the carbon is bonded to silver so the lone pair on nitrogen attacks (or Ag+ assists leaving of I− to give a carbocation-like centre attacked at N).

Practice 176 L2 Cyanide

A nitrile A (C4H7N) is made from an alkyl bromide and KCN. Hydrolysis of A gives an acid C4H8O2; reduction gives an amine C4H11N. Give all possibilities for A, the bromides and the amines.

Show answer
  1. C4H7N = C3H7–C≡N. The cyanide carbon is the extra carbon, so the alkyl halide was C3H7Br (n-propyl or isopropyl). Neither amine is chiral.

A = butanenitrile (from 1-bromopropane; acid = butanoic acid; amine = butan-1-amine) or 2-methylpropanenitrile (from 2-bromopropane; acid = 2-methylpropanoic acid; amine = 2-methylpropan-1-amine).

Practice 179 L2 Mixed

Why do nitrobenzene and benzonitrile not dissolve in dilute HCl but aniline does?

Show answer

Nitrobenzene and benzonitrile contain N with no available lone pair (N+(=O)O− and sp N respectively) and are essentially non-basic; aniline has an available (though delocalised) lone pair that is protonated by dilute HCl.

Practice 180 L2 Mixed

The azo dye methyl orange is yellow at pH 6 and red at pH 2. Explain the colour change structurally.

Show answer

At low pH the azo nitrogen attached to the sulphonated ring is protonated, giving a quinoid-type resonance form (Me2N+=C6H4=N–NH–C6H4SO3−) with extended conjugation that absorbs at longer wavelength (red). In neutral/alkaline medium the neutral azo form absorbs in the blue (appears yellow-orange).
