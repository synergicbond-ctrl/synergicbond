16.1 Reimer–Tiemann reaction (ortho-formylation)

Phenol + CHCl3 + aqueous NaOH (10–40 %), about 340 K, then H+ → salicylaldehyde (2-hydroxybenzaldehyde) as the major product, with a little p-hydroxybenzaldehyde. With CCl4 in place of CHCl3 the product is salicylic acid. Mechanism.

  • Hydroxide removes the proton of chloroform: CHCl3 + OH− ⇌ CCl3− + H2O.
  • α-Elimination: CCl3− → :CCl2 + Cl−. Dichlorocarbene is a neutral, electron-deficient electrophile (a sextet at carbon).
  • NaOH also converts phenol to phenoxide, whose ring carbons (ortho, para) are strongly nucleophilic. The carbene attacks the ortho carbon (the Na+ ion pairs with both oxygen and the carbene, delivering it to the ortho position) to give an o-dichloromethyl cyclohexadienone anion.
  • Proton transfer re-aromatises it to the phenoxide of o-(dichloromethyl)phenol, ArCHCl2.
  • Hydrolysis by hydroxide replaces the two Cl (through a benzylic-type cation stabilised by the O−, an o-quinone methide): ArCH(OH)2 → ArCHO. Acidification liberates the phenol.

Trap Reimer–Tiemann works for phenols and electron-rich heterocycles (naphthols, pyrrole, indole). The electrophile is :CCl2, not CHCl2+. The reaction is carried out in biphasic aqueous base–chloroform; yield is low (20–50 %), ortho is preferred to para (about 2 : 1 or better) because of the Na+ chelation. If both ortho positions are blocked, the para product forms. Do not confuse it with the Gattermann–Koch reaction (CO + HCl/AlCl3, no phenols), the Gattermann (HCN/HCl), the Vilsmeier–Haack (DMF/POCl3) or the Duff reaction (hexamine).
Example 46 L1 Foundation

Reimer–Tiemann products

Give the product of (a) phenol + CHCl3/NaOH; (b) phenol + CCl4/NaOH; (c) 2-naphthol + CHCl3/NaOH; (d) p-cresol + CHCl3/NaOH.

Show solution
  1. (a) Salicylaldehyde (2-hydroxybenzaldehyde) + a little 4-hydroxybenzaldehyde.
  2. (b) Salicylic acid (2-hydroxybenzoic acid).
  3. (c) 2-Hydroxy-1-naphthaldehyde (substitution at C-1, the most reactive position of 2-naphthol).
  4. (d) The para position is blocked, so substitution goes ortho to OH: 2-hydroxy-5-methylbenzaldehyde. (A small amount of the “abnormal” product, a dichloromethyl dienone, also forms.)
