8. Deoxygenation of carbonyl groups: C=O to CH2

Three classical methods replace a carbonyl oxygen by two hydrogens. They differ in medium, so the choice depends on what else is in the molecule.

  • Clemmensen: Zn(Hg), concentrated HCl, heat. Strongly acidic. Best for aryl alkyl ketones, such as those made by Friedel–Crafts acylation. Unsuitable for acid-sensitive groups (acetals, tertiary alcohols, many N-protecting groups). It also reduces nitro groups to amines.
  • Wolff–Kishner (Huang-Minlon variant): N2H4, KOH, ethylene glycol, about 200 °C. Strongly basic. The hydrazone is deprotonated and loses N2 to give a carbanion that is protonated. Suitable for acid-sensitive compounds; unsuitable for base-sensitive groups (esters, alkyl halides prone to elimination).
  • Mozingo (thioacetal): HSCH2CH2SH with BF3 gives a 1,3-dithiolane; Raney Ni (with its adsorbed H2) then removes both sulfur atoms. Near-neutral conditions, but Raney Ni also reduces C=C, C≡C and nitro groups.

Key idea. Pick the method by the other functional groups: acid-sensitive substrate → Wolff–Kishner; base-sensitive substrate → Clemmensen; both sensitive, and no reducible C=C or nitro group → thioacetal/Raney Ni. Only aldehydes and ketones react; carboxylic acid derivatives are not deoxygenated this way.
