10.4 Phenols

Tests for phenols
TestReagentObservationRemarks
Neutral FeCl31 to 2 drops neutral FeCl3Violet (phenol); blue, green or red for some phenolsSalicylic acid: violet; enols (ethyl acetoacetate) also give colour; not given by alcohols
Bromine waterBr2/H2OWhite ppt of 2,4,6-tribromophenol; Br2 colour lostActivated ring; aniline also
Liebermann's nitrosoNaNO2 + conc. H2SO4; then dilute; then NaOHDeep green/blue → red on dilution → blue/green with alkaliPhenols with free para position
Phthalein dyePhthalic anhydride, conc. H2SO4, heat; then NaOHPink/red fluorescence (phenolphthalein)Phenol gives phenolphthalein
Azo-dye (coupling)Benzenediazonium chloride, NaOH (cold)Orange/red azo dyePara-coupling
Alkali solubilityAq. NaOHDissolves; regenerated with CO2/HClNot soluble in NaHCO3 (unless nitro-substituted: 2,4-dinitrophenol and picric acid are)
Formula ArOH + OH− → ArO− + H2O ArO− + CO2 + H2O → ArOH↓ + HCO3− (phenol is weaker than carbonic acid; carboxylic acids are not liberated this way)

Why FeCl3 gives a colour. Phenols form coloured iron(III) phenoxide complexes, for example [Fe(OC6H5)6]3−, violet. The reagent must be *neutral* (a drop of dilute NaOH is added to a solution of FeCl3 until a faint brown precipitate persists, then filtered), because free acid HCl suppresses the ionisation of the phenol, and strong base precipitates Fe(OH)3.
