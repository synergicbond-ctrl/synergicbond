11. Conformations of ethane and butane, and cycloalkane strain

Conformations are arrangements generated by rotation about single bonds. In ethane the dihedral angle between C–H bonds on adjacent carbons changes continuously. The staggered form (60°) is the minimum; the eclipsed form (0°) is the maximum, 12 kJ mol−1 higher. This torsional strain comes from three H/H eclipsing interactions of about 4.0 kJ mol−1 each.

In butane, viewing C2–C3, the conformers differ in the relative position of the two CH3 groups. Using the interaction energies H/H 4.0, H/CH3 6.0 and CH3/CH3 eclipsing 11 kJ mol−1:

  • Anti (CH3/CH3 dihedral 180°): the most stable, taken as 0.
  • Gauche (60°): +3.8 kJ mol−1, steric (van der Waals) strain from the two close methyls.
  • Eclipsed (120°, CH3 with H): 4.0 + 6.0 + 6.0 = +16 kJ mol−1.
  • Fully eclipsed (0°, CH3 with CH3): 11 + 4.0 + 4.0 = +19 kJ mol−1, the highest.

The stability order is anti < gauche < eclipsed < fully eclipsed in energy. For a 1,2-disubstituted ethane such as ClCH2CH2Cl the dipole moments of the C–Cl bonds cancel in the anti conformer (µ = 0) but not in the gauche conformer, so the measured dipole is the population-weighted value and is non-zero.

Cycloalkanes. Baeyer strain theory measures angle strain as ½(109°28′ − internal angle of the planar ring); it works for cyclopropane (24°44′) and cyclobutane but fails for rings larger than cyclopentane because they pucker. The heat of combustion per CH2 compared with an unstrained reference gives the real strain energy. Cyclohexane in the chair form is essentially strain-free (all C–C–C angles about 111°, all bonds staggered) and has 6 axial and 6 equatorial hydrogens. Energy order: chair < twist-boat < boat < half-chair.

Cyclopropane to cyclohexane.

Trap. Gauche is not a high-energy eclipsed form; it is a staggered conformer that is simply less stable than anti. A butane conformer at 120° is eclipsed, not gauche.
