1. What this chapter covers and how to use it

Hydrocarbons contain only carbon and hydrogen. They are the skeleton on which every other functional group in organic chemistry is hung, and in JEE Advanced they are the chapter where mechanism, stereochemistry and product counting are tested together. A question on an alkene is almost never "what is the product"; it is "what is the product, which stereoisomers form, how many, and what changes if the reagent is changed from one to another". This chapter is written rule by rule. Every reaction is given in the same order: reagent and conditions, mechanism, regiochemistry (where the group goes), stereochemistry (how it goes), exceptions and traps, a worked example. Read the mechanism once; after that you should be able to derive the rule rather than memorise it.

  • Part A (sections 2–9): classification, alkanes, preparation, free-radical halogenation, counting of products, other reactions, conformations.
  • Part B (sections 10–15): cycloalkanes, ring strain, chair cyclohexane, decalin, bicyclic and spiro systems, reactions of small rings.
  • Part C (sections 16–29): alkenes: stability, every preparation with elimination stereochemistry, every addition reaction, carbocation rearrangements, ozonolysis counting, allylic substitution, carbenes, polymerisation, reagent table.
  • Part D (sections 30–36): alkynes: acidity, preparation, reduction, addition, hydration, hydroboration, acetylide chemistry.
  • Part E (sections 37–42): dienes: allenes, conjugation, kinetic vs thermodynamic control, Diels–Alder with stereochemistry, pericyclic summary, polymers.
  • Part F (sections 43–44): interconversions, synthesis planning and the master reagent table.
  • Part G (sections 45–54): a graded problem bank: Level 1 foundation, Level 2 JEE Advanced multi-correct, matrix and paragraph, Level 3 integer and hard multi-step, plus a drawing drill; every problem has a worked answer.
