29. Practice set, Level 1 (Main): elimination and competition

Practice 68 Main

Zaitsev product

Predict the major alkene: (a) 2-bromobutane + alc. KOH (b) 2-bromo-2-methylbutane + NaOEt/EtOH (c) 3-bromo-3-methylpentane + alc. KOH (d) 2-chloro-3-methylbutane + alc. KOH.

Show answer
  1. Zaitsev: the more substituted (more stable) alkene.
  2. (a) but-2-ene (trans major). (b) 2-methylbut-2-ene. (c) 3-methylpent-2-ene (E major). (d) 2-methylbut-2-ene (hydrogen from the tertiary C3).
Practice 69 Main

Hofmann product

Predict the major alkene from 2-bromo-2-methylbutane with (a) NaOEt/EtOH (b) t-BuOK/t-BuOH. Which rule is operating in each?

Show answer
  1. (a) 2-Methylbut-2-ene: Zaitsev (small base reaches the internal hydrogens). (b) 2-Methylbut-1-ene: Hofmann (bulky base removes the less hindered, terminal hydrogen).
Practice 70 Main

Count

How many alkenes (including geometrical isomers) can be formed from dehydrobromination of 2-bromopentane?

Show answer
  1. H can be removed from C1 (→ pent-1-ene) or from C3 (→ pent-2-ene: E and Z). Total 3 alkenes.
Practice 71 Main

Count

How many alkenes (including stereoisomers) form on dehydrochlorination of (a) 2-chloro-2,3-dimethylbutane (b) 3-chloro-3-methylhexane?

Show answer
  1. 2-Chloro-2,3-dimethylbutane: β-H on the CH3 groups (→ 2,3-dimethylbut-1-ene) and on C3 (→ 2,3-dimethylbut-2-ene, no E/Z since two identical substituents on each carbon): 2 alkenes.
  2. 3-Chloro-3-methylhexane: C3 carries CH3, C2H5, C3H7. β-H from the methyl (→ 3-methylidenehexane, i.e. 2-ethylpent-1-ene), from the ethyl CH2 (→ 3-methylhex-2-ene, E and Z), from the propyl CH2 (→ 3-methylhex-3-ene, E and Z): 1 + 2 + 2 = 5 alkenes.
Practice 72 Main

Dehydrohalogenation order

Arrange in decreasing rate of E2 with alcoholic KOH: (a) CH3CH2CH2Br (b) (CH3)2CHBr (c) (CH3)3CBr.

Show answer
  1. E2 rate increases with substitution at the α-carbon (more substituted alkene being formed and relief of steric strain). (c) > (b) > (a).
Practice 73 Main

Aqueous vs alcoholic

Predict the major product of 2-bromopropane with (a) aq. KOH (b) alc. KOH, heat. Explain.

Show answer
  1. (a) Propan-2-ol (substitution; OH− solvated in water, acting as a nucleophile, with some propene). (b) Propene (elimination; in ethanol the stronger base ethoxide is present and the less polar medium promotes E2).
Practice 74 Main

Anti-periplanar

The Newman projection below (looking along C2–C3 of 2-bromobutane) shows the conformation required for E2 loss of H from C3. Name the alkene formed when the two methyl groups are anti, and when they are gauche.

Front carbon C2: Br, H, CH3. Back carbon C3: H (anti to Br), CH3, H. Here the two CH3 groups are gauche.
Show answer
  1. Br on C2 and the leaving H on C3 are anti (180°). With H and Br anti, the two methyl groups are either anti or gauche: if CH3(C2) and CH3(C3) are anti the product is trans-but-2-ene (E); if gauche the product is cis (Z). The anti-methyl arrangement is the more stable conformer, hence trans-but-2-ene predominates.
Practice 75 Main

Stereospecific elimination

meso-2,3-Dibromobutane reacts with NaI in acetone to give an alkene. Give its geometry. What would the racemic (dl) dibromide give?

Show answer
  1. Iodide removes both Br in anti elimination. In the meso form the two Br can be anti while the two CH3 are also anti: trans-but-2-ene. For the dl form the anti-Br conformer places the methyls gauche: cis-but-2-ene.

Alkyne from dihalide

Write the product of (a) 1,2-dibromopropane + 3 NaNH2, then H2O (b) 2,2-dichlorobutane + 2 KOH (alc.), Δ (c) 1,1-dichloroethane + 2 NaNH2.

Show answer
  1. (a) propyne (via the sodium salt, protonated in work-up). (b) but-2-yne mostly (and some but-1-yne). (c) acetylene (ethyne).
Practice 77 Main

E1 or E2?

Identify E1 or E2: (a) rate = k[RX][B] (b) rearranged alkene forms (c) stereospecific anti elimination (d) no deuterium isotope effect (e) occurs in ethanol without added base, on heating, with a tertiary halide.

Show answer

Practice 78 Main

Isotope effect

Elimination of HBr from C6H5CH2CH2Br with EtO− is about 7 times faster than from C6H5CD2CH2Br. Interpret kH/kD ≈ 7.

Show answer
  1. A large primary isotope effect (kH/kD ≈ 3–8) means the C–H (C–D) bond is broken in the rate-determining step: concerted E2 (β-hydrogen removal). In the substrate PhCH2CH2Br, the β-hydrogens are those on the benzylic carbon (these are the acidic ones).
Practice 79 Main

Hofmann elimination preview

Why does the E2 reaction of 2-fluorobutane with ethoxide give mostly but-1-ene?

Show answer
  1. Fluoride is a poor leaving group, so C–F breaks late and the transition state has carbanion character; the more acidic H (on the less substituted carbon, the terminal CH3) is removed: Hofmann orientation.
Practice 80 Main

Cyclohexane

Which isomer eliminates HBr faster with NaOEt: cis- or trans-1-bromo-4-tert-butylcyclohexane? Give the reason.

Show answer
  1. cis. With t-Bu equatorial, the cis isomer has Br axial and two axial β-hydrogens anti to it; the trans isomer has Br equatorial and cannot be anti-periplanar to any ring β-H without flipping to the t-Bu-axial chair (≈ 5 kcal mol−1 higher).
Practice 81 Main

E1cB

Write the mechanism type and product for CH3CH(OH)CH2CHO with dilute NaOH on warming.

Show answer
  1. The α-H (next to CHO) is acidic; hydroxide forms the enolate; the enolate expels HO− from the β-carbon (E1cB). Product: crotonaldehyde (but-2-enal), CH3CH=CHCHO, conjugated.
Practice 82 Main

E1 reaction conditions

Identify the major organic product(s) on heating 2-methyl-2-butanol with conc. H2SO4 and explain the mechanism.

Show answer
  1. Protonation of OH, loss of water gives the 3° cation; loss of H+ (E1): 2-methylbut-2-ene (major, trisubstituted) and 2-methylbut-1-ene (minor).
Practice 83 Main

Rearranged alkene

Heating 3,3-dimethylbutan-2-ol with H2SO4 gives mainly 2,3-dimethylbut-2-ene. Explain.

Show answer
  1. The 2° cation formed after loss of water undergoes a 1,2-methyl shift to the more stable 3° cation (CH3)2C+–CH(CH3)2; loss of H+ gives the tetrasubstituted alkene 2,3-dimethylbut-2-ene (Zaitsev).
Practice 84 Main

SN2 or E2?

Predict the major product, SN2 or E2: (a) CH3CH2Br + NaOEt/EtOH (b) (CH3)2CHBr + NaOEt/EtOH (c) CH3CH2CH2Br + t-BuOK (d) (CH3)3CBr + NaOEt (e) CH3CH2CH2Br + NaCN/DMSO.

Show answer
  1. (a) SN2: diethyl ether. (b) E2: propene (≈ 80 %). (c) E2: propene (bulky base). (d) E2: isobutene only. (e) SN2: butanenitrile.
Practice 85 Main

Temperature

Why does raising the temperature increase the proportion of elimination relative to substitution?

Show answer
  1. Elimination produces more molecules (R–X + B → alkene + BH + X−: ΔS more positive) and has a higher activation energy. In ΔG = ΔH − TΔS the entropy term gets heavier as T increases and the rate constant for the higher-Ea path grows faster (Arrhenius).
Practice 86 Main

Solvolysis

Predict products of tert-butyl chloride in boiling 80 % aqueous ethanol and in cold water.

Show answer
  1. SN1 and E1 compete: tert-butyl alcohol/ethyl tert-butyl ether (major) and isobutene. In cold water: mainly tert-butyl alcohol; heating increases the proportion of isobutene.
Practice 87 Main

Choose the base

Which base is best for converting 2-bromo-2-methylbutane into (i) the less substituted alkene (ii) the more substituted alkene? (A) NaOH (B) NaOEt (C) t-BuOK

Show answer
  1. (i) The less substituted alkene (2-methylbut-1-ene) is obtained with the bulky t-BuOK: C. (ii) The more substituted alkene (2-methylbut-2-ene) is obtained with small bases such as NaOEt (B) or NaOH (A).
Practice 88 Main

HX loss in hexachlorocyclohexane

Why is the β-isomer of hexachlorocyclohexane (all Cl equatorial) much less reactive towards dehydrochlorination than the γ-isomer?

Show answer
  1. E2 requires H and Cl trans-diaxial. In β-BHC all six Cl are equatorial and all H are axial: an equatorial Cl is gauche (not anti) to the neighbouring axial H, so no anti-periplanar H–C–C–Cl pair exists. A ring flip would put all six Cl axial (severe 1,3-diaxial repulsions), so this isomer is very unreactive. The γ-isomer has axial Cl atoms with anti axial H neighbours, so it eliminates easily.
Practice 89 Main

Dehalogenation

Write the product of Zn/ethanol with (a) 1,2-dibromoethane (b) 1,3-dibromopropane (c) 1,2-dibromocyclohexane.

Show answer
  1. (a) ethene; (b) cyclopropane (intramolecular, 3-exo; Zn in ethanol, the Freund reaction); (c) cyclohexene.
