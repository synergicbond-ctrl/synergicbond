Practice 105 L2 Order

Arrange in decreasing basic strength: (i) p-methoxyaniline, (ii) aniline, (iii) p-chloroaniline, (iv) p-nitroaniline, (v) m-methoxyaniline.

p-anisidine aniline m-anisidine p-chloroaniline p-nitroaniline

Practice 106 L2 SIR

Which is the stronger base, N,N-dimethylaniline or N,N-dimethyl-o-toluidine (N,N,2-trimethylaniline)? Explain using steric inhibition of resonance (SIR).

N,N-dimethylaniline N,N-dimethyl-o-toluidine
Show answer
  1. The ortho CH3 twists the bulky –N(CH3)2 group out of the plane of the ring, so the N lone pair no longer overlaps with the π system. With resonance (the main cause of low basicity in anilines) switched off, the lone pair is more available.
  2. Contrast with primary anilines: there the small –NH2 stays planar and the usual ortho effect (hindered solvation of –NH3+) makes the ortho isomer weaker than the para isomer.

N,N-Dimethyl-o-toluidine is the stronger base.

Practice 107 L2 Pyridines

Arrange in increasing basicity (pKa of conjugate acid in brackets): pyridine (5.2), 4-aminopyridine (9.2), 3-aminopyridine (6.0), 4-nitropyridine (≈ 1.6). Explain why 4-aminopyridine is far more basic than 3-aminopyridine.

pyridine 4-aminopyridine 3-aminopyridine 4-nitropyridine
Show answer
  1. Protonation occurs at the ring N. In 4-aminopyridine the exocyclic NH2 donates its lone pair (+M) through the ring directly to N+H (structure H2N+=C…N–H), which strongly stabilises the cation. In the 3-isomer the NH2 is meta to the ring N and cannot conjugate with it, so only a modest −I/+I net effect operates. Nitro at 4 withdraws strongly.

4-nitropyridine < pyridine < 3-aminopyridine < 4-aminopyridine.

Practice 108 L2 Heterocycles

In imidazole, which nitrogen is protonated and why is imidazole (pKa 7.0) a stronger base than pyridine (5.2) although both have an sp2 N?

imidazole
Show answer
  1. The pyrrole-like N1 lone pair is part of the aromatic sextet and is not available. After protonation of N3 the positive charge is delocalised N1 ↔ N3, an amidinium ion.

The pyridine-like N3 (lone pair in the ring plane) is protonated. The conjugate acid, imidazolium, is stabilised by two equivalent resonance structures with the charge shared between both N atoms (amidinium-type delocalisation).

Practice 109 L2 Bridged

Explain why quinuclidine (pKa 11.0) is a stronger base and a much better nucleophile than triethylamine (pKa 10.8).

quinuclidine triethylamine
Show answer
  1. Basicity is similar since both are 3° alkylamines; toward CH3I quinuclidine is about 40–60 times faster than Et3N (a comparison made in Ref. [2], Organic Chemistry), the difference being steric.

The bicyclic framework holds the three alkyl arms back, leaving the lone pair exposed; in Et3N free rotation lets the ethyl groups shield N (steric hindrance to solvation and to attack).

Practice 111 L2 Buffer

A buffer contains 0.20 M methylamine and 0.10 M methylammonium chloride. Find its pH (pKb of CH3NH2 = 3.38).

Show answer
  1. pKa = 14 − 3.38 = 10.62. pH = pKa + log([base]/[acid]) = 10.62 + log 2 = 10.62 + 0.30 = 10.92.

Practice 112 L2 Titration

25.0 mL of 0.100 M aniline (pKb = 9.38) is titrated with 0.100 M HCl. Find the pH (a) at the half-equivalence point, (b) at the equivalence point.

Show answer
  1. (a) Half-way, [ArNH2] = [ArNH3+], so pH = pKa = 14 − 9.38 = 4.62.
  2. (b) At equivalence the solution is 0.0500 M anilinium chloride (25 + 25 = 50 mL). [H+] = √(KaC) = √(2.40 × 10−5 × 0.0500) = 1.10 × 10−3; pH = 2.96 ≈ 3.0.

(a) 4.62; (b) ≈ 3.0.

Practice 114 L2 pKa order

Arrange in decreasing pKa of the conjugate acids: piperidine, pyridine, aniline, acetamide, benzylamine.

Show answer
  1. sp3 N with +I: piperidine. Benzylamine: sp3 N with a weak −I phenyl. Pyridine: sp2. Aniline: delocalised lone pair. Acetamide: amide resonance.

Piperidine (11.1) > benzylamine (9.3) > pyridine (5.2) > aniline (4.6) > acetamide (−0.5).

Practice 115 L2 Ortho effect

2,6-Dimethylaniline has a pKa (conjugate acid) of about 3.9, lower than aniline (4.6). Why is the pKa lower although two methyls are electron donating?

Show answer
  1. In the free base the NH2 group is small and little affected.

The two ortho methyls crowd the –NH3+ group and hinder its solvation (hydrogen bonding to water) – the ortho effect – which destabilises the cation more than the +I effect stabilises it.

Practice 116 L2 Intramolecular

o-Nitroaniline (pKa −0.3) is a weaker base than p-nitroaniline (1.0). Give two reasons.

Show answer

The nitro group at ortho exerts a stronger −I effect (closer) and −M effect; also intramolecular N–H···O hydrogen bonding in the neutral base stabilises it, and the ortho group hinders solvation of the –NH3+ ion.

Practice 118 L2 Solvent effect

The order of basicity of the methylamines is Me2NH > MeNH2 > Me3N > NH3 in water but in chlorobenzene it is Me3N > Me2NH > MeNH2 > NH3. Explain.

Show answer
  1. In water, solvation of RNH3+ (3 N–H) > R2NH2+ (2) > R3NH+ (1) modifies the +I order.

Chlorobenzene does not hydrogen bond with the ammonium ions, so the number of N–H bonds no longer matters and the inductive effect alone decides.

Practice 119 L2 Acidity of N–H

Rank in decreasing acidity of the N–H bond: pyrrole, diethylamine, acetamide, phthalimide, aniline.

Show answer
  1. The N–H acidity increases with stabilisation of the conjugate anion: two flanking C=O (phthalimide) > one C=O (amide) > aromatic ring (pyrrole, aniline) > alkyl.

Phthalimide (pKa 8.3) > acetamide (≈ 15–17) ≈ pyrrole (17.5) > aniline (≈ 30) > diethylamine (≈ 35–40).

Practice 122 L2 Guanidine

Explain why guanidine is protonated on the =NH nitrogen rather than on an –NH2, and why the pKa of guanidinium is 13.6.

Show answer

Protonation at =NH gives the cation H2N–C(=N+H2)–NH2, which has three equivalent resonance structures, so the positive charge is spread symmetrically over three nitrogens (Y-delocalisation); protonating an –NH2 would leave the lone pair of the other nitrogens unsymmetrically conjugated, giving a less stable cation.

Practice 123 L2 Nucleophile versus base

DABCO (1,4-diazabicyclo[2.2.2]octane) is a much better nucleophilic catalyst than triethylamine although it is a little less basic (pKa of conjugate acid 8.8 versus 10.8). Explain.

DABCO
Show answer
  1. Basicity (proton, tiny) is governed by electron density; nucleophilicity at carbon also by steric accessibility.

In DABCO the carbon arms are tied back by the bicyclic cage, so the lone pairs are exposed and unhindered – high nucleophilicity. Its lower basicity arises because after one N is protonated the second N+-neighbour withdraws electrons inductively (through the cage).

Practice 127 L2 Hybrid

Why does the pKb of aniline (9.38) not change much on N-methylation (9.30) while changing N-methylation of ammonia to methylamine reduces pKb by 1.4 units (4.75 → 3.38)?

Show answer

In aniline the lone pair delocalisation into the ring dominates the basicity; the +I effect of CH3 has a small effect. In ammonia the methyl group changes the electron density on N directly and also stabilises the ion by dispersion and solvation.

Practice 129 L2 Effect

In p-aminobenzoic acid the pKa of the carboxylic acid is about 4.9 and that of the anilinium ion about 2.4. What species predominates at (a) pH 1, (b) pH 3.5, (c) pH 7?

p-aminobenzoic acid
Show answer
  1. Below pH 2.4 the amine is protonated; above 4.9 the carboxylic acid is ionised. In between (≈ 3.65 is the isoelectric region) the neutral molecule predominates, since here the amine is a very weak base and the zwitterion is not favoured.

Practice 130 L2 Application

Why is the pKa of the –NH3+ group in glycine (9.6) lower than that of ethylammonium (10.7)?

Show answer

The nearby carboxylate/carboxylic acid group is electron withdrawing (−I), which stabilises the free amine relative to its cation, so –NH3+ is the stronger acid (the amine the weaker base).

Practice 131 L2 Hammett

The pKa values of substituted anilinium ions are: p-OCH3 5.3; p-CH3 5.1; H 4.6; m-Cl 3.5; m-NO2 2.5; p-NO2 1.0. Why does p-NO2 lower the pKa more than m-NO2?

Show answer

At para the nitro group acts through −M as well as −I: the amine lone pair is delocalised directly onto the nitro group in the free base (H2N+=C6H4=NO2−), stabilising it relative to the cation. At meta there is no such conjugation, only the weaker inductive effect.

Practice 132 L2 Quaternary

Why can tetramethylammonium hydroxide not be considered a Brønsted base in the same way as methylamine, even though its solutions are strongly alkaline?

Show answer

(CH3)4N+OH− is an ionic strong base (fully dissociated); the basicity is that of OH−, not of N. There is no lone pair on N, no equilibrium with water.
