30. The Mannich reaction

An enolisable carbonyl compound reacts with formaldehyde (or another non-enolisable aldehyde) and a primary or secondary amine (usually as its hydrochloride) to form a β-amino carbonyl compound (Mannich base).

  • Step 1: R2NH + HCHO give the iminium ion CH2=N+R2 (Eschenmoser-type electrophile).
  • Step 2: the enol of the ketone attacks the iminium ion at carbon.
  • Step 3: loss of H+ gives the β-aminoketone.
+ + HCl, EtOH, Δ⟶
Acetone + HCHO + dimethylamine hydrochloride → 4-(dimethylamino)butan-2-one

Mannich bases are valuable because heating (or quaternising with MeI and then base) eliminates the amine and gives a vinyl ketone, e.g. methyl vinyl ketone from the above.

Elimination of the amine from the Mannich base gives methyl vinyl ketone (but-3-en-2-one)

Phenols and indoles also act as the nucleophile (aminomethylation of aromatic rings), and the Mannich reaction is the biochemical route to tropane alkaloids (Robinson's tropinone synthesis, a one-pot double Mannich).
