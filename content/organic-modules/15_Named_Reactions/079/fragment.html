72. Stork enamine alkylation

A ketone and a secondary amine (pyrrolidine, morpholine) with acid and removal of water give an enamine (a nitrogen analogue of an enol, much more nucleophilic at carbon than an enol or enolate-equivalent under neutral conditions). The β-carbon attacks an electrophile (CH3I, allyl bromide, benzyl bromide, α,β-unsaturated carbonyl compound, acyl chloride), giving an iminium salt; aqueous acid hydrolyses the iminium to the α-substituted ketone and regenerates the amine.

+ H⁺, –H₂O⟶
Cyclohexanone + pyrrolidine → enamine
+ + 1. SN2 2. H₃O⁺⟶ + +
Enamine + CH3I, then hydrolysis → 2-methylcyclohexanone + pyrrolidine (as HI salt)
Shortcut Why use it? Mono-alkylation (the enamine alkylates once; enolate alkylation often gives poly-alkylation), neutral conditions, and regiochemistry (the enamine from an unsymmetrical ketone has its C=C on the less substituted side, so alkylation occurs at the less substituted α-carbon, like the LDA kinetic enolate).
