Key idea To predict: (1) cis + anti → racemic, trans + anti → meso; (2) cis + syn → meso, trans + syn → racemic. (This holds for symmetric alkenes like but-2-ene or stilbene; for unsymmetric ones count the diastereomers.)
Example 68 Main

Predict the products

Give the stereochemistry of the product formed when (a) cis-but-2-ene adds Br2; (b) trans-but-2-ene is treated with cold, dilute alkaline KMnO4; (c) (E)-stilbene reacts with Br2; (d) (Z)-but-2-ene is epoxidised with m-CPBA and the epoxide hydrolysed with aqueous acid.

Show solution
  1. (a) anti addition to a cis alkene: (±)-(2R,3R)- and (2S,3S)-2,3-dibromobutane (racemic, optically inactive overall).
  2. (b) syn dihydroxylation of a trans alkene: (±)-(2R,3R)/(2S,3S)-butane-2,3-diol (racemic).
  3. (c) (E)-stilbene is trans: anti addition gives the meso dibromide ((1R,2S)-1,2-dibromo-1,2-diphenylethane).
  4. (d) The epoxide from a cis alkene is the cis (meso) epoxide; acid-catalysed hydrolysis is anti (backside attack at either carbon): the product is the racemic (R,R)/(S,S) diol, the same as anti dihydroxylation of a cis alkene.
(E)-stilbene meso-1,2-dibromo-1,2-diphenylethane (R,S)
Example 69 Main

Mechanism consequence of the bromonium ion

Why does anti addition of Br2 give a racemate from a cis alkene?

Show solution
  1. Br2 forms a cyclic bromonium ion on either face of the alkene with equal probability. Bromide opens the ring from the face opposite the bridging Br at either carbon. Because the cis alkene is symmetric, attack at C-2 and at C-3 give enantiomers; both pathways and both faces are equally likely: a 1:1 mixture of (R,R) and (S,S). Net: racemic (optically inactive).

Trap Hydroboration–oxidation of 1-methylcyclohexene gives trans-2-methylcyclohexanol. H and B add to the same face, so the new H on C-1 and the new OH on C-2 are cis, which puts the CH3 on C-1 and the OH on C-2 on opposite faces. Read the question to see which pair (H/OH or CH3/OH) it asks about.
Example 70 Adv

Stereochemistry of hydroboration

1-Methylcyclohexene is treated with BH3·THF then H2O2/NaOH. Name the product and its stereochemistry.

Show solution
  1. Boron adds to the less substituted carbon (C-2) and H to C-1 (the more substituted carbon), both from the same face (syn addition). Oxidation replaces B by OH with retention. So the new C-1 H and C-2 OH are cis, which places the CH3 at C-1 trans to the OH: trans-2-methylcyclohexanol, formed as the racemate ((1R,2R) and (1S,2S)).

24.4 Alkynes: syn and anti reductions

Partial hydrogenation of an alkyne is stereoselective: Lindlar catalyst (Pd/CaCO3, quinoline-poisoned) or P-2 nickel with H2: syn addition to give the cis (Z) alkene. Na or Li in liquid NH3: anti addition through a radical anion, giving the trans (E) alkene. Complete hydrogenation gives the alkane.

but-2-yne (Z)-but-2-ene (Lindlar or Ni₂B) (E)-but-2-ene (Na, liq. NH₃)
hex-3-yne (Z)-hex-3-ene (E)-hex-3-ene

24.5 Cycloaddition and other stereospecific reactions

The Diels–Alder reaction is a concerted suprafacial [4+2] cycloaddition: the geometry of the dienophile is retained in the product (cis substituents stay cis).

butadiene + maleic acid: cis-cyclohex-4-ene-1,2-dicarboxylic acid (meso) butadiene + fumaric acid: trans diacid (chiral)

Additional examples: the Simmons–Smith cyclopropanation (syn, stereospecific); ozonolysis (the stereocentres in the fragments are untouched; the alkene geometry is lost); epoxidation (retention of alkene geometry); Claisen/Cope rearrangements (chair transition states).
