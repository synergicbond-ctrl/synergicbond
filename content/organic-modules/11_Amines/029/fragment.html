29. Structure, nomenclature and properties

The nitro group is –NO2: nitrogen bonded to carbon and carrying a formal positive charge, with two oxygen atoms sharing one negative charge. The two N–O bonds are identical (≈ 122 pm, between a single and a double bond) because the group is a resonance hybrid of two equivalent forms; the group is planar. The nitro group is strongly electron-withdrawing by both −I and −M effects. Nitroalkanes are named nitro-alkanes (nitromethane, 2-nitropropane); aryl: nitrobenzene, 1,3-dinitrobenzene, 2,4,6-trinitrotoluene (TNT).

Nitro compounds

Properties: nitroalkanes are colourless polar liquids (nitromethane b.p. 101 °C, dipole ≈ 3.5 D) – their boiling points are much higher than those of the isomeric alkyl nitrites (R–O–N=O, e.g. ethyl nitrite b.p. 17 °C) because of the strong dipole. Nitrobenzene (b.p. 211 °C) is a pale-yellow, dense (1.2 g mL-1), poorly soluble liquid with a smell of bitter almonds; it is poisonous. It is used as a solvent and to make aniline.

Key idea Nitro compound versus nitrite ester: R–NO2 has a C–N bond; R–O–N=O has a C–O bond. Nitro compounds are reduced to amines (Sn/HCl) and are not hydrolysed by aqueous NaOH; alkyl nitrites are hydrolysed by alkali to the alcohol and nitrite, and reduced to alcohols + NH3.
