The examples below are longer than the earlier ones. Each is solved step by step; try to cover the solution and write your own answer first.

Example 57 Adv

One ketone, three outcomes

Butanone is treated separately with (i) I2/NaOH, then H+; (ii) m-CPBA; (iii) NH2OH, then H2SO4. Give the organic product(s) in each case and the key mechanistic point.

Show solution
  1. (i) Haloform: CH3CO– becomes CI3CO–, then OH− cleaves it. Products: propanoic acid (as the carboxylate before acidification) and CHI3. The point: the methyl side is the reacting side; the carbon chain loses one carbon.
  2. (ii) Baeyer–Villiger: ethyl (primary alkyl) migrates in preference to methyl, so ethyl ethanoate. The point: migratory aptitude, not geometry.
  3. (iii) The oxime is formed as E and Z isomers (CH3 vs C2H5 on carbon). In the Beckmann step the group anti to OH migrates to N: the isomer with OH anti to ethyl gives N-ethylethanamide, the isomer with OH anti to methyl gives N-methylpropanamide. The point: the outcome depends on the oxime geometry, so a mixture can result.

Example 58 Adv

Controlling a mixed aldol

Design syntheses of (a) 4-phenylbut-3-en-2-one and (b) 3-hydroxy-2-methylbutanal from simple carbonyl compounds, and say how self-condensation is suppressed in each.

Show solution
  1. (a) Claisen–Schmidt: PhCHO (no α-H, cannot self-condense) + acetone in NaOH. Acetone is used in excess or added slowly to the mixture of PhCHO and base so that its enolate meets PhCHO rather than another acetone. Dehydration is spontaneous because the C=C is conjugated with both the phenyl ring and C=O.
  2. (b) CH3CH(OH)–CH(CH3)–CHO has the carbinol carbon from ethanal (acceptor) and the α-carbon from propanal (donor). Both partners enolise, so the plain mixture gives four products. Use a preformed enolate: convert propanal to a lithium enolate or its silyl enol ether, then add ethanal at −78 °C (directed or Mukaiyama aldol).

Example 59 Adv

Protecting groups in a longer route

Convert 4-oxopentanal, OHC–CH2CH2–CO–CH3, into 4-hydroxypentanal (reduce the ketone only).

Show solution
  1. The aldehyde is more reactive than the ketone towards both hydride and alcohols, so NaBH4 alone would reduce the aldehyde first. Therefore protect the aldehyde selectively: one equivalent of ethane-1,2-diol (or methanol) with H+ at low temperature forms the acetal at the aldehyde; the ketone is left free.
  2. Reduce the ketone with NaBH4; remove the acetal with dilute H3O+. The product 4-hydroxypentanal then closes to its cyclic hemiacetal (five-membered ring), which is the form that is isolated.

Example 60 Adv

Kinetic versus thermodynamic alkylation

2-Methylcyclohexanone is converted to its enolate (a) with LDA in THF at −78 °C, (b) with NaH in refluxing THF; each is then treated with allyl bromide. Name both products and the key feature.

Show solution
  1. (a) Kinetic enolate: the bulky LDA removes the more accessible C-6 hydrogen. Allylation at C-6 gives 2-allyl-6-methylcyclohexanone.
  2. (b) Thermodynamic enolate (tetrasubstituted C=C toward the methyl group): allylation at C-2 gives 2-allyl-2-methylcyclohexanone, a quaternary α-carbon.

Example 61 Adv

Hidden carbonyl compounds

Compound P (C8H8O2) dissolves in NaOH, gives a violet colour with FeCl3, an orange precipitate with 2,4-DNP, a yellow precipitate with I2/NaOH and no silver mirror. It is steam-volatile. Clemmensen reduction gives Q (C8H10O). Identify P and Q.

Show solution
  1. DBE of P = (2×8 + 2 − 8)/2 = 5: a benzene ring (4) plus one C=O. Soluble in NaOH and violet with FeCl3: a phenolic OH. DNP positive and iodoform positive, Tollens' negative: a methyl ketone, CH3CO–, not an aldehyde. So P is a hydroxyacetophenone.
  2. Steam volatility points to intramolecular hydrogen bonding between OH and C=O, which is possible only in the ortho isomer (the para isomer associates through intermolecular hydrogen bonds and is not steam volatile). P = 2-hydroxyacetophenone.
  3. Clemmensen replaces C=O by CH2: Q = 2-ethylphenol (C8H10O).
P Q
Example 62 Adv

Wittig design

Design a synthesis of (E)-1,2-diphenylethene (stilbene) and of (Z)-pent-2-ene using the Wittig reaction. Which ylide types are needed?

Show solution
  1. (E)-Stilbene: PhCHO + benzylidenetriphenylphosphorane (from PhCH2Br and PPh3); the semi-stabilised ylide gives mostly E (or use the Horner–Wadsworth–Emmons route with diethyl benzylphosphonate for high E).
  2. (Z)-Pent-2-ene: ethanal + propylidenetriphenylphosphorane (from n-propyl bromide), an unstabilised ylide under salt-free conditions, which favours the Z alkene. The alternative disconnection (propanal + ethylidene ylide) is equally acceptable here because both halides are primary.

Example 63 Adv

Mannich, elimination, Michael

Starting from cyclohexanone, outline a three-step preparation of 2-(3-oxobutyl)cyclohexanone without using methyl vinyl ketone from a bottle.

Show solution
  1. Step 1: Mannich reaction of acetone with HCHO and (CH3)2NH·HCl gives 4-(dimethylamino)butan-2-one. Step 2: heat to eliminate the amine, giving but-3-en-2-one in situ (or quaternise with CH3I and treat with base). Step 3: Michael addition of the cyclohexanone enamine (pyrrolidine) or enolate, then H3O+.
  2. This is the usual solution when MVK, which polymerises easily, must be generated in the flask.

Example 64 Adv

Tishchenko or Cannizzaro?

Compare the outcome of treating benzaldehyde with (i) conc. aqueous NaOH, (ii) aluminium benzyloxide/benzyl alcohol (dry, no water). Give the products and the type of hydride donor.

Show solution
  1. (i) Cannizzaro: PhCH2OH + PhCOO−; hydride from the hydrate dianion PhCH(O−)2.
  2. (ii) Tishchenko-type: benzyl benzoate, PhCOOCH2Ph; hydride from the aluminium hemiacetal alkoxide, then the ester forms directly (no water, no free carboxylate).
+ Al(OBn)₃, anhydrous⟶
Example 65 Adv

Three components, one pot

Benzaldehyde, acetone (1 equiv.) and aniline are mixed in methanol with acid catalyst. Which carbonyl–amine interaction is fastest and what product class results? Contrast with the base-catalysed reaction without aniline.

Show solution
  1. With acid catalyst and a primary arylamine, PhCHO forms the imine PhCH=NPh (benzylideneaniline) rapidly. Acetone then reacts as a nucleophile (enol) with the iminium ion in a Mannich-type step, giving a β-amino ketone, PhCH(NHPh)CH2COCH3.
  2. Without aniline, in NaOH, the Claisen–Schmidt condensation gives benzalacetone (PhCH=CHCOCH3). So the catalyst and the added amine decide between amination (Mannich) and olefination (aldol condensation).

Example 66 Adv

Multiple carbonyl groups: selectivity table

A molecule contains an aldehyde, a ketone and an ester. Rank the groups in order of reactivity towards (a) NaBH4, (b) LiAlH4, (c) a Grignard reagent (1 equiv.), and say what is observed with 1 equivalent of each reagent at −78 °C.

Show solution
  1. Reactivity of C=O: aldehyde > ketone > ester for all three nucleophiles. NaBH4 (1 equiv., cold) reduces the aldehyde selectively; LiAlH4 also attacks the aldehyde first but reduces ketone and ester quickly when warmed; RMgX (1 equiv., −78 °C) adds mainly to the aldehyde. The ester survives all the cold 1-equivalent conditions.
  2. To touch only the ketone, protect the aldehyde as an acetal first (acetals are formed faster from aldehydes).
Example 67 Adv

Quantitative: haloform of an unknown ketone

0.500 g of a methyl ketone gives 1.47 g of iodoform (M = 394) in 100 % yield. Vigorous oxidation of the ketone gives benzene-1,4-dicarboxylic acid. Find the molar mass and identify the ketone.

Show solution
  1. n(CHI3) = 1.47/394 = 3.73 × 10−3 mol = n(ketone), one CHI3 per CH3CO– unit.
  2. M(ketone) = 0.500/3.73 × 10−3 = 134 g mol-1, formula C9H10O (DBE 5: ring + C=O).
  3. Methyl ketones of this formula: Ar–CO–CH3 with Ar = 134 − 43 = 91 (C7H7, a tolyl group) or PhCH2COCH3. Oxidation to the 1,4-diacid fixes a para relationship between CH3 and the carbonyl side chain: 4-methylacetophenone.
