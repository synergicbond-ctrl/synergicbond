3. Stereochemistry of hydride attack

In 4-tert-butylcyclohexanone the large tert-butyl group is equatorial and locks the ring. Hydride may approach the carbonyl carbon from the axial face or the equatorial face. Small reagents (NaBH4, LiAlH4) prefer axial attack: this avoids torsional strain with the adjacent axial-side C–H bonds and delivers the new H axial, so the OH ends up equatorial, giving mainly trans-4-tert-butylcyclohexanol. A bulky reagent such as L-Selectride (lithium tri-sec-butylborohydride) is blocked on the axial face by the syn-axial hydrogens at C-3 and C-5; it attacks along the equatorial trajectory, placing the OH axial and giving mainly the cis alcohol.

Reactant:

Reagent: NaBH4 or LiAlH4 (small H−, axial attack)

Product:

Major: trans (OH equatorial).

Reactant:

Reagent: L-Selectride, THF, −78 °C (bulky H−, equatorial attack)

Product:

Major: cis (OH axial).

Acyclic carbonyls next to a stereocentre follow the Felkin–Anh model. The three substituents on the α carbon are ranked large (L), medium (M) and small (S):

  1. The conformer is chosen with L perpendicular to the plane of C=O, so that it is anti to the incoming nucleophile.
  2. The nucleophile approaches at the Bürgi–Dunitz angle (about 107° to C=O) on the side of S, not M.
  3. The diastereomer formed this way is the Felkin product; it is normally the major one.
Key idea. Chelation control reverses this preference when the α (or β) carbon bears OR or NR2 and a chelating Lewis acid (Zn2+, Mg2+, e.g. Zn(BH4)2) bridges the carbonyl oxygen and the heteroatom. The ring-locked conformer is attacked from its less hindered face, often giving the anti-Felkin diastereomer.
