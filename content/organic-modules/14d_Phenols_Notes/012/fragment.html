13. Separation of phenols by acid–base extraction

Organic compounds that differ in acid–base character can be separated by shaking their ether solution with aqueous reagents of increasing strength. A compound that is converted into its salt moves into the aqueous layer, and is recovered by neutralising that layer. The order of reagents follows the order of acid strengths: carboxylic acid (pKa ≈ 4–5) is removed by NaHCO3; a phenol (≈ 10) needs NaOH; an amine is removed by dilute HCl; neutral compounds (hydrocarbons, ethers, aryl halides, nitro compounds) remain in the organic layer.

  1. Step 1: extract the ether solution with aqueous NaHCO3. Benzoic acid goes into water as sodium benzoate; phenol stays in ether (its pKa is above that of carbonic acid).
  2. Step 2: extract the remaining ether layer with aqueous NaOH. Phenol goes into water as sodium phenoxide.
  3. Step 3: extract the remaining ether layer with dilute HCl. A basic amine goes into water as ammonium chloride.
  4. Step 4: evaporate the ether to recover the neutral component.
  5. Step 5: acidify the benzoate and phenoxide extracts with strong acid (HCl) to regenerate benzoic acid and phenol; regenerate the amine from the ammonium extract by adding base.
Trap. Do not use NaOH first when a carboxylic acid and a phenol are present together: NaOH dissolves both and no separation is achieved. NaHCO3 is selective because pKa(H2CO3) ≈ 6.4 lies between those of carboxylic acids and phenols.

A second trick uses carbon dioxide. If both benzoic acid and phenol are dissolved in NaOH, bubbling CO2 through the solution releases only the phenol (a weaker acid than carbonic acid) while the benzoate remains in solution. Phenols with strongly electron-withdrawing groups (such as picric acid, 2,4-dinitrophenol) are stronger acids and may extract into NaHCO3 like carboxylic acids.

A phenol can also be separated from an alcohol, because the alcohol does not form a salt with NaOH, and from the nitrophenol isomers by steam distillation: only o-nitrophenol is steam volatile because of its intramolecular hydrogen bond, while p-nitrophenol is associated by intermolecular hydrogen bonds and stays in the flask.
