50. Mechanism: electrophilic substitution on aniline — bromination, nitration, sulphonation

  • Bromination (Br2/H2O). Step 1: the π electrons (ring HOMO, high because of N donation) polarise Br–Br: attack through the para carbon gives the σ-complex (stabilised by the N lone pair) and Br−. Step 2: loss of H+ gives p-bromoaniline. Step 3: the product is still strongly activated, so the process repeats at the two ortho positions, giving 2,4,6-tribromoaniline. No Lewis acid is needed: the ring is nucleophilic enough to attack Br2 directly, and water helps to polarise it.
  • Nitration with NO2+. Step 1: HNO3 + H2SO4 → H2NO3+ + HSO4−; loss of H2O gives NO2+ (linear, LUMO π* at N). Step 2: the ring π HOMO attacks N → σ-complex; step 3: loss of H+. Why the anilinium ion gives meta: in –NH3+ the N has no lone pair and acts as a σ-acceptor; in the arenium ion from para or ortho attack the positive charge would sit on the carbon adjacent to N+: destabilising. Meta attack avoids this.
  • Sulphonation (baking). Aniline + H2SO4 → anilinium hydrogen sulphate. On heating, some free aniline reacts with SO3/H2SO4 at the para position (the sulphonation is reversible, so the sterically and electronically favoured para isomer accumulates): p-aminobenzenesulphonic acid, which exists as the zwitterion.
