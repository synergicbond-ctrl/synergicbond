16. Nucleophilic aromatic substitution (SNAr)

Aryl halides do not undergo SN1 (aryl cation is very unstable, sp2 C–X bond is strong and partly double-bonded by resonance) or SN2 (backside attack is impossible, the ring blocks it). Chlorobenzene needs 623 K and 300 atm with NaOH for hydrolysis (the Dow process). But if the ring carries strong electron-withdrawing groups ortho and/or para to the halogen, substitution proceeds in solution at modest temperature by the addition–elimination (SNAr) mechanism.

16.1 Mechanism and the Meisenheimer complex

  • 1. The nucleophile adds to the ipso carbon (the one bearing the leaving group) to give a cyclohexadienyl anion (Meisenheimer complex): the ring is temporarily non-aromatic and the negative charge is delocalised over the ortho and para carbons (relative to the ipso carbon).
  • 2. If an electron-withdrawing group (–NO2) is ortho or para, the charge is delocalised onto it (nitronate form), which stabilises the intermediate strongly.
  • 3. The leaving group (X−) is expelled, restoring aromaticity.

16.2 The leaving-group order is "upside down": F ≫ Cl ≈ Br > I

In SN2 the order of leaving groups is I > Br > Cl > F. In SNAr it is reversed (F ≫ Cl ≈ Br > I): the rate-determining step is nucleophilic addition, not loss of X−. A highly electronegative F makes the ipso carbon more electrophilic (strongest –I effect) and stabilises the developing negative charge in the Meisenheimer complex. C–X bond cleavage occurs after the slow step, so C–F bond strength is irrelevant.

Example 36 Main

Rank by rate of reaction with CH3O− in CH3OH

(a) 1-fluoro-4-nitrobenzene, 1-chloro-4-nitrobenzene, 1-iodo-4-nitrobenzene, chlorobenzene. (b) 1-chloro-2,4-dinitrobenzene, 1-chloro-4-nitrobenzene, 1-chloro-3-nitrobenzene, 4-chlorotoluene.

Show solution
  1. (a) 1-Fluoro-4-nitrobenzene > 1-chloro-4-nitrobenzene ≈ 1-bromo-4-nitrobenzene > 1-iodo-4-nitrobenzene ≫ chlorobenzene (the last does not react under these conditions).
  2. (b) 2,4-Dinitro > 4-nitro (para) ≫ 3-nitro > 4-methyl (the CH3 is a donor and slows the reaction; the m-nitro group activates only inductively, and so is much less effective than para-nitro).
Example 37 Adv

Substituent effects

Why does 1-chloro-2,4-dinitrobenzene react with water at 368 K whereas 1-chloro-2,4-dimethoxybenzene is inert? Why is 4-chloronitrobenzene attacked by CH3O− but not by CH3OH?

Show solution
  1. Two NO2 groups ortho and para stabilise the Meisenheimer anion (negative charge on both nitro groups); OCH3 groups donate electrons and destabilise it: no addition.
  2. Methanol is a neutral, poor nucleophile; the addition step needs an anionic or strongly nucleophilic species (CH3O−) to attack the electron-poor ring carbon.
Example 38 Adv

Mixed example

Predict the product and say how many NO2 groups are needed: (a) ClC6H4NO2(p) + NaOCH3 (b) 2,4,6-trinitroanisole + NH3 (c) 4-fluoronitrobenzene + piperidine (d) 1-chloro-2,4-dinitrobenzene + CH3CH2SH/base.

Show solution
  1. (a) 4-Nitroanisole (p-methoxynitrobenzene): the leaving group is Cl−.
  2. (b) 2,4,6-Trinitroanisole: the OCH3 is a leaving group (it is attached to an electron-poor ring), giving picramide (2,4,6-trinitroaniline) + CH3OH.
  3. (c) Fluoride is displaced by the amine nitrogen: 1-(4-nitrophenyl)piperidine.
  4. (d) 2,4-Dinitrophenyl ethyl sulphide.

16.3 Other nucleophilic substitutions on rings

  • Nitro group as leaving group: 1,2,4-trinitrobenzene reacts at the NO2 ortho to the other two.
  • Hydride as leaving group (Chichibabin on pyridine): the σ-adduct of NH2− with pyridine loses H− (as H2 with NH2−); a rare case in which H is the leaving group.
  • SN1 (aryl cation): diazonium ions. ArN2+ ⟶ Ar+ + N2 (N2 is an excellent leaving group): hydrolysis of ArN2+ to phenol, Schiemann. The aryl cation has an empty sp2 orbital in the plane and is not resonance stabilised, so it needs the best leaving group (N2).
  • Copper-mediated couplings: Ullmann reaction: 2 ArI + Cu, 473–523 K → Ar–Ar (works best with ortho-nitro aryl halides); Fittig, Wurtz–Fittig reaction: ArX + RX + 2 Na.
