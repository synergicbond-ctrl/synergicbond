Part B. Carbonyl condensation reactions

A carbonyl compound has two reactive faces: the electrophilic carbon of C=O and the nucleophilic α-carbon (through the enol or enolate, which exist because the α-C–H is acidic: pKa of an aldehyde or ketone ≈ 17–20, an ester ≈ 25, a nitrile ≈ 25, a β-dicarbonyl ≈ 9–13, nitroalkane ≈ 10). Almost all the named reactions of this Part are one electrophile plus one nucleophile chosen from this list, followed by (or instead of) a dehydration, a decarboxylation or an elimination of alkoxide. Learn the three decisions for every such reaction: which partner is the enolate, which partner is the electrophile, and what happens to the tetrahedral/aldol adduct next.
