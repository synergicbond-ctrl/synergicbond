21.3 Other methods

  • Alkoxymercuration–demercuration: alkene + ROH + Hg(OAc)2, then NaBH4: Markovnikov ether, no rearrangement, suitable for tert-alkyl and hindered ethers. 3,3-Dimethylbut-1-ene + CH3OH → 2-methoxy-3,3-dimethylbutane.
  • Acid-catalysed addition of an alcohol to an alkene (via carbocation): isobutene + CH3OH (sulphonic-acid resin) → MTBE (an octane booster).
  • From alkyl halide and dry silver oxide: 2RX + Ag2O → R2O + 2AgX.
  • Dimethyl sulphate / methyl iodide with phenoxides or alkoxides (Williamson with a methylating agent); diazomethane (CH2N2) methylates phenols and carboxylic acids.
  • Epoxide + alkoxide: gives β-hydroxy ethers (cellosolves: 2-ethoxyethanol).
  • Ullmann synthesis of diaryl ethers: aryl halide + phenoxide with copper catalyst, high temperature.

Example 57 L2 Main

Williamson design

Write the best Williamson route to (a) 1-ethoxy-2-methylpropane; (b) cyclohexyl methyl ether; (c) 2-methoxy-2-methylbutane; (d) 4-methoxytoluene.

Show solution
  1. (a) Both groups are primary, but the isobutyl group is branched at the β-carbon, which slows SN2 at isobutyl halide. Put the branched group on the alkoxide: (CH3)2CHCH2O−Na+ + CH3CH2Br.
  2. (b) Cyclohexyl is secondary: cyclohexoxide + CH3I.
  3. (c) The carbon is tertiary: sodium 2-methylbutan-2-oxide + CH3I. (A tertiary halide would only eliminate.)
  4. (d) The aryl group must be on the oxygen side: sodium 4-methylphenoxide + CH3I (or dimethyl sulphate).
Example 58 L2 Main

Why does it fail?

Explain why each attempt fails: (i) NaOC2H5 + (CH3)3CBr to make ethyl tert-butyl ether; (ii) NaOCH3 + C6H5Cl to make anisole at 300 K.

Show solution
  1. (i) The tertiary halide cannot be attacked from the back; ethoxide, a strong base, abstracts a β-H (E2): the products are isobutene + ethanol.
  2. (ii) Chlorobenzene’s carbon is sp2, the C–Cl bond has partial double-bond character, and the ring blocks backside attack; unactivated aryl halides do not do SN2 or SN1. Only with strongly activating o/p-NO2 groups (SNAr) or very strong base (benzyne) does substitution occur.
