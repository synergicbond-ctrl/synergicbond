Important Crystal Structures and Their Stoichiometry

10-part continuous textbook chapter

7.1 A method for analysing any structure

For every named crystal structure, answer the same questions in order: Which species forms the host lattice? Which holes are occupied? What fraction of those holes is occupied? What is the formula? What are the coordination numbers? How many formula units occur in the conventional cell? What nearest-neighbour geometry connects ionic radii to cell dimensions?

  • Step 1: identify the Bravais lattice and basis, not just the visual positions.
  • Step 2: count each species using boundary fractions.
  • Step 3: determine coordination by actual nearest neighbours.
  • Step 4: derive the nearest-neighbour distance from fractional coordinates.
  • Step 5: check that the counted composition matches charge neutrality.
Structure type versus chemical identity

Many compounds adopt the same structure type. ‘NaCl type’ describes topology and coordination, not the requirement that Na and Cl be present.

7.3 CsCl structure

One ion occupies the corners of a primitive cubic cell and the other occupies the cubic hole at the body centre. The two chemical sites form a basis on a primitive cubic Bravais lattice.

Bravais descriptionPrimitive cubic with a two-ion basis
FormulaMX

Why it is not BCC

A BCC Bravais lattice requires corner and body-centre lattice points to be equivalent. In CsCl they carry different ions, so the Bravais lattice is primitive cubic.

7.4 Zinc blende and wurtzite

Both ZnS polymorphs have tetrahedral 4:4 coordination and half of the tetrahedral holes occupied. They differ in anion stacking: zinc blende uses CCP (ABC), while wurtzite uses HCP (AB).

In zinc blende, the occupied tetrahedral sites may be represented by coordinates such as (1/4,1/4,1/4)(1/4,1/4,1/4)(1/4,1/4,1/4) relative to the FCC anion cell. Only four of the eight tetrahedral holes are occupied.

Worked example

Hole fraction

Anions form CCP and the compound has formula MX with cations only in tetrahedral holes. What fraction of tetrahedral holes is occupied?

  1. For NNN anions, there are 2N2N2N tetrahedral holes.
  2. Formula MX requires NNN cations.
  3. Occupied fraction =N/(2N)=1/2=N/(2N)=1/2=N/(2N)=1/2.
One-half of the tetrahedral holes is occupied.

Worked example

Coordination accounting

In CaF2_22​, how many nearest Ca2+^{2+}2+ neighbours surround each F−^-−, and how many F−^-− surround each Ca2+^{2+}2+?

  1. Fluoride occupies a tetrahedral site in the Ca FCC array, so it has 4 Ca neighbours.
  2. Each Ca is surrounded by the eight tetrahedral-site fluorides at cube-corner directions.

7.6 NiAs and rutile structures

In NiAs, As atoms form an HCP array and Ni atoms occupy all octahedral holes. Each Ni is octahedrally surrounded by six As, while each As is surrounded by six Ni in a trigonal-prismatic arrangement.

StructurePacking/site modelCoordinationFormula
NiAsHCP As; all octahedral holes filled by NiNi 6 (octahedral), As 6 (trigonal prismatic)MX
Rutile TiO2_22​Distorted chains of edge-sharing TiO6_66​ octahedraTi 6, O 3MO2_22​

Rutile is tetragonal. Its TiO6_66​ octahedra are distorted and share edges along one direction and corners between chains. The structure cannot be understood completely as a simple ideal close-packed-hole occupancy.

Same CN does not mean same polyhedron

Both ions in NiAs have coordination number 6, but Ni sees an octahedron whereas As sees a trigonal prism.

7.7 Perovskite structure

In the ideal cubic perovskite ABO3_33​, A occupies cube corners, B the body centre, and O the six face centres. Counting gives one A, one B and three O per cell. B is octahedrally coordinated by oxygen; A occupies a 12-coordinate cuboctahedral cavity.

Worked example

Perovskite counting

Count the particles in the ideal cubic ABO3_33​ cell.

  1. A at 8 corners: 8(1/8)=18(1/8)=18(1/8)=1.
  2. B at body centre: 1.
  3. O at 6 face centres: 6(1/2)=36(1/2)=36(1/2)=3.
The cell contains one ABO3_33​ formula unit.

7.8 Spinel and inverse spinel

In a spinel AB2_22​O4_44​, oxide ions form an approximately CCP array. Per 32 O2−^{2-}2− conventional spinel cell there are 64 tetrahedral and 32 octahedral holes; only 8 tetrahedral and 16 octahedral sites are occupied by cations.

Occupancy fractions

Relative to all holes in the oxygen CCP array, a spinel occupies 1/81/81/8 of tetrahedral holes and 1/21/21/2 of octahedral holes.

7.9 Corundum, diamond and graphite

StructureEssential descriptionCoordination
Corundum, Al2_22​O3_33​O approximately HCP; Al occupies 2/32/32/3 of octahedral holesAl 6; O 4
DiamondFCC lattice with (0,0,0)(0,0,0)(0,0,0) and (1/4,1/4,1/4)(1/4,1/4,1/4)(1/4,1/4,1/4) basis; 3D sp3sp^3sp3 networkC 4
GraphiteHexagonal sheets of fused sp2sp^2sp2 carbon rings; weak interlayer forcesC 3 within a layer

Graphite's strong in-plane covalent bonds and weak interlayer interactions explain its anisotropic conductivity, easy cleavage and lubricating behaviour. Diamond's three-dimensional network explains hardness and high thermal conductivity.

Coordination versus bond count

In graphite, each carbon has three nearest covalent neighbours, while the delocalised π\piπ system extends over the layer. Coordination number 3 does not imply three localised single bonds only.

7.11 Wigner–Seitz cell and truncated-octahedron geometry

A Wigner–Seitz cell is the region closer to one lattice point than to any other. It is constructed by drawing perpendicular bisector planes to vectors joining a chosen lattice point to its neighbours. For the BCC lattice, the Wigner–Seitz cell is a truncated octahedron.

Truncated-octahedron featureValue
Total faces14
Regular hexagonal faces8
Square faces6
Edges36
Vertices24
Why it appears in crystallography

The Wigner–Seitz cell is always primitive: it contains one lattice point. Its shape need not resemble the conventional cubic cell.

1

Analyse structures by host lattice, hole occupancy, stoichiometry, coordination, ZZZ and nearest-neighbour geometry.

2

NaCl is 6:6 with all octahedral holes occupied; CsCl is 8:8 on a primitive cubic lattice with basis.

3

Zinc blende and wurtzite both have tetrahedral 4:4 coordination but different stacking.

4

Fluorite and antifluorite reverse which species forms the FCC host.

5

Perovskite, spinel and corundum are best understood through coordination polyhedra and site occupancy.
