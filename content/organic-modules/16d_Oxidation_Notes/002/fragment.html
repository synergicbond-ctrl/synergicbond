3. Other alcohol oxidants: MnO2, Oppenauer, Swern, Dess–Martin and TEMPO

Several non-chromium methods are chosen for selectivity. Activated manganese dioxide (MnO2, suspended in CH2Cl2 or hexane) oxidises allylic and benzylic alcohols to aldehydes or ketones and leaves ordinary saturated alcohols untouched, because the reaction depends on adsorption on the oxide and on the weak, activated C–H bond next to the π system. Thus HOCH2CH=CH(CH2)2OH is oxidised only at the allylic hydroxyl.

Reactant:

Reagent: Activated MnO2, CH2Cl2, room temperature

Product:

Only the allylic primary alcohol reacts; the homoallylic OH is untouched.

The Oppenauer oxidation is the reverse of the Meerwein–Ponndorf–Verley reduction. A secondary alcohol is heated with aluminium isopropoxide, Al(OiPr)3, in a large excess of acetone; hydride transfers through a six-membered cyclic transition state from the alcohol carbon to the carbonyl carbon of acetone, and the equilibrium is driven by the excess acetone. The conditions are basic and non-oxidising towards C=C, so it is useful for acid-sensitive and allylic secondary alcohols. The method is used mainly for secondary alcohols, because an aldehyde formed under the basic conditions tends to undergo aldol condensation.

In the Swern oxidation DMSO is activated by oxalyl chloride at −78 °C to give the chlorodimethylsulfonium salt (with release of CO and CO2); the alcohol displaces chloride to give an alkoxysulfonium salt, and triethylamine then forms a sulfonium ylide that removes the carbinol hydrogen through a five-membered cyclic transition state, delivering the carbonyl compound and dimethyl sulfide. Dess–Martin periodinane (a hypervalent iodine(V) reagent) oxidises alcohols to aldehydes and ketones at room temperature under neutral conditions. TEMPO, used catalytically with a terminal oxidant such as NaOCl, generates an oxoammonium ion that oxidises primary alcohols in preference to secondary ones because of steric demand; the product is the aldehyde in a buffered two-phase system.

  1. DMSO + (COCl)2 → Me2S+–Cl Cl− + CO + CO2 (−78 °C).
  2. R2CHOH + Me2S+Cl → R2CH–O–S+Me2 + HCl.
  3. Et3N removes the S-methyl proton giving the ylide; intramolecular proton transfer from the carbinol C–H through a five-membered cyclic transition state gives R2C=O + Me2S.
Key idea. Swern, Dess–Martin, PCC and Collins reagent all stop primary alcohols at the aldehyde. None of them produces a carboxylic acid, because none operates through an aqueous hydrate.
Trap. In the Oppenauer oxidation acetone is the oxidant and is used in large excess; in the MPV reduction the same Al(OiPr)3 is used with excess propan-2-ol as hydride donor. The direction is controlled only by which carbonyl or alcohol is in excess.
