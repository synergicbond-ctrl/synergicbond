16. Amino resins

16.1 Melamine–formaldehyde

Melamine (2,4,6-triamino-1,3,5-triazine) has six N–H positions; formaldehyde adds to give N-hydroxymethyl groups –NH–CH2OH, which condense with other N–H groups, releasing water and forming methylene bridges (–NH–CH2–NH–) and ether bridges. A tightly cross-linked network results. Hard, heat-, scratch- and stain-resistant: unbreakable crockery (Melmac), laminates (Formica), fire-resistant coatings.

Step 1: the amine nitrogen (HOMO: lone pair, weakly nucleophilic because it is conjugated into the electron-poor triazine) adds to the HCHO carbonyl.

Two triazine rings joined by a –NH–CH₂–NH– bridge; repeating this on all six N–H positions gives the network.

16.2 Urea–formaldehyde

Urea (NH2)2C=O + HCHO → mono-, di-, tri-methylolureas → –NH–CH2–NH– bridges. Cheaper than melamine resin; used as adhesive for plywood and particle board, electrical fittings, buttons, and as a moulding powder. Moisture and heat slowly release formaldehyde.

+ 1 N-methylolation⟶ 2 + urea, –H₂O⟶
Methylene-diurea: the first cross-link unit.
