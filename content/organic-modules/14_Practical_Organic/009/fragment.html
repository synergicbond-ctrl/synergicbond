3.1 Simple distillation

A liquid is heated to its boiling point, the vapour is condensed in a water-cooled condenser and collected. Used when the liquid is non-volatile impurity-contaminated or when the mixture consists of two liquids whose boiling points differ by a large amount (a rule of thumb quoted in textbooks is more than about 40 K). Examples: chloroform (b.p. 334 K) with aniline (b.p. 457 K); ether (308 K) with toluene (384 K); hexane with a tar.
