45. Mechanism: nucleophilic aromatic substitution (SNAr) on nitro-activated rings

The addition–elimination pathway for 1-chloro-2,4-dinitrobenzene + OH−.

  • Step 1 – addition (rate determining). The HOMO of OH− attacks the ring carbon bearing Cl, i.e. the LUMO of the ring, the lowest π* orbital. The nitro groups lower the energy of this LUMO (they are π-acceptors) and have large coefficients at the ortho and para carbons. The result is the Meisenheimer complex, an anion in which the ring carbon is sp3.
  • Step 2 – delocalisation. The negative charge is spread over the ortho and para positions and onto the O atoms of the o-/p-nitro groups (resonance). Why meta fails: a meta nitro group cannot accept charge by resonance from the attacked carbon; only −I operates.
  • Step 3 – elimination. Cl− leaves, the ring re-aromatises: 2,4-dinitrophenol (in base the phenoxide).

Why F is the best leaving group in SNAr: the first step (addition) is rate determining; the very electronegative F polarises C–F strongly, making the ipso carbon more electrophilic and stabilising the Meisenheimer complex; breaking C–F occurs after the slow step and does not matter for the rate. This is opposite to SN2. Why aryl halides do not undergo SN2: backside attack on an sp2 carbon is blocked by the ring; and the C–X σ* orbital lies in the plane of the ring where the nucleophile cannot approach along the axis.
