11. Epoxide ring opening: acidic versus basic conditions

Ring opening is nucleophilic substitution at carbon with the epoxide oxygen as the leaving group, and the regiochemistry depends on the conditions. The stereochemical outcome is always inversion at the carbon attacked, so a cyclic epoxide gives a trans (anti) product.

  • Basic or strongly nucleophilic conditions (RO−, HO−, RMgX, LiAlH4, CN−, NH3, amines): a pure SN2 attack on the less substituted carbon. The alkoxide that results is protonated in a later step. The strain of the ring supplies the driving force.
  • Acidic conditions (H3O+, ROH/H+, HX): the oxygen is protonated first. The C–O bond to the more substituted carbon is longer and weaker and carries more positive charge, so the weak nucleophile attacks the more substituted carbon from the back side. The mechanism is a borderline SN2 with SN1 character; for a tertiary carbon it is close to SN1, but inversion still dominates.

Reactant:

Reagent: CH3OH, cat. H2SO4

Product:

Acid: methanol bonds to the tertiary carbon, giving 2-methoxy-2-methylpropan-1-ol.

Reactant:

Reagent: CH3ONa, CH3OH

Product:

Base: methoxide bonds to the CH2 carbon, giving 1-methoxy-2-methylpropan-2-ol.

Reactant:

Reagent: H3O+

Product:

trans-Cyclohexane-1,2-diol, formed as the racemic pair (1R,2R)/(1S,2S). The meso cis diol is not formed.

Trap. With a 1,2-disubstituted symmetrical epoxide (cyclohexene oxide) the two carbons are equally likely to be attacked, so both enantiomers of the trans product form and the product is racemic. The mixture is optically inactive.

A useful isotope-labelling consequence: in the acid-catalysed hydrolysis of an unsymmetrical epoxide with H218O the label ends up on the more substituted carbon, whereas with H18O− it ends up on the less substituted carbon. The other oxygen of the diol is always the original epoxide oxygen.
