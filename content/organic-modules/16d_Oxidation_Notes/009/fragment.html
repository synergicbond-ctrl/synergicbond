10. Oxidative cleavage of 1,2-diols: periodic acid and lead tetraacetate

Vicinal diols are cleaved at the C–C bond between the two hydroxyl-bearing carbons by periodic acid (HIO4, or NaIO4) in water or aqueous organic solvents (Malaprade reaction), or by lead tetraacetate Pb(OAc)4 in non-aqueous solvents such as benzene or acetic acid (Criegee reaction). Each carbinol carbon becomes a carbonyl carbon: a carbon with two substituents gives a ketone, a carbon bearing one hydrogen gives an aldehyde, and a CH2OH group gives methanal.

  1. The diol displaces two OH ligands on iodine to give a cyclic periodate ester (five-membered ring, I(VII)).
  2. The ester collapses in a concerted fragmentation: the C–C bond between the carbinol carbons breaks, two C=O bonds form and iodine is reduced to iodate, I(VII) → I(V).
  3. Each C–C bond cleaved consumes exactly one equivalent of HIO4 (2 e−); with Pb(OAc)4 Pb(IV) → Pb(II).

Reactant:

Reagent: HIO4 (aq.)

Product:

Pinacol is cleaved to two molecules of propanone.

Reactant:

Reagent: NaIO4, H2O

Product:

The ring is opened: hexanedial.

The cyclic ester requires the two oxygens to be close together, so a cis-diol on a ring is cleaved faster than the corresponding trans-diol, and a trans-diol whose hydroxyl groups cannot come close together may react very slowly or not at all; 1,3-diols and simple alcohols are not cleaved. The reagent also cleaves compounds in which a hydroxyl is next to a carbonyl: α-hydroxy aldehydes and α-hydroxy ketones are cleaved too. A middle CHOH carbon of a triol therefore ends at the carboxylic acid level, because after the first cleavage it becomes an α-hydroxy aldehyde.

Stoichiometry follows the number of C–C cleavages. Ethane-1,2-diol uses 1 equivalent and gives 2 HCHO. Glycerol, HOCH2CH(OH)CH2OH, has two vicinal cleavage points and consumes 2 equivalents to give 2 HCHO + HCOOH, the middle carbon ending at the acid level because it is oxidised on the second cleavage. Erythritol, HOCH2(CHOH)2CH2OH, consumes 3 equivalents (2 HCHO + 2 HCOOH). In general a chain of n adjacent CHOH or CH2OH carbons consumes n − 1 equivalents.

Reactant:

Reagent: Excess HIO4

Product:

Glycerol: 2 equivalents of HIO4; products 2 HCHO + HCOOH.

Key idea. OsO4 (cat.) with NaIO4 (Lemieux–Johnson) combines syn-dihydroxylation and glycol cleavage in one pot: it cleaves an alkene to the same carbonyl compounds as ozonolysis with reductive work-up.
Trap. The count of HIO4 equivalents equals the number of C–C bonds cleaved, not the number of OH groups. Propane-1,3-diol consumes none.
