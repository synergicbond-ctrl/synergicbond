56. Beckmann rearrangement

Reagents: a ketoxime R2C=N–OH + acid (conc. H2SO4, PCl5, SOCl2, polyphosphoric acid, TsCl/base). Product: an N-substituted amide (lactam from a cyclic ketone's oxime). Mechanism: the oxygen of the oxime is made a good leaving group (protonation or O-sulfonylation); the group anti to the leaving group migrates from C to N as the leaving group departs (concerted, with retention in the migrating group), giving a nitrilium ion R–C≡N+–R′, which captures water to give the imidic acid, tautomerising to the amide. The anti relationship is the stereochemical requirement: the migrating group is the one *trans* (anti) to the OH.

H₂SO₄ or PCl₅⟶
(E)-Acetophenone oxime (OH anti to phenyl) → phenyl migrates → N-phenylethanamide (acetanilide)
H₂SO₄⟶
(Z)-Acetophenone oxime (OH anti to methyl) → CH3 migrates → N-methylbenzamide
H₂SO₄ (oleum), 390 K⟶
Cyclohexanone oxime → ε-caprolactam (monomer of nylon-6): ring expansion by N insertion
(CH₃CO)₂O, Δ⟶
Aldoximes with an H anti to OH dehydrate to nitriles (the "abnormal" outcome)
Trap Oxime geometry is usually mixed (E/Z equilibrate under acid), so JEE tells you the geometry explicitly. If the oxime of an unsymmetrical ketone is not specified, state that the group anti to OH migrates. Cyclic ketoximes give lactams with ring size n + 1 (cyclopentanone → 6-membered δ-valerolactam; cyclohexanone → 7-membered ε-caprolactam; cyclododecanone → laurolactam, nylon-12).
Example 51 Main

Beckmann products

Give the lactam from (a) cyclopentanone oxime, (b) 2-methylcyclohexanone oxime where the OH is anti to the CH(CH3) carbon.

Show solution
  1. (a) Ring expands by one atom: 2-piperidone (δ-valerolactam, 6-membered).
  2. (b) The C-2 (CH–CH3) carbon anti to OH migrates to N: N is inserted between C-1 and C-2: 7-methylazepan-2-one (7-methyl caprolactam, the methyl on the carbon attached to N).
Example 52 Adv

Beckmann vs Schmidt vs Hofmann

Which of the following give the same amide from the same ketone: (i) Beckmann of the oxime, (ii) Schmidt with HN3/H2SO4, (iii) Baeyer–Villiger? Give the product from cyclohexanone in each.

Show solution
  1. (i) Beckmann: caprolactam (ε-lactam, 7-ring with N). (ii) Schmidt: caprolactam (same, since the same nitrilium ion forms). (iii) Baeyer–Villiger: ε-caprolactone (7-ring with O). So (i) and (ii) coincide; (iii) is the oxygen analogue.
