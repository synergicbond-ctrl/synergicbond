72. Sources cross-checked and verification notes

Books and problem collections actually opened while preparing this chapter (used to find missing topics, standard facts and question types; all explanations and problems are written in my own words):

  1. L. G. Wade, Organic Chemistry, 9th ed., Chapter 19 (Amines): nomenclature (pp. 943–944), basicity and hybridisation, including the pKb values of pyridine, piperidine and acetonitrile (p. 952), amine salts (p. 952), exhaustive methylation and Hofmann elimination including the Hofmann rule and the anti-periplanar requirement (pp. 967–969).
  2. J. Clayden, N. Greeves and S. Warren, Organic Chemistry, 2nd ed. (2012): the comparison of nucleophilicity of triethylamine, quinuclidine and DABCO toward methyl iodide (pp. 818–819).
  3. M. S. Chouhan, Advanced Problems in Organic Chemistry for JEE, 16th ed.: the diazonium-coupling and amine-test questions on pp. 543–546 and 555–558 and the differentiation questions on p. 586 were used only to see which question types are asked (order of reactivity of substituted diazonium ions, triazene/aminoazo chemistry, butter yellow, aniline vs cyclohexylamine tests). No problem text was copied.

Verification performed by computer.

  • Every structure drawn in the schemes and answer boxes was parsed by RDKit (invalid SMILES stop the build).
  • Isomer counts (C3H9N: 4; C4H11N: 8 with classes 4/3/1; C5H13N: 17 with classes 8/6/3 and 4 chiral isomers) were obtained by exhaustive enumeration.
  • Molecular formulae and molar masses quoted for named compounds (e.g. Sudan I C16H12N2O 248.3; methyl orange C14H14N3NaO3S 327.3) were computed with RDKit; numerical answers (pH, Kjeldahl, Dumas, chloroplatinate, yields) were recomputed in Python (see the verification script).

What was not independently verified. The pKa/pKb values of substituted anilines, heterocycles and the other tabulated constants are standard literature values quoted from memory and from Wade where indicated; their exact third digits may differ between sources, and the exam-relevant orders are what the notes rely on. Colour and observation details of classical tests (for example the shade of nitrolic acid and pseudonitrole colours, aniline–bleaching-powder violet) follow the usual textbook statements and were not re-observed. Reaction conditions (temperatures and pH windows) are the standard textbook ones.
