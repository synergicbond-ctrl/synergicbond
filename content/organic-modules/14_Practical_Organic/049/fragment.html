14. Quantitative analysis: from percentage composition to formula

Qualitative analysis says *which* elements are present; quantitative analysis says *how much*. Every method converts the element to a simple, weighable or measurable substance, quantitatively and without loss: carbon → CO2, hydrogen → H2O, nitrogen → N2 (volume) or NH3 (titration), halogen → AgX, sulphur → BaSO4, phosphorus → Mg2P2O7. The percentage of an element then follows from a gravimetric factor:

Formula % of element = (mass of element in one mole of the weighed compound / molar mass of the weighed compound) × (mass of weighed compound / mass of organic sample) × 100

All the "formulas" of this chapter are this single sentence with numbers filled in. Keep the structure: element mass in the product ÷ product mass × product obtained ÷ sample × 100.

Validity Check Each gravimetric formula assumes quantitative conversion of the element to the weighed form, no loss, and a pure precipitate; a compound with two halogens or with S + halogen needs extra relations (see the worked illustrations).
