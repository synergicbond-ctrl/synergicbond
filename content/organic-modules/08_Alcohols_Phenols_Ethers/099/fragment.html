29.1 Alcohols

Practice 328 L1 ALC

Classify each alcohol as primary, secondary or tertiary: (a) 2-methylpropan-1-ol; (b) 2-methylbutan-2-ol; (c) cyclohexanol; (d) 3-methylbutan-2-ol; (e) benzyl alcohol.

Show answer
  1. Count the carbon groups on the carbinol carbon.
  2. (a) CH2OH attached to one C: primary. (b) the carbinol carbon bears three carbons: tertiary. (c) ring carbon with two ring carbons: secondary. (d) CH(OH) with two carbon groups: secondary. (e) C6H5CH2OH: primary (benzylic).
Practice 329 L1 ALC

Write the IUPAC name of (CH3)2CHCH(OH)CH3.

Show answer
  1. Longest chain containing the carbinol carbon: four carbons, OH on C-2 (lowest locant), methyl on C-3.
  2. 3-Methylbutan-2-ol.
Practice 330 L1 ALC

Write the IUPAC name of HOCH2CH(OH)CH3.

Show answer
  1. Two OH on adjacent carbons of a three-carbon chain: propane-1,2-diol (keep the “e” before “diol”).
Practice 331 L1 ALC

Name CH3CH(OH)CH2CHO.

Show answer
  1. The aldehyde outranks the alcohol, so numbering starts at CHO and OH becomes a prefix: 3-hydroxybutanal.
Practice 332 L1 ALC

Why is the boiling point of ethanol (78 °C) so much higher than that of dimethyl ether (−24 °C) although both have M = 46?

Show answer
  1. Ethanol molecules hydrogen-bond to each other through O–H···O; breaking these bonds on vaporisation needs energy.
  2. Dimethyl ether has no O–H, so only weak dipole–dipole and dispersion forces act.
Practice 333 L1 ALC

Arrange in increasing boiling point: butane, butan-1-ol, propan-2-ol.

Show answer
  1. Butane (M 58) has only dispersion forces: −1 °C. Propan-2-ol (M 60) H-bonds: 82 °C. Butan-1-ol (M 74) H-bonds and has a larger chain: 118 °C.
  2. Butane < propan-2-ol < butan-1-ol.
Practice 334 L1 ALC

Which has the higher boiling point, butan-1-ol or 2-methylpropan-2-ol? Give the reason.

Show answer
  1. Butan-1-ol (118 °C vs 82 °C). The straight chain has a larger surface area and can pack and H-bond better; the compact tertiary alcohol has weaker intermolecular attractions.
Practice 335 L1 ALC

Why is methanol miscible with water but hexan-1-ol only slightly soluble?

Show answer
  1. Both form H-bonds with water through OH. Methanol’s one-carbon hydrocarbon part is small, so H-bonding dominates. The six-carbon chain of hexanol is hydrophobic and outweighs the gain from one OH.
Practice 336 L1 ALC

What products are formed in the acid-catalysed hydration of propene? Which is major and why?

Show answer
  1. Markovnikov addition through the more stable secondary carbocation: propan-2-ol (major). Propan-1-ol is formed only in trace amounts (primary cation unfavourable).
Practice 337 L1 ALC

Give the reagent for converting propene into propan-1-ol.

Show answer
  1. Hydroboration–oxidation: (i) BH3·THF; (ii) H2O2/NaOH (anti-Markovnikov, syn addition).
Practice 338 L1 ALC

Which reagent converts an alkene to a Markovnikov alcohol without rearrangement?

Show answer
  1. Hg(OAc)2/H2O (oxymercuration), then NaBH4 (demercuration). A mercurinium ion forms instead of a free carbocation.
Practice 339 L1 ALC

Give the product of NaBH4 reduction of (a) butanal, (b) butanone, (c) ethyl butanoate.

Show answer
  1. (a) Butan-1-ol. (b) Butan-2-ol. (c) No reaction (NaBH4 does not reduce esters under normal conditions).
Practice 340 L1 ALC

Give the product of LiAlH4 reduction followed by H3O+ of (a) ethanoic acid, (b) ethyl ethanoate.

Show answer
  1. (a) Ethanol. (b) Ethanol (two molecules): the acyl part gives ethanol and the alkoxy part is released as ethanol.
Practice 341 L1 ALC

Which Grignard reagent and carbonyl compound give propan-2-ol?

Show answer
  1. The carbinol carbon of propan-2-ol carries two CH3 groups and H. So CH3MgBr + CH3CHO (ethanal), then H3O+. (Alternatively CH3MgBr + HCOOCH3, 2 equivalents.)
Practice 342 L1 ALC

What product results when CH3MgBr reacts with HCHO followed by H3O+?

Show answer
  1. Methanal gives a primary alcohol with one more carbon: ethanol.
Practice 343 L1 ALC

What product is formed from C2H5MgBr + ethylene oxide, then H3O+?

Show answer
  1. The Grignard attacks the CH2 of the epoxide, adding two carbons: butan-1-ol.
Practice 344 L1 ALC

Name the product of CH3MgBr (2 equivalents) with ethyl ethanoate after acid work-up.

Show answer
  1. The ester gives a ketone (propanone) which reacts again: 2-methylpropan-2-ol (tertiary alcohol).
Practice 345 L1 ALC

Arrange in decreasing acidity: water, methanol, ethanol, propan-2-ol, 2-methylpropan-2-ol.

Show answer
  1. Alkyl groups (+I and hindered solvation) destabilise the alkoxide: water > methanol > ethanol > propan-2-ol > 2-methylpropan-2-ol.
Practice 346 L1 ALC

Arrange in decreasing acidity: ethanol, 2-chloroethanol, 2,2,2-trichloroethanol.

Show answer
  1. Chlorine withdraws electrons (−I) and stabilises RO−: CCl3CH2OH > ClCH2CH2OH > CH3CH2OH.
Practice 347 L1 ALC

Write the reaction of ethanol with sodium. What gas is evolved?

Show answer
  1. 2C2H5OH + 2Na → 2C2H5ONa + H2. The gas is hydrogen.
Practice 348 L1 ALC

Arrange in decreasing reactivity towards sodium: propan-1-ol, propan-2-ol, 2-methylpropan-2-ol.

Show answer
  1. Steric hindrance lowers reactivity: propan-1-ol > propan-2-ol > 2-methylpropan-2-ol.
Practice 349 L1 ALC

What is the product of ethanol with acetic acid (conc. H2SO4, heat)? Name the reaction.

Show answer
  1. Ethyl ethanoate (ethyl acetate) + water: Fischer esterification (reversible).
Practice 350 L1 ALC

Which is the product when ethanol reacts with CH3COCl in pyridine?

Show answer
  1. Ethyl ethanoate; HCl is neutralised as pyridinium chloride.
Practice 351 L1 ALC

Arrange in decreasing reactivity with Lucas reagent: propan-2-ol, 2-methylpropan-2-ol, propan-1-ol.

Show answer
  1. Rate follows carbocation stability: 2-methylpropan-2-ol (3°, immediate) > propan-2-ol (2°, about 5 minutes) > propan-1-ol (1°, no reaction at room temperature).
Practice 352 L1 ALC

What is Lucas reagent and what does a positive test look like?

Show answer
  1. Anhydrous ZnCl2 dissolved in concentrated HCl. A positive test is turbidity (cloudiness) caused by the insoluble alkyl chloride separating.
Practice 353 L1 ALC

In the Victor Meyer test, what colours are shown by primary, secondary and tertiary alcohols?

Show answer
  1. Primary: red; secondary: blue; tertiary: colourless.
Practice 354 L1 ALC

Write the product of ethanol with PCl5. How can the reaction be recognised?

Show answer
  1. C2H5OH + PCl5 → C2H5Cl + POCl3 + HCl. Steamy fumes of HCl are seen (test for –OH).
Practice 355 L1 ALC

What are the products of ethanol with SOCl2? Why is this reagent preferred?

Show answer
  1. C2H5Cl + SO2 + HCl. The by-products are gases, so the chloride is obtained pure.
Practice 356 L1 ALC

What happens when ethanol is heated with conc. H2SO4 at (a) 413 K with excess ethanol; (b) 443 K with excess acid?

Show answer
  1. (a) Diethyl ether (SN2). (b) Ethene (elimination).
Practice 357 L1 ALC

Arrange in increasing ease of acid-catalysed dehydration: butan-1-ol, butan-2-ol, 2-methylpropan-2-ol.

Show answer
  1. Ease follows cation stability: butan-1-ol < butan-2-ol < 2-methylpropan-2-ol.
Practice 358 L1 ALC

What is the major alkene from dehydration of butan-2-ol? State the rule.

Show answer
  1. But-2-ene (mostly trans), by Zaitsev’s rule: the more substituted alkene is the major product.
Practice 359 L1 ALC

What is formed on passing ethanol vapour over hot copper at 573 K? On passing propan-2-ol? On passing 2-methylpropan-2-ol?

Show answer
  1. Ethanol → ethanal (+ H2). Propan-2-ol → propanone (+ H2). 2-Methylpropan-2-ol → 2-methylpropene (dehydration, no α-H).
Practice 360 L1 ALC

Which reagent oxidises propan-1-ol to propanal and stops there: PCC or acidified K2Cr2O7?

Show answer
  1. PCC in CH2Cl2 (anhydrous; no over-oxidation). Dichromate in aqueous acid gives propanoic acid.
Practice 361 L1 ALC

Give the product of oxidation of butan-2-ol with CrO3/H2SO4.

Show answer
  1. Butan-2-one (ethyl methyl ketone).
Practice 362 L1 ALC

Which of these give a positive iodoform test: methanol, ethanol, propan-1-ol, propan-2-ol?

Show answer
  1. Need CH3CH(OH)– : ethanol and propan-2-ol.
Practice 363 L1 ALC

Name the yellow solid obtained in the iodoform reaction and give its formula.

Show answer
  1. Iodoform, CHI3 (triiodomethane), mp 119 °C.
Practice 364 L1 ALC

What is ethylene glycol used for?

Show answer
  1. As antifreeze in car radiators and for making terylene (PET).
Practice 365 L1 ALC

How is ethylene glycol prepared from ethene using a laboratory reagent?

Show answer
  1. By treating ethene with cold, dilute, alkaline KMnO4 (Baeyer’s reagent), which adds two OH groups.
Practice 366 L1 ALC

What does glycerol give when heated with KHSO4?

Show answer
  1. Acrolein (propenal, CH2=CH–CHO), which has a sharp, irritating smell.
Practice 367 L1 ALC

Write the product of glycerol with a mixture of conc. HNO3 and conc. H2SO4.

Show answer
  1. Glycerol trinitrate (nitroglycerine), C3H5(ONO2)3.
Practice 368 L1 ALC

What is the product of HIO4 with ethylene glycol?

Show answer
  1. Two molecules of methanal (HCHO); one mole of HIO4 is consumed.
Practice 369 L1 ALC

What is the pinacol rearrangement? Give the product from pinacol.

Show answer
  1. Acid-catalysed rearrangement of a vicinal diol to a carbonyl compound by a 1,2-shift. Pinacol gives pinacolone, (CH3)3C–CO–CH3.
Practice 370 L1 ALC

How is pinacol prepared from acetone?

Show answer
  1. By bimolecular reduction of propanone with magnesium amalgam (or Mg/Hg), followed by water.
Practice 371 L1 ALC

Name two industrial sources of ethanol.

Show answer
  1. (i) Fermentation of sugars (molasses) with yeast (invertase, zymase). (ii) Hydration of ethene (H3PO4, 570 K, 70 atm).
Practice 372 L1 ALC

What is denatured alcohol?

Show answer
  1. Ethanol made unfit for drinking by adding methanol, pyridine or a dye (and thereby free of excise duty).
Practice 373 L1 ALC

Why is methanol poisonous?

Show answer
  1. It is oxidised in the body to methanal and methanoic acid, which damage the optic nerve (blindness) and cause acidosis.
