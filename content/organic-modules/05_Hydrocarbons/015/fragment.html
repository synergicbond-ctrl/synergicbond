14. Bridged, fused and spiro hydrocarbons (von Baeyer nomenclature)

Bicyclic alkanes are named bicyclo[x.y.z]alkane, where x ≥ y ≥ z are the numbers of carbons in the bridges between the two bridgeheads (not counting the bridgeheads), and the parent name counts all the carbons. Numbering starts at a bridgehead, goes round the longest bridge to the second bridgehead, continues round the next longest, and finally the shortest bridge. Spiro compounds (one atom common to two rings) are named spiro[x.y]alkane with x ≤ y, numbering starting next to the spiro atom in the smaller ring.

Example 20 Foundation

Count carbons in bicyclo names

How many carbon atoms in (a) bicyclo[2.2.1]heptane (b) bicyclo[3.2.1]octane (c) spiro[4.5]decane? What is the molecular formula of (a)?

Show solution
  1. Carbons = x + y + z + 2 (the bridgeheads). (a) 2 + 2 + 1 + 2 = 7; (b) 3 + 2 + 1 + 2 = 8; (c) spiro: 4 + 5 + 1 = 10.
  2. Bicyclic alkanes have DoU = 2: CnH2n−2: (a) C7H12.

Bredt's rule: a double bond cannot be placed at a bridgehead of a small bicyclic system (it would be impossibly twisted in a planar sp2 arrangement). Bicyclo[2.2.1]hept-1-ene does not exist as a stable species; with a ring of eight or more in the largest ring, e.g. bicyclo[3.3.1]non-1-ene, it can exist.
