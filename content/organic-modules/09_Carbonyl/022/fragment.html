18. Olefination reactions

18.1 Wittig reaction A phosphorus ylide (phosphorane) converts C=O into C=C with complete control of where the double bond goes.

  • Preparation of the ylide: PPh3 + R–CH2–X (SN2, best with primary or methyl halide) gives the phosphonium salt Ph3P+–CH2R X−; a strong base (BuLi, NaH, NaNH2, or NaOEt for acidified ones) removes an α-proton giving the ylide Ph3P+–C−HR ↔ Ph3P=CHR.
  • Reaction: the ylide carbon attacks C=O; a four-membered oxaphosphetane forms and collapses to alkene + triphenylphosphine oxide. The very strong P=O bond (more than 500 kJ mol-1) is the driving force.

Ph₃P + CH₃Br → phosphonium salt → methylene ylide

Methylenecyclohexane: C=O replaced by C=CH₂ — Wittig forms alkenes that cannot be made by dehydration

Features:

  • The product double bond is exactly where the C=O was – no isomeric mixtures as in dehydration of alcohols.
  • It works for aldehydes and ketones. Hindered ketones react slowly.
  • Unstabilised ylides (R = alkyl) give mainly the Z-alkene (kinetic, salt-free conditions); stabilised ylides (R = C=O, CN, ester, e.g. Ph3P=CHCOOEt) give the E-alkene (the first step is reversible and the more stable trans oxaphosphetane leads to E).
  • Semi-stabilised ylides (R = aryl, allyl) give mixtures.

18.3 Horner–Wadsworth–Emmons (HWE) Phosphonate carbanions, (EtO)2P(O)CH−R (from (EtO)3P + RCH2X by the Arbuzov reaction, then NaH), react like ylides but are more nucleophilic, react with hindered ketones, and give E-alkenes selectively. Example 19 Main

Wittig products

Give the Wittig product of (a) benzaldehyde + Ph3P=CH2; (b) cyclopentanone + Ph3P=CHCH3; (c) acetone + Ph3P=C(CH3)2. Which is hardest to make and why?

Show solution
  1. (a) Styrene PhCH=CH2. (b) Ethylidenecyclopentane. (c) 2,3-Dimethylbut-2-ene (tetrasubstituted alkene): hardest, since a ketone with a disubstituted ylide meets severe steric hindrance in the oxaphosphetane, and Ph3P=C(CH3)2 requires a secondary halide (2-bromopropane) for the salt.

Choosing the pair

Show two Wittig routes to (CH3)2C=CHCH2CH3 (2-methylpent-2-ene) and say which is better.

Show solution
  1. The C=C connects C-2 and C-3. Route A: (CH3)2C=O + Ph3P=CHCH2CH3; the salt comes from 1-bromopropane (primary): good. Route B: CH3CH2CHO + Ph3P=C(CH3)2; the salt needs 2-bromopropane (secondary): poor yield. Route A is better.

Example 21 Adv

Wittig versus dehydration

Why does dehydration of 1-methylcyclohexanol give a mixture, while a Wittig reaction of cyclohexanone with Ph3P=CH2 gives a single alkene? What is that alkene?

Show solution
  1. Dehydration (E1, H2SO4/Δ) gives mainly the more stable, more substituted alkene, 1-methylcyclohexene, with a little methylenecyclohexane.
  2. The Wittig reaction forms the C=C only between the former carbonyl carbon and the ylide carbon: methylenecyclohexane exclusively, an isomer that is hard to obtain by dehydration.
