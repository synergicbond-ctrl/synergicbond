19.3 Basicity of acids and acidity of bases: the silver salt and chloroplatinate methods

Organic acid (silver salt method). The silver salt RCOOAg is ignited to leave metallic silver. For a monobasic acid the equivalent mass of the salt Esalt = (mass of salt/mass of Ag) × 108, and the equivalent mass of the acid = Esalt − 107 (replace one Ag, 108, by one H, 1). The basicity = molar mass of the acid / equivalent mass of the acid.

Example 40 Adv

Basicity from silver salt

1.000 g of the silver salt of an organic acid on ignition left 0.6545 g of silver. The molar mass of the acid is 118. Find the basicity of the acid and its formula as far as possible.

Show solution
  1. Equivalent mass of salt = (1.000/0.6545) × 108 = 165.0.
  2. Equivalent mass of acid = 165.0 − 107 = 58.0.
  3. Basicity = 118/58 = 2.03 ≈ 2 (dibasic). Silver succinate Ag2C4H4O4 (M = 330) fits: succinic acid HOOC–CH2CH2–COOH, M = 118.

Organic base (chloroplatinate method). The base B forms (B·HCl)2PtCl6 on treatment with HCl and PtCl4; ignition leaves platinum. For a monoacidic base, M of the salt = 2(MB + 36.5) + 408 (Pt 195 + 6Cl 213). Acidity = n in (B·HCl)nPtCl6-type salts.

Example 41 Adv

Molecular mass of a base

0.667 g of the chloroplatinate of a monoacidic base left 0.195 g of platinum on ignition (Pt = 195). Find the molecular mass of the base.

Show solution
  1. Moles of Pt = 0.195/195 = 1.00 × 10-3 = moles of salt, so M(salt) = 0.667/1.00 × 10-3 = 667.
  2. For (BH)2PtCl6: 2(MB + 1) + 195 + 213 = 667 ⇒ 2MB = 667 − 410 = 257 ⇒ MB ≈ 128.5 (about 129; for example quinoline, C9H7N).
