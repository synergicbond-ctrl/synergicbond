5. Corey–House synthesis (lithium dialkylcuprate coupling)

Reagents: (i) R–X + 2 Li (ether, dry) → R–Li + LiX; (ii) 2 R–Li + CuI → R2CuLi (Gilman reagent, lithium dialkylcuprate) + LiI; (iii) R2CuLi + R′–X → R–R′ + RCu + LiX. Only one R of the cuprate is transferred, so the cuprate is expensive in the sense of using 2 RLi per coupling. Scope: R′X may be primary alkyl halide (best), secondary (cyclic ones react with inversion), allylic, benzylic, vinyl and aryl halides; vinyl halides couple with retention of alkene geometry. Tertiary R′X fails (elimination). R in the cuprate can be 1°, 2° or 3° or aryl, vinyl.

Key idea This is *the* way to make an unsymmetrical alkane R–R′ cleanly (compare Wurtz). Design rule: let the electrophile R′X be the primary halide and put the other fragment on the cuprate. Because the alkyl halide can be primary and the cuprate can carry a 1°, 2° or 3° group, almost any branched alkane can be disconnected at some C–C bond into (cuprate R) + (primary R′X).

Example 6 Adv

Choose the method

Which method (Wurtz, Kolbe, Corey–House) can be used to prepare (a) n-hexane, (b) 2-methylbutane, (c) 3-methylpentane?

Show solution
  1. (a) n-Hexane CH3CH2CH2–CH2CH2CH3 is a symmetrical dimer of the propyl radical: Wurtz (1-bromopropane), Kolbe (sodium butanoate) or Corey–House all work.
  2. (b) 2-Methylbutane (CH3)2CH–CH2CH3 is not a dimer of one radical, so Wurtz and Kolbe give mixtures. Corey–House: (CH3)2CHCH2Br (primary) + (CH3)2CuLi → (CH3)2CHCH2–CH3.
  3. (c) 3-Methylpentane CH3CH2CH(CH3)CH2CH3: no C–C bond splits it into two identical halves, so only Corey–House: CH3CH2CH(CH3)CH2Br + (CH3)2CuLi → CH3CH2CH(CH3)CH2–CH3.
