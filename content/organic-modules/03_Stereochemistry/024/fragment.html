21. Prochirality and topicity

21.1 Prochiral centres and the substitution test

A tetrahedral atom with two identical groups and two different groups, X(a)(a)(b)(c), is prochiral: replacing one of the a groups by a new group d would create a stereocentre. The two identical groups are related to each other in one of three ways, found by the substitution test: replace each group in turn by a different group (isotopically, D for H) and compare the products.

  • Homotopic: the products are identical. The groups are interchangeable by a rotation (Cn axis): indistinguishable by any reagent or by NMR.
  • Enantiotopic: the products are enantiomers. The groups are related by a mirror plane (or inversion centre). Indistinguishable in an achiral medium; distinguishable by chiral reagents, enzymes, or chiral solvents.
  • Diastereotopic: the products are diastereomers. Distinguishable even in an achiral medium: different chemical shifts in NMR (an AB pattern) and different reactivity.

Faces of a trigonal centre. The two faces of a planar sp2 centre (C=O, C=C) are likewise classified. Homotopic faces: formaldehyde or acetone (attack from either face gives the same product). Enantiotopic faces: butanone or acetophenone (attack from either face gives enantiomers; this is why NaBH4 reduction gives a racemate). Diastereotopic faces: a carbonyl in a molecule that already has a stereocentre (2-methylcyclohexanone, an α-chiral aldehyde): attack from the two faces gives diastereomers, in unequal amounts. In NMR, enantiotopic protons give one signal (in an achiral solvent) but diastereotopic protons give two (and couple to each other, an AB pattern).
Example 62 Main

Topicity

Classify the two hydrogens on C-1 of ethanol, the two methyls in propan-2-ol, and the two hydrogens on C-3 of butan-2-ol.

Show solution
  1. Replace one H of ethanol's CH2 by D: CH3CHDOH is chiral; the other H gives its mirror image: enantiotopic.
  2. Propan-2-ol's CH3 groups: replacing H in one methyl gives CH2D–CH(OH)–CH3, chiral, and the other methyl gives the enantiomer: enantiotopic methyl groups.
  3. Butan-2-ol's C-3 H atoms: replacing either gives a molecule with two stereocentres: the two products are diastereomers: diastereotopic.
Example 63 Adv

Enzyme selectivity

Alcohol dehydrogenase removes one particular hydrogen of the CH2 group of ethanol. Why can an enzyme distinguish the two hydrogens although they are chemically equivalent in solution?

Show solution
  1. The two H on C-1 of ethanol are enantiotopic. In an achiral medium they behave identically, but the enzyme's active site is chiral (made of L-amino acids), so the two H form diastereomeric transition states with it and are removed at different rates (only the pro-R hydrogen is transferred).

21.2 Summary sheet: resolution and topicity

  • Racemate formation: achiral + achiral → racemate; planar intermediates; α-H next to C=O racemises.
  • Resolution: make diastereomeric salts with a pure resolving agent (brucine, quinine, tartaric acid), crystallise, regenerate; or enzymes, chiral HPLC.
  • Asymmetric synthesis: chiral pool, auxiliary, catalyst; measure ee.
  • Topicity by substitution test: identical = homotopic (Cn), enantiomers = enantiotopic (σ, i), diastereomers = diastereotopic (a stereocentre is present).
