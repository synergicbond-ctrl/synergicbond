13. Counting monohalogenated products including stereoisomers

To count the monohalo derivatives of an alkane, group the hydrogens by symmetry: hydrogens that can be interchanged by a symmetry operation (or by fast rotation about C–C bonds, as in CH3) give the same product. Number of constitutional monohalides = number of non-equivalent hydrogen sets. To include stereoisomers, examine every product: a product with one stereocentre counts as 2 (enantiomers); two stereocentres in an unsymmetrical product give up to 4; with identical ends (as in 2,3-dichlorobutane) the meso form reduces 4 to 3; a double bond that can be E or Z counts as 2.

  1. Write the skeleton and number the carbons.
  2. Mark the set of equivalent carbons by symmetry, then the set of equivalent H on each carbon.
  3. Replace one H of each set by X and write the product.
  4. For each product test every carbon for four different groups; double the count for each chiral product with a single centre.

Pentane, 2-methylbutane, 2,2-dimethylpropane.

  • Pentane: 3 constitutional monochlorides (1-, 2-, 3-); 2-chloropentane is chiral, so 4 with stereoisomers.
  • 2-Methylbutane: 4 constitutional (H sets 6 : 1 : 2 : 3); 1-chloro-2-methylbutane and 2-chloro-3-methylbutane are chiral, so 6 with stereoisomers.
  • 2,2-Dimethylpropane: 1 only (all 12 H equivalent).
  • C6H14 isomers: hexane 3 (5 with stereoisomers); 2-methylpentane 5 (8); 3-methylpentane 4 (8); 2,3-dimethylbutane 2 (3); 2,2-dimethylbutane 3 (4).

For polyhalogenation the same procedure is applied again to every monohalide and duplicates are removed. Butane gives 6 constitutional dichlorides (1,1-; 1,2-; 1,3-; 1,4-; 2,2-; 2,3-) and 10 stereoisomers in all; propane gives 4 constitutional and 5 stereoisomeric dichlorides.

Trap. Do not count a position twice because two carbons look different on paper: in 2,2-dimethylbutane the three methyls on C-2 are equivalent (9 H), but the CH3 of the ethyl group (3 H) is a separate set. Always test equivalence by symmetry, not by position in the name.
