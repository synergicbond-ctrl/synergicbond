22. Reactions of aniline hydrochloride and the identity tests of aniline

  • Aniline hydrochloride C6H5NH3+Cl−: soluble salt; aniline is regenerated with NaOH (oily drops). Aniline salts are hydrolysed to a small extent (pKa 4.6: weakly acidic solutions).
  • Tests: (i) bromine water → white precipitate; (ii) carbylamine: foul smell; (iii) diazotisation/coupling with β-naphthol → scarlet red dye; (iv) bleaching powder → transient violet colour.
Example 23 Main

Mono- versus tri-bromination

Why does aniline give 2,4,6-tribromoaniline with bromine water but only the para product from acetanilide?

Show solution
  1. In aniline the N lone pair is fully available (+M): all o/p positions are strongly activated, and bromine water (itself polar and able to supply a good electrophile) attacks all three.
  2. In acetanilide the N lone pair is shared with C=O, so activation is much weaker and the group is bulky; the electrophile enters the less hindered para position only, giving p-bromoacetanilide.
Example 24 Adv

Why only 47 % meta?

Explain why p-nitroaniline is not the only major product of direct nitration of aniline in HNO3/H2SO4.

Show solution
  1. In H2SO4 aniline (pKa 4.6) exists largely as C6H5NH3+; –NH3+ is a −I group (no lone pair) and directs the electrophile to the meta position (≈ 47 %). The small equilibrium amount of free aniline is far more reactive and reacts at the para position (≈ 51 %); ortho (≈ 2 %) is disfavoured by the proximity of the positive charge and by sterics.
Example 25 Adv

Multi-step conversion

Convert aniline to (a) p-nitroaniline, (b) 2,4,6-tribromophenol, (c) 1,3,5-tribromobenzene, (d) 4-aminoacetophenone.

Show solution
  1. (a) PhNH2 → Ac2O → PhNHAc → HNO3/H2SO4 → p-O2NC6H4NHAc → H3O+/OH− → p-nitroaniline.
  2. (b) PhNH2 → NaNO2/HCl, 273 K → PhN2+Cl− → H2O, warm → phenol → Br2/H2O → 2,4,6-tribromophenol.
  3. (c) PhNH2 → Br2/H2O → 2,4,6-tribromoaniline → NaNO2/HCl, 273 K → diazonium → H3PO2 (or EtOH) → 1,3,5-tribromobenzene (the NH2 is used as a temporary director and then removed).
  4. (d) PhNH2 → PhNHAc → CH3COCl/AlCl3 → p-CH3CO–C6H4–NHAc → H3O+ → 4-aminoacetophenone.
