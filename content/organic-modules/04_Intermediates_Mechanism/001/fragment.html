Prerequisites

The chapter assumes: electronegativity and polarity of bonds; formal charge and resonance; hybridisation (sp, sp2, sp3); the IUPAC names of simple compounds; acid–base behaviour (pKa); the inductive and mesomeric effects; the basic ideas of reaction rate and activation energy; and the idea of stereochemistry (chirality, R/S, cis/trans). The Hückel method used in section 4.6 is introduced there from first principles.

Learning objectives

After studying the chapter you should be able to:

  • write a mechanism with correct curved arrows and check it for valence, charge and consistency with the conditions;
  • classify a reaction by change, by reagent and by mechanism, and identify the intermediate from the reagents and conditions;
  • read and construct energy profiles and apply the Hammond postulate, kinetic and thermodynamic control, and the Curtin–Hammett principle;
  • use frontier-orbital arguments to explain geometry and selectivity (backside attack, Bürgi–Dunitz angle, anti-periplanar elimination, hyperconjugation, aromaticity);
  • order carbocations, carbanions and radicals by stability using quoted data and state the conditions under which an order holds;
  • predict products and stereochemistry for reactions through carbocations (including rearrangement), carbanions (including enolates), radicals, carbenes, nitrenes and arynes.

Entry check

Attempt these before starting; the answers show where to review.

Concept check. Draw the resonance contributors of the allyl cation. How many π electrons does it have?
Show answer
CH2=CH–CH2+ ↔ +CH2–CH=CH2; two π electrons delocalised over three carbons.
Concept check. Which is the stronger acid, CH3COOH (pKa 4.8) or CH3OH (pKa 15.5), and by what factor?
Show answer
Acetic acid, by about 1010.7.
Concept check. State the hybridisation of the carbon in CH3+, CH3− and the methyl radical, and the shape of each.
Show answer
sp2 planar (cation); sp3 pyramidal (anion); the radical is planar or a very shallow pyramid.
Concept check. Is the reaction of an alkyl halide with a nucleophile in one step first order or second order in the halide and nucleophile?
Show answer
Second order overall: first order in each (SN2).
Concept check. What is the relation between activation energy and rate?
Show answer
A larger activation energy gives a smaller rate constant (k = A e−Ea/RT).

Assumptions made in preparing this module

The module covers the intermediates and mechanism basics that underpin the later chapters of organic chemistry for JEE Main and Advanced; stereochemical aspects not needed for mechanisms and the detailed chemistry of each functional group are assigned to their own chapters. Data are quoted from the books listed in the source notes at the end and from calculations described there; where a value could not be verified it is not given. No previous-year question is reproduced, because none could be verified. Previous-year questions are therefore not part of this edition; the problems are original. In the print layout the body font is a Century Schoolbook-style serif at 9 pt; where that typeface is not available on the machine used to render, a similar serif (Palatino) is substituted.
