1.1 What is a carbohydrate?

Carbohydrates are polyhydroxy aldehydes or ketones, or compounds that give these on hydrolysis. The name comes from the old empirical formula Cn(H2O)m, but the formula is only a rough guide.

  • Glucose C6H12O6 = C6(H2O)6 fits, but so do formaldehyde (CH2O), acetic acid (C2H4O2) and lactic acid (C3H6O3), none of which is a sugar.
  • Rhamnose (C6H12O5) and 2-deoxyribose (C5H10O4) are carbohydrates that do not fit Cn(H2O)m.
  • The real definition is structural: an α-hydroxy carbonyl system (or its cyclic hemiacetal / acetal), with further –OH groups along an unbranched chain.

Combine the first two rows to get the usual names: an aldohexose is a six-carbon sugar with an aldehyde; a ketopentose is a five-carbon sugar with a ketone.

Trap "Sugar" in everyday use means sucrose. In chemistry a sugar is any simple carbohydrate (mono- or oligosaccharide, sweet, water soluble, crystalline). Starch and cellulose are carbohydrates but not sugars.
