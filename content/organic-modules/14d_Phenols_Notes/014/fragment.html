Practice 1 L1 Foundation

Which of the following lists ethanol, water, phenol and acetic acid in order of increasing acid strength in water?

(A) water < ethanol < phenol < acetic acid

(B) phenol < ethanol < water < acetic acid

(C) ethanol < water < phenol < acetic acid

(D) ethanol < phenol < water < acetic acid

Show answer
  1. Approximate pKa values are ethanol ≈ 16, water ≈ 15.7, phenol ≈ 10 and acetic acid ≈ 4.8. A larger pKa means a weaker acid.
  2. Ordering from the largest pKa to the smallest gives ethanol < water < phenol < acetic acid, which is option C.

Answer: C

Practice 2 L1 Foundation

Which of the following dissolve in cold dilute aqueous NaOH, forming a salt?

(A) Phenol

(B) Benzyl alcohol

(C) p-Nitrophenol

(D) Benzoic acid

Show answer
  1. NaOH (conjugate acid water, pKa 15.7) is a strong enough base to deprotonate any compound with pKa well below 15: phenol (≈10), p-nitrophenol (≈7) and benzoic acid (≈4.2).
  2. Benzyl alcohol has pKa ≈ 15–16, about the same as water, so it is not converted to its alkoxide to any useful extent and remains insoluble.
  3. Hence A, C and D.

Answer: A,C,D

Practice 3 L1 Foundation

Which of the following give a violet or purple colour with neutral FeCl3 solution?

(A) Phenol

(B) Salicylic acid

(C) Anisole

(D) p-Cresol

Practice 9 L1 Foundation

The reactive electrophile in the Reimer–Tiemann reaction of phenol (CHCl3, aqueous NaOH) is

Practice 10 L1 Foundation

How many resonance structures does the phenoxide ion have, counting the two Kekulé forms with the charge on oxygen plus one structure for each ring carbon that can bear the charge?

Show answer
  1. Negative charge on oxygen with the aromatic ring: 2 Kekulé structures.
  2. Negative charge on an ortho carbon (2 positions) and on the para carbon (1 position), each as a cyclohexadienone-type structure: 3 structures.
  3. Total = 2 + 3 = 5. The meta carbons never carry the charge.

Answer: 5

Practice 11 L1 Foundation

The C–O bond length in phenol (≈136 pm) is shorter than in methanol (≈143 pm). The best explanation is

(A) Oxygen in phenol is sp3 and in methanol is sp2

(B) Delocalisation of an oxygen lone pair into the ring gives the C–O bond partial double-bond character

(C) Hydrogen bonding in phenol shortens the bond

(D) The benzene ring is larger than a methyl group

Show answer
  1. Resonance of the oxygen lone pair with the aromatic π system places some C=O double-bond character on the C–O bond, and the bond carbon is sp2 (shorter than sp3).
  2. The other options either reverse the hybridisation or are unrelated to bond length. Answer B.

Answer: B

Practice 12 L1 Foundation

A mixture of o- and p-nitrophenol is steam distilled. Which isomer is found in the distillate, and why?

(A) p-Nitrophenol, because it has intermolecular hydrogen bonds

(B) o-Nitrophenol, because it has an intramolecular hydrogen bond

(C) Both, in equal amounts

(D) Neither; nitrophenols are not volatile

Show answer
  1. In o-nitrophenol the OH forms a hydrogen bond with the neighbouring NO2 within the same molecule, so there is little association between molecules.
  2. The molecules are therefore volatile and are carried over by steam; p-nitrophenol is held together by intermolecular hydrogen bonds and stays behind. Answer B.

Answer: B

Which is the correct order of increasing acidity?

Practice 22 L1 Foundation

Which statements correctly explain why phenol is a stronger acid than cyclohexanol?

(A) The negative charge of the phenoxide ion is delocalised into the ring

(B) The cyclohexoxide ion is stabilised by resonance

(C) The oxygen in phenol is attached to an sp2 carbon, which is more electron-withdrawing than an sp3 carbon

(D) The pKa of phenol is about 16

Show answer
  1. Resonance delocalisation of the charge of phenoxide onto the ortho and para carbons stabilises the conjugate base (A true).
  2. The cyclohexoxide ion has no π system for delocalisation (B false). The more electronegative sp2 carbon also stabilises the anion inductively (C true).
  3. The pKa of phenol is about 10, not 16 (D false). Answer A, C.

Answer: A,C

Practice 23 L1 Foundation

In the acid-catalysed rearrangement of cumene hydroperoxide, which group migrates from carbon to the electron-deficient oxygen?

Practice 28 L2 Main

Which is the correct order of decreasing acidity for p-nitrophenol, m-nitrophenol, phenol and p-methoxyphenol?

(A) p-Nitrophenol > m-nitrophenol > phenol > p-methoxyphenol

(B) m-Nitrophenol > p-nitrophenol > phenol > p-methoxyphenol

(C) p-Nitrophenol > m-nitrophenol > p-methoxyphenol > phenol

(D) Phenol > p-methoxyphenol > m-nitrophenol > p-nitrophenol

Show answer
  1. From the para position, NO2 exerts both –I and –R, delocalising the negative charge onto its oxygens (pKa ≈ 7). From the meta position it exerts only –I (pKa ≈ 8.4).
  2. Phenol is ≈ 10. In p-methoxyphenol the +R effect of OCH3 opposes charge delocalisation and slightly lowers acidity (≈ 10.2).
  3. The order is p-nitro > m-nitro > phenol > p-methoxy, option A.

Answer: A

Practice 29 L2 Main

Arrange in order of increasing acidity: (i) phenol, (ii) m-methoxyphenol, (iii) p-methoxyphenol.

(A) (iii) < (i) < (ii)

(B) (i) < (ii) < (iii)

(C) (ii) < (i) < (iii)

(D) (i) < (iii) < (ii)

Show answer
  1. At the para position the methoxy group donates electrons by resonance (+R), which destabilises the phenoxide ion; p-methoxyphenol is less acidic than phenol.
  2. At the meta position the +R effect cannot reach the oxygen bearing the charge, and the –I effect of the electronegative oxygen stabilises the anion; m-methoxyphenol (pKa ≈ 9.7) is more acidic than phenol.
  3. Order of increasing acidity: p-methoxy < phenol < m-methoxy, i.e. (iii) < (i) < (ii), option A.

Answer: A

Practice 30 L2 Main

Which of the following properties are greater for p-nitrophenol than for o-nitrophenol?

(A) Boiling point

(B) Solubility in water

(C) Volatility with steam

(D) Melting point

Show answer
  1. o-Nitrophenol has an intramolecular hydrogen bond, so its molecules are not associated with each other or with water to the same extent: it has lower boiling point, lower melting point (≈ 45 °C versus ≈ 114 °C), lower water solubility, and is steam volatile.
  2. p-Nitrophenol forms intermolecular hydrogen bonds (to itself and water), so it is higher in boiling point, melting point and water solubility, but not steam volatile.
  3. Answer A, B, D.

Which statements about acidity are correct?

(A) Picric acid is a stronger acid than acetic acid

(B) Phenol is a stronger acid than carbonic acid

Practice 32 L2 Main

Carbon dioxide is bubbled through a cold aqueous solution of sodium phenoxide. What is observed?

(A) No reaction, since phenol is a stronger acid than carbonic acid

(B) The solution turns turbid because phenol is liberated and sodium hydrogencarbonate is formed

(C) Sodium salicylate crystallises immediately

(D) Benzoic acid precipitates

Practice 33 L2 Main

An ether solution contains benzoic acid, phenol, aniline and naphthalene. Which sequences of aqueous washes separate all four compounds (each wash is applied to the remaining ether layer)?

(A) aq. NaOH, then aq. NaHCO3, then dilute HCl

(B) aq. NaHCO3, then aq. NaOH, then dilute HCl

(C) dilute HCl, then aq. NaHCO3, then aq. NaOH

(D) dilute HCl, then aq. NaOH, then aq. NaHCO3

Show answer
  1. NaHCO3 extracts only the carboxylic acid; NaOH extracts the phenol (and the carboxylic acid if still present); HCl extracts only the amine; naphthalene stays in ether throughout.
  2. The two acids can be separated only if NaHCO3 is used before NaOH. In A and D, NaOH removes benzoic acid and phenol together, so they are not separated.
  3. The amine can be removed first or last without affecting the acids. B and C therefore work.

Answer: B,C

Practice 39 L2 Main

Which statements about the Kolbe–Schmitt reaction of sodium and potassium phenoxide are correct?

(A) Sodium phenoxide at about 400 K gives mainly salicylate
