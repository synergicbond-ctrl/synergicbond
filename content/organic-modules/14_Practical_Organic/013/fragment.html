3.5 Steam distillation

This is used for liquids that are immiscible (or nearly so) with water, steam-volatile, and decompose near their b.p., and that are contaminated by non-volatile or non-steam-volatile impurities. Steam is passed through the mixture; the organic liquid distils along with water, and the distillate is separated in a separating funnel. Because the two liquids do not dissolve in each other, each exerts its own vapour pressure independently: the total vapour pressure is p = porg + pw. The mixture boils when this sum equals the atmospheric pressure, so it boils at a temperature below the boiling point of water (373 K) and far below the boiling point of the organic compound.

Formula porg + pw = Patm at the boiling temperature. worg/wwater = (porg × Morg)/(pw × Mwater) (because n ∝ p in the vapour)

Examples that steam-distil: aniline, nitrobenzene, bromobenzene, o-nitrophenol (intramolecular H-bond makes it volatile), turpentine and essential oils (clove, lavender, eucalyptus), chlorobenzene.

Exam Trap p-Nitrophenol does NOT steam-distil: intermolecular H-bonding between its molecules (and with water) makes it non-volatile. So steam distillation separates o-nitrophenol (distils) from p-nitrophenol (stays in the flask). The same applies to salicylaldehyde (volatile) and p-hydroxybenzaldehyde (not volatile).
Example 3 Adv

Steam distillation numerical

Bromobenzene (M = 157) is steam-distilled at 368 K (95 °C), at which the vapour pressure of water is 634 mmHg and the atmospheric pressure is 760 mmHg. Calculate the mass of bromobenzene that distils over per gram of water.

Show solution
  1. At the boiling temperature, porg = P − pw = 760 − 634 = 126 mmHg.
  2. worg/wwater = (126 × 157)/(634 × 18) = 19782/11412 = 1.73 g per g of water.
  3. The ratio is large even though the organic liquid has a small vapour pressure, because its molar mass is much larger than 18. This is why heavy organic compounds are efficiently steam-distilled.
Example 4 Adv

Finding the steam-distillation temperature from data

A water-immiscible liquid Y (M = 108) is steam-distilled at 760 mm Hg. Vapour pressures (mm Hg): at 94 °C water 610.9, Y 66; at 95 °C water 633.9, Y 69; at 96 °C water 657.6, Y 73; at 97 °C water 682.1, Y 78; at 98 °C water 707.3, Y 83. At what temperature does the mixture boil and what is the mass ratio Y : water in the distillate?

Show solution
  1. The mixture boils when pw + pY = 760. Sums: 94 °C: 677; 95 °C: 703; 96 °C: 731; 97 °C: 760.1; 98 °C: 790. So the boiling point is 97 °C (370 K), below that of pure water.
  2. wY/ww = (78 × 108)/(682.1 × 18) = 0.69.
Example 5 Adv

Where do mixtures boil?

Why does a mixture of nitrobenzene (b.p. 484 K) and water boil near 372 K at 1 atm in steam distillation, and why can aniline be separated from non-volatile tar by this method?

Show solution
  1. Nitrobenzene and water are essentially immiscible, so their vapour pressures simply add (no mixing lowers either). The boiling temperature is the temperature at which pPhNO2 + pH2O = 1 atm; since pH2O alone already is close to 1 atm at 373 K, and pPhNO2 is small but not zero, the sum reaches 1 atm just below 373 K. (Nitrobenzene thus distils nearly 110 K below its own boiling point.)
  2. The tar is non-volatile (it contributes essentially no vapour pressure), so only aniline and water distil and the tar remains in the flask.
Validity Check The mass-ratio formula assumes that the vapours behave ideally and that the two liquids are completely immiscible. If the organic liquid dissolves slightly in water, its partial pressure is a little below its pure-liquid value and the amount distilled is slightly smaller than calculated.
Exception & Its Reason A liquid miscible with water (ethanol, glycerol) cannot be steam distilled: it lowers the water's vapour pressure by dissolving, so the additive-pressure argument fails.
Concept check

Why does the steam-distillation temperature of a mixture lie below 373 K and below the b.p. of the organic liquid?

Show answer

Each liquid contributes its own vapour pressure; porg + pwater reaches Patm before either alone does.
