7.2 Retained parent names: the substituent that gives the name is C-1

Some substituents have retained names that serve as the parent name. When the ring carries a group that is the retained name (OH → phenol; NH₂ → aniline; COOH → benzoic acid; CHO → benzaldehyde; CH₃ → toluene …) that carbon is C-1 and the remaining substituents are numbered for the lowest locants (alphabetical order breaks ties).

Why It Happens The group that supplies the retained name is already contained in the parent name, so its carbon is numbered 1 and the other groups are located relative to it. Only a principal group can supply the parent name.
IMP Seniority still applies. Phenol and aniline are parents only when OH or NH₂ is the principal group. If a higher group (COOH, CHO, CN, CONH₂, SO₃H) is present, the compound is named as a benzoic acid, benzaldehyde and so on, and the OH or NH₂ becomes a prefix: 2-hydroxybenzoic acid, 4-aminobenzaldehyde.
Exception & Its Reason Toluene, anisole and styrene name a hydrocarbon-type substituent and have no priority as a suffix, so with any real principal group present the compound is named on that group: 4-methylbenzoic acid, not 4-carboxytoluene.

Example 46 Standard

Name the compound.

Show solution
  1. COOH is the principal group → benzoic acid, COOH carbon = C1.
  2. Substituents: nitro on the ortho carbon, chloro on the carbon para to... counting from C1: the nitro carbon is adjacent (C2); going the same way, the chloro-bearing carbon is C4. The other direction would give {4,6}.
  3. Lowest set {2,4}. Alphabetical: chloro, nitro.

4-Chloro-2-nitrobenzoic acid.

Example 47 Standard

Name the compound and give its trivial name.

Show solution
  1. OH is the principal group (phenol), C1. Nitro groups at both ortho positions and the para position.
  2. Lowest locants {2,4,6}; "tri" is used for the three identical groups.

2,4,6-Trinitrophenol, trivial name picric acid (a strong acid because of the three –M, –I nitro groups).

Example 48 Standard

Name the compound where two directions give the same locant set.

Show solution
  1. Phenol, OH at C1. Bromo and chloro both ortho. Locants {2,6} in either direction.
  2. Tie → alphabetical: bromo gets the lower locant 2.

2-Bromo-6-chlorophenol.
