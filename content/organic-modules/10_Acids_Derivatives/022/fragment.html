22. Hydroxy acids

Hydroxy acids contain both –OH and –COOH. Their behaviour on heating depends on the relative position of the two groups, because dehydration/esterification forms a ring where possible (Part G rule: 5- and 6-membered rings form easily).

<h4>22.1 Lactic acid</h4>

2-Hydroxypropanoic acid, a chiral molecule with one stereocentre: (S)-(+)-lactic acid (L-, sarcolactic acid, in muscle) and (R)-(−) (D-); the racemate from fermentation of lactose by Lactobacillus (sour milk) is optically inactive; the acid is a liquid, miscible with water. Preparation: acetaldehyde cyanohydrin hydrolysis (gives racemate); from 2-bromopropanoic acid (HVZ then aq. KOH). Reactions: dehydrates (lactide); with dilute H2SO4 + heat gives acetaldehyde + formic acid; with conc. H2SO4 gives CH3CHO + CO + H2O; mild oxidation (KMnO4) gives pyruvic acid CH3COCOOH; strong oxidation gives acetic acid + CO2; iodoform test is positive (CH3–CH(OH)–).

Example 56 Main

Lactic acid problem

How many stereoisomers does lactic acid have? Name the product of its oxidation with acidified KMnO4 (mild) and give the result of the iodoform test.

Show solution
  1. One stereocentre (C-2 with H, OH, CH3, COOH): 2 stereoisomers (R and S enantiomers). Mild oxidation of the secondary alcohol gives the ketone pyruvic acid (2-oxopropanoic acid); vigorous oxidation cleaves it to acetic acid + CO2.
  2. Lactic acid has the CH3CH(OH)– group, so it gives iodoform (yellow) with I2/NaOH.

22.2 Tartaric acid

HOOC–CH(OH)–CH(OH)–COOH (2,3-dihydroxybutanedioic acid) has two identical stereocentres: three stereoisomers: (2R,3R) (dextro, natural, from grapes: L-(+)), (2S,3S) (laevo) and the meso form (2R,3S), which is optically inactive due to an internal mirror plane. An equimolar mixture of the (+) and (−) forms is the racemic (±) “racemic acid” (paratartaric acid). Distinguish: meso and dl are both optically inactive; the dl mixture can be resolved, the meso compound cannot. The monopotassium salt is cream of tartar; the sodium potassium salt is Rochelle (Seignette) salt used in Fehling’s solution (alkaline cupric tartrate); tartrate keeps Cu2+ in solution at high pH.

22.3 Malic and citric acid

  • Malic acid HOOC–CH(OH)–CH2–COOH (hydroxysuccinic acid; apples): one stereocentre, 2 enantiomers. Dehydration gives fumaric/maleic acid (loss of H2O across C2–C3).
  • Citric acid 2-hydroxypropane-1,2,3-tricarboxylic acid: achiral (the central carbon has two identical –CH2COOH arms), tribasic (pKa 3.13, 4.76, 6.40); tertiary alcohol, so not oxidised by mild oxidants (Fehling, Tollens negative); on heating gives aconitic acid by dehydration; on heating with conc. H2SO4: acetonedicarboxylic acid (loses CO and H2O) → acetone.

22.4 Salicylic acid and aspirin

Salicylic acid (2-hydroxybenzoic acid, mp 159 °C) is prepared by the Kolbe–Schmitt reaction. Properties: intramolecular H-bond → volatile in steam (the para isomer is not); violet with neutral FeCl3 (phenol OH); dissolves in NaHCO3 with CO2 (carboxylic acid; pKa 2.97); heated with soda lime gives phenol. Derivatives: with acetic anhydride → aspirin (acetylsalicylic acid; no FeCl3 colour, because the phenol OH is acylated, unless hydrolysed); with methanol/H+ → methyl salicylate (oil of wintergreen); with phenol/POCl3 → salol (phenyl salicylate).

NaOH stoichiometry

(a) How many moles of NaOH are consumed per mole of aspirin on prolonged heating with excess NaOH? (b) How many moles of NaHCO3 react at room temperature? (c) How many mL of 0.100 M NaOH neutralise 0.180 g of aspirin (M = 180) to the carboxylate only?

Show solution

Example 58 Adv

Ortho vs para

Why is o-hydroxybenzoic acid steam-volatile and a stronger acid, but p-hydroxybenzoic acid is not steam-volatile and a weaker acid?

Show solution
  1. In the ortho isomer the OH and COOH form an intramolecular hydrogen bond (a six-membered chelate ring): intermolecular association is reduced, so the compound is volatile with steam; the salicylate anion is also stabilised by the O–H···O− bond, so it is a much stronger acid (pKa 2.97).
  2. In the para isomer, only intermolecular H-bonding is possible: high mp (214 °C), not steam volatile; the +M effect of OH at the para position also weakens the acid (pKa 4.58).
