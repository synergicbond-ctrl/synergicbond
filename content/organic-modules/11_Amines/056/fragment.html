56. Mechanism: Bamberger and benzidine rearrangements (what happens to PhNHOH and PhNHNHPh in acid)

  • Bamberger rearrangement of phenylhydroxylamine. Acid protonates the OH group; loss of water would give the phenylnitrenium ion C6H5–N+–H, in which the positive charge is delocalised on to the ortho and para ring carbons (iminium-type resonance structures). Water attacks the para carbon (largest LUMO coefficient, less hindered), and loss of H+ gives p-aminophenol. Why para: the positive charge on carbon is greatest there and attack is least hindered.
  • Benzidine rearrangement of hydrazobenzene. In dilute acid the N–N compound is diprotonated; the N–N bond weakens and the two rings, held face to face, join through their para carbons while the N–N bond breaks (for the diprotonated species the best-supported pathway is a concerted [5,5]-sigmatropic-type shift). Proton loss from each ring restores aromaticity: 4,4'-diaminobiphenyl (benzidine) as the main product, with o-semidine, diphenyline and similar by-products.
