◆Concept

This part places polycrystalline solids immediately after the crystalline–amorphous comparison. The key idea is that a bulk specimen may be made of many tiny crystals with different orientations while each crystallite remains internally crystalline.

1. Crystallites, Grains and Grain Boundaries

◆Concept

A polycrystalline solid is an aggregate of many small crystalline domains called grains or crystallites. Each grain possesses its own long-range periodic order and crystallographic orientation.

Where two differently oriented crystallites meet, the periodic arrangements do not match perfectly. The interface is called a grain boundary.

  • Many ordinary metallic samples are polycrystalline.
  • A grain is crystalline; a grain boundary is only the interfacial region between grains.
  • A large visible piece can therefore contain an enormous number of microscopic crystals.

2. Orientation Averaging and Macroscopic Behaviour

◆Concept

A single crystal can show direction-dependent properties. If a polycrystal contains very many randomly oriented grains, those directional effects may statistically average, making the bulk specimen approximately isotropic.

★Precision

Polycrystalline does not mean amorphous. Long-range order exists inside every crystallite, although the orientation changes from grain to grain.

3. Spatial Map

◆Concept

The line separating regions is a grain boundary; the stroke direction inside each region symbolizes a different crystallographic orientation.

⚠JEE Advanced traps & edge cases

  • Polycrystalline ≠ amorphous.
  • Macroscopic isotropy does not prove absence of crystallinity.
  • A textured polycrystal can remain macroscopically anisotropic because the grain orientations are not random.

▶Solved examples

Worked example 1

Example 1 — Conceptual

A metal sample is isotropic in bulk but electron microscopy shows many ordered grains. Is the sample amorphous?

  1. Ordered grains prove crystallinity inside each domain.
  2. Random orientation can average anisotropy at the macroscopic scale.

The sample is polycrystalline, not amorphous.

Worked example 2

Example 2 — Multi-correct

Select correct statements: (A) grain boundaries separate different orientations; (B) every polycrystal is isotropic; (C) each grain may be anisotropic; (D) polycrystals have no long-range order.

  1. A is correct.
  2. B is false because texture can produce anisotropy.
  3. C is correct.
  4. D is false because each grain has long-range order.

Correct options: A and C.
