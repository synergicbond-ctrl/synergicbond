30.4 Epoxides, crown ethers and thiols

Practice 139 L2 EPX

Give the major product with name: (a) propylene oxide + C2H5ONa/C2H5OH; (b) propylene oxide + HBr; (c) isobutylene oxide + CH3OH/H+; (d) isobutylene oxide + CH3ONa.

Show answer
  1. (a) 1-Ethoxypropan-2-ol. (b) 2-Bromopropan-1-ol (acid: more substituted C). (c) 2-Methoxy-2-methylpropan-1-ol. (d) 1-Methoxy-2-methylpropan-2-ol.
Practice 140 L2 EPX

Give the product with stereochemistry: (a) cyclohexene oxide + NaN3; (b) cyclohexene oxide + NH3; (c) cyclopentene oxide + C2H5MgBr; (d) cis-2,3-dimethyloxirane + H3O+.

Show answer
  1. (a) trans-2-Azidocyclohexanol (racemic). (b) trans-2-Aminocyclohexanol (racemic). (c) trans-2-Ethylcyclopentanol (racemic). (d) (±)-Butane-2,3-diol, the racemic (2R,3R)/(2S,3S) pair.
Practice 141 L2 EPX

trans-But-2-ene is converted to its epoxide with mCPBA and then hydrolysed with dil. H2SO4. What is the stereochemistry of the diol? Compare with the product of cis-but-2-ene with OsO4.

Show answer
  1. Epoxidation (syn) gives trans-epoxide; anti opening gives meso-butane-2,3-diol. cis-But-2-ene with OsO4 (syn) also gives meso-butane-2,3-diol. So both routes give the same meso diol from opposite alkenes.
Practice 142 L2 EPX

Why does the acid-catalysed opening of 2,2-dimethyloxirane occur at the tertiary carbon while base opening occurs at CH2?

Show answer
  1. In acid the protonated epoxide is attacked in a transition state with considerable carbocation character: positive charge is best carried by the tertiary carbon. With base there is no such assistance and steric hindrance decides: the unhindered CH2.
Practice 143 L2 EPX

(R)-2-Methyloxirane reacts with CH3O−. Give the product with configuration. Repeat with CH3OH/H+.

Show answer
  1. Base: attack at CH2, stereocentre untouched: (R)-1-methoxypropan-2-ol. Acid: attack at the stereocentre with inversion: (S)-2-methoxypropan-1-ol.
Practice 144 L2 EPX

Starting from ethylene oxide, prepare (a) butan-1-ol; (b) 2-phenylethanol; (c) 2-ethoxyethanol; (d) 2-mercaptoethanol.

Show answer
  1. (a) C2H5MgBr then H3O+. (b) C6H5MgBr then H3O+. (c) C2H5OH/H+ (or NaOC2H5). (d) NaSH (or H2S with base): HOCH2CH2SH.
Practice 145 L2 EPX

How many moles of ethylene oxide react with ammonia to give triethanolamine? Write the equation.

Show answer
  1. NH3 + 3 C2H4O → N(CH2CH2OH)3: 3 mol.
Practice 146 L2 EPX

Estimate the mass of ethylene oxide needed to make 62 kg of ethylene glycol (100 % yield).

Show answer
  1. C2H4O + H2O → C2H6O2; 62 kg glycol = 1000 mol → 1000 mol ethylene oxide = 44 kg.
Practice 147 L2 THI

A solution of KF in acetonitrile reacts with 1-bromooctane only in the presence of 18-crown-6. Explain.

Show answer
  1. KF is insoluble in organic solvent. The crown ether binds K+, bringing the salt into solution with unsolvated (“naked”) F−, a potent nucleophile that gives 1-fluorooctane by SN2.
Practice 148 L2 THI

Compare ethanol and ethanethiol with respect to acidity, nucleophilicity of the conjugate base and oxidation.

Show answer
  1. Acidity: thiol is stronger (pKa 10.5 vs 16: weaker S–H bond, larger anion). Nucleophilicity: C2H5S− > C2H5O− (larger, more polarisable). Oxidation: thiol gives the disulphide with mild oxidants; ethanol gives ethanal/ethanoic acid.
Practice 149 L2 THI

How is mustard gas thought to alkylate nucleophiles? Name the intermediate.

Show answer
  1. The sulphur lone pair displaces Cl− intramolecularly (neighbouring-group participation) to give a cyclic episulphonium (thiiranium) ion, which is attacked by the nucleophile.
Practice 150 L2 THI

18-Crown-6 (1 g) is used to dissolve KMnO4 in benzene. Calculate the moles of crown ether (M = 264.3) and the maximum mass of KMnO4 (M = 158) that can be carried at 1:1 stoichiometry.

Show answer
  1. n = 1/264.3 = 3.78 × 10−3 mol. Mass KMnO4 = 3.78 × 10−3 × 158 = 0.598 g.
