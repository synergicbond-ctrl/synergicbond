58. Dakin oxidation and the cumene (Hock) rearrangement

Dakin: an ortho- or para-hydroxybenzaldehyde (or acetophenone) + H2O2 + NaOH → catechol (from salicylaldehyde) or hydroquinone (from 4-hydroxybenzaldehyde). It is a Baeyer–Villiger in which the electron-rich aryl group migrates in preference to H: the formate ester Ar–O–CHO is formed and then hydrolysed. Hock (cumene process): cumene hydroperoxide + dilute H2SO4 → phenol + acetone. The phenyl group migrates from carbon to the electron-deficient oxygen as water leaves; the resulting oxocarbenium ion adds water and collapses to phenol and acetone.
