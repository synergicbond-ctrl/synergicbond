1.3 Structure of glucose: the experimental evidence

Glucose (C6H12O6, M = 180) was assigned its structure by a chain of simple chemical facts. Each fact points to one structural feature; JEE tends to ask "what does this reaction prove?".

Key idea The ring-size proof: the methyl glucoside (made by HCl/CH3OH) is permethylated; hydrolysis of the acetal gives 2,3,4,6-tetra-O-methyl-D-glucose. Oxidation of that product (HNO3) cleaves next to the free –OH carbon and gives trimethoxyglutaric acid (C-1 to C-5), which shows that the free –OH, and therefore the ring oxygen, was on C-5: a pyranose. A furanoside (free OH on C-4) would give dimethoxysuccinic acid (C-1 to C-4) instead.
Trap The inability of glucose to give a bisulphite addition compound or to react with Schiff's reagent is not a sign that the aldehyde is absent; the free-aldehyde form is under 0.01 % at equilibrium.

Fischer's logic: why glucose is RLRR

Fischer (1891) had no X-rays. He used only two kinds of reasoning: symmetry of the aldaric acid (optical activity of the diacid after HNO3) and chain-lengthening/shortening (Kiliani–Fischer and Ruff relationships). The crux (restated in modern language), taking the D-series as an arbitrary but fixed convention for (+)-glucose:

  • 1. Glucose, mannose and fructose give one osazone, so glucose and mannose are C-2 epimers: they share the pattern at C-3 to C-5.
  • 2. Kiliani–Fischer on the pentose arabinose gives glucose + mannose, and Ruff degradation of either gives arabinose back. So C-3 to C-5 of both hexoses = the C-2 to C-4 of arabinose.
  • 3. Arabinose gives an optically active pentaric acid (this excludes ribose RRR and xylose RLR from being arabinose-type: ribose and xylose acids are meso), and Ruff degradation of arabinose gives erythrose (its diacid is meso-tartaric acid). Pentoses that give erythrose are ribose (RRR) and arabinose (LRR); the active-acid fact selects D-arabinose = LRR.
  • 4. Hence glucose and mannose are RLRR and LLRR in some order. Both give optically active diacids (they are not allose or galactose).
  • 5. The order is fixed by one more experiment: the aldaric acid of glucose is also obtained from a second aldohexose (L-gulose; the relation is a 180° rotation of the code: RLRR → LLRL), whereas mannaric acid (LLRR, which maps onto itself on rotation) has no partner. Only RLRR has a partner, so glucose = RLRR, mannose = LLRR.
Shortcut For exams you are given the answer (D-glucose = RLRR); what is tested is whether you can run the logic with a new data set. See the worked examples below and the Level 3 problems.

Example 5 Adv

Deducing a sugar from aldaric acids

An aldopentose A is oxidised by HNO3 to an optically inactive dicarboxylic acid. Ruff degradation of A gives an aldotetrose that is oxidised to optically inactive tartaric acid. Which D-pentose(s) are possible? Use Fischer codes.

Show solution
  1. D-Pentoses and their codes: ribose RRR, arabinose LRR, xylose RLR, lyxose LLR.
  2. Optically inactive aldaric acid needs a palindromic code: RRR (ribose) and RLR (xylose). Arabinose (LRR) and lyxose (LLR) are not palindromes.
  3. Ruff degradation removes C-1, leaving codes RR (from RRR; D-erythrose) or LR (from RLR; D-threose).
  4. D-Erythrose (RR) is a palindrome: meso-tartaric acid, inactive. D-Threose (LR) gives optically active tartaric acid.
  5. Therefore the pentose is D-ribose.
Example 6 Adv

The unknown aldohexose

An aldohexose X gives an optically active aldaric acid, gives the same osazone as D-glucose, and on Ruff degradation gives a pentose Y whose aldaric acid is optically active. Identify X.

Show solution
  1. The same osazone as D-glucose means X differs from glucose only at C-2 (or X is fructose, but X is stated to be an aldohexose): X = D-mannose (LLRR).
  2. Check: mannaric acid code LLRR is not a palindrome, so it is optically active ✓.
  3. Ruff removes C-1: pentose LRR = D-arabinose; its aldaric acid (LRR) is not a palindrome, so it is active ✓.
Example 7 Main

Formula check

How many moles of acetic anhydride are consumed by one mole of glucose in making the pentaacetate and what is the molar mass of the product?

Show solution
  1. Five OH → five OAc: 5 mol of Ac2O (each gives one acetyl and one AcOH).
  2. Pentaacetate C16H22O11: M = 180 + 5 × 42 = 390 g mol−1.
