20. Aryl halides: structure and general reactivity

Aryl halides (haloarenes) have X attached directly to an sp2 carbon of a benzene ring. Their chemistry is split into three kinds of reaction:

  • Reactions at the C–X bond: nucleophilic aromatic substitution (SNAr, addition–elimination), benzyne (elimination–addition), metal insertion (Mg, Li, Na, Cu).
  • Reactions on the ring: electrophilic aromatic substitution (halogen is o,p-directing but ring-deactivating).
  • Reactions of other substituents (e.g. the nitro group of nitrochlorobenzene), not discussed here.

Why ordinary SN1/SN2/E2 fail was explained in section 19.2 (resonance, sp2 C–X, unstable cation, blocked back side). A halogen on a benzene ring is, however, not completely unreactive: it can be replaced under three sets of conditions.

Key idea The three routes for replacing ArX: (1) SNAr: strong electron-withdrawing group (NO2) at ortho/para + nucleophile; (2) benzyne: very strong base (NaNH2/liq. NH3) and an ortho hydrogen; (3) forcing industrial conditions or metal catalysis (Dow process: chlorobenzene + NaOH, 623 K, 300 atm → phenoxide; also Cu-catalysed ammonolysis). The first two have defined mechanisms and are the exam favourites.
