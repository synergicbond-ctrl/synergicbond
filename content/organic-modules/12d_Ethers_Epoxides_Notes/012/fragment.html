13. Crown ethers and cation binding

Crown ethers are macrocyclic polyethers built from repeating –CH2CH2O– units. Their names give the ring size followed by 'crown' and the number of oxygen atoms: 12-crown-4, 15-crown-5 and 18-crown-6. In the preferred conformation all the oxygen lone pairs point into the cavity, while the CH2 groups form a hydrocarbon outer surface.

12-Crown-4 (C8H16O4), 15-crown-5 (C10H20O5) and 18-crown-6 (C12H24O6).

A metal cation sits in the cavity and is held by ion–dipole attraction to all the oxygen atoms. Binding is strongest when the cation fits the cavity: 12-crown-4 prefers Li+, 15-crown-5 prefers Na+, and 18-crown-6 prefers K+. Three-dimensional analogues called cryptands bind even more strongly.

The complexed cation is surrounded by a lipophilic shell, so ionic compounds become soluble in non-polar solvents. The counter-anion is carried along but is poorly solvated, a so-called 'naked' anion, and is therefore far more reactive. This is the basis of phase-transfer applications.

  • KMnO4 with 18-crown-6 dissolves in benzene to give a purple solution that oxidises alkenes and alcohols.
  • KF with 18-crown-6 in acetonitrile is a strong nucleophilic fluoride source for halide displacement.
  • KOH or KCN become soluble and more reactive in aprotic solvents.
Key idea. Selectivity comes from size match between the cation and the cavity. A cation too large cannot enter; one too small is not held by all the oxygen atoms.
