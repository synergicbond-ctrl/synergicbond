23. Mass spectrometry in a nutshell

In electron-impact mass spectrometry the molecule loses an electron to form the molecular ion M+•, which fragments. The spectrum plots abundance against m/z. The molecular ion peak (highest m/z of the main cluster) gives the molar mass; the most intense peak is the base peak.

  • Nitrogen rule: a molecule with an *odd* number of nitrogen atoms has an *odd* molecular mass; an even number (including zero) gives an even mass (CH3NH2 31, NH2CH2CH2NH2 60, benzene 78, aniline 93).
  • Isotope patterns: chlorine: M and M+2 in the ratio 3 : 1 (³⁵Cl : ³⁷Cl); bromine: M and M+2 about 1 : 1 (⁷⁹Br : ⁸¹Br). Carbon-13 gives a small M+1 (about 1.1 % per C).
  • Common fragments (m/z): 15 CH3+; 29 C2H5+ or CHO+; 43 C3H7+ or CH3CO+; 57 C4H9+; 77 C6H5+; 91 C7H7+ (benzyl/tropylium: toluene, alkylbenzenes); 105 C6H5CO+ (benzoyl); loss of 18 (water, alcohols), 28 (CO, C2H4), 44 (CO2).
HYF M+• of 2-chloropropane is at m/z 78 and 80 (3 : 1); bromoethane at m/z 108 and 110 (1 : 1). Acetophenone M = 120, base peak 105 (loss of CH3), then 77.
Example 51 Main

Using MS

A compound shows M+• peaks at m/z 78 and 80 in the intensity ratio 3 : 1, and a base peak at m/z 43. Suggest the compound.

Show solution
  1. 3 : 1 doublet two units apart ⇒ one Cl. M = 78 with ³⁵Cl ⇒ C3H7Cl (36 + 7 + 35 = 78). Base peak 43 = C3H7+ (loss of Cl): propyl or isopropyl chloride. The compound is C3H7Cl; ¹H NMR separates the two (isopropyl: septet + doublet 6H).
Validity Check The nitrogen rule holds for neutral molecules with the usual valences; an odd molar mass means an odd number of N atoms.
Concept check

A compound has M+• at m/z 93 and a base peak at 66. Which element is certain to be present?

Show answer

An odd molar mass means an odd number of nitrogen atoms (nitrogen rule): at least one N (aniline, M = 93).
