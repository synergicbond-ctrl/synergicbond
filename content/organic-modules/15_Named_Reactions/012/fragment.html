9. Claisen–Schmidt (crossed aldol with an aromatic aldehyde)

ArCHO + R–CO–CH2R′ (ketone or aldehyde with α-H), dilute NaOH/EtOH, room temperature → ArCH=C(R′)–COR directly (dehydration is spontaneous because the C=C is conjugated with both the aromatic ring and C=O). The double bond is E (trans) because it is the more stable arrangement.

+ NaOH, EtOH⟶
Benzaldehyde + propanone → benzalacetone (E-4-phenylbut-3-en-2-one)
+ NaOH, 0–5 °C⟶
Benzaldehyde + ethanal → cinnamaldehyde (E-3-phenylprop-2-enal)
+ + NaOH, EtOH, excess PhCHO⟶
Two moles PhCHO + acetone → dibenzalacetone (1,5-diphenylpenta-1,4-dien-3-one)
Shortcut If an aldehyde ketone pair is told to give the "aldol condensation" product with a non-enolisable aldehyde, write the carbon skeleton as Ar–CH=C(R′)–CO–R: the aldehyde carbon becomes the new =CH– and the ketone's α-carbon becomes the other alkene carbon. The product always has the C=C in conjugation with the original C=O.
Example 16 Main

Predict the condensation product

Give the product of (a) benzaldehyde + acetophenone with NaOH/EtOH, (b) benzaldehyde + cyclohexanone (2 : 1).

Show solution
  1. (a) PhCH=CH–CO–Ph, chalcone (E-1,3-diphenylprop-2-en-1-one). Acetophenone only enolises on its CH3.
  2. (b) Both α-carbons of cyclohexanone react: 2,6-dibenzylidenecyclohexanone.
Example 17 Adv

Why Claisen–Schmidt works without self-condensation

Why does PhCHO + CH3CHO in dilute NaOH give mainly cinnamaldehyde and not 2-butenal?

Show solution
  1. PhCHO has no α-H, so it can only be the electrophile, and it is used in excess while ethanal is added slowly: each ethanal enolate meets PhCHO before another ethanal. The self-aldol of ethanal is reversible, whereas dehydration of the crossed aldol gives a product conjugated with the ring and the C=O, which is irreversible and drains the equilibrium toward cinnamaldehyde.
