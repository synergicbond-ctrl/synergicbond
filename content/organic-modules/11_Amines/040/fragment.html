40. Mechanism: the carbylamine (isocyanide) reaction

  • Step 1 – α-elimination. OH− removes the acidic H of CHCl3 (the CCl3− anion is stabilised by three Cl atoms, −I). The anion loses Cl−, forming dichlorocarbene :CCl2 (singlet). MO lens: a singlet carbene has a filled sp2-type σ lone pair (HOMO) and an empty p orbital (LUMO) on the same carbon, so it is both nucleophilic and strongly electrophilic. The chlorine lone pairs donate into the empty p orbital and stabilise it.
  • Step 2 – amine adds to the empty p orbital. nN(RNH2) → p(C) gives the zwitterion R–N+H2–C−Cl2.
  • Step 3 – proton transfer. H+ moves from N to C: R–NH–CHCl2.
  • Step 4 – E2/E1cB elimination (1). OH− removes N–H; Cl− leaves: R–N−–CHCl2 → R–N=CHCl (imidoyl chloride).
  • Step 5 – elimination (2). OH− removes the C–H; Cl− leaves, giving the isocyanide R–N+≡C−. Why only 1° amines: two N–H hydrogens must be lost; a 2° amine can lose only one and would be stuck at R2N–CHCl2.
