71. Simmons–Smith reaction and carbene additions to alkenes

Simmons–Smith: alkene + CH2I2 + zinc–copper couple (Zn(Cu)), ether → cyclopropane. The reagent is the carbenoid ICH2ZnI (not a free carbene); addition is concerted and syn (stereospecific): a cis-alkene gives a cis-disubstituted cyclopropane and a trans alkene a trans-disubstituted cyclopropane. No rearrangement; an adjacent –OH directs the reagent to the same face. Dichlorocarbene (CHCl3 + t-BuOK or aq. NaOH/phase-transfer catalyst) adds in the same stereospecific way to give a gem-dichlorocyclopropane. Diazomethane with UV light (CH2: carbene) or Cu catalysis also cyclopropanates.

CH₂I₂, Zn(Cu), ether⟶
Cyclohexene → bicyclo[4.1.0]heptane (norcarane)
CHCl₃, t-BuOK⟶
Cyclohexene + :CCl2 → 7,7-dichlorobicyclo[4.1.0]heptane
Key idea Carbene stereochemistry: singlet carbenes (:CCl2, :CH2 from diazomethane photolysis in liquid, Simmons–Smith) add stereospecifically syn (cis stays cis). Triplet carbenes add stepwise with a diradical, so stereochemistry is lost (a mixture of cis and trans).
