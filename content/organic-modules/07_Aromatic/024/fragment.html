Level 1: Main (single concept, direct application)

A. Aromaticity and structure of benzene

Practice 1 L1 · aromaticity

Which of the following is aromatic? (A) cyclooctatetraene (B) cyclopentadienyl anion (C) cyclopropenyl anion (D) cyclopentadienyl cation

Show answer
  1. (B). The cyclopentadienyl anion is cyclic, planar, fully conjugated with 6 π electrons (4n + 2, n = 1).
  2. (A) tub-shaped, non-planar, non-aromatic. (C) 4 π electrons: antiaromatic. (D) 4 π electrons: antiaromatic.
Practice 2 L1 · aromaticity

Which species is NOT aromatic? (A) tropylium cation (B) cyclopentadienyl anion (C) cyclopropenyl cation (D) cycloheptatrienyl anion

Show answer
  1. (D). The cycloheptatrienyl anion has 8 π electrons (6 from three C=C + 2 from the carbanion): 4n, antiaromatic if planar.
  2. (A) 6π aromatic. (B) 6π aromatic. (C) 2π aromatic.
Practice 3 L1 · aromaticity

The lone pair on nitrogen in pyridine (A) is part of the aromatic sextet (B) lies in an sp2 orbital in the plane of the ring (C) is in a p orbital perpendicular to the ring (D) is delocalised onto the ring carbons.

Show answer
  1. (B). Pyridine's six π electrons come from three C=C/C=N π bonds. The lone pair is in an in-plane sp2 orbital, perpendicular to the π system: it is available for protonation, which is why pyridine is basic (pKaH 5.2).
Practice 4 L1 · aromaticity

Why is pyrrole an extremely weak base, in contrast to pyridine?

Show answer
  1. The nitrogen lone pair of pyrrole is part of the aromatic 6π sextet (it is in a p orbital perpendicular to the ring). Protonating it would destroy the aromaticity. In pyridine the lone pair is in the plane and is not needed for the sextet.
  2. pKaH: pyrrole ≈ −3.8, pyridine 5.2.
Practice 5 L1 · aromaticity

Heats of hydrogenation: cyclohexene −120 kJ mol-1, benzene −208 kJ mol-1. Calculate the resonance energy of benzene.

Show answer
  1. Expected for a hypothetical cyclohexa-1,3,5-triene with three isolated double bonds: 3 × (−120) = −360 kJ mol-1.
  2. Resonance energy = 360 − 208 = 152 kJ mol-1 (≈ 36 kcal mol-1).
Practice 6 L1 · aromaticity

How many π electrons are present in the periphery of (a) naphthalene (b) anthracene (c) phenanthrene (d) azulene?

Show answer
  1. (a) 10, (b) 14, (c) 14, (d) 10. All satisfy 4n + 2 (n = 2, 3, 3, 2). The π electron count equals the number of sp2 carbons: C10, C14, C14, C10.
Practice 7 L1 · aromaticity

Arrange in decreasing acidity (C–H): cyclopentadiene, cyclohexa-1,3-diene, cycloheptatriene, cyclohexane.

Show answer
  1. cyclopentadiene (pKa ≈ 16) > cyclohexa-1,3-diene (≈ 35) ≈ cycloheptatriene (≈ 36) > cyclohexane (≈ 50).
  2. Only the cyclopentadienyl anion is aromatic (6π). The cycloheptatrienyl anion would have 8π and be antiaromatic.
Practice 8 L1 · aromaticity

Which compound ionises most readily in water to give a bromide ion? (A) 5-bromocyclopenta-1,3-diene (B) 7-bromocyclohepta-1,3,5-triene (C) 3-bromocyclohexene (D) 1-bromocyclopropane

Show answer
  1. (B). Loss of Br− from 7-bromocycloheptatriene gives the aromatic tropylium cation (6π), so the compound is ionic in water. (A) would give the antiaromatic cyclopentadienyl cation (4π). (C) gives an allylic cation, less stabilised. (D) gives a cyclopropyl cation (unstable).
Practice 9 L1 · aromaticity

How many Kekulé (resonance) structures can be drawn for (a) benzene (b) naphthalene (c) anthracene (d) phenanthrene?

Show answer
  1. (a) 2, (b) 3, (c) 4, (d) 5. (Counted as the number of ways to place alternating double bonds, i.e. perfect matchings of the carbon skeleton.)
  2. Resonance energies rise with the count (150, 255, 350, 385 kJ mol-1) but fall per ring.
Practice 10 L1 · aromaticity

In naphthalene, how do the C1–C2 and C2–C3 bond lengths compare, and why?

Show answer
  1. C1–C2 (136 pm) is shorter than C2–C3 (142 pm). C1–C2 is a double bond in 2 of the 3 Kekulé structures (bond order 5/3 ≈ 1.67) and C2–C3 in only 1 of 3 (bond order 4/3 ≈ 1.33).
Practice 11 L1 · aromaticity

Which has the greater resonance energy per ring: benzene, naphthalene or anthracene?

Show answer
  1. Benzene (150 kJ mol-1), then naphthalene (≈ 128), then anthracene (≈ 117). Each additional fused ring is less aromatic than benzene, which is why anthracene and naphthalene react more readily by addition.
Practice 12 L1 · aromaticity

How many 13C signals (aromatic carbons only) do the following show: benzene, toluene, o-xylene, p-xylene, mesitylene?

Show answer
  1. Benzene 1; toluene 4 (ipso, o, m, p); o-xylene 3; p-xylene 2; mesitylene 2 (C–CH3 and C–H).

Name: (a) C6H5CH2OH (b) C6H5OCH3 (c) C6H5CHO (d) p-CH3C6H4OH (e) C6H5COCH3.

How many isomeric trichlorobenzenes, tetrachlorobenzenes and dichlorotoluenes (chlorines on the ring) exist?

How many aromatic isomers are there with the formula C8H10 (including the benzene ring)?

Show answer

C. EAS: reagents, mechanism and basic facts

Practice 19 L1 · EAS

The electrophile in nitration with conc. HNO3 + conc. H2SO4 is (A) NO2 (B) NO2+ (C) NO+ (D) HNO3

Show answer
  1. (B). HNO3 + 2 H2SO4 ⇌ NO2+ + H3O+ + 2 HSO4−. NO2+ (nitronium) is linear; evidenced by Raman (1400 cm-1) and cryoscopy (i = 4).
Practice 20 L1 · EAS

In the nitration of benzene and hexadeuteriobenzene the rates are almost identical. What does this tell us?

Show answer
  1. No primary kinetic isotope effect (kH/kD ≈ 1): the C–H bond is not broken in the rate-determining step. The slow step is the attack of NO2+ on the ring, giving the arenium ion; the loss of H+ is fast.
Practice 21 L1 · EAS

Name the active electrophile in (a) bromination with Br2/FeBr3 (b) sulphonation with oleum (c) Friedel–Crafts acylation (d) Gattermann–Koch formylation (e) Vilsmeier–Haack formylation.

Show answer
  1. (a) polarised Br–Br···FeBr3 acting as Br+; (b) SO3; (c) the acylium ion RC≡O+; (d) the formyl cation HC≡O+ (from CO + HCl + AlCl3); (e) the chloroiminium ion [Me2N=CHCl]+ (from DMF + POCl3).
Practice 22 L1 · EAS

The product of benzene with CH3CH2CH2Cl/anhydrous AlCl3 is mainly (A) n-propylbenzene (B) isopropylbenzene (C) 1,2-dipropylbenzene (D) 1-chloropropylbenzene

Show answer
  1. (B). The primary cation formed undergoes a 1,2-hydride shift to the more stable secondary cation, which attacks benzene. n-Propylbenzene is made by acylation with CH3CH2COCl, then Zn–Hg/HCl.
Practice 23 L1 · EAS

Which cannot be prepared by Friedel–Crafts alkylation of benzene? (A) ethylbenzene (B) tert-butylbenzene (C) n-butylbenzene (D) isopropylbenzene

Show answer
  1. (C). 1-Chlorobutane gives the 2-butyl cation by a hydride shift, so sec-butylbenzene is formed. n-Butylbenzene needs acylation (butanoyl chloride) then reduction.
Practice 24 L1 · EAS

Why is nitrobenzene used as a solvent in Friedel–Crafts reactions?

Show answer
  1. The nitro group strongly deactivates the ring toward electrophiles, so nitrobenzene does not itself undergo Friedel–Crafts reaction, and it dissolves AlCl3 complexes. It is also polar enough to dissolve the ionic intermediates.
Practice 25 L1 · EAS

Which of the following fails in Friedel–Crafts alkylation? (A) anisole (B) toluene (C) nitrobenzene (D) chlorobenzene

Show answer
  1. (C). Nitrobenzene's ring is strongly deactivated. Anisole, toluene and chlorobenzene (weakly deactivated) all react.
Practice 26 L1 · EAS

The acylating agent in Friedel–Crafts acylation requires at least one full equivalent of AlCl3 because

Show answer
  1. the ketone product forms a stable 1 : 1 Lewis acid–base complex with AlCl3 through its carbonyl oxygen, removing the catalyst. The ketone is released by aqueous work-up. (An anhydride needs about two equivalents because the carboxylate by-product also complexes AlCl3.)
Practice 27 L1 · EAS

Acylation of benzene never gives polyacylation, but alkylation often gives polyalkylation. Explain.

Show answer
  1. An alkyl group activates the ring (+I, hyperconjugation), so the product is more reactive than benzene and is alkylated again. An acyl group is electron-withdrawing (–M, –I): the ketone is deactivated, so the reaction stops at one acyl group.
Practice 28 L1 · EAS

What is the main product of the reaction of benzene with CO + HCl in the presence of AlCl3/CuCl? (A) benzoic acid (B) benzaldehyde (C) benzyl alcohol (D) phenol

Show answer
  1. (B) benzaldehyde (Gattermann–Koch): the electrophile is the formyl cation HC≡O+.
Practice 29 L1 · EAS

Benzene sulphonic acid on heating with dilute H2SO4 (or in superheated steam) gives

Show answer
  1. Benzene (desulphonation; the reverse of sulphonation). This is the reason that –SO3H can be used as a blocking group.
Practice 30 L1 · EAS

Which reagent is used to iodinate benzene? (A) I2 only (B) I2 + HNO3 (C) KI (D) HI

Show answer
  1. (B). The oxidising agent (HNO3) converts the HI by-product back to I2 (or generates I+); otherwise the equilibrium C6H6 + I2 ⇌ C6H5I + HI lies to the left.
Practice 31 L1 · EAS

Aniline reacts with bromine water to give (A) o-bromoaniline (B) p-bromoaniline (C) 2,4,6-tribromoaniline (D) m-bromoaniline

Show answer
  1. (C). NH2 activates the ring so strongly that no catalyst is needed and all three ortho/para positions are brominated (white precipitate). To obtain the monobromo-derivative, protect NH2 as the acetamide first.
Practice 32 L1 · EAS

Phenol is treated with dilute nitric acid at room temperature. The products are separated by steam distillation. Which isomer distils and why?

Show answer
  1. o-Nitrophenol distils with steam: it has an intramolecular hydrogen bond (O–H···O of NO2), so it has less intermolecular association and is more volatile. p-Nitrophenol has intermolecular hydrogen bonds, is less volatile and stays in the flask.
Practice 33 L1 · EAS

What are the products when benzene reacts with chlorine under UV light in the absence of a catalyst?

Show answer
  1. Hexachlorocyclohexane (benzene hexachloride, BHC; the γ-isomer is lindane), C6H6Cl6: radical addition of three Cl2 molecules. This is not substitution.
Practice 34 L1 · EAS

Toluene is treated with Cl2/FeCl3 in the dark, and separately with Cl2/hν. Name the major products.

Show answer
  1. Fe/dark: o- and p-chlorotoluene (ring substitution). hν: benzyl chloride (C6H5CH2Cl), further chlorination gives benzal chloride and benzotrichloride (side chain, radical).

D. Directing effects and reactivity

Practice 35 L1 · directing

Arrange in decreasing rate of nitration: benzene, toluene, chlorobenzene, nitrobenzene, phenol.

Show answer
  1. phenol > toluene > benzene > chlorobenzene > nitrobenzene.
  2. OH (strong +M) ≫ CH3 (+I, hyperconjugation) > H > Cl (–I > +M) > NO2 (–M, –I).
Practice 36 L1 · directing

Which group is o,p-directing but deactivating? (A) –CH3 (B) –Cl (C) –NO2 (D) –OCH3

Show answer
  1. (B). Halogens withdraw electrons inductively (deactivating) but donate by resonance toward the o/p positions in the arenium ion (o/p-directing).
Practice 37 L1 · directing

Predict the main mononitration product of (a) nitrobenzene (b) benzoic acid (c) benzaldehyde (d) trifluoromethylbenzene (e) acetanilide.

Show answer
  1. (a) m-dinitrobenzene; (b) m-nitrobenzoic acid; (c) m-nitrobenzaldehyde; (d) m-nitrobenzotrifluoride; (e) p-nitroacetanilide (some ortho).
Practice 38 L1 · directing

Why does –NH2 act as o/p-director but –NH3+ as a m-director?

Show answer
  1. –NH2 has a lone pair that donates (+M) into the arenium ion for o/p attack. –NH3+ has no lone pair; it carries a full positive charge and withdraws electrons strongly by –I, so o/p attack (which puts positive charge on the carbon next to N+) is disfavoured and meta attack is the least bad.
Practice 39 L1 · directing

The major product from the bromination of toluene with Br2/FeBr3 is (A) o- and p-bromotoluene (B) m-bromotoluene (C) benzyl bromide (D) 2,4,6-tribromotoluene

Show answer
  1. (A) (para > ortho in bromination). Benzyl bromide would need light/NBS; polybromination needs excess reagent and more forcing conditions.
Practice 40 L1 · directing

Why is nitration of chlorobenzene slower than that of benzene, though the products are o and p isomers?

Show answer
  1. Cl withdraws electrons by –I through the σ-bond, which lowers the electron density of the whole ring and destabilises the arenium ion (slower). Its lone pairs donate by +M, stabilising the arenium ion only when the charge is at the carbon bearing Cl, i.e. in o/p attack (selective for those positions). The –I effect controls the rate, the +M effect the orientation.
Practice 41 L1 · directing

Which is the major product of mononitration of p-nitrotoluene? (A) 2,4-dinitrotoluene (B) 3,4-dinitrotoluene (C) 2,6-dinitrotoluene (D) 3,5-dinitrotoluene

Show answer
  1. (A). CH3 directs ortho to itself (C2/C6); the NO2 directs meta to itself (also C2/C6). Reinforcing: 2,4-dinitrotoluene (renumbered so CH3 = 1, NO2 at 2 and 4).
Practice 42 L1 · directing

Which of the following will be nitrated at the position shown? (a) p-cresol (b) p-chlorotoluene (c) m-xylene (d) p-nitroaniline. Give the position relative to the strongest director.

Show answer
  1. (a) p-Cresol: ortho to OH (position 2, i.e. meta to CH3): 4-methyl-2-nitrophenol.
  2. (b) p-Chlorotoluene: ortho to CH3: 4-chloro-2-nitrotoluene.
  3. (c) m-Xylene: C4 (ortho to one CH3, para to the other): 4-nitro-m-xylene.
  4. (d) p-Nitroaniline: ortho to NH2: 2,4-dinitroaniline (in practice acetylate first).
Practice 43 L1 · directing

Rank the activating power: –OH, –OCH3, –NHCOCH3, –CH3, –NH2.

Show answer
  1. –NH2 > –OH > –OCH3 > –NHCOCH3 > –CH3. N is a better donor than O; in the amide and ester the lone pair is shared with the C=O group. (OH and OCH3 are close; OH is conventionally ranked slightly higher.)
Practice 44 L1 · directing

How many of the following groups are meta-directing: –CHO, –COOH, –CN, –SO3H, –NO2, –CH2Cl, –CF3, –NHCOCH3, –CH=CHCOOH?

Show answer
  1. –CHO, –COOH, –CN, –SO3H, –NO2, –CF3 = 6. (–CH2Cl and –CH=CH–COOH are o/p; –NHCOCH3 is o/p.)

E. Side-chain reactions, ring addition and reduction

Practice 45 L1 · side chain

Which of the following gives benzoic acid on oxidation with hot alkaline KMnO4? (A) toluene (B) ethylbenzene (C) isopropylbenzene (D) tert-butylbenzene

Show answer
  1. (A), (B), (C). Any alkyl group with at least one benzylic H is oxidised to COOH. tert-Butylbenzene has no benzylic H and is unchanged.
Practice 46 L1 · side chain

Give the product: C6H5CH2CH3 + NBS/(PhCO)2O2, CCl4.

Show answer
  1. 1-Bromo-1-phenylethane (C6H5CHBrCH3). NBS gives a low concentration of Br2; the benzylic radical is the most stable and bromine radical is highly selective, so the secondary benzylic C–H reacts exclusively.
Practice 47 L1 · side chain

What is the product of the reaction of toluene with CrO2Cl2 in CS2, followed by hydrolysis? Name the reaction.

Show answer
  1. Benzaldehyde. Étard reaction: the chromium complex ArCH(OCrOHCl2)2 is hydrolysed, and the oxidation stops at the aldehyde stage.
Practice 48 L1 · side chain

Give the product of heating o-xylene with hot alkaline KMnO4 followed by acid, and the product obtained on heating this product strongly.

Show answer
  1. Phthalic acid (benzene-1,2-dicarboxylic acid); heating gives phthalic anhydride (loss of water, five-membered cyclic anhydride).
Practice 49 L1 · side chain

Styrene reacts with HBr (no peroxide) to give mainly (A) 2-bromo-1-phenylethane (B) 1-bromo-1-phenylethane (C) 1,2-dibromo-1-phenylethane (D) 3-bromostyrene

Show answer
  1. (B). Markovnikov addition: protonation gives the benzylic cation PhCH+CH3, which Br− captures. With peroxide the radical pathway gives (A).
Practice 50 L1 · side chain

Cumene is oxidised by air and the product is treated with dilute acid. What are the two organic products and why is the process industrially important?

Show answer
  1. Phenol and acetone (cumene → cumene hydroperoxide → Hock rearrangement). It is the main industrial source of both (≈ 90 % of the world's phenol), and uses benzene and propene as feedstock.
Practice 51 L1 · ring reduction

What is the product of the Birch reduction (Na, NH3(l), EtOH) of (a) benzene (b) anisole (c) benzoic acid?

Show answer
  1. (a) Cyclohexa-1,4-diene. (b) 1-Methoxycyclohexa-1,4-diene (OMe on a double bond). (c) Cyclohexa-2,5-diene-1-carboxylic acid (COOH on the sp3 carbon).
Practice 52 L1 · ring reduction

Hydrogenation of benzene with H2/Ni at 450 K gives (A) cyclohexene (B) cyclohexane (C) cyclohexa-1,3-diene (D) a mixture of partially hydrogenated products

Show answer
  1. (B). Partially hydrogenated intermediates (cyclohexadienes, cyclohexene) hydrogenate faster than benzene itself, so benzene goes directly to cyclohexane.
Practice 53 L1 · ring reduction

Ozonolysis of benzene (followed by Zn/H2O) gives how many molecules of glyoxal per molecule of benzene?

Show answer
  1. 3. Each C=C of the Kekulé structure is cleaved to give OHC–CHO; three C=C give three glyoxal molecules.
Practice 54 L1 · side chain

Name the compounds formed when toluene is chlorinated in sunlight successively with 1, 2 and 3 moles of Cl2, and the product on hydrolysis of the dichloro compound.

Show answer
  1. Benzyl chloride, benzal chloride (benzylidene dichloride), benzotrichloride (benzylidyne trichloride). Benzal chloride on hydrolysis gives benzaldehyde; benzotrichloride on hydrolysis gives benzoic acid.

F. Polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons

Practice 55 L1 · polycyclic

Naphthalene undergoes nitration mainly at (A) C1 (B) C2 (C) equally at C1 and C2 (D) C9

Show answer
  1. (A) C1 (α). α-Attack gives an arenium ion with two benzenoid resonance contributors (benzene ring intact); β-attack has only one.
Practice 56 L1 · polycyclic

Sulphonation of naphthalene at 353 K gives mainly the (A) 1-sulphonic acid (B) 2-sulphonic acid; and at 433 K mainly the (C) 1-sulphonic acid (D) 2-sulphonic acid.

Show answer
  1. 353 K: (A) 1-sulphonic acid (kinetic product). 433 K: (D) 2-sulphonic acid (thermodynamic product, less peri strain).
Practice 57 L1 · polycyclic

Oxidation of naphthalene with V2O5/O2 at 650 K gives

Show answer
  1. Phthalic anhydride (industrial route). With CrO3/AcOH, 1,4-naphthoquinone is obtained instead.
Practice 58 L1 · polycyclic

Anthracene is oxidised by CrO3/AcOH to (A) phthalic acid (B) anthraquinone (C) benzoic acid (D) naphthoquinone

Show answer
  1. (B) anthraquinone: the central ring (C9, C10) is oxidised, leaving two benzene rings intact.
Practice 59 L1 · polycyclic

How many monochloro derivatives are possible for (a) naphthalene (b) anthracene (c) phenanthrene?

Show answer
  1. (a) 2; (b) 3 (1, 2, 9); (c) 5 (1, 2, 3, 4, 9).
Practice 60 L1 · polycyclic

1-Nitronaphthalene is nitrated further. Which dinitro compounds form?

Show answer
  1. 1,8-dinitronaphthalene (major) and 1,5-dinitronaphthalene. The nitro group deactivates its own ring, so the second NO2 enters the other ring at an α-position (C5 or C8).
Practice 61 L1 · polycyclic

Vigorous oxidation of 1-nitronaphthalene gives 3-nitrophthalic acid, but 1-aminonaphthalene gives phthalic acid. Explain.

Show answer
  1. The more electron-rich ring is oxidised. In 1-nitronaphthalene, NO2 deactivates its ring, so the unsubstituted ring is cleaved and the nitro ring survives (3-nitrophthalic acid). In 1-naphthylamine, NH2 activates its ring, which is the one cleaved, and the unsubstituted ring survives as phthalic acid.

G. Heterocycles

Practice 62 L1 · heterocycles

The order of reactivity toward electrophilic substitution is (A) furan > pyrrole > thiophene (B) pyrrole > furan > thiophene (C) thiophene > furan > pyrrole (D) benzene > pyrrole > furan

Show answer
  1. (B) pyrrole > furan > thiophene > benzene. N donates its lone pair best (less electronegative than O); S, in spite of low electronegativity, overlaps poorly and keeps the most aromatic character.
Practice 63 L1 · heterocycles

In which position does electrophilic substitution occur in (a) pyrrole (b) pyridine (c) naphthalene (d) thiophene?

Show answer
  1. (a) C2 (α); (b) C3 (β); (c) C1 (α); (d) C2 (α).
Practice 64 L1 · heterocycles

Which is the strongest base: (A) pyridine (B) pyrrole (C) aniline (D) piperidine?

Show answer
  1. (D) piperidine (pKaH 11.1; sp3 N lone pair). Pyridine 5.2 > aniline 4.6 > pyrrole −3.8.
Practice 65 L1 · heterocycles

Pyridine reacts with sodamide (NaNH2) at 383 K to give (A) 2-aminopyridine (B) 3-aminopyridine (C) 4-aminopyridine (D) piperidine

Show answer
  1. (A). Chichibabin amination; NH2− adds at C2, where the negative charge of the intermediate is carried by the electronegative nitrogen; H− is lost (as H2).
Practice 66 L1 · heterocycles

Which test is used to detect thiophene in benzene? (A) Lassaigne (B) Indophenin (C) Fehling's (D) Tollens'

Show answer
  1. (B). Isatin + conc. H2SO4 gives a blue dye (indophenin) with thiophene. Thiophene can also be removed from coal-tar benzene by shaking with conc. H2SO4.
Practice 67 L1 · heterocycles

Write the product of Paal–Knorr synthesis from hexane-2,5-dione and (a) NH3 (b) P4S10.

Show answer
  1. (a) 2,5-dimethylpyrrole; (b) 2,5-dimethylthiophene. (With P2O5 the product is 2,5-dimethylfuran.)

Practice 68 L1 · heterocycles

Why does pyridine not undergo Friedel–Crafts alkylation or acylation?

Show answer
  1. (i) The ring is electron-poor (π-deficient, like nitrobenzene). (ii) The basic N lone pair complexes with AlCl3, placing a positive charge on the ring N (further deactivation).
Practice 69 L1 · heterocycles

Which is more reactive in SNAr with methoxide: 2-chloropyridine or 3-chloropyridine? Why?

Show answer

Pyrrole is treated with CHCl3/KOH. What is formed?

Show answer
  1. 3-Chloropyridine (Ciamician–Dennstedt ring expansion): dichlorocarbene adds across the C2–C3 bond; the cyclopropane opens with loss of Cl− to give the six-membered ring.

H. SNAr, benzyne and diazonium reactions

Practice 71 L1 · SNAr

Which of the following reacts fastest with NaOH(aq)? (A) chlorobenzene (B) 4-nitrochlorobenzene (C) 2,4-dinitrochlorobenzene (D) 3-nitrochlorobenzene

Show answer
  1. (C). Two NO2 groups (ortho and para) stabilise the Meisenheimer anion most. Order: C > B ≫ D ≈ A.
Practice 72 L1 · SNAr

In SNAr, the relative reactivity of leaving groups in 4-X-nitrobenzene toward CH3O− is (A) F > Cl > Br > I (B) I > Br > Cl > F (C) all equal (D) Cl > F > Br > I

Practice 80 L1 · synthesis

Outline the conversion of benzene into m-dinitrobenzene.

Show answer
  1. Benzene + conc. HNO3/conc. H2SO4, 330 K → nitrobenzene; then fuming HNO3/conc. H2SO4, 370 K → m-dinitrobenzene (NO2 is a m-director).
Practice 81 L1 · synthesis

Convert toluene into (a) p-nitrobenzoic acid (b) m-nitrobenzoic acid.

Show answer
  1. (a) Nitrate first (HNO3/H2SO4, separate p-nitrotoluene), then oxidise (KMnO4/OH−, then H3O+).
  2. (b) Oxidise first (to benzoic acid, a m-director), then nitrate.
