4. Radicals: a verified case

The triphenylmethyl radical is far more stable than the diphenylmethyl or benzyl radical. Its three phenyl rings are not coplanar; they are twisted by about 30° out of a plane, like a propeller. The delocalisation is therefore less than ideal (Ref. [2], p. 979).

Example 8 · Radical · twist reduces delocalisation

Stability: Ph₃C• is much more stable than Ph₂CH• or PhCH₂•

Ref. [2] states that the stability is due mainly to steric shielding of the central carbon, not to electronic delocalisation. The twist is evidence of reduced π-overlap, but it is not the main cause of the stability.

Source: Ref. [2], p. 979.
