16.3 Fries rearrangement

An aryl ester (made from a phenol and an acid chloride/anhydride) with a stoichiometric amount of AlCl3 rearranges to o- and p-hydroxyaryl ketones. The acyl group migrates from O to the ring.

AlCl₃, low T (about 298 K)⟶
Low temperature, polar solvent: p-hydroxyacetophenone (kinetic product)
AlCl₃, high T (above 433 K)⟶
High temperature, non-polar solvent: o-hydroxyacetophenone (thermodynamic; chelated to Al, intramolecular H-bond)

Mechanism: AlCl3 coordinates to the carbonyl oxygen (the more basic one), polarises the ester and releases an acylium ion RCO+ (an AlCl3–phenoxide complex remains); the acylium ion then does an electrophilic substitution on the ring of the complexed phenoxide (the reaction is largely intermolecular), mostly at the para position at low temperature and at the ortho position (more stable chelate) at high temperature.

Trap In the Fries rearrangement more than one equivalent of AlCl3 is required, because the product ketone and the phenoxide both complex the Lewis acid. Direct Friedel–Crafts acylation of phenol with RCOCl/AlCl3 gives mainly the phenyl ester (O-acylation), not the ketone; the Fries rearrangement is the indirect route to hydroxyaryl ketones. Electron-withdrawing groups on the ring stop the reaction; bulky acyl groups favour the para product.
