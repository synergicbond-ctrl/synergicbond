Problem set B: detection of elements (Lassaigne and related tests)

Practice 27 Foundation · MCQ

The blue colour in the Lassaigne test for nitrogen is due to: (A) Fe(CN)3 (B) Fe4[Fe(CN)6]3 (C) Na4[Fe(CN)6] (D) [Fe(SCN)]2+

Show answer
  1. Na4[Fe(CN)6] is yellow-green and colourless in solution. With Fe3+ it forms ferric ferrocyanide (Prussian blue), Fe4[Fe(CN)6]3. (D) is blood red.

Answer: (B)

Practice 28 Foundation · MCQ

The violet colour in the sodium nitroprusside test for sulphur is due to: (A) PbS (B) [Fe(CN)5NOS]4− (C) Na2S (D) [Fe(SCN)]2+

Show answer
  1. S2− attacks the NO ligand of [Fe(CN)5NO]2− forming the violet [Fe(CN)5NOS]4−.

Answer: (B)

Practice 29 Foundation · MCQ

Which silver halide dissolves in dilute ammonia but not the others? (A) AgI (B) AgBr (C) AgCl (D) AgF

Show answer
  1. Only AgCl (Ksp 1.8 × 10-10) dissolves readily in dilute NH3; AgBr dissolves slightly (only in conc. NH3) and AgI not at all. AgF is itself soluble in water.

Answer: (C)

Practice 30 Foundation

A compound gives a blood-red colour with FeCl3 in its sodium fusion extract but no Prussian blue. Name the ion responsible and the elements present.

Show answer
  1. Blood red: [Fe(SCN)]2+. SCN− forms from Na + C + N + S when sodium is insufficient, so N and S are both present.

Answer: SCN−; N and S

Practice 31 Application · integer

How many of the following do NOT give Prussian blue in Lassaigne's test for nitrogen: C6H5N2+Cl−, H2N–NH2, NH2OH·HCl, C6H5NH2, (NH2)2CO, C6H5NO2?

Show answer
  1. Diazonium salt (loses N2 on heating), hydrazine and hydroxylamine hydrochloride (no carbon) fail. Aniline, urea and nitrobenzene succeed.

Answer: 3

Practice 32 Application · integer

How many of these compounds give a positive test for both nitrogen (Prussian blue, with excess sodium) and sulphur: thiourea, cysteine, sulphanilic acid, benzenesulphonic acid, methyl isothiocyanate, thiophenol?

Show answer
  1. Thiourea (N, S), cysteine (N, S), sulphanilic acid (N in NH2, S in SO3H), methyl isothiocyanate CH3NCS (N, S) contain both; benzenesulphonic acid and thiophenol contain only S.

Answer: 4

Practice 33 Application

Nitrogen from 0.28 g of N in an organic compound is converted entirely to Prussian blue Fe4[Fe(CN)6]3 (anhydrous M = 859.2). What mass of Prussian blue could form? (Each formula unit contains 18 CN groups.)

Show answer
  1. Moles N = 0.28/14.0 = 0.0200 mol = moles CN−.
  2. Moles Prussian blue = 0.0200/18 = 1.111 × 10−3 mol.
  3. Mass = 1.111 × 10−3 × 859.2 = 0.954 g.

Answer: 0.95 g

Practice 34 Advanced

What minimum equilibrium concentration of NH3 is needed to dissolve 0.010 mol of AgBr in 1.0 L? Ksp(AgBr) = 5.0 × 10-13, Kf[Ag(NH3)2]+ = 1.7 × 107. What is the initial NH3 concentration needed?

Show answer
  1. AgBr(s) + 2NH3 ⇌ [Ag(NH3)2]+ + Br−; K = KspKf = 8.50 × 10−6.
  2. K = s2/[NH3]2 ⇒ [NH3] = s/√K = 0.010/2.915 × 10−3 = 3.43 M (equilibrium).
  3. Initial = equilibrium + 2s = 3.43 + 0.02 = 3.45 M: needs concentrated ammonia, which is why AgBr is only sparingly soluble in the laboratory reagent.

Answer: ≈ 3.4 M

Practice 35 Advanced

Repeat for 0.050 mol of AgCl in 1.0 L (Ksp = 1.8 × 10-10). Initial NH3 concentration?

Show answer
  1. K = 3.06 × 10−3; equilibrium [NH3] = 0.050/√K = 0.90 M; initial = 0.90 + 0.10 = 1.00 M.
  2. So AgCl dissolves in modest ammonia (less than 1 M), whereas AgBr needs more than 5 M.

Answer: ≈ 1.00 M

Practice 36 Foundation

Why is the sodium fusion extract boiled with dilute HNO3 before AgNO3 is added in the test for halogens?

Show answer
  1. To decompose NaCN to HCN and Na2S to H2S, which escape; otherwise AgCN (white) and Ag2S (black) would precipitate and mask or mimic the silver halide.
Practice 37 Foundation

Write the balanced equation for the formation of the lead sulphide precipitate from sodium sulphide and lead acetate, and name the colour.

Show answer
  1. Na2S + (CH3COO)2Pb → PbS↓ + 2CH3COONa. PbS is black.

Answer: black ppt

Practice 39 Application

A compound X gives the following results in its Lassaigne extract. (i) No Prussian blue. (ii) Black ppt with lead acetate. (iii) After boiling with HNO3, AgNO3 gives a pale yellow precipitate that dissolves slightly in conc. NH3. Which elements are present?

Show answer
  1. (i) no N. (ii) S2−: sulphur. (iii) pale yellow, sparingly soluble in NH3: AgBr. So X contains C, S and Br (plus H/O possibly).

Answer: S and Br (no N)

Practice 40 Application

In the "layer test" an acidified Lassaigne extract with CCl4 and chlorine water gave a violet layer which disappeared on adding more chlorine water. Identify the halogen and write the equation for the disappearance.

Show answer
  1. Violet I2 ⇒ iodide present. Excess Cl2: I2 + 5Cl2 + 6H2O → 2HIO3 + 10HCl; iodic acid is colourless.

Answer: iodine; I2 → HIO3

Practice 41 Foundation

Name the reagent used to detect phosphorus in an organic compound and the precipitate formed.

Show answer
  1. The compound is fused with Na2O2 (or oxidised with conc. HNO3) to phosphate; ammonium molybdate then gives a canary-yellow precipitate of (NH4)3PO4·12MoO3.

Answer: ammonium molybdate; yellow ppt

Practice 42 Application

Dry organic compound (0.2 g) was heated with CuO and the gases passed into lime water, which turned milky but cleared on prolonged passage of gas. Explain with equations.

Show answer
  1. CO2 + Ca(OH)2 → CaCO3↓ + H2O (milky). Excess CO2: CaCO3 + CO2 + H2O → Ca(HCO3)2 (soluble), so the milkiness disappears.
Practice 43 Application · multi-correct

Which statements about Lassaigne's test are correct? (A) Sodium is used in excess. (B) Fresh FeSO4 must be used. (C) The extract is boiled with HNO3 before testing for sulphur. (D) For a compound with N and S in insufficient sodium, FeCl3 gives a blood-red colour.

Show answer
  1. (A) true: excess ensures all N, S, X are converted and SCN− is broken down. (B) true: aged FeSO4 contains Fe3+/hydrolysed products. (C) false: HNO3 destroys S2− as H2S; the S test is done without boiling with HNO3. (D) true.

Answer: A, B, D

Practice 44 Application

On adding FeSO4 to a sodium fusion extract and boiling, then acidifying, a green solution results instead of blue. Give two possible reasons and the remedy.

Show answer
  1. (i) Very small amount of nitrogen so only little Prussian blue forms, mixed with yellow Fe3+/Fe2+ species giving green; (ii) old FeSO4 or insufficient aeration. Remedy: add a drop of FeCl3, concentrate, allow to stand, or repeat with a larger sample and fresh reagent.
Practice 45 Application

Why does Beilstein's test sometimes give a green flame with compounds that do not contain halogen? Name a class of compounds that give this false positive.

Show answer
  1. The green flame arises from volatile copper compounds. Copper cyanide/nitrogen-containing species formed from some nitrogen compounds (urea, pyridine derivatives, some amides) are volatile and give a similar flame; the test is therefore preliminary and is confirmed by Lassaigne's test.

Answer: N-containing compounds, e.g. urea

Practice 46 Application · integer

How many of the following give a precipitate with AgNO3 after the sodium fusion extract has been boiled with HNO3: CCl4, C6H5Cl, C6H5F, CHI3, C6H5NH2, CH3CH2SH?

Show answer
  1. Halogen compounds giving AgX: CCl4 (AgCl), C6H5Cl (AgCl), CHI3 (AgI). C6H5F gives NaF, which does not precipitate with Ag+ (AgF soluble). Aniline and ethanethiol have no halogen (S2− lost as H2S).

Answer: 3

Practice 47 Advanced

A compound C2H3NS gives, with excess sodium, Prussian blue and a violet colour with nitroprusside; with insufficient sodium, a blood-red colour but no Prussian blue. Is this consistent? Suggest two structures.

Show answer
  1. C2H3NS contains N and S: with excess sodium CN− and S2− both form (Prussian blue, nitroprusside violet). With insufficient sodium SCN− forms ⇒ blood red, no free CN−. Consistent.
  2. DBE = (4 + 2 + 1 − 3)/2 = 2. Structures: CH3–S–C≡N (methyl thiocyanate) and CH3–N=C=S (methyl isothiocyanate).

Answer: consistent; CH3SCN or CH3NCS

Practice 48 Application

Explain why nitrobenzene gives a positive Lassaigne nitrogen test but a nitro group would give an unreliable Kjeldahl result.

Show answer
  1. In the sodium fusion the C–N bond is cleaved by Na at red heat and NaCN forms regardless of the oxidation state of N. In Kjeldahl digestion with H2SO4, the nitro nitrogen (oxidised) is not quantitatively converted to NH4+; part is lost as N2/N-oxides.
Practice 49 Foundation · MCQ

Which of the following tests is NOT used for sulphur? (A) lead acetate (B) sodium nitroprusside (C) BaCl2 after oxidation (D) FeSO4/Prussian blue

Show answer
  1. Prussian blue is the nitrogen test.

Answer: (D)

Practice 50 Application

A compound (0.1 g) is strongly heated with CuO; the vapours turn anhydrous CuSO4 blue and lime water milky. Does this prove the compound is organic? What additional test confirms the elements?

Show answer
  1. It proves C and H are present (CO2 and H2O formed), though carbonates and hydrated salts would mislead (a hydrated carbonate gives both). Confirm further by Lassaigne's test and combustion analysis; organic character is supported by the C–H content and the solubility behaviour.
Practice 51 Application

To distinguish aniline from nitrobenzene by an element test would be impossible. Why? Suggest a simple chemical test instead.

Show answer
  1. Both contain C, H, N, (O) so both give Prussian blue. Use a functional-group test: aniline dissolves in dil. HCl, gives carbylamine and azo-dye tests; nitrobenzene does not.
