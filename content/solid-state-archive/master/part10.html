10.1 Origin of magnetic moments

Magnetism arises mainly from electron orbital motion and electron spin. In many transition-metal and lanthanide compounds, partially filled shells carry permanent moments. In closed-shell species, applied fields induce small opposing currents that produce diamagnetism.

μ=−gμBJℏ\boldsymbol{\mu}= -g\mu_B\frac{\mathbf{J}}{\hbar}μ=−gμB​ℏJ​

For many first-row transition-metal ions in complexes where orbital angular momentum is substantially quenched, the spin-only approximation is useful.

μso=n(n+2) μB\mu_{so}=\sqrt{n(n+2)}\,\mu_Bμso​=n(n+2)​μB​

nnn is the number of unpaired electrons.

10.3 Diamagnetism and paramagnetism

Diamagnetic substances contain no required permanent net moment in zero field and develop an induced moment opposite to the applied field. Their susceptibility is nearly temperature independent. Paramagnets contain permanent moments that are randomly oriented without a field and partially align when a field is applied.

χ=CT\chi=\frac{C}{T}χ=TC​

Curie's law for an ideal paramagnet.

μeff=2.828χMT μB\mu_{eff}=2.828\sqrt{\chi_MT}\,\mu_Bμeff​=2.828χM​T​μB​

10.4 Ferromagnetism and domains

In a ferromagnet, exchange interactions favour parallel alignment of neighbouring moments below the Curie temperature TCT_CTC​. A macroscopic specimen divides into domains to reduce magnetostatic energy. In an unmagnetised sample, domain magnetisations can cancel even though each domain is internally ordered.

An applied field moves domain walls and rotates domain moments. After the field is removed, some magnetisation can remain, producing hysteresis.

Hysteresis termMeaningMaterial implication
SaturationAll accessible domains alignedMaximum magnetisation
RemanenceMagnetisation at zero field after saturationHigh for permanent magnets
Coercive fieldReverse field required to reduce MMM to zeroLow for transformer cores; high for permanent magnets
Loop areaEnergy dissipated per magnetic cycleSmall for soft magnetic materials

Above TCT_CTC​, thermal disorder destroys long-range ferromagnetic order and the material becomes paramagnetic, often following a Curie–Weiss law.

χ=CT−θ\chi=\frac{C}{T-\theta}χ=T−θC​

For a ferromagnet, θ\thetaθ is generally positive and close to TCT_CTC​.

3
Worked example

Soft versus hard magnet

Which material is preferred for a transformer core: one with a narrow or wide hysteresis loop?

  1. The core is magnetised and demagnetised every ac cycle.
  2. Energy loss per cycle equals the loop area.
  3. A narrow loop also implies low coercivity.
A soft magnetic material with a narrow hysteresis loop is preferred.

10.5 Antiferromagnetism and ferrimagnetism

In an antiferromagnet, neighbouring sublattice moments align antiparallel with equal magnitude, giving zero ideal net magnetisation below the Néel temperature TNT_NTN​. In a ferrimagnet, sublattice moments are antiparallel but unequal, so a net moment remains.

OrderSublattice relationNet momentExamples
FerromagneticParallelLargeFe, Co, Ni
AntiferromagneticAntiparallel and equalIdeally zeroMnO, NiO, Cr2_22​O3_33​
FerrimagneticAntiparallel and unequalNon-zeroFe3_33​O4_44​, ferrites MFe2_22​O4_44​

In magnetite, Fe3+^{3+}3+ moments on tetrahedral and octahedral sites largely cancel, while Fe2+^{2+}2+ moments on octahedral sites produce the net ferrimagnetic moment in the simplified ionic picture.

Paramagnetic does not mean non-interacting forever

A material can be paramagnetic above an ordering temperature and become ferro-, antiferro- or ferrimagnetic below it.

Revision strategy

For any numerical problem, first identify the structural model and write one geometrical relation, one counting relation and one physical-property relation. Most difficult-looking questions reduce to a controlled combination of these three.

10.10 Critical fields and magnetic measurement

For an ideal Type I superconductor, a commonly used empirical temperature dependence of the thermodynamic critical field is

Hc(T)=Hc(0)[1−(TTc)2]H_c(T)=H_c(0)\left[1-\left(\frac{T}{T_c}\right)^2\right]Hc​(T)=Hc​(0)[1−(Tc​T​)2]

Type II superconductors instead possess lower and upper critical fields Hc1H_{c1}Hc1​ and Hc2H_{c2}Hc2​, with quantised vortices in the mixed state between them.

MethodWhat it measures
Gouy balanceForce on a sample in a non-uniform magnetic field; used to estimate susceptibility
Faraday balanceMagnetic force on a small sample in a field gradient

Measurement sign

Diamagnetic samples are driven toward weaker field; paramagnetic samples toward stronger field in a field gradient.
