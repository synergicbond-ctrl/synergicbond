36. Reduction to alcohols: stereochemistry and selectivity

For a ketone with two different groups, hydride attack on the two faces of the planar C=O gives enantiomers (racemic mixture). For a cyclic ketone, attack can be axial or equatorial.

  • Small hydrides (NaBH4, LiAlH4) attack cyclohexanones mainly from the axial direction, giving the equatorial alcohol (trans in 4-tert-butylcyclohexanone, about 80–90 % trans with NaBH4).
  • Very bulky hydrides (L-Selectride, lithium tri-sec-butylborohydride) attack from the equatorial direction because the axial approach is blocked by the axial hydrogens at C-3 and C-5, giving the axial alcohol (cis, over 90 %).

36.1 Reductive amination
An aldehyde or ketone is condensed with ammonia or an amine, and the imine is reduced in situ (H2/Ni, NaBH3CN, or NaBH(OAc)3). NaBH3CN reduces the protonated imine (iminium) faster than the carbonyl at pH 6–7, so no alcohol forms.
