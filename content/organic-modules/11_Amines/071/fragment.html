Practice 134 L2 Mechanism

Write the sequence of intermediates in the Hofmann bromamide reaction of benzamide and say which step is the rearrangement.

Br₂, OH⁻⟶ OH⁻, −Br⁻ (Ph migrates)⟶ H₂O⟶ −CO₂⟶
Benzamide → N-bromobenzamide → phenyl isocyanate → carbamic acid → aniline
Show answer
  1. The rearrangement is the concerted step in which C6H5 migrates from the carbonyl carbon to nitrogen as bromide leaves.

N-bromoamide → (deprotonation) bromoamide anion → phenyl isocyanate (the 1,2-shift of Ph from C to N with loss of Br−) → carbamic acid → aniline + CO2.

Practice 136 L2 Stereo

(R)-2-Phenylpropanamide is subjected to Hofmann degradation. Predict the product and its configuration.

(R)-2-phenylpropanamide
Show answer
  1. The migrating carbon never becomes free; the migration is intramolecular and suprafacial, so the spatial arrangement is kept. Priorities in the amide: C(=O)N [O,O,N] > phenyl [C,C,C] > CH3 > H; in the amine: N > phenyl > CH3 > H. The same order, so the same descriptor (R).

1-Phenylethanamine with retention of configuration. (R)-amide gives (R)-amine because the CIP priority order is unchanged: NH2 replaces CONH2 in the same position.

Practice 138 L2 Synthesis

Propose a synthesis of the following from benzene and any inorganic reagents: (a) aniline, (b) benzylamine, (c) N-methylaniline.

Show answer
  1. Note the care needed: acetylation + LiAlH4 gives the N-ethyl compound. Formylation + LiAlH4 gives N-methyl.

Practice 141 L2 Zinin

m-Dinitrobenzene gives m-nitroaniline with (NH4)2S but m-phenylenediamine with Sn/HCl. Explain the selectivity.

Show answer
  1. After the first NO2 is reduced to NH2 (an electron donor), the remaining NO2 is less easily reduced by the mild reagent. Both are reduced by metal/acid.

(NH4)2S (a mild, selective sulphide reductant: Zinin) reduces only one nitro group; Sn/HCl is a strong reducing system that reduces both.

Practice 142 L2 Nitrile

Benzonitrile is treated with (i) CH3MgBr in ether, then H3O+; the product is then treated with (ii) NaBH4. Identify the products and explain why the Grignard reagent does not add twice to the nitrile.

Show answer
  1. NaBH4 reduces the C=O of acetophenone to CH–OH.

After (i): acetophenone. After (ii): 1-phenylethanol. The first addition gives an imine salt (C=N−MgBr+), which is negatively charged and not electrophilic enough for a second attack; hydrolysis then gives the ketone.

Practice 143 L2 Elimination

Predict the major alkene in the Hofmann elimination of (a) 2-aminobutane, (b) 2-aminopentane, (c) 3-aminopentane, (d) 2-amino-2-methylbutane (after exhaustive methylation, Ag2O, heat).

Show answer
  1. Rule: least substituted alkene. (c) has no choice: the only β-hydrogens are on the two equivalent CH2 groups and the alkene is pent-2-ene (E and Z mixture, E major). (d) α-carbon bears CH3 (×2) and CH2CH3; abstraction from the CH3 gives CH2=C(CH3)CH2CH3.

(a) but-1-ene; (b) pent-1-ene; (c) pent-2-ene (only choice – both β-carbons are CH2, giving CH3CH=CHCH2CH3); (d) 2-methylbut-1-ene.

Practice 144 L2 Elimination

Explain why the Hofmann elimination of (CH3)3N+CH2CH2C6H5 gives styrene (PhCH=CH2) rather than a "least-substituted" product.

Show answer
  1. Only one alkene is possible: styrene, conjugated and stable.

There is only one β-carbon (the benzylic CH2); the benzylic hydrogen is also the most acidic β-hydrogen (the E1cB-like transition state is stabilised by the phenyl ring).

Practice 147 L2 Cope

Predict the products of the Cope elimination of N,N-dimethylcyclohexylamine N-oxide and say why the elimination is syn.

Show answer

Cyclohexene + N,N-dimethylhydroxylamine, (CH3)2NOH. The N-oxide oxygen abstracts a β-hydrogen through a planar five-membered cyclic transition state (Ei), which forces the H and N+ to lie on the same side (syn).

Practice 148 L2 Gabriel limit

Why does the Gabriel synthesis fail with secondary alkyl halides in practice though a secondary amine product is not formed? What reagent would you use to prepare isopropylamine instead?

Show answer

Phthalimide anion is a bulky nucleophile; with 2° halides E2 competes. Use reductive amination of acetone (NH3, H2/Ni or NaBH3CN) or the Hofmann degradation of 2-methylpropanamide.

Practice 149 L2 Separation

A mixture of ethylamine, diethylamine and triethylamine is treated with excess benzenesulphonyl chloride/aqueous KOH. Describe how the three can be separated.

Show answer
  1. This is the classical Hinsberg separation.

Filter the insoluble material: diethylamine's sulphonamide (solid) and unreacted triethylamine (oil) remain, while the ethylamine sulphonamide stays dissolved as the potassium salt. Acidifying the filtrate precipitates the 1° sulphonamide (hydrolysed by acid to ethylamine). Distil or extract the residue with dilute HCl: the 3° amine dissolves; the 2° sulphonamide remains and is hydrolysed (H2SO4, heat) to diethylamine.

Practice 150 L2 Mechanism

Write the mechanism of the carbylamine reaction in three steps for CH3NH2.

Show answer

(i) CHCl3 + OH− → :CCl2 + Cl− + H2O. (ii) CH3NH2 adds to :CCl2: CH3N+H2–C−Cl2, which loses H+ and gains H+ to give CH3NH–CHCl2. (iii) Two E2 eliminations of HCl by KOH give CH3–N+≡C−.

Practice 155 L2 Equivalent

0.930 g of a primary amine A is neutralised exactly by 10.0 mL of 1.00 M HCl, and A gives a stable diazonium salt at 273 K. Find the molar mass and identify A.

Show answer
  1. Moles HCl = 0.0100 = moles of monoacidic amine → M = 0.930/0.0100 = 93. A saturated aliphatic 1° amine would need 14n + 17 = 93 → n = 5.4 (no); a 1° aromatic amine C6H5NH2 (M = 93) fits and gives a stable diazonium salt.

M = 93 g mol-1; A = aniline.

Practice 156 L2 Methylation count

Two isomeric amines A and B (C4H11N) each consume three equivalents of CH3I and each gives, after Ag2O and heating, trimethylamine and 2-methylpropene as the only alkene. Identify A and B.

Show answer
  1. Three CH3I → both are 1°. In A the only β-hydrogen is the tertiary C–H of CH(CH3)2; removing it with loss of NMe3 from CH2 gives CH2=C(CH3)2. In B the β-hydrogens are on the three equivalent methyl groups; elimination gives the same alkene CH2=C(CH3)2.

A = 2-methylpropan-1-amine, (CH3)2CHCH2NH2; B = tert-butylamine, (CH3)3CNH2.
