7. Friedel–Crafts alkylation and acylation

Alkylation: Ar–H + R–X + AlCl3 (or R–OH/H+, or alkene/H+) → Ar–R. AlCl3 removes Cl− from RCl to give R+ (or a polarised complex); the carbocation attacks the ring (σ-complex), and H+ is lost. Acylation: Ar–H + RCOCl (or (RCO)2O) + ≥ 1 equiv. AlCl3 → Ar–CO–R. The electrophile is the acylium ion R–C≡O+; it cannot rearrange, and the product ketone is deactivated and complexed to AlCl3 (which is why a full equivalent of catalyst is needed).

Key idea Straight-chain alkylbenzene (n-propyl, n-butyl) = acylate, then reduce. Branched alkylbenzene with a tertiary carbon on the ring (t-butylbenzene) = alkylation (with t-BuCl, or isobutene/H+), since the tertiary cation is the most stable one.
Trap Alkylation needs a carbocation-forming alkylating agent. Vinyl and aryl halides (CH2=CHCl, C6H5Cl) do not alkylate benzene, because their carbocations are too unstable. Aryl–aryl bonds cannot be made by FC.
Example 11 Main

Predict the FC product

Give the major product with benzene and AlCl3: (a) 1-chlorobutane, (b) 2-chloro-2-methylpropane, (c) 3-methylbutanoyl chloride then Zn–Hg/HCl.

Show solution
  1. (a) CH3CH2CH2CH2+ → 2° cation by hydride shift; the major product is sec-butylbenzene (butan-2-ylbenzene), not n-butylbenzene.
  2. (b) tert-Butyl cation: tert-butylbenzene; only one product since the t-butyl group blocks further alkylation sterically.
  3. (c) Acylation gives (CH3)2CHCH2COC6H5; Clemmensen reduces C=O to CH2: isopentylbenzene (3-methylbutylbenzene).
Example 12 Adv

Carbon-skeleton rearrangement

How many monoalkylated isomers (structural, ignoring o/m/p position and stereochemistry) can form when benzene is treated with 1-chloro-2-methylpropane and AlCl3? Which is the major one?

Show solution
  1. Isobutyl cation (primary) shifts hydride at once to the tertiary t-butyl cation, which is far more stable. The only product of any consequence is tert-butylbenzene. Isobutylbenzene is a trace product only. So 1 major isomer (tert-butylbenzene) and 1 trace isomer (isobutylbenzene).
