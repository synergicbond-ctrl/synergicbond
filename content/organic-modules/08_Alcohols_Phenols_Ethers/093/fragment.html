24.4 Reactions of epoxides with organometallics and hydrides; polymerisation

  • Grignard reagents: RMgX attacks the less hindered carbon (SN2): ethylene oxide gives RCH2CH2OH (a primary alcohol with two more carbons); propylene oxide gives RCH2CH(OH)CH3. An alkoxide-magnesium halide intermediate may also, with MgX2, rearrange the epoxide to a carbonyl compound; use a copper catalyst or LiR/CuI to avoid that.
  • LiAlH4: H− attacks the less hindered carbon: propylene oxide → propan-2-ol; 1-methylcyclohexene oxide → 1-methylcyclohexan-1-ol (a tertiary alcohol: the Markovnikov hydration product, produced without a carbocation).
  • Acetylides (RC≡C−) give homopropargylic alcohols; enolates and cyanide add two-carbon units.
  • Lewis-acid rearrangement (Meinwald): styrene oxide with BF3 gives phenylacetaldehyde (hydride shift to the benzylic cation).
  • Polymerisation: ethylene oxide + base (HO−) or Lewis acid gives polyethylene glycol (PEG, carbowax), HO(CH2CH2O)nH: an addition polymer of ring-opening type. With a little water/ethylene glycol, di-, tri-ethylene glycol form.
  • Epoxy resins: bisphenol A (two phenoxide ions) + epichlorohydrin → diglycidyl ether prepolymer; hardened with a diamine, which opens the terminal epoxides (adhesives, Araldite).

Example 70 L2 Main

Synthesis with epoxides

Show how to make (a) butan-1-ol from ethylene oxide; (b) 2-phenylethanol from benzene; (c) pentan-2-ol from propylene oxide.

Show solution
  1. (a) C2H5MgBr + ethylene oxide, then H3O+: CH3CH2–CH2CH2OH butan-1-ol.
  2. (b) Benzene → bromobenzene (Br2/FeBr3) → C6H5MgBr (Mg, ether) → + ethylene oxide, then H3O+ → 2-phenylethanol.
  3. (c) Propylene oxide + C2H5MgBr attacks the CH2: CH3CH(OH)CH2CH2CH3, pentan-2-ol.
Example 71 L3 Advanced

Reasoning about regiochemistry

An optically active epoxide E (C3H6O) is treated with (A) CH3ONa/CH3OH to give F, and (B) CH3OH/H+ to give G (both C4H10O2). F gives a positive iodoform test; G does not. Identify E, F, G, and give the relationship between the configuration of E and of F.

Show solution
  1. The only C3H6O epoxide is 2-methyloxirane (propylene oxide), which is chiral.
  2. Base: methoxide attacks the CH2: F = CH3CH(OH)CH2OCH3, 1-methoxypropan-2-ol, containing CH3CH(OH)– (iodoform positive ✓).
  3. Acid: methanol attacks the more substituted CH: G = CH3CH(OCH3)CH2OH, 2-methoxypropan-1-ol, a primary alcohol without CH3CH(OH)– (iodoform negative ✓).
  4. In F the stereocentre is not attacked: its configuration is retained, and since the priorities (O > CH2O > CH3 > H) are the same, (R)-E gives (R)-F.
