Crystal Defects, Non-Stoichiometry and Colour Centres

10-part continuous textbook chapter

8.1 Perfect crystals and real crystals

A perfect crystal is an ideal reference in which every lattice site has its prescribed occupant and the periodic arrangement extends without interruption. At any temperature above absolute zero, real crystals contain thermodynamically generated point defects. Additional defects arise during growth, deformation, irradiation and doping.

DimensionalityDefect classExamples
0DPoint defectVacancy, interstitial, substitutional impurity
1DLine defectEdge and screw dislocations
2DPlanar defectGrain boundary, stacking fault, twin boundary
3DVolume defectPore, inclusion, precipitate

The elementary chemistry chapter concentrates on point defects because they directly affect stoichiometry, density, colour and electrical behaviour.

8.2 Stoichiometric intrinsic defects

A stoichiometric defect preserves the overall cation:anion ratio of the ideal compound.

8.3 Thermodynamics of defect formation

Creating a defect costs enthalpy but increases configurational entropy. The equilibrium concentration therefore increases with temperature.

n≈Nexp⁡(−ΔHfkT)n\approx N\exp\left(-\frac{\Delta H_f}{kT}\right)n≈Nexp(−kTΔHf​​)

Schematic dependence for independent point defects; exact prefactors and exponents depend on the defect reaction.

Derivation · Schottky-pair scaling
KS∝(nN)2K_S\propto\left(\frac{n}{N}\right)^2KS​∝(Nn​)2

A neutral pair contains one cation vacancy and one anion vacancy.

KS=exp⁡(−ΔGSkT)K_S=\exp\left(-\frac{\Delta G_S}{kT}\right)KS​=exp(−kTΔGS​​)

Equilibrium follows the free energy of pair formation.

Result
nN∝exp⁡(−ΔGS2kT)\frac{n}{N}\propto\exp\left(-\frac{\Delta G_S}{2kT}\right)Nn​∝exp(−2kTΔGS​​)

JEE questions usually test the qualitative trend: defect concentration rises exponentially with temperature and falls as formation energy increases.

8.4 Impurity defects and charge compensation

A substitutional impurity of different charge requires compensation to preserve macroscopic electroneutrality. Compensation can occur through vacancies, interstitials or changes in oxidation state.

1
Worked example

SrCl$_2$ doped NaCl

A crystal contains 10−310^{-3}10−3 mol SrCl2_22​ substitutionally dissolved in 1 mol NaCl. Neglect other defects. How many Na+^++ vacancies are created?

  1. Each Sr2+^{2+}2+ occupies one Na+^++ site but supplies one extra positive charge relative to Na+^++.
  2. One Na+^++ vacancy, carrying one effective negative charge, compensates each Sr2+^{2+}2+ substitution.
  3. Therefore moles of Na vacancies equal moles of SrCl2_22​.
10−310^{-3}10−3 mol of Na+^++ vacancies are created.
SrCl2 in NaCl:[VNa]=[SrNa∙]\mathrm{SrCl_2\ in\ NaCl}:\quad [V_{Na}]=[Sr_{Na}^{\bullet}]SrCl2​ in NaCl:[VNa​]=[SrNa∙​]

Effective-charge notation is shown schematically.

Vacancy count, not missing pairs

One divalent cation replacing a monovalent cation creates one monovalent-cation vacancy per dopant, not one anion vacancy.

8.5 Metal-excess defects

A metal-excess solid contains effectively more metal than required by the ideal stoichiometric ratio, usually because negative charge is stored as electrons.

MechanismDescriptionExample/effect
Anion vacancy with trapped electronAn anion leaves; an electron occupies the vacancy to maintain neutrality. The electron-containing vacancy is an F-centre.NaCl heated in Na vapour; yellow colour
Interstitial cation plus electronExtra metal ions enter interstitial sites and electrons occupy nearby interstitial regions or conduction states.ZnO on heating loses O2_22​ and becomes yellow, n-type
OO→12O2(g)+VO∙∙+2e−\mathrm{O_O\rightarrow\tfrac12O_2(g)+V_O^{\bullet\bullet}+2e^-}OO​→21​O2​(g)+VO∙∙​+2e−

F-centres absorb visible light because the trapped electron has quantised states in the vacancy potential. The absorbed wavelength and observed colour depend on the host lattice.

2
Worked example

Stoichiometry of oxygen-deficient ZnO

Write the qualitative formula of zinc oxide after slight oxygen loss.

  1. Oxygen vacancies reduce the oxygen content below one per zinc.
  2. Electrons left behind preserve charge balance and produce n-type conductivity.
The composition is written ZnO1−x\mathrm{ZnO_{1-x}}ZnO1−x​ with x>0x>0x>0.

8.6 Metal-deficiency defects

Metal deficiency commonly occurs when a metal can adopt more than one oxidation state. Some lower-valent cation sites are vacant, while other cations oxidise to maintain electroneutrality.

In wüstite, often written Fe1−x_{1-x}1−x​O, some Fe2+^{2+}2+ sites are vacant and a corresponding fraction of Fe2+^{2+}2+ ions becomes Fe3+^{3+}3+.

3
Worked example

Fe$_{0.95}$O oxidation-state fraction

Assume oxide is O2−^{2-}2−. In Fe0.95_{0.95}0.95​O, let yyy mol of iron per formula be Fe3+^{3+}3+ and the rest Fe2+^{2+}2+. Find yyy.

  1. Charge balance: 3y+2(0.95−y)=23y+2(0.95-y)=23y+2(0.95−y)=2.
  2. 3y+1.90−2y=23y+1.90-2y=23y+1.90−2y=2.
  3. y=0.10y=0.10y=0.10.
Per Fe0.95_{0.95}0.95​O, 0.10 Fe is Fe3+^{3+}3+ and 0.85 Fe is Fe2+^{2+}2+; about 10.53% of occupied Fe sites are Fe3+^{3+}3+.

8.7 Density changes caused by defects

The theoretical density formula can be modified by replacing the ideal cell mass with the actual mass after defects. Cell-volume change is often neglected in elementary problems unless stated.

ρ′=mideal−mmissing+maddedVcell\rho'=\frac{m_{ideal}-m_{missing}+m_{added}}{V_{cell}}ρ′=Vcell​mideal​−mmissing​+madded​​

Worked example

Schottky fraction from density

A crystal has ideal density ρ0\rho_0ρ0​ and measured density 0.99ρ00.99\rho_00.99ρ0​. Assume the cell volume is unchanged and only whole formula units are missing through Schottky defects. Estimate the fraction of formula units missing.

  1. Density is proportional to mass per cell when volume is unchanged.
  2. ρ/ρ0=0.99\rho/\rho_0=0.99ρ/ρ0​=0.99 means 99% of ideal mass remains.

8.8 Extended defects and mechanical behaviour

Dislocations allow plastic deformation at stresses far below those required to shear an entire perfect plane simultaneously. Grain boundaries interrupt periodicity and can impede dislocation motion, which is why reducing grain size often strengthens metals.

DefectStructural ideaImportant consequence
Edge dislocationExtra half-plane of atomsPlastic slip
Screw dislocationHelical mismatch around a linePlastic slip and crystal growth
Stacking faultIncorrect local layer sequenceChanges close-packed polytype/local energy
Grain boundaryInterface between orientationsDiffusion path; can strengthen or embrittle
Scope

Detailed dislocation theory is beyond the standard JEE syllabus, but recognising the link between defects and real mechanical properties prevents the false idea that defects are merely imperfections with no function.

Part review

1

Schottky disorder removes stoichiometric ion pairs and lowers density.

2

Frenkel disorder moves an ion to an interstitial site and leaves density nearly unchanged.

3

Aliovalent doping requires charge compensation.

4

F-centres are electrons trapped in anion vacancies.

5

Metal deficiency is commonly balanced by oxidation of some remaining cations.
