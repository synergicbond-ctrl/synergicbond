5.1 With active metals and bases

2ROH + 2Na → 2RONa + H2↑. The reaction with sodium is used to detect –OH and is slower than with water. Reactivity towards Na: CH3OH > 1° > 2° > 3° (steric hindrance to approach to the metal surface and the order of acidity). The alkoxide is a stronger base than hydroxide; tert-butoxide (K+ salt in tert-butanol or DMSO) is the classic bulky base that favours Hofmann elimination. 2ROH + Mg → (RO)2Mg + H2; 6ROH + 2Al (HgCl2, Hg) → 2Al(OR)3 + 3H2 (aluminium isopropoxide and aluminium tert-butoxide are used in MPV reduction and Oppenauer oxidation). ROH + CH3MgBr → CH4 + ROMgBr; the volume of methane measures the number of active hydrogens (Zerewitinoff determination).

Example 12 L2 Main

Active hydrogen counting

How many moles of methane are evolved when one mole of each of the following reacts with excess CH3MgI: (a) glycerol, (b) salicylic acid, (c) HC≡C–CH2–CH2OH, (d) diethyl ether?

Show solution
  1. Count the hydrogens attached to O, N, S and sp C (acidic enough to protonate CH3−).
  2. (a) Glycerol has three O–H: 3 mol CH4.
  3. (b) Salicylic acid has COOH and phenolic OH: 2 mol CH4.
  4. (c) Terminal alkyne C–H (pKa 25) and the OH: 2 mol CH4.
  5. (d) Diethyl ether has no active hydrogen: 0 (the ether just coordinates to Mg).
