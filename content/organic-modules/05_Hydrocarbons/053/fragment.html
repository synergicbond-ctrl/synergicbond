Practice 295 L3 Elucidation

Unknown alkyne and its alkenes

A hydrocarbon P (C6H10) gives a single acid, propanoic acid, on oxidative ozonolysis. Hydrogenation with Lindlar's catalyst gives Q, and Na/liq. NH3 gives R. Identify P, Q, R; state the relationship between Q and R; and say what Br2/CCl4 gives with each.

Show answer
  1. Br2 anti addition: Q (cis) → racemic (3R,4R)/(3S,4S)-3,4-dibromohexane; R (trans) → meso-3,4-dibromohexane.

One acid with three carbons from a C6 chain: symmetrical P = hex-3-yne. Q = (Z)-hex-3-ene (syn addition); R = (E)-hex-3-ene (anti). Q and R are geometrical isomers (diastereomers).

Practice 296 L3 Elucidation

Ring size from ozonolysis

A cycloalkene C8H14 on reductive ozonolysis gives one product, C8H14O2, which does not give the iodoform test. Identify the cycloalkene and the product.

Show answer

A single dicarbonyl product with all 8 carbons means a ring: cyclooctene → octanedial, OHC(CH2)6CHO (C8H14O2 ✔; a methyl ketone would give iodoform, none here).

Practice 297 L3 Elucidation

Skipped diene from ozonolysis

A hydrocarbon C9H16 gives, on reductive ozonolysis, two molecules of acetone and one molecule of malonaldehyde (OHC–CH2–CHO). Identify it.

Show answer

Join the carbonyl carbons across C=C bonds: each acetone carbon links to one CHO carbon of malonaldehyde: (CH3)2C=CH–CH2–CH=C(CH3)2 (2,6-dimethylhepta-2,5-diene), C9H16 ✔, an isolated (skipped) diene.

Practice 298 L3 Synthesis

Glycerol from propene

Outline a synthesis of propane-1,2,3-triol (glycerol) from propene.

Cl₂, 500 °C⟶ Cl₂, H₂O (HOCl)⟶ aq. NaOH, Δ⟶
Show answer

Propene + Cl2 at 500 °C → allyl chloride (allylic substitution). Cl2/H2O (HOCl) adds across C=C (Cl to the terminal carbon, OH to C-2), mainly giving 2,3-dichloropropan-1-ol (about 70 %, with some 1,3-dichloropropan-2-ol). Aqueous NaOH (via epichlorohydrin) replaces both Cl by OH: glycerol. This is the industrial route.

Practice 299 L3 Synthesis

meso-Dibromobutane from but-1-yne

Convert but-1-yne into meso-2,3-dibromobutane.

Show answer

Practice 300 L3 Synthesis

Convert but-2-yne to (±)-butane-2,3-diol by two routes.

Practice 301 L3 Synthesis

Switch the position of the double bond

Convert but-1-ene to but-2-ene, and but-2-ene to but-1-ene.

Show answer

But-1-ene + HBr → 2-bromobutane → alc. KOH → but-2-ene (Saytzeff). But-2-ene + HBr (Markovnikov, same 2-bromobutane) → t-BuOK (Hofmann) → but-1-ene (about half); cleaner route: but-2-ene + BH3/H2O2, OH− → butan-2-ol → tosylate → t-BuOK.

Practice 303 L3 Synthesis

Ethene to butane

Prepare butane from ethene in three steps (two routes).

Show answer

(1) HBr → CH3CH2Br; (2) Wurtz: 2 CH3CH2Br + 2 Na, dry ether → CH3CH2CH2CH3. Second route: CH3CH2Br → (CH3CH2)2CuLi, then + CH3CH2Br.

Practice 304 L3 Synthesis

Methane to ethyne

Outline preparation of ethyne from methane.

Show answer

2 CH4 → C2H2 + 3 H2 (partial combustion at 1500 °C); or CH4 → CH3Cl (Cl2, hν) → ethane (Na, Wurtz) → ethene (cracking) → Br2 → 1,2-dibromoethane → 2 NaNH2 → ethyne.

Practice 306 L3 Synthesis

Isopropyl methyl ether

Prepare 2-methoxypropane from propene in one step, and explain why the Williamson route from 2-bromopropane is inferior.

Show answer

Hg(OAc)2 in CH3OH, then NaBH4: Markovnikov alkoxymercuration–demercuration gives CH3CH(OCH3)CH3 without rearrangement. Williamson with the secondary 2-bromopropane and CH3O− gives much E2 (propene); the better Williamson uses sodium isopropoxide + CH3I.

Practice 307 L3 Synthesis

Two alcohols from one alkene

Convert 3-methylbut-1-ene into 3-methylbutan-1-ol and into 3-methylbutan-2-ol without rearrangement.

Show answer

3-Methylbutan-1-ol: BH3·THF, then H2O2/OH−. 3-Methylbutan-2-ol: Hg(OAc)2/H2O, then NaBH4 (aqueous acid would rearrange to the tertiary alcohol).

Practice 310 L3 Mechanism

Ring expansion

Predict the major product of HCl addition to methylenecyclobutane.

HCl⟶
Show answer

Protonation of the exocyclic CH2 gives the tertiary 1-methylcyclobutyl cation (no ring expansion needed): 1-chloro-1-methylcyclobutane. Ring expansion occurs with the cyclobutylcarbinyl cation (vinylcyclobutane + HCl or cyclobutylmethyl alcohol + acid) which gives 1-methylcyclopentyl products.

Practice 311 L3 Mechanism

Dehydration of a cyclic alcohol

Predict the major products from the acid-catalysed dehydration of cyclopentylmethanol.

Show answer

Loss of water would give a primary cation, so a hydride shift occurs with ionisation to the tertiary 1-methylcyclopentyl cation; loss of H+ gives 1-methylcyclopentene (major). Ring expansion to the secondary cyclohexyl cation competes slightly, giving some cyclohexene.

Practice 312 L3 Mechanism

Isotope labelling in hydroboration

1-Methylcyclopentene reacts with BD3·THF followed by H2O2/NaOH. How many stereoisomers of the labelled alcohol form?

Show answer

B goes to C-2 and D to C-1 (the carbon bearing CH3) on the same face; oxidation replaces B by OH with retention. The alcohol has D on the methyl-bearing carbon, cis to OH (so CH3 is trans to OH), as one diastereomer in both enantiomeric forms: 2 stereoisomers (a racemate).

Practice 313 L3 Mechanism

E2 in a cyclohexane

trans-1-Bromo-2-methylcyclohexane and cis-1-bromo-2-methylcyclohexane are each treated with NaOEt/EtOH. Which gives the more substituted alkene, and which reacts faster?

Show answer

E2 needs axial Br and an axial anti H. In the trans isomer the stable chair has both groups equatorial; the flip puts Br and CH3 axial, so the H at C-2 is equatorial: only H at C-6 is anti to Br: gives the less substituted 3-methylcyclohexene (slow). In the cis isomer one chair has Br axial and CH3 equatorial: the H on C-2 is axial and anti to Br: gives the trisubstituted 1-methylcyclohexene (Saytzeff), and the reaction is faster.

Practice 314 L3 Mechanism

Which proton does base remove?

2-Bromo-3-methylbutane (a 2° bromide with a 3° β-H) is treated with (i) NaOEt/EtOH (ii) t-BuOK. Major products?

Show answer

(i) Saytzeff: 2-methylbut-2-ene (loss of the 3° H). (ii) Hofmann-type: 3-methylbut-1-ene (loss of CH3 H's).

Practice 315 L3 Calc

Energy of conformers

Estimate the energy of the diaxial conformer of trans-1,2-dimethylcyclohexane relative to the diequatorial: gauche Me/Me = 3.8 kJ; each axial CH3 has two gauche interactions with ring CH2 of 3.8 kJ each.

Show answer

Diequatorial: one gauche Me/Me = 3.8. Diaxial: four CH3/CH2 gauche interactions = 15.2 kJ (no Me/Me gauche). Difference ≈ 11.4 kJ mol-1 in favour of ee.

Practice 316 L3 Calc

cis-1,2 energy

Estimate the energy difference between cis- and trans-1,2-dimethylcyclohexane using the same data.

Show answer

cis (a,e): one axial CH3 (2 × 3.8 = 7.6) + gauche Me/Me (3.8) = 11.4 kJ. trans (e,e): 3.8. Difference 7.6 kJ mol-1 (experimental ≈ 7.5 kJ).

Practice 317 L3 Calc

Equilibrium of dimethylcyclohexanes

At 298 K, what is the ratio trans : cis at equilibrium for 1,4-dimethylcyclohexane if ΔG = 7.6 kJ mol-1 (cis less stable)? (Take the two-fold degeneracy of cis conformers into account roughly.)

Show answer

Practice 318 L3 Calc

Thermochemistry of HBr addition

Estimate ΔH for CH2=CH2 + HBr → CH3CH2Br using bond energies (kJ mol-1): C=C 614, C–C 347, C–H 413, H–Br 366, C–Br 285.

Show answer

Broken: C=C (614) + H–Br (366) = 980. Formed: C–C (347) + C–H (413) + C–Br (285) = 1045. ΔH = 980 − 1045 = −65 kJ mol-1.

Practice 319 L3 Calc

Heat of hydrogenation from bond energies

Estimate ΔH for CH2=CH2 + H2 → CH3CH3: H–H 436, C=C 614, C–C 347, C–H 413.

Show answer

Broken: 614 + 436 = 1050. Formed: 347 + 2 × 413 = 1173. ΔH = −123 kJ mol-1 (experimental −137).
