10. Cycloalkanes: structure, nomenclature and isomerism

Cycloalkanes CnH2n contain one saturated ring. They are isomeric with alkenes (same formula, DoU = 1), so a formula CnH2n always allows both. Rings of 3–4 carbons are small, 5–7 common, 8–11 medium, 12 and more large. Naming rules: prefix cyclo; number the ring to give the lowest locants to substituents (alphabetical order decides ties); with only one substituent no number is needed. When the ring has more carbons than the chain it is the parent; with a longer chain, the ring is a substituent (cyclopropyl...). Cis/trans (or Z/E) designates the relative position of two substituents on a ring.

Isomers of C4H8 and C5H10

How many structural isomers (alkene + cycloalkane) have the formula (a) C4H8, (b) C5H10? How many including stereoisomers?

but-1-ene but-2-ene (E/Z) 2-methylpropene cyclobutane methylcyclopropane
The five structural isomers of C₄H₈
Show solution
  1. (a) C4H8: but-1-ene, but-2-ene, 2-methylpropene, cyclobutane, methylcyclopropane = 5 structural. But-2-ene is E/Z (cis/trans) → 6 stereoisomers in all.
  2. (b) C5H10: alkenes: pent-1-ene, pent-2-ene, 2-methylbut-1-ene, 3-methylbut-1-ene, 2-methylbut-2-ene (5); cycloalkanes: cyclopentane, methylcyclobutane, ethylcyclopropane, 1,1-dimethylcyclopropane, 1,2-dimethylcyclopropane (5). Total 10 structural.
  3. Stereoisomers: pent-2-ene (E, Z: +1); 1,2-dimethylcyclopropane: cis (meso, achiral) and trans (a pair of enantiomers) = 3 forms (+2). Total = 10 + 1 + 2 = 13.
