2.2 Lowest locants: the first point of difference

Write the locant set obtained from each direction in increasing order and compare the two sets one term at a time from the left. The first term at which they differ decides, whichever set has the larger total.

Why It Happens The rule treats each locant as a "digit": a smaller number earlier in the list outweighs any later number, exactly as 1,9,9 comes before 2,2,2 in alphabetical-style ordering. A sum would let a very low locant be cancelled by a high one and would not give a unique answer for sets with equal sums.
Example 6 Comparison

ClCH₂(CH₂)₆CCl₂CH₃: name it, and explain why the lower total of locants is not used.

Show solution
  1. Nine-carbon chain. From the ClCH₂ end the chlorines sit on carbons 1, 8, 8 → {1,8,8}; sum 17.
  2. From the CH₃ end: CH₃ is 1, CCl₂ is 2, … ClCH₂ is 9 → {2,2,9}; sum 13.
  3. First point of difference: 1 < 2, so {1,8,8} wins although its sum is bigger.

1,8,8-Trichlorononane, not 2,2,9-trichlorononane.

Counterexample Sets {1,8,8} and {2,2,9}: the sums are 17 and 13, so a "lowest sum" rule would choose {2,2,9}. The correct choice is {1,8,8}, because 1 < 2 at the first term. A lowest-sum rule is not part of IUPAC practice; it only sometimes gives the right answer by coincidence.
