10. Neighbouring-group participation (anchimeric assistance)

A group with a lone pair (or a π-bond or an aryl ring) located two atoms (β) away from the leaving group can attack the carbon bearing LG intramolecularly, displacing it with inversion to form a cyclic cationic or neutral intermediate. The external nucleophile then opens the ring, again by inversion. Two inversions give net retention of configuration; also the rate is much higher than expected for the unassisted substrate.

Key idea Experimental signs of NGP: (1) rate far higher than for a similar compound without the participating group; (2) net retention of configuration; (3) rearranged skeleton or migration of the group (halogen, aryl) to the neighbouring carbon.
Participating groups, in rough order of effectiveness: R2N−, S–R > Br, I, Cl (halonium) > COO− > OH/OR (via oxonium/epoxide) > phenyl (phenonium ion) > C=C (non-classical).
H2O⟶
2-Chloroethyl ethyl sulfide hydrolyses orders of magnitude faster than 1-chloropropane analogues; sulfur displaces Cl to give a cyclic episulfonium (thiiranium) ion, which water opens. (Sulfur mustard, ClCH2CH2SCH2CH2Cl, is the toxic example of this reactivity; same applies to nitrogen mustards via aziridinium ions.)

(a) α-Halo carboxylate. (S)-2-bromopropanoate with dilute NaOH or Ag2O/H2O gives (S)-lactate with retention: the carboxylate oxygen attacks the α-carbon (inversion) forming a strained α-lactone; hydroxide/water opens the α-lactone by attack at the α-carbon (second inversion). With a high concentration of strong nucleophile OH−, direct SN2 occurs and gives inversion ((R)-lactate).

dil. OH− or Ag2O⟶
Retention by double inversion via the α-lactone. In conc. OH−, SN2 gives the inverted (R)-lactate: C[C@@H](O)C(=O)[O-].
Trap The same substrate gives opposite stereochemical outcomes under two different conditions: low [Nu] → NGP → retention; high [Nu−] → direct SN2 → inversion. Examiners love this.
(b) Halogen participation (halonium). trans-2-Bromocyclohexyl tosylate or similar substrates; and the HBr reaction of 3-bromobutan-2-ol: the neighbouring Br displaces OH2+ giving a bromonium ion, opened by Br−: retention overall (stereospecific: erythro → meso-dibromide).
(c) Acetoxy participation. trans-2-Acetoxycyclohexyl tosylate solvolyses with retention (several hundred times faster than the cis isomer, which cannot reach the back side). The cyclic acetoxonium ion is the intermediate.
(d) Phenyl participation (phenonium ion). The aryl π-system attacks from the back, forming a bridged phenonium ion (spiro cyclopropane-cyclohexadienyl cation); the nucleophile opens it. Retention and sometimes aryl migration (e.g. in 2-phenylethyl tosylate with labelled carbon, the label scrambles).
Shortcut In an NGP question, ask: is there an X, O, N, S, COO−, or aryl at the β-position (two carbons away) that can displace LG intramolecularly to make a 3-membered (or 5-membered, with COO− when the ring is 3) ring? If so, retention overall.
