4. Catalytic hydrogenation: mode and selectivity

Heterogeneous catalysts (Pd/C, PtO2 (Adams), Raney Ni, Rh/C) adsorb H2 and the alkene on the metal surface. Both hydrogens are transferred to the same face of the double bond, so the addition is syn. Hydrogenation of 1,2-dimethylcyclohexene therefore gives cis-1,2-dimethylcyclohexane, and D2 added to (Z)-but-2-ene gives meso-2,3-dideuteriobutane.

Reactant:

Reagent: H2, Pd/C, 1 atm, 25 °C

Product:

syn addition: cis product (meso).

Rates fall as substitution increases because a crowded alkene binds the surface poorly: CH2=CH2 &gt; RCH=CH2 &gt; RCH=CHR (cis faster than trans) &gt; R2C=CHR &gt; R2C=CR2. A diene with a vinyl group and a tetrasubstituted double bond can therefore be reduced at the vinyl group alone with one equivalent of H2.

Poisoned catalysts stop at an intermediate stage. The Lindlar catalyst (Pd on CaCO3 treated with Pb(OAc)2 and quinoline) reduces alkynes to cis-alkenes only, because the alkene desorbs before further reduction. Ni2B (P-2 catalyst) behaves similarly. The Rosenmund catalyst (Pd/BaSO4, quinoline or sulfur) stops acid chlorides at the aldehyde.

Reactant:

Reagent: H2, Lindlar catalyst

Product:

Trap. Poisoning the catalyst cuts activity: Lindlar conditions leave alkenes alone, whereas ordinary Pd/C reduces alkynes all the way to alkanes. Note also that the same catalyst can isomerise alkenes or cleave benzylic C–O bonds (Section 6).
