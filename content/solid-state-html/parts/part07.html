◆Concept

This part classifies unit cells as primitive/simple or centred: body-centred, face-centred and base/end-centred.

1. Primitive / Simple Cell (P)

◆Concept

A primitive cell contains lattice points only at corners. The total lattice-point contribution is one.

∑Formula

8 × 1/8 = 1 lattice point per primitive conventional cell.

2. Body-Centred Cell (I)

◆Concept

Corner lattice points plus one equivalent lattice point at the body centre.
  • Representative centring translation: (1/2,1/2,1/2).
  • Total lattice points in the conventional cell: 2.

3. Face-Centred Cell (F)

◆Concept

Corner points plus the centres of all six faces.
  • Independent centring translations: (1/2,1/2,0), (1/2,0,1/2), (0,1/2,1/2).
  • Total lattice points per conventional cell: 4.

4. Base / End-Centred Cell (C or E)

◆Concept

Corner points plus centres of one pair of opposite faces.
  • Representative centring translation: (1/2,1/2,0), depending on the chosen centred-face pair.
  • Total lattice points per conventional cell: 2.

5. Hard-Sphere Contact Caveat

◆Concept

In the simple cubic equal-sphere model, corner spheres touch along the edge. In BCC and FCC, corner spheres do not touch along the cube edge; contact occurs along the body diagonal and face diagonal respectively. This is a packing statement, not the definition of centring.

⚠JEE Advanced traps & edge cases

  • Do not define BCC or FCC by “atoms touching” — centring is a translational lattice concept.
  • A CsCl crystal has a corner ion and a body-centre ion but is not a BCC Bravais lattice because the two sites are chemically different.
  • Face-centred and base-centred are not interchangeable.

▶Solved examples

Worked example 1

Example 1 — Lattice-point count

Find lattice points per conventional FCC cell.

  1. Corners contribute 1.
  2. Six face centres contribute 6×1/2 = 3.

FCC conventional cell contains 4 lattice points.

Worked example 2

Example 2 — End-centred

A cell has corner points and centres on only top and bottom faces. What is the centring type and count?

  1. This is one pair of opposite centred faces → base/end-centred.
  2. Count = 1 from corners + 2×1/2 = 2.

End-centred (C/E), 2 lattice points.
