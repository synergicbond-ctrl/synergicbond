Practice 190 Test 1 · single-correct

A hot saturated solution of a solid contains insoluble brown resin particles and dissolved coloured impurity. Which single step removes both before cooling? (A) addition of more solvent (B) boiling with activated charcoal and filtering hot (C) cooling immediately (D) sublimation of the solution

Show answer
  1. Charcoal adsorbs the dissolved coloured matter and hot filtration removes both charcoal and insoluble resin. More solvent removes neither; cooling first would crystallise the product along with insoluble material.

Answer: (B)

Practice 192 Test 1 · numerical

50 mL of aqueous solution containing a solute (K = Corg/Caq = 5) is extracted twice, each time with 10 mL of an immiscible organic solvent. What percentage of the solute remains in the aqueous layer?

Show answer
  1. Fraction per extraction = 50/(50 + 5 × 10) = 0.50.
  2. After two extractions = 0.50² = 0.25 ⇒ 25 %.

Answer: 25

Practice 194 Test 1 · single-correct

The sodium fusion extract of a compound gives a violet colour with sodium nitroprusside and no Prussian blue (excess sodium was used). The compound contains: (A) N only (B) S only (C) N and S (D) S and a halogen necessarily

Show answer
  1. Violet nitroprusside ⇒ S2−; no Prussian blue with excess Na ⇒ no nitrogen. S only (a halogen is not required by the data).

Answer: (B)

Match the observation (P-S) with the species responsible (1-4). P: blood-red colour with Fe3+; Q: Prussian blue; R: violet colour with nitroprusside; S: black precipitate with lead acetate. 1: [Fe(CN)5NOS]4−; 2: PbS; 3: Fe4[Fe(CN)6]3; 4: [Fe(SCN)]2+

Practice 196 Test 1 · single-correct

For a compound containing N and S but no halogen, which error can give a false positive for halogen with AgNO3 on the sodium fusion extract? (A) not boiling the extract with HNO3 first (B) using excess sodium (C) boiling the extract with HNO3 first (D) using distilled water for the extraction

Show answer
  1. Without the HNO3 treatment CN− and S2− give AgCN and Ag2S, which resemble silver halides. Excess sodium, HNO3 boiling and distilled water are all correct practice.

Answer: (A)

Practice 197 Test 1 · single-correct

Pentan-2-one and pentan-3-one are distinguished by: (A) 2,4-DNP (B) I2/NaOH (C) Tollens' reagent (D) Fehling's solution

Show answer
  1. Both give 2,4-DNP hydrazones; neither reduces Tollens' or Fehling's. Only pentan-2-one has CH3CO– and gives CHI3.

Answer: (B)

Practice 198 Test 1 · paragraph

An ether solution contains toluene, phenol, benzoic acid and aniline. Reagents: aq. NaHCO3, aq. NaOH, dil. HCl, used one after another. (i) Which compound is extracted by NaHCO3? (ii) Which one by NaOH after that? (iii) Which one remains in ether finally? (iv) What is added to the HCl extract to recover the amine?

Show answer
  1. (i) Benzoic acid (pKa 4.2 < 6.4). (ii) Phenol (pKa 10). (iii) After HCl removes aniline, toluene remains. (iv) NaOH (aq.) liberates aniline from anilinium chloride.

Answer: benzoic acid; phenol; toluene; NaOH

Practice 199 Test 1 · single-correct

A secondary amine is treated with benzenesulphonyl chloride and excess aqueous NaOH. The product: (A) dissolves completely (B) is an insoluble solid that does not dissolve in alkali (C) evolves N2 (D) gives no reaction

Show answer
  1. R2N–SO2Ph has no N–H hydrogen, so it cannot form a soluble sodium salt and remains as an insoluble solid. A primary amine would dissolve.

Practice 200 Test 1 · numerical

Aniline (M = 93) steam-distils at 98.5 °C under 760 mm Hg, at which the vapour pressure of water is 720 mm Hg. Calculate the mass of aniline in the distillate per gram of water (two decimals).

Show answer
  1. paniline = 760 − 720 = 40 mm Hg.
  2. waniline/wwater = (40 × 93)/(720 × 18) = 0.29.

Answer: 0.29

Practice 203 Test 1 · descriptive

Describe how you would separate benzoic acid from naphthalene and recover both, naming the principle.

Show answer
  1. Dissolve in ether; shake with aqueous NaHCO3: benzoic acid forms sodium benzoate (aqueous). Separate layers. Acidify the aqueous layer with HCl: benzoic acid precipitates; filter and recrystallise. Evaporate the ether layer to obtain naphthalene (sublimation also possible). Principle: conversion of the acid into a water-soluble salt, neutral naphthalene stays in ether.
