30.2 Phenols

Practice 100 L2 PHE

Arrange in decreasing acidity and justify: p-nitrophenol, m-nitrophenol, o-nitrophenol, phenol.

Show answer
  1. p-Nitrophenol (7.15) ≳ o-nitrophenol (7.2) > m-nitrophenol (8.4) > phenol (10).
  2. At p and o, NO2 withdraws by −M and −I; at m only −I. In o-nitrophenol the intramolecular H-bond in the neutral molecule slightly offsets the extra −I effect.
Practice 101 L2 PHE

Arrange in increasing acidity: 4-methylphenol, 4-methoxyphenol, 4-chlorophenol, 4-cyanophenol, phenol.

Show answer
  1. 4-Methylphenol (10.3) < 4-methoxyphenol (10.2) < phenol (10.0) < 4-chlorophenol (9.4) < 4-cyanophenol (7.95). Strictly, p-cresol and p-methoxyphenol are within 0.1 pK of each other; both are weaker than phenol (+I, +M).
Practice 102 L2 PHE

Why is salicylic acid (pKa1 2.97) a stronger acid than benzoic acid (4.20) and than p-hydroxybenzoic acid (4.54)?

Show answer
  1. In the salicylate anion the carboxylate is stabilised by an intramolecular H-bond from the ortho OH (and −I of OH at the near position). In p-hydroxybenzoic acid the OH donates by +M to the carboxyl through the ring, destabilising the anion.
Practice 103 L2 PHE

Which is a stronger acid, 2,4-dinitrophenol or 2,6-dinitrophenol? Which would you expect to have lower pKa: 2,4,6-trinitrophenol or 2,4,6-trichlorophenol?

Show answer
  1. 2,4-Dinitrophenol (4.1) and 2,6-dinitrophenol (3.7) are close; the 2,6-isomer has two ortho NO2, each −I and −M close to O−, so it is slightly stronger. Picric acid (0.4) is much stronger than 2,4,6-trichlorophenol (6.0): NO2 withdraws by both −M and −I, Cl only by −I (its +M works against it).
Practice 104 L2 PHE

Predict the products of the following and name them: (a) phenol + Br2/H2O; (b) p-cresol + Br2/H2O; (c) m-cresol + Br2/H2O (excess).

Show answer
  1. (a) 2,4,6-Tribromophenol. (b) The para position is blocked: 2,6-dibromo-4-methylphenol. (c) 2,4,6-Tribromo-3-methylphenol.
Practice 105 L2 PHE

A mixture of o- and p-nitrophenol is steam distilled. Which is in the distillate? Which has the higher solubility in benzene?

Show answer
  1. o-Nitrophenol (intramolecularly H-bonded, volatile); it is also more soluble in benzene (non-polar) than p-nitrophenol.
Practice 106 L2 PHE

Explain why phenol undergoes bromination without a catalyst while benzene needs FeBr3.

Show answer
  1. The OH group donates its lone pair into the ring (+M), making the ring far more nucleophilic: Br2 is polarised by the π system enough for substitution; the arenium ion is stabilised by an oxonium resonance form.
Practice 107 L2 PHE

Write the mechanism of the Reimer–Tiemann reaction in five steps.

Show answer
  1. (1) OH− + CHCl3 → CCl3− + H2O. (2) CCl3− → :CCl2 + Cl−. (3) Phenoxide ortho carbon attacks :CCl2 to give a dichloromethyl cyclohexadienone anion. (4) Proton shift re-aromatises: o-(dichloromethyl)phenoxide. (5) OH− hydrolyses CHCl2 to CHO (via the gem-diol); acidification gives salicylaldehyde.
Practice 108 L2 PHE

What are the products when phenol is treated with (i) CHCl3/NaOH, (ii) CCl4/NaOH, (iii) CO2/NaOH, 400 K, 5 atm? Give the common name for each.

Show answer
  1. (i) 2-Hydroxybenzaldehyde (salicylaldehyde). (ii) 2-Hydroxybenzoic acid (salicylic acid). (iii) Salicylic acid (Kolbe–Schmitt), as the sodium salt.
Practice 109 L2 PHE

In the Kolbe–Schmitt reaction sodium phenoxide gives mainly the ortho acid, potassium phenoxide at higher temperature gives the para acid. Explain.

Show answer
  1. Na+ chelates the phenoxide O and CO2, delivering CO2 to the ortho carbon (kinetic). K+ binds less tightly; at higher temperature the reaction is reversible and the thermodynamically more stable para isomer (no steric/chelation penalty) accumulates.
Practice 110 L2 PHE

How would you prepare aspirin starting from benzene? Give the steps.

Show answer
  1. Benzene → phenol (cumene process) → sodium phenoxide (NaOH) → salicylic acid (CO2, 400 K, 5 atm; H+) → aspirin ((CH3CO)2O, H+).
Practice 111 L2 PHE

Calculate the mass of salicylic acid required to prepare 90 g of aspirin if the yield of acetylation is 75 %. (M of salicylic acid 138; aspirin 180.)

Show answer
  1. Aspirin 90 g = 0.50 mol. At 75 % yield: salicylic acid required = 0.50/0.75 = 0.667 mol = 0.667 × 138 = 92 g.
Practice 112 L2 PHE

Phenyl acetate is heated with AlCl3 at 433 K and at 298 K. What are the products? Why do they differ?

Show answer
  1. 433 K: 2-hydroxyacetophenone (thermodynamic, chelated with Al, intramolecular H-bond). 298 K: 4-hydroxyacetophenone (kinetic; less hindered).
Practice 113 L2 PHE

Write the product and the type of rearrangement: crotyl phenyl ether (CH3CH=CH–CH2–O–C6H5) heated at 473 K.

Show answer
  1. A [3,3]-sigmatropic Claisen rearrangement with allylic inversion: 2-(but-3-en-2-yl)phenol, C6H4(OH)CH(CH3)CH=CH2.
Practice 114 L2 PHE

2,6-Dimethylphenyl allyl ether is heated. Give the product and the sequence of steps.

Show answer
  1. Claisen ([3,3]) to the ortho carbon (quaternary, cannot tautomerise), then Cope ([3,3]) to the para carbon, then tautomerisation: 4-allyl-2,6-dimethylphenol.
Practice 115 L2 PHE

Which carbon of the allyl group of an allyl aryl ether becomes bonded to the ring in the Claisen rearrangement, and how can this be shown?

Show answer
  1. The terminal (γ) carbon forms the new C–C bond with the ortho carbon; the O-bound carbon becomes the terminal =CH2 of the new allyl group.
  2. Proof: a crotyl ether, ArO–CH2CH=CHCH3, gives the branched product ArCH(CH3)CH=CH2, which can only arise by bonding through the γ-carbon (allylic inversion).
Practice 116 L2 PHE

Give the structure of the product in each case: (a) phenol + HCHO (base, 300 K); (b) the product of (a) + more phenol, heat, acid.

Show answer
  1. (a) o- and p-hydroxybenzyl alcohols. (b) Methylene-bridged oligomers (novolac): 2,2′/2,4′/4,4′-dihydroxydiphenylmethanes, which cross-link to Bakelite.
Practice 117 L2 PHE

How is picric acid prepared from phenol, and why is the two-step method used?

Show answer
  1. Phenol + conc. H2SO4 → 2,4-disulphonic acid; then conc. HNO3 replaces SO3H groups by NO2 and adds the third. Direct nitration with conc. HNO3 oxidises phenol to tar.
Practice 118 L2 PHE

Why does picric acid dissolve in NaHCO3 with effervescence while p-nitrophenol does not?

Show answer
  1. pKa of picric acid 0.4 is below that of H2CO3 (6.35): it protonates HCO3− to give CO2. p-Nitrophenol (7.15) is a weaker acid than H2CO3.
Practice 119 L2 PHE

Separate benzoic acid, phenol and toluene from a mixture in ether using only NaHCO3, NaOH and HCl.

Show answer
  1. Extract with aq. NaHCO3: benzoic acid → aqueous layer (acidify with HCl to recover). Extract the ether layer with aq. NaOH: phenol → aqueous layer (acidify or bubble CO2). Toluene remains in the ether.
Practice 120 L2 PHE

Why is the C–O bond in phenol stronger than in ethanol? What is the experimental evidence?

Show answer
  1. The lone pair is delocalised into the ring (partial double bond) and the carbon is sp2. C–O is 136 pm in phenol vs 142 pm in methanol; phenol does not react with HX or SOCl2 by substitution.
Practice 121 L2 PHE

Phenol reacts with PCl5 to give mostly triphenyl phosphate and only a little chlorobenzene. Why?

Show answer
  1. The C–O bond cannot be broken by SN2 at an sp2 carbon; the oxygen attacks phosphorus to form the phosphate ester (ArO)3P=O.
Practice 122 L2 PHE

How does Dow’s process differ from the benzenesulphonate fusion and cumene process in terms of by-products?

Show answer
  1. Dow: NaCl (no organic by-product). Fusion: Na2SO3 (inorganic waste). Cumene: acetone (valuable organic co-product, 0.6 t per tonne of phenol).
Practice 123 L2 PHE

What is the role of Na+ in the Reimer–Tiemann and Kolbe–Schmitt reactions, and why is the ortho isomer favoured?

Show answer
  1. Na+ associates with the phenoxide oxygen and with the electrophile (CCl2 or CO2) in a cyclic arrangement that delivers the electrophile to the ortho carbon.
Practice 124 L2 PHE

A compound A (C7H8O) gives a violet colour with FeCl3. On mononitration it gives only one product, which is steam volatile. Identify A.

Show answer
  1. A phenol of formula C7H8O is a cresol. Steam volatility means NO2 is ortho to OH (intramolecular H-bond).
  2. In p-cresol the para position is blocked, so the single mononitro product is 2-nitro-4-methylphenol (NO2 ortho to OH), steam volatile. o- and m-Cresol would give mixtures. A = p-cresol.
Practice 125 L2 PHE

How do you convert phenol into (a) benzoquinone, (b) cyclohexanone (two steps), (c) aniline (industrially)?

Show answer
  1. (a) Na2Cr2O7/H2SO4. (b) H2/Ni (cyclohexanol), then Cu 573 K or CrO3/PCC. (c) NH3, catalyst (ZnCl2 or Al2O3), 573 K (Bucherer-type amination of the keto tautomer).
Practice 126 L2 PHE

Compare phenol and benzyl alcohol with respect to: NaOH, NaHCO3, FeCl3, HBr, Br2/H2O.

Show answer
  1. NaOH: phenol dissolves, benzyl alcohol does not. NaHCO3: neither reacts. FeCl3: phenol violet, benzyl alcohol none. HBr: benzyl alcohol → benzyl bromide (SN1), phenol none. Br2/H2O: phenol gives white ppt of 2,4,6-tribromophenol; benzyl alcohol is oxidised slowly, ring not brominated.
