22.3 Spin-spin splitting

Protons on adjacent carbons influence each other's magnetic field and split each other's signals. A proton with n equivalent neighbours (on the adjacent atom) gives n + 1 lines, with Pascal's-triangle intensities: singlet (1), doublet (1 : 1), triplet (1 : 2 : 1), quartet (1 : 3 : 3 : 1), quintet (1 : 4 : 6 : 4 : 1), sextet, septet (1 : 6 : 15 : 20 : 15 : 6 : 1).

  • Equivalent protons do not split each other (CH3 gives a singlet by itself; Cl–CH2–CH2–Cl: one singlet).
  • The splitting constant J (Hz) is the same for coupled partners; the partners are the two signals that appear as mirror multiplets (a quartet at 4.1 with a triplet at 1.2 means CH3–CH2).
  • Protons on O and N usually do not split or get split (rapid exchange): the CH3CH2OH signal for OH is a singlet and the CH2 is a quartet (plus small coupling in very dry samples).
  • Aromatic rings: mono-substituted ring gives a complex multiplet integrating to 5H (or 2 + 2 + 1); para-disubstituted rings with two different substituents give two doublets (2H each); a symmetrical para ring with identical substituents (p-xylene) gives a singlet.
