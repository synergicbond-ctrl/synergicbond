19. Substituted cyclohexanes

19.1 Monosubstituted: why equatorial wins

An axial substituent sits on the same face as the two axial H atoms on carbons 3 and 5 (two 1,3-diaxial interactions). For methyl, each 1,3-diaxial CH3/H interaction is identical to one gauche-butane interaction (3.8 kJ mol-1); there are two, so the penalty is about 7.6 kJ mol-1 (experimental: 7.3 kJ mol-1, 1.74 kcal mol-1).

methylcyclohexane, equatorial CH₃ (favoured)
axial CH₃: two 1,3-diaxial interactions (+7.3 kJ/mol)

The preference is expressed as the A-value (conformational free energy ΔG° = Gaxial − Gequatorial). Larger groups, with greater 1,3-diaxial repulsion, have larger A-values.

Formula ΔG° = −RT ln K with K = [equatorial]/[axial] = exp(ΔG°/RT). At 298 K: RT = 2.478 kJ mol-1 = 0.592 kcal mol-1.

Trap Halogens (Cl, Br, I) have small A-values (≈ 0.4–0.5 kcal/mol) in spite of their large size, because the long C–X bond places the halogen far from the axial H atoms. Iodine is not much bulkier than chlorine in this sense. A-values: tert-butyl ≫ phenyl > isopropyl > ethyl ≈ methyl ≫ halogens.
Key idea The tert-butyl group is a conformational lock: its A-value (≈ 21 kJ mol-1) is so large that the ring effectively stays in the conformation with t-Bu equatorial. Any other substituent trans/cis to it is then forced axial or equatorial according to the cis/trans relationship.

Example 54 Main

Which isomer is the more stable?

Compare (a) cis- and trans-1,2-dimethylcyclohexane; (b) cis- and trans-1,3-dimethylcyclohexane; (c) cis- and trans-1,4-dimethylcyclohexane. In each pair, which is more stable and by roughly how much? Use 7.3 kJ/mol for an axial methyl and 3.8 kJ/mol for a gauche CH3/CH3 interaction.

Show solution
  1. Take the best chair of each isomer.
  2. (a) trans-1,2 (e,e): one gauche CH3/CH3 between adjacent equatorial methyls: 3.8. cis-1,2 (a,e): axial methyl 7.3 plus the gauche CH3/CH3 between the neighbours 3.8 = 11.1. trans is more stable by about 7 kJ/mol (experiment: about 7).
  3. (b) cis-1,3 (e,e): 0. trans-1,3 (a,e): 7.3. cis is more stable by about 7 kJ/mol.
  4. (c) trans-1,4 (e,e): 0. cis-1,4 (a,e): 7.3. trans is more stable by about 7 kJ/mol.
Example 55 Adv

Energies with 1,3-diaxial CH₃/CH₃

In cis-1,3-dimethylcyclohexane the ring-flipped (a,a) chair has two axial methyls on the same face, which interact with each other (syn-diaxial CH3/CH3, about 15.4 kJ/mol) as well as with one axial H each (3.8 kJ/mol each). Estimate ΔG between the (a,a) and (e,e) chairs and the equilibrium percentage of (e,e) at 298 K.

Show solution
  1. Count the 1,3-diaxial interactions in the (a,a) chair. Each axial CH3 meets the axial H on the far side (C-5): 2 × 3.8 = 7.6 kJ/mol. The two axial CH3 groups meet each other (syn-diaxial CH3/CH3), about 15.4 kJ/mol. Total ≈ 7.6 + 15.4 = 23 kJ/mol above the diequatorial chair.
  2. K = exp(23/2.478) = 1.1 × 104, so (e,e) ≈ 99.99 %.
Example 56 Adv

A locked ring

In cis-1-tert-butyl-4-methylcyclohexane, which substituent is axial in the more stable chair? Estimate the energy penalty and the percentage of the more stable conformer, using A(t-Bu) = 21 kJ/mol and A(CH3) = 7.3 kJ/mol.

tert-butyl equatorial, methyl axial (more stable chair)
tert-butyl axial (disfavoured by ~14 kJ/mol more)
Show solution
  1. cis-1,4 is (a,e): either the t-Bu is axial (penalty 21) or the CH3 is axial (penalty 7.3). The t-Bu stays equatorial; the methyl is axial.
  2. Energy difference between the chairs = 21 − 7.3 = 13.7 kJ/mol; K = exp(13.7/2.478) = 250, so the t-Bu-equatorial chair is 99.6 % populated.
Example 57 Adv

Which has more axial substituents?

Two isomers of 1-tert-butyl-3-methylcyclohexane (cis and trans): in the stable chair, which has the axial methyl?

cis-1,3 (e,e): no axial group
trans-1,3 (e,a): axial methyl
Show solution
  1. t-Bu equatorial in both. cis-1,3 = (e,e): methyl equatorial, no strain. trans-1,3 = (a,e): the methyl is axial with the t-Bu equatorial: it is ≈ 7.3 kJ/mol less stable. cis is more stable.

19.3 When "equatorial wins" fails

  • Intramolecular hydrogen bonding: cis-cyclohexane-1,3-diol prefers the diaxial conformer in non-polar solvents because the two axial OH groups (separated by about 2.5 Å) form an O–H···O hydrogen bond, which outweighs the small A-value of OH (0.9 kcal/mol). In water the diequatorial form wins.
  • trans-1,2-Dihalocyclohexanes: in the vapour phase and non-polar solvents the diaxial conformer is significant (and for dibromides, competitive) because two equatorial halogens, 60° apart, repel by dipole–dipole interaction and gauche strain. In polar solvents the diequatorial form wins.
  • Large rings and heteroatoms: 2-halo, 2-alkoxy tetrahydropyrans prefer the axial substituent (the anomeric effect: lone pair on ring O donates into σ*(C–X)). This is why α-D-glucopyranose (axial OH at C-1) is present to about 36 % in water, even though all other groups are equatorial.
  • Syn-diaxial strain: two axial substituents on the same face at C-1 and C-3 (cis-1,3-diaxial) clash strongly, more than the sum of the two A-values.
Example 58 Adv

Hydrogen bonding

Explain why cis-cyclohexane-1,3-diol is more stable in the diaxial conformation in non-polar solvent, whereas trans-cyclohexane-1,3-diol never has both OH groups hydrogen-bonded.

cis-1,3-diol (a,a): the two axial OH groups can hydrogen-bond
trans-1,3-diol (a,e): the OH groups are too far apart
Show solution
  1. cis-1,3: the diaxial conformer places the two OH groups on the same face about 2.5 Å apart, a good geometry for an intramolecular O–H···O bond that offsets the 1,3-diaxial O/H repulsion.
  2. trans-1,3: one OH is always axial and the other equatorial; they point to different faces, too far apart to bond. Either chair has a free OH.

19.4 Fused and bridged systems

trans-Decalin has both ring fusions equatorial to the other ring (each ring is a chair with the other ring as two equatorial substituents). It is rigid: it cannot undergo ring flip. cis-Decalin is flexible: the two chairs interconvert. trans-Decalin is more stable than cis by about 11 kJ/mol (two extra gauche interactions in the cis). Steroids and terpenoid skeletons are built from trans-fused chairs.

trans-decalin (rigid) cis-decalin (can flip) adamantane (all chairs) norbornane (locked boat)

The two stereo-SMILES drawings were checked by 3-D embedding: the ring-fusion H–C–C–H dihedral is about 176° in the trans isomer and about 44° in the cis isomer. Glucose. In β-D-glucopyranose every substituent (four OH and the CH2OH) is equatorial in the ⁴C₁ chair, which explains its stability and why it is the most abundant natural monosaccharide. In α-D-glucopyranose the anomeric OH is axial.

β-D-glucopyranose: all groups equatorial (ring O at position 6 of this drawing)
α-D-glucopyranose: anomeric OH axial
Trap In the chair drawings of sugars the ring oxygen sits at the right-hand back position. Draw by relating each substituent to the neighbour: a substituent that is "up" in the Haworth structure is axial-up or equatorial-up depending on the position. Do not assume all "up" groups are axial.
Example 59 Adv

Menthol

(−)-Menthol is (1R,2S,5R)-2-isopropyl-5-methylcyclohexanol. In the more stable chair all three substituents are equatorial. Show that this is possible: which of the 1,2 and 1,3 (here 1,5 = 1,3) relationships is required?

menthol, all equatorial chair
Show solution
  1. OH (C-1) and iPr (C-2): trans-1,2 = (e,e) possible. OH (C-1) and CH3 (C-5): these are 1,3 related (through C-6), so cis-1,3 = (e,e). A chair with all three equatorial therefore needs: OH/iPr trans, OH/CH3 cis. That is exactly the relative configuration of natural (−)-menthol; its diastereomer neomenthol has one axial group.

19.5 Summary sheet: rings

  • Baeyer deviation d = ½(109°28′ − planar angle): 24°44′ (3), 9°44′ (4), 0°44′ (5), −5°16′ (6).
  • Cyclohexane: chair 0 < twist-boat 23 < boat 30 < half-chair 45 kJ/mol.
  • Ring flip: ax ↔ eq; cis/trans and R/S unchanged. Barrier ≈ 45 kJ/mol.
  • A-values: t-Bu ≈ 21 kJ ≫ Ph (11.7) > iPr (9.2) > Et (7.5) ≈ Me (7.3) ≫ OH (3.6) > Cl (1.8). tBu locks the ring.
  • Disubstituted: 1,2 and 1,4: trans = e,e, cis = a,e; 1,3: cis = e,e, trans = a,e.
  • trans-Decalin is rigid; cis-decalin flips. β-D-glucose is all-equatorial.
