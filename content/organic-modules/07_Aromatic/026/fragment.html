Which nitrogen of 4-aminopyridine is protonated, and why is it more basic than pyridine?

Why is 3-aminopyridine only slightly more basic than pyridine?

Show answer
  1. The 3-amino group cannot delocalise its lone pair onto the ring N (meta-like relationship). It acts mainly through the –I and weak +M effect; pKaH = 6.0 shows only a small rise.
Practice 245 L3 · paragraph 7, Q3

Pyridine is about 106 times less basic than piperidine. Account for this in terms of hybridisation.

Show answer
  1. In pyridine the lone pair is in an sp2 orbital (33 % s character), held closer to the nucleus and less available for bonding to H+; in piperidine it is in an sp3 orbital (25 % s). The difference in pKaH (5.2 vs 11.1) corresponds to a factor of about 106 in Ka.

Paragraph 8. The relative rate constants for the reaction of 1-X-2,4-dinitrobenzenes with piperidine in methanol (X = F, Cl, Br, I) are approximately F : Cl : Br : I = 3300 : 4.3 : 4.3 : 1.

Practice 246 L3 · paragraph 8, Q1

What does the order F ≫ Cl ≈ Br > I say about the rate-determining step?

Show answer
  1. Breaking the C–X bond is not rate-determining (otherwise I, Br would be faster and F slowest). The rate-determining step is nucleophilic attack at the ipso carbon, which is accelerated by the highly electronegative F (the largest positive charge on C-ipso and the best stabilisation of the Meisenheimer complex).
Practice 247 L3 · paragraph 8, Q2

How would the rate change if the 4-NO2 group were removed (leaving 2-nitro only), or placed at the 3-position instead?

Show answer
  1. Removing the 4-NO2 lowers the rate sharply: only one NO2 stabilises the Meisenheimer complex. A nitro group at the 3-position (meta to the leaving group) cannot delocalise the negative charge by resonance and acts only by its –I effect, so it activates much less than the 4-NO2. Order: 2,4-dinitro ≫ 2-nitro (or 4-nitro) > 3-nitro.

Show answer
  1. Loss of H− restores the aromatic pyridine ring (strong driving force), and the hydride is removed irreversibly by reaction with an acidic N–H (of the 2-aminopyridine product or NH3), releasing H2. The product is isolated as the sodium salt of 2-aminopyridine.
