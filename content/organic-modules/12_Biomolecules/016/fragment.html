2.5 Peptides and the peptide bond

Two α-amino acids join by an amide (peptide) bond, –CO–NH–, formed between the carboxyl of one and the amino group of the next with loss of water. A chain of n residues (n − 1 bonds, n − 1 H2O lost) is a peptide: dipeptide, tripeptide, oligopeptide (≤ 10–20), polypeptide (> 50 residues; a protein is a polypeptide with a defined fold).

Gly–Ala (a dipeptide, Gly is N-terminal) Gly–Ala–Val glutathione (γ-Glu–Cys–Gly) aspartame (Asp–Phe methyl ester)
Peptides are written from the N-terminus (free NH₂) on the left to the C-terminus (free COOH). Glutathione's first bond is through the side-chain (γ) carboxyl of glutamate; aspartame is the methyl ester of the dipeptide Asp–Phe
Key idea Naming: the N-terminal residue keeps its name as an acyl: Gly–Ala = glycylalanine; Gly–Ala–Val = glycylalanylvaline. The C-terminal residue keeps the full amino acid name.
<h4>Structure of the peptide bond</h4>
  • The C–N bond is partly double: the nitrogen lone pair delocalises into C=O (resonance: –C(=O)–NH– ↔ –C(O−)=N+H–). Bond length ≈ 1.32 Å, between a C–N single bond (1.47 Å) and a C=N double bond (1.27 Å).
  • Therefore the six atoms Cα–C(=O)–N(H)–Cα′ lie in one plane, with a rotation barrier of ≈ 75–85 kJ mol−1. The usual arrangement is trans (the two α-carbons on opposite sides; the cis form is more strained, ≈ 8 kJ mol−1 less stable; proline peptides are the exception where cis occurs in ≈ 5 %).
  • Rotation is free about the N–Cα bond (φ) and the Cα–C bond (ψ); the pair (φ, ψ) defines the backbone shape (Ramachandran plot).
  • The N is not basic (amide), the peptide group has a large dipole moment (≈ 3.5 D) and forms H-bonds (C=O as acceptor, N–H as donor).
Trap A peptide bond is not a "carbon–nitrogen double bond" with E/Z isomerism in the usual sense, and the peptide N is not sp3: it is sp2-like. It is stable to cold water; it is hydrolysed by strong acid (6 M HCl, 110 °C, 24 h), strong base, or proteases.
<h4>Counting problems (common in exams)</h4>
Formula Number of distinct tripeptides from three different amino acids each used once = 3! = 6. Using each of 20 amino acids any number of times: 20n n-peptides. With residues A, A, B: 3!/2! = 3 sequences.
  • With n different amino acids each used once: n! linear peptides (the N→C direction matters: Gly–Ala ≠ Ala–Gly).
  • Cyclic peptides (no free ends): (n − 1)! sequences up to rotation (more if you count mirror/direction).
  • Water lost in making an n-peptide = n − 1; mass M(peptide) = Σ M(amino acids) − 18(n − 1).
  • A peptide of n residues has n − 1 peptide bonds; hydrolysis consumes n − 1 H2O.
  • A peptide with one free –NH2 (N-terminal) and one free –COOH (C-terminal) has pKa values ≈ 3.1–3.5 (C-terminal COOH, higher than the 2.3 of a free amino acid because no ammonium ion is nearby to stabilise the carboxylate) and ≈ 7.8–8.2 (N-terminal NH3+, lower than the 9.6 of a free amino acid because the neighbour is a neutral amide, not a carboxylate): pI(Gly–Gly) ≈ ½(3.12 + 8.17) = 5.65.
Example 44 Main

Count the tripeptides

(a) How many tripeptides can be made using Gly, Ala and Val once each? (b) How many tripeptides are possible if each of the three can be used any number of times? (c) How many tripeptides contain both Gly and Ala at least once among these three?

Show solution
  1. (a) 3! = 6.
  2. (b) 33 = 27.
  3. (c) Sequences from {G, A, V} that contain both G and A: total 27 − (no G) 8 − (no A) 8 + (neither: only V) 1 = 12 by inclusion–exclusion.
Example 45 Adv

Mass of a tetrapeptide

Calculate the molar mass of Gly–Ala–Val–Leu (amino acid masses 75.07, 89.09, 117.15, 131.18) and the mass of water produced per mole.

Show solution
  1. Σ amino acids = 75.07 + 89.09 + 117.15 + 131.18 = 412.49. Three peptide bonds → 3 H2O = 54.05.
  2. M = 412.49 − 54.05 = 358.4 g mol−1; water produced = 54 g per mole of peptide.
Example 46 Adv

Composition from the molar mass

A pentapeptide made only of Gly (75) and Ala (89) has M = 331 g mol−1. Find its composition and the number of different sequences with that composition.

Show solution
  1. For a Gly + (5 − a) Ala: M = 75a + 89(5 − a) − 4 × 18 = 373 − 14a.
  2. 373 − 14a = 331 → a = 3: three Gly and two Ala.
  3. Different sequences: 5!/(3! 2!) = 10.
  4. (Always check integrality: M = 337 would give a = 2.57, impossible.)
Example 47 Main

Peptide bond features

Which statements are correct? (a) The peptide C–N bond has partial double-bond character. (b) All six atoms of a peptide unit are coplanar. (c) The amide nitrogen is strongly basic. (d) Rotation about the C–N bond is free at 298 K.

Show solution
  1. (a) ✓ (b) ✓ (c) ✗ the lone pair is delocalised; amides are very weak bases (pKaH ≈ −0.5) (d) ✗ barrier ≈ 80 kJ mol−1, so rotation is slow on the NMR timescale.
