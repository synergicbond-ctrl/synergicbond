24.1 Preparation of epoxides

(a) Peroxy-acid epoxidation (Prilezhaev reaction). Alkene + RCO3H (peracetic, perbenzoic, mCPBA, in CH2Cl2) → epoxide + RCOOH. It is a concerted “butterfly” transition state in which the terminal O of the peracid is transferred to both carbons at once. It is stereospecific and syn: cis-alkene → cis-epoxide (meso for symmetrical alkenes); trans-alkene → trans-epoxide (racemic). Electron-rich alkenes react faster (more substituted alkene reacts first in a diene). The peracid’s O–H ends up on the carboxylic acid.

mCPBA, CH₂Cl₂⟶
cis-But-2-ene → cis-2,3-dimethyloxirane (meso; 2R,3S)
mCPBA, CH₂Cl₂⟶
trans-But-2-ene → trans-2,3-dimethyloxirane (racemic; one enantiomer, 2R,3R, drawn)

(b) Halohydrin route: alkene + X2/H2O (Cl2, Br2 or NBS/H2O) gives a β-halo alcohol (anti addition; OH at the more substituted carbon); NaOH then removes the O–H proton and the alkoxide displaces halide in an intramolecular SN2 (backside attack, so OH and X must be anti): epoxide. This is the route to propylene oxide (industrial chlorohydrin process).

1. Br₂, H₂O 2. NaOH⟶
Propene → bromohydrin (CH₃CH(OH)CH₂Br) → propylene oxide

(c) Other: Darzens condensation (α-chloroester + ketone/NaOEt → glycidic ester); H2O2/NaOH on α,β-unsaturated carbonyl (Weitz–Scheffer); Corey–Chaykovsky (sulphur ylide + C=O → epoxide); Sharpless asymmetric epoxidation of allylic alcohols (Ti(OiPr)4, tert-BuOOH, diethyl tartrate) gives a single enantiomer.

Example 65 L1 Foundation

Preparation of epoxides

Give two methods of converting cyclohexene to cyclohexene oxide.

Show solution
  1. (i) mCPBA (or peracetic acid) in CH2Cl2: direct, concerted, syn epoxidation.
  2. (ii) Br2/H2O to give trans-2-bromocyclohexanol, then NaOH: the alkoxide attacks the carbon bearing Br from the back (the Br and O are anti, in the diaxial conformer), closing the epoxide.
