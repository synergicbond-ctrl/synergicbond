3.6 Counting the isomers of C₄H₈ and C₅H₁₀

C₄H₈ has 5 constitutional isomers (but-1-ene, but-2-ene, 2-methylpropene, cyclobutane, methylcyclopropane) and 6 stereoisomers in all, because but-2-ene exists as a cis and a trans form. C₅H₁₀ has 10 constitutional isomers (5 alkenes + 5 cycloalkanes: cyclopentane, methylcyclobutane, ethylcyclopropane, 1,1-dimethylcyclopropane, 1,2-dimethylcyclopropane) and 13 stereoisomers in all (pent-2-ene gives 2; 1,2-dimethylcyclopropane gives 3: the cis meso form and the trans enantiomeric pair).

Think Further

Show that the two names pent-2-ene and (E)-pent-2-ene differ in what they specify, and explain why but-1-ene has no (E)/(Z) pair.

Show solution

Pent-2-ene without a descriptor names the constitution and so covers both geometric isomers; (E)-pent-2-ene names one of them. In but-1-ene the terminal carbon C1 carries two hydrogens, so one end of the double bond has two identical groups and no geometric isomerism is possible.
