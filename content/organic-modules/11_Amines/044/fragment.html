44. Mechanism: Hofmann elimination (E2) and the Cope elimination (Ei), with stereoelectronics

44.1 Hofmann elimination
  • Step 0 – hydroxide from the iodide. R–N+(CH3)3I− + Ag2O + H2O → R–N+(CH3)3OH− + AgI↓ (AgI precipitation removes I−); the neat hydroxide is a very strong base.
  • Step 1 – alignment. The C–H bond on the β-carbon and the C–N+ bond on the α-carbon must be anti-periplanar (180°). MO lens: the filled σ(C–H) orbital donates into the empty σ*(C–N+) only when they are collinear (maximum overlap) – the stereoelectronic requirement of E2.
  • Step 2 – concerted E2. OH− removes the β-H; the electron pair of the broken C–H bond becomes the new C=C π bond; the C–N+ bond breaks, expelling the neutral NR3 molecule.
1. anti-periplanar C–H and C–N⁺; 2. OH⁻ removes β-H (E2)⟶
E2 of sec-butyltrimethylammonium: removal of a β-H from the CH₃ group (Hofmann) gives but-1-ene

Why the Hofmann (least substituted) alkene? In ordinary E2 (alkyl halides) the transition state resembles the alkene and the more substituted alkene (Zaitsev) is more stable. With a poor-leaving-group-like, bulky, positively charged NMe3: (i) the C–N+ bond is not broken far in the transition state; the transition state has carbanion character (E1cB-like), so it is the most acidic β-H (least substituted C–H: primary > secondary > tertiary, since alkyl groups destabilise a carbanion) that is removed; (ii) the bulky NMe3+ group makes the conformation needed for removing an H from the more crowded β-carbon (the CH2) unfavourable. Hence the Hofmann product.

44.2 Cope elimination
  • Step 1 – the amine N-oxide. R2N+(CH3)–O−: the N–O− oxygen is a strong internal base.
  • Step 2 – cyclic five-membered transition state. The O− is positioned to reach a β-hydrogen on the same side of the C–C bond (syn to N). In one concerted step the C–H bond breaks (H transferred to O), the C=C bond forms and the C–N bond breaks: alkene + R2N–OH. It is a thermally allowed, suprafacial 6-electron process: a pericyclic Ei reaction (2 electrons in the C–H σ bond, 2 in the C–N σ bond, 2 in the O lone pair → six electrons in a cyclic array, Hückel-type aromatic transition state).
