11D · Unique and less-discussed theory

Enrichment Numbering as digit comparison. Locant sets are compared like numbers written in decreasing significance from the left. This gives a unique answer for every structure; a sum does not, because two different sets can have the same sum. (§2.2)
Enrichment Ring count from a skeleton. For a carbon skeleton the number of rings equals bonds minus atoms plus one. Cyclohexane has six bonds and six atoms, giving one ring; bicyclo[2.2.1]heptane has eight bonds and seven atoms, giving two rings. (§6.7)
Enrichment Duplicate atoms in CIP. A double bond is treated as two single bonds to two duplicate atoms, so C=O on a carbon counts as C(O,O). This is why an aldehyde carbon {O,O,H} ranks above a hydroxymethyl carbon {O,H,H}. (§8.2)
Enrichment Heteroatom low locants. In a heterocycle the heteroatom receives locant 1 and the others follow the ring; the Greek letters α, β, γ are counted from the heteroatom in a heterocycle but from the ring fusion in naphthalene. (§6.5)
Enrichment Name conversion table. A functional-class name is converted by identifying the alkyl groups and the suffix: alkyl + amine gives an amine name with N-substituents, and alkyl + halide gives a haloalkane. (§2.7, §4.6)
