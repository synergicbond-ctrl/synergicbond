How to use this chapter

This chapter is written as a book, not as a summary. Part A covers structure, naming, physical properties and spectra; Part B covers preparation of aldehydes and ketones. Part C (nucleophilic addition) is the engine of the whole chapter: once the attack on C=O is understood, every named reaction is a variation. Part D covers α-hydrogen chemistry (enols, enolates, halogenation, haloform and the aldol reaction). Part E covers Cannizzaro, benzoin, Perkin, Knoevenagel, Mannich, Michael, Robinson and related reactions. Part F covers reductions, oxidations and the Baeyer–Villiger and related rearrangements. Part G covers α,β-unsaturated carbonyl compounds (1,2 versus 1,4 addition). Part H is the test, reagent and problem-solving toolkit. Part I adds topics found in the cross-check of other books (β-keto acids, acetoacetic ester synthesis, labelling, oxidation numbers, aldol retrosynthesis) and Part J gives longer worked problems. The three problem sets (more than 600 problems at three levels, each with a full solution) and two appendices (named-reaction index and sources cross-checked) close the chapter.

Key idea Read every mechanism as a sequence of electron-pushing steps and ask three questions each time: (1) what is the nucleophile and what makes it nucleophilic, (2) what is the electrophile and what activates it, (3) which step is reversible and which step drives the reaction to products. Almost every question in this chapter, however complicated, is answered by these three questions.
Shortcut Study order for revision: do the summary box at the end of each section first; if you can reproduce it without looking, skip to the examples; if not, read the section again.
