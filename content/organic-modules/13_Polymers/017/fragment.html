13. Polyamides

13.1 Why the amide link makes a strong fibre

The amide group is planar because the nitrogen lone pair (nN) mixes with the carbonyl π* (the resonance O−–C=N+). That gives the C–N bond partial double-bond character (a rotational barrier of roughly 75 kJ mol-1), so the chain is flat and stiff between the sp3 stretches, and each amide has a strongly polar N–H (donor) and C=O (acceptor). Neighbouring chains line up in sheets held by N–H···O=C hydrogen bonds; this lowers solubility, raises the melting point (nylon-6,6 melts near 265 °C) and, when the fibre is drawn, aligns the sheets along the fibre axis for high tensile strength.

13.2 The amide-forming step, one arrow at a time

An amide is made from an acid and an amine by nucleophilic acyl substitution. Orbital reasons for each step:

+ 1 fast proton transfer at low T⟶ +
First the acid protonates the amine: the carboxylate is a far poorer electrophile, which is why direct amide formation needs heat.

1. Proton transfer. The amine (basic, HOMO = N lone pair) and the carboxylic acid give an ammonium carboxylate salt almost instantaneously. For nylon-6,6 this salt, hexamethylenediammonium adipate (“nylon salt”), is isolated, recrystallised and its 1:1 stoichiometry is exact by construction. 2. Heating (≈ 553 K, 15–20 atm to hold the water in the melt at first) shifts a small equilibrium amount back to free amine + free acid. The free amine nitrogen lone pair (HOMO) attacks the C=O π* (LUMO) of the –COOH at the Bürgi–Dunitz angle (≈ 107°): maximum HOMO–LUMO overlap while avoiding the filled σ framework.

+ 2 N attacks C=O (π*), proton shifts⟶
Neutral tetrahedral intermediate (carbon is now sp³; negative charge was on O, later protonated).

3. Collapse of the tetrahedral intermediate: the O lone pair pushes back to re-form C=O and expels H2O (after protonation of an OH, which makes it a good leaving group). The product amide is stabilised by nN→π*C=O delocalisation, making the step effectively irreversible *once the water is removed*.

3 lose H₂O (acid-catalysed)⟶ +
Amide + water.

4. Because each monomer has two such groups, the dimer H2N–R–NHCO–R’–COOH still has a free –NH2 and a free –COOH, and the cycle repeats at both ends.

IMP Why do acid chlorides work at room temperature? Cl is an inductive acceptor, lowering the π*(C=O) energy (a better electrophile, LUMO lower), and Cl− is an excellent leaving group (the collapse step is fast and irreversible). Hence interfacial polymerisation of sebacoyl chloride (in hexane) with hexamethylenediamine (in water/NaOH) at the interface gives nylon-6,10 as a continuous “rope” at room temperature (the nylon rope trick); NaOH neutralises the HCl so that the amine is not protonated and thereby deactivated.
MOT Lens In the amine + carboxylic acid step the key interaction is HOMO (nitrogen lone pair, nN) with LUMO (π* of C=O). Nucleophilic attack follows the Bürgi–Dunitz trajectory (about 107° to the C=O axis), where overlap with π* is large and repulsion with the filled σ and lone-pair orbitals is small. The carboxylic acid has a relatively high π* (the –OH donates into the carbonyl), the acid chloride a low π* (Cl withdraws), which is why the acid chloride reacts at room temperature and the acid only on heating.
Bonding Insight Resonance forms and MOs describe the same amide. In MO terms, the N lone pair, the carbonyl C and the carbonyl O contribute three p-type orbitals; they combine into three π orbitals holding four electrons (bonding, non-bonding, antibonding). The lowest is delocalised over N–C–O and the next has a node at carbon: this is the allyl-anion-like pattern, and the extra bonding gives the C–N bond partial double-bond character and the amide its planarity. The resonance structure with charges is a bookkeeping device for that delocalisation.

13.3 Nylon-6,6

adipic acid (hexanedioic acid) hexamethylenediamine (hexane-1,6-diamine) nylon-6,6 repeat unit
Condensation at 553 K, high pressure; 2 H₂O per repeat unit in the monomer ratio, one fewer overall for a chain.

The name “6,6” counts the carbons in the diamine and the diacid. Formula of the repeat unit C12H22N2O2. Properties: tough, lustrous, high tensile strength, abrasion-resistant, absorbs a little water (amide N–H···OH2), softening about 230 °C, melting about 265 °C. Uses: textiles (stockings, ropes, fishing lines), tyre cord, gears, bearings, carpets, parachutes, bristles.

13.4 Nylon-6

+ 533 K, H₂O (initiator)⟶
Water opens a few caprolactam rings to 6-aminohexanoic acid.
+ amine of the growing chain attacks lactam C=O⟶
The amino acid’s –NH₂ opens another lactam ring: ring-opening chain growth with no loss of water.

Caprolactam (azepan-2-one, a seven-membered ring: ε-caprolactam) comes from cyclohexanone by oxime formation and the Beckmann rearrangement (cyclohexanone oxime + conc. H2SO4). Nylon-6 is a polyamide of one monomer (homopolymer) with the repeat unit –[NH–(CH2)5–CO]–, C6H11NO = 113.16 g mol-1, which is the same formula as caprolactam. m.p. ≈ 220 °C. Uses: tyre cord, fabrics, ropes, toothbrush bristles, mountaineering rope, carpets.

Exam Trap In nylon-6 the “6” means one monomer unit has 6 carbons, including the carbonyl carbon. Nylon-6,6 = two six-carbon monomers. Nylon-6,10 (sebacic acid, 10 C, + 6 C diamine) and nylon-6,12 follow the same rule: the diamine count comes first, then the diacid.

13.5 Nylon-2-nylon-6 (biodegradable)

Alternating copolymer of glycine (H2N–CH2–COOH, two carbons) and 6-aminohexanoic acid (six carbons), hence the name: –[NH–CH2–CO–NH–(CH2)5–CO]–n. The glycine-derived amide bonds are peptide-like and attacked by bacteria and enzymes.

glycine 6-aminohexanoic acid repeat unit of nylon-2-nylon-6
An alternating polyamide: amide bonds that resemble peptide bonds.

13.6 Aramids: Kevlar and Nomex

terephthaloyl chloride p-phenylenediamine Kevlar repeat unit
Para-linked aromatic rings give a straight, rigid rod. Made in a polar amide solvent (NMP + CaCl₂) from the acid chloride and the diamine; the free acid reacts only at high temperature.

Kevlar (poly-p-phenylene terephthalamide): the chain is straight, stiff, completely flat and strongly H-bonded in sheets; it is spun from liquid-crystalline solution in H2SO4 into a fibre of tensile strength about five times that of steel on a weight basis. Uses: bullet-proof vests, helmets, tyre reinforcement, ropes, sails. Nomex is the meta isomer (isophthaloyl + m-phenylenediamine): kinked chains, flame-resistant (fire-fighters’ clothing).

isophthaloyl chloride (meta) m-phenylenediamine Nomex repeat unit
Meta linkages bend the chain, giving the fire-resistant Nomex.
Example 14 Adv

Nylon-6,10

Write the monomers and repeat unit of nylon-6,10 and compare its water uptake with nylon-6,6.

Show solution
  1. Monomers: hexane-1,6-diamine and decanedioic (sebacic) acid HOOC(CH2)8COOH. Repeat unit: –[NH(CH2)6NHCO(CH2)8CO]–.
  2. It has more CH2 per amide (CH2/CONH ratio = 7 vs 5 for nylon-6,6), so it is less hydrophilic: lower moisture absorption, lower m.p. (≈ 215 °C), better dimensional stability: used for bristles, fishing lines.
Compare & Distinguish Polyamide vs polyester fibres. Nylon-6,6 relies on N–H···O=C hydrogen bonds between chains; PET has no N–H donor and relies on stiff, planar terephthalate rings and dipole–dipole forces. Both melt near 260 °C because the stiff aromatic ring in PET compensates for the missing hydrogen bonds.
