8.2 The CIP priority rules (needed for both E/Z and R/S)

Definition. The Cahn–Ingold–Prelog (CIP) rules rank the groups on an atom: compare the atoms attached directly to it; if these tie, move outward and compare the sets of atoms attached to them, in order of decreasing rank.

  • Rule 1: higher atomic number wins (I > Br > Cl > S > F > O > N > C > H).
  • Rule 2: if the attached atoms are identical, compare the atoms they carry. The sets are compared in order from the highest member: {O,H,H} beats {C,C,C} because O outranks C at the first member.
  • Rule 3: a double or triple bond is treated as if each multiple-bond atom were bonded to extra duplicate atoms: C=O counts as C(O,O); C≡N counts as C(N,N,N); C=C as C(C,C).
  • Rule 4: isotopes with higher mass number win (D > H).
