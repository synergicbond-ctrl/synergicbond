Chapter map and prerequisites

Practical organic chemistry is the part of the subject in which an unknown compound is handled as a laboratory worker would handle it. The chapter follows that order.

Prerequisites (assumed known; where each is used):

  • Mole concept, percentage composition, empirical and molecular formula, gas laws and STP molar volume (Sections 14-19).
  • Acid–base ideas: pKa, conjugate acid–base pairs, relative acid strength of RCOOH, H2CO3, ArOH, RNH3+ (Section 13).
  • Functional groups, basic nomenclature, SN1/SN2 and carbocation stability (Sections 9-12).
  • Colligative properties, Raoult's law, vapour pressure and boiling (Sections 3, 19).
  • Solubility product and complex-ion equilibria (Section 7.5).
  • Bonding, resonance and inductive effects (IR and NMR trends, Sections 21-22).

Assumptions made in this module. (i) Atomic masses are rounded as in JEE papers (H 1, C 12, N 14, O 16, S 32, Cl 35.5, Br 80, Ag 108); molar volume at STP is 22 400 mL. (ii) Textbook colours and observations are given as the standard laboratory result; real samples can differ. (iii) Where an empirical trend, not an exact law, is used it is said so.

IMP Official syllabus wording for JEE Advanced: detection of elements (N, S, halogens); detection and identification of hydroxyl (alcoholic and phenolic), carbonyl (aldehyde and ketone), carboxyl, amino and nitro groups; chemical methods of separation of monofunctional organic compounds from binary mixtures. Purification and the quantitative methods are regular numerical questions in the "isolation, purification and characterisation" topic. IR, NMR and mass spectrometry are not listed in that wording; they are taught here only to the level used in structure-deduction passages, and are marked as enrichment where they go beyond it.
