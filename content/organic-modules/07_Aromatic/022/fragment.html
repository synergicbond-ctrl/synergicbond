20.3 Classic traps, together

Trap 1. FC alkylation rearranges; acylation does not. 2. Aniline cannot be FC-alkylated or acylated; nitration of aniline in acid gives meta (47 %). 3. Halogens deactivate but direct o/p. 4. –CH2Cl and –CH=CH–COOH are o/p, not m. 5. tert-Butylbenzene is not oxidised by KMnO4. 6. Toluene gives more ortho than para in nitration. 7. SNAr order F > Cl > Br > I; benzyne order Br > I > Cl > F. 8. Chlorobenzene + NaOH needs 623 K, 300 atm; add o/p NO2 to make it milder. 9. Naphthalene sulphonation: 353 K → 1-; 433 K → 2-. 10. Pyridine: EAS at C3, SNAr at C2/C4; pyrrole, furan, thiophene: EAS at C2. 11. Pyridine is a weaker base than piperidine but stronger than aniline and pyrrole. 12. 1,3,5-tribromobenzene needs aniline + deamination. 13. Phenol Kolbe–Schmitt: Na → ortho (salicylic), K at high T → para. 14. Fries: low T → para; high T → ortho. 15. Birch: donors stay on the double bond; acceptors on sp3.

Example 47 Main

Mixed quick questions

Answer in one line each: (a) Which is nitrated faster, toluene or p-xylene? (b) Product of PhCH3 + Cl2/hν (excess)? (c) How many 13C aromatic signals does 1,3,5-trimethylbenzene show? (d) Reagent to turn PhCOCH3 into PhCH2CH3? (e) Why is p-nitrotoluene oxidised to p-nitrobenzoic acid by KMnO4 but 4-tert-butylnitrobenzene is not oxidised?

Show solution
  1. (a) p-Xylene (two activating CH3 groups).
  2. (b) PhCCl3 (benzotrichloride), via PhCH2Cl and PhCHCl2.
  3. (c) 2 (C–CH3 carbons and C–H carbons).
  4. (d) Zn–Hg/HCl (Clemmensen) or N2H4/KOH/Δ (Wolff–Kishner).
  5. (e) The first has a benzylic H; the second has none (tert-butyl).
