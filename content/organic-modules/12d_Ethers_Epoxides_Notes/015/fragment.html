16. Counting isomers of ethers; metamerism

Ethers are functional isomers of alcohols: both classes have the formula CnH2n+2O. Two ethers with the same molecular formula but different alkyl groups on the oxygen are called metamers (for example methoxypropane and ethoxyethane). Metamerism is a kind of chain isomerism that is specific to compounds with a polyvalent functional atom such as O, S or N.

  1. Divide the total number of carbon atoms between the two alkyl groups: (1, n−1), (2, n−2), and so on.
  2. For each division count the alkyl isomers of each size (1, 1, 2, 4, 8 for C1 to C5).
  3. If the two groups have different sizes, multiply the numbers of alkyl isomers; if the sizes are the same, count unordered pairs, m(m+1)/2 for m alkyl isomers.
  4. Add the contributions. Add stereoisomers only if the question asks for them.

Worked counts. C4H10O: (1,3) gives 1 × 2 = 2, and (2,2) gives 1; total 3 ethers. C5H12O: (1,4) gives 4 and (2,3) gives 2; total 6 constitutional ethers (7 including the enantiomers of methyl sec-butyl ether). C6H14O: (1,5) gives 8, (2,4) gives 4, and (3,3) gives 3 unordered pairs of the two propyl groups; total 15 constitutional ethers.

The three ethers of formula C4H10O.

Key idea. Cyclic ethers of formula C4H8O: oxolane, 2-methyloxetane, 3-methyloxetane, 2-ethyloxirane, 2,2-dimethyloxirane and 2,3-dimethyloxirane are six constitutional isomers. Counting stereoisomers (2-methyloxetane and 2-ethyloxirane each exist as an enantiomeric pair; 2,3-dimethyloxirane as cis-meso and a trans pair) raises the total to ten.
