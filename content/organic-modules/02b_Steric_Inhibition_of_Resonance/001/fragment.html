2. Why the overlap falls

For two p-orbitals on adjacent atoms the overlap integral is proportional to cos θ, where θ is the dihedral angle between the two p-axes. At θ = 0° the overlap is maximal. At θ = 90° it is zero, so the π-interaction vanishes completely. Partial twisting gives partial loss of delocalisation, which is why the effect is graded rather than all-or-nothing.

MOT lens: conjugation is a π-overlap effect. A twisted group still has its σ-inductive effect, which does not depend on the dihedral angle, so SIR specifically removes the resonance part.
