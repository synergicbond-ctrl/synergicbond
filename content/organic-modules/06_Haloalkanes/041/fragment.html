40. Further worked examples: multi-step problems

Example 46 Adv

Synthesis

Prepare 2-methylbutan-2-ol from 2-methylbutane in as few steps as possible, with the reagents.

Show solution
  1. Step 1: Br2, hν (125 °C): selective bromination of the tertiary C–H gives 2-bromo-2-methylbutane (> 90 %).
  2. Step 2: H2O (warm) or moist Ag2O: SN1 hydrolysis gives 2-methylbutan-2-ol. Aqueous NaOH would give much alkene.
Example 47 Adv

Synthesis

Convert propene into but-2-yne using halide chemistry.

Show solution
  1. Propene + Br2/CCl4 → 1,2-dibromopropane. Excess NaNH2 (3 equivalents), then H2O → propyne. Propyne + NaNH2 → CH3C≡C−Na+; + CH3I (SN2 on a methyl halide) → but-2-yne.
Example 48 Adv

Synthesis

Prepare propanoic acid from ethene in three steps (keep halide chemistry).

Show solution
  1. Ethene + HBr → bromoethane. Bromoethane + KCN (alcohol/DMSO) → propanenitrile. Propanenitrile + H3O+, heat → propanoic acid.
Example 49 Adv

Synthesis

Convert 1-bromobutane to 1-pentanol and to 2-hexanol using Grignard chemistry.

Show solution
  1. BuBr + Mg → BuMgBr. (a) With HCHO: butyl–CH2OH = pentan-1-ol. (b) With CH3CHO: butyl–CH(OH)CH3 = hexan-2-ol.
Example 50 Adv

Synthesis

Prepare cyclohexyl methyl ether without making the elimination product as the major.

Show solution
  1. Methyl is the primary partner: cyclohexanol + NaH → sodium cyclohexoxide; + CH3I (SN2 on methyl iodide) → cyclohexyl methyl ether. The alternative pair (sodium methoxide + bromocyclohexane) would give mostly cyclohexene.
Example 51 Adv

Synthesis

How can 4-nitrophenol and 2-nitrophenol be prepared from chlorobenzene?

Show solution
  1. Nitration of chlorobenzene (HNO3/H2SO4) gives 4-nitro and 2-nitrochlorobenzene (separate by crystallisation). Each is heated with 15 % NaOH (443 K), then acidified: 4-nitrophenol and 2-nitrophenol (SNAr).
Example 52 Adv

Synthesis

Outline the synthesis of p-chlorobenzoic acid and of m-chlorobenzoic acid from toluene.

Show solution
  1. para: toluene + Cl2/FeCl3 → p-chlorotoluene (after separation from ortho) → hot KMnO4 (side-chain oxidation) → 4-chlorobenzoic acid.
  2. meta: oxidise toluene to benzoic acid first (KMnO4) and then chlorinate (Cl2/FeCl3): the COOH group is a meta director → 3-chlorobenzoic acid. The order of steps decides the orientation.
Example 53 Adv

Synthesis

Convert chlorobenzene to 2,4-dinitrophenylhydrazine and name the reaction type of the last step.

Show solution
  1. Chlorobenzene + conc. HNO3/H2SO4, heat → 1-chloro-2,4-dinitrobenzene. Then N2H4 (EtOH) → 2,4-dinitrophenylhydrazine. The last step is nucleophilic aromatic substitution (addition–elimination).
Example 54 Adv

Mechanism logic

Account for the formation of both 2-methylpropene and tert-butyl alcohol from tert-butyl bromide in aqueous ethanol at 25 °C, and explain why raising the temperature changes the ratio.

Show solution
  1. Ionisation gives the tert-butyl cation. Water/ethanol attack on carbon gives the alcohol (and ether) (SN1); loss of a β-proton to the solvent gives the alkene (E1). Elimination has the higher activation energy and the more favourable entropy, so at higher temperature the alkene fraction rises.
Example 55 Adv

Mechanism logic

Why does benzyl chloride react with aqueous NaOH faster than n-butyl chloride, but benzyl chloride in water alone also reacts at an appreciable rate whereas n-butyl chloride does not?

Show solution
  1. In SN2 the benzylic transition state is stabilised by conjugation, so OH− attacks benzyl chloride faster. In neutral water, benzyl chloride can ionise (SN1) to the resonance-stabilised benzyl cation, a path not available to n-butyl chloride (a primary alkyl cation is too unstable); so benzyl chloride hydrolyses slowly in water while butyl chloride is essentially inert.
