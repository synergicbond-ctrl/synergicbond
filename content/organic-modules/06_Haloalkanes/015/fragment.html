14. The E1 reaction

E1 shares the slow ionisation step with SN1, then the carbocation loses a β-proton to a weak base (solvent) in a fast second step.

slow⟶ −H+, solvent⟶
Ionisation then deprotonation (isobutene).
Formula rate = k [R–X] (first order; no isotope effect on C–H; no stereospecificity)
  • Conditions: tertiary (or stabilised secondary) halide or protonated alcohol; weak base, polar protic solvent; heat. Dehydration of 2° and 3° alcohols in conc. H2SO4/H3PO4 is E1.
  • Orientation: Zaitsev (most stable alkene), trans > cis, because the cation can freely rotate and the proton is lost to give the most stable alkene.
  • Rearrangement: possible and common (hydride and alkyl shifts) because a free cation is present.
  • Competition with SN1: both from the same intermediate. High temperature (and a poorly nucleophilic, non-polar environment) favour E1.

E1 dehydration of 2,3-dimethylbutan-2-ol: Zaitsev product 2,3-dimethylbut-2-ene (major) via the tertiary cation.
Trap Stereo: a given enantiomer of an E1 substrate gives the same alkene mixture (since a planar cation forms), unlike E2. If a question shows a stereospecific outcome, that cannot be E1.
