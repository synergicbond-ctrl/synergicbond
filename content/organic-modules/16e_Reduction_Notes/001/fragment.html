2. Hydride reagents compared

Hydride reagents transfer H− from boron or aluminium to an electrophilic carbon. Their strength depends on the polarity of the M–H bond and on the groups attached to the metal: electron-withdrawing or bulky ligands make the reagent milder and more selective. Selectivity summary:

  • NaBH4 (EtOH, MeOH, water): reduces aldehydes, ketones and acid chlorides; leaves esters, amides, nitriles, carboxylic acids, nitro groups and isolated C=C unchanged.
  • LiAlH4 (dry ether or THF only): reduces aldehydes, ketones, acids, esters, acid chlorides, anhydrides, lactones, amides, nitriles, epoxides, oximes, azides and alkyl halides; isolated C=C is untouched.
  • DIBAL-H ((iBu)2AlH, toluene, −78 °C): stops esters, lactones and nitriles at the aldehyde (or lactol) level after aqueous work-up; excess reagent or higher temperature gives alcohols or amines.
  • LiAlH(OtBu)3 (THF, −78 °C): bulky and attenuated; converts acid chlorides into aldehydes.
  • NaBH3CN (pH 6–7): the cyano group lowers hydride reactivity; iminium ions are reduced much faster than aldehydes or ketones.
  • NaBH4/CeCl3 in MeOH (Luche): reduces α,β-unsaturated ketones at C=O only (1,2-reduction) to give allylic alcohols.
  1. Hydride transfers from B–H or Al–H to the carbonyl carbon, forming a tetrahedral alkoxide bound to B or Al.
  2. The alkoxide-metal species still carries hydrogens, so one BH4− or AlH4− can reduce up to four carbonyl groups.
  3. Aqueous or dilute acid work-up protonates the alkoxide and gives the alcohol.
