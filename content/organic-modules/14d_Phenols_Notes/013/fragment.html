14. Important phenols and their uses

Salicylic acid (2-hydroxybenzoic acid) is made by the Kolbe–Schmitt reaction. It contains both a carboxylic acid and a phenolic OH, which react independently. Esterification of the carboxyl group with methanol gives methyl salicylate (oil of wintergreen, a liniment); acetylation of the phenolic OH gives aspirin (acetylsalicylic acid), an analgesic and anti-inflammatory drug.

Reactant:

Reagent: CH3OH, H2SO4 (cat.), heat

Product:

Methyl salicylate: the carboxylic acid is esterified, the phenol is left unchanged.

Aspirin is hydrolysed by aqueous alkali to sodium salicylate and sodium acetate; 3 mol of NaOH are consumed per mole (carboxylic acid, ester, and the phenol released). In a cold titration with phenolphthalein only the carboxylic acid is neutralised, so 1 mol NaOH is used per mole.

Picric acid (2,4,6-trinitrophenol) is a yellow crystalline solid and a strong acid (pKa well below 1). It forms crystalline picrate salts with organic bases and is an explosive. 2,6-Di-tert-butyl-4-methylphenol (BHT, C15H24O) is a hindered phenolic antioxidant added to foods, fuels and plastics. The cresols (methylphenols), obtained from coal tar, are used as disinfectants and as starting materials for resins and dyes. Paracetamol is the N-acetyl derivative of p-aminophenol.

Picric acid, BHT and paracetamol.

Reactant:

Reagent: (CH3CO)2O (1 equiv.), neutral conditions

Product:

The amino group is far more nucleophilic than the phenolic OH, so N-acetylation is selective: paracetamol.

Formula. Molar masses (C = 12, H = 1, N = 14, O = 16, Br = 80): phenol 94; salicylic acid 138; aspirin 180; picric acid 229; 2,4,6-tribromophenol 331; acetone 58.
