6. Kolbe–Schmitt, Reimer–Tiemann and Gattermann reactions

Carbon dioxide is a very weak electrophile, so it reacts only with the highly nucleophilic phenoxide ion, not with neutral phenol. Dry sodium phenoxide is heated with CO2 at about 400 K and 4–7 atm; acidification gives salicylic acid. The sodium ion holds the phenoxide oxygen and an oxygen of CO2 together, which delivers the CO2 to the ortho position. With the larger potassium ion at higher temperature, the thermodynamically more stable para acid (p-hydroxybenzoic acid) becomes the major product.

Reactant:

Reagent: (i) CO2, 400 K, 4–7 atm; (ii) H+

Product:

Sodium phenoxide → salicylic acid.

In the Reimer–Tiemann reaction, phenol is heated at about 340 K with chloroform and aqueous alkali. The active electrophile is dichlorocarbene, produced from chloroform by base.

  1. Step 1: HO– removes H from CHCl3 to give the trichloromethyl anion, CCl3–.
  2. Step 2: CCl3– loses Cl– (slow step) to give electron-deficient :CCl2 (a sextet at carbon).
  3. Step 3: phenoxide attacks the carbene through its ortho carbon, giving a dienone-type carbanion that regains aromaticity by proton transfer, forming the ortho ArCHCl2 phenoxide.
  4. Step 4: hydroxide replaces the two chlorines (via the gem-chlorohydrin) to give the aldehyde, which is liberated as the phenol on acidification.

Reactant:

Reagent: (i) CHCl3, aq. NaOH, 340 K; (ii) H+

Product:

Salicylaldehyde (major) with a little p-hydroxybenzaldehyde. If CCl4 replaces CHCl3 the –CCl3 group is hydrolysed to –COOH and salicylic acid results.

In the Gattermann reaction, HCN and HCl with ZnCl2 introduce a formyl group through an iminium electrophile (HC+=NH). Unlike the Gattermann–Koch variant (CO/HCl), this version works with phenols and their ethers, and the imine intermediate is hydrolysed to the aldehyde in the work-up.

Reactant:

Reagent: (i) HCN, HCl, ZnCl2; (ii) H2O

Product:

Resorcinol → 2,4-dihydroxybenzaldehyde, formylation at the position ortho to one OH and para to the other.

Key idea. Reimer–Tiemann: electrophile is :CCl2, medium is basic, product is an o-hydroxyaldehyde. Kolbe–Schmitt: electrophile is CO2, substrate is phenoxide, product is an o-hydroxy acid (Na+) or p-hydroxy acid (K+, high temperature).
