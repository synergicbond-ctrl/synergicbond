38. The Baeyer–Villiger oxidation

A ketone reacts with a peracid (peracetic acid, perbenzoic acid, m-chloroperbenzoic acid mCPBA, trifluoroperacetic acid, or H2O2 with a Lewis acid) to give an ester; cyclic ketones give lactones. An oxygen atom is inserted between the carbonyl carbon and one of its substituents.

  • Step 1: the peracid adds to the carbonyl (acid catalysed) to give the tetrahedral Criegee intermediate.
  • Step 2: one substituent migrates from carbon to the adjacent oxygen with simultaneous loss of the carboxylate (RCOO−); this step is rate-determining. The migrating group moves with its electron pair, so it must be able to stabilise a developing positive charge.
  • Step 3: loss of H+ from the oxocarbenium oxygen gives the ester.
RCO₃H⟶
Propanone → methyl ethanoate (acetone's two methyls are equivalent: one product)
Formula Migratory aptitude: H > tertiary alkyl > cyclohexyl (secondary) > secondary alkyl ≈ benzyl > phenyl > primary alkyl > methyl
The order is only approximate for close pairs: secondary alkyl and phenyl are very close (with CF3CO3H, isopropyl phenyl ketone gives about 2 : 1 isopropyl : phenyl migration, and tert-butyl phenyl ketone gives about 40 : 1 in favour of tert-butyl), while ethyl is clearly poorer than phenyl and methyl is the poorest.
Retention of configuration at the migrating carbon (the migrating group does not invert), shown with optically active migrating groups.

In aldehydes the hydrogen usually migrates, giving the carboxylic acid (this is the peracid oxidation of benzaldehyde to benzoic acid). Aryl aldehydes with strongly electron-donating groups (p-OMe, o/p-OH) have aryl migration competing: the Dakin variation gives a formate ester which hydrolyses to a phenol. Dakin oxidation. o- or p-Hydroxybenzaldehyde (or ketone) + H2O2/NaOH → catechol or hydroquinone.

Trap Baeyer–Villiger and Beckmann give different regiochemistry: BV depends on migratory aptitude (electronic), Beckmann on geometry (the group anti to the OH migrates). BV forms esters (O inserted); Beckmann forms amides (N inserted).
Trap In acetophenone, the product is phenyl acetate (C6H5OCOCH3), not methyl benzoate. A very common mistake is to draw the ester the wrong way round. The migrating group becomes attached to oxygen.

Predict the products of reaction with a peracid: (a) butanone, (b) 3-methylbutan-2-one, (c) acetophenone, (d) cyclobutanone, (e) benzaldehyde, (f) 2-methylcyclohexanone.

Show solution

Reaction sequence

Convert acetophenone into phenol using Baeyer–Villiger, and into benzoic acid via haloform. Compare the two routes.

Show solution
  1. Route 1: C6H5COCH3 + RCO3H → CH3COOC6H5 (phenyl ethanoate); hydrolysis with aqueous NaOH, then acid, gives phenol + ethanoic acid.
  2. Route 2: C6H5COCH3 + 3I2/NaOH → C6H5COO− + CHI3; acidification gives benzoic acid.
  3. BV inserts O between Ph and C=O (the carbon skeleton is cut at the aryl-C bond); haloform cuts the C–CH3 bond (loses the methyl carbon as CHI3).
