42. Mechanism: the fate of the diazonium ion — Sandmeyer, Balz–Schiemann, hydrolysis, deamination

42.1 Sandmeyer (CuCl, CuBr, CuCN): a single-electron-transfer chain
  • Step 1 – electron transfer. Cu(I) donates one electron into the LUMO of Ar–N2+ (a π* orbital that is delocalised over the N≡N unit and the ring). The extra electron populates an anti-bonding orbital, the C–N bond breaks and N2 leaves: aryl radical Ar• + N2 + Cu(II)Cl2. Why: Cu+ is a good one-electron reductant and the diazonium ion a good one-electron oxidant; the irreversible loss of N2 drives the step.
  • Step 2 – halogen transfer. The aryl radical (a σ radical, the singly occupied orbital in the plane of the ring) abstracts Cl from Cu(II)Cl2: Ar–Cl + Cu(I)Cl. Why Cu(II)–Cl: the Cu–Cl bond is weak and Cu(II) → Cu(I) is favoured, so the halogen is transferred rapidly.
  • Step 3 – catalytic turnover. Cu(I) is regenerated, so only catalytic amounts are needed in principle (older procedures use stoichiometric Cu(I)).
1. Cu(I)Cl: e⁻ transfer, −N₂⟶ 2. Cl from Cu(II)Cl₂⟶
Sandmeyer chloro-dediazoniation: aryl radical intermediate (biaryl by-products arise from radical coupling)

Evidence for radicals: biaryls and azo compounds appear as by-products; the reaction is initiated by Cu(I) only (no reaction with Cu(II) alone). The product halide is the same as the counter-ion in CuX, as the halogen is transferred from copper. Why KI needs no copper: I− is itself an excellent one-electron donor (low oxidation potential), so it can transfer an electron to ArN2+ directly (radical-chain with I2).

42.2 Balz–Schiemann

Ar–N2+BF4− is dry-heated (or thermolysed in an inert solvent).

  • Step 1 – loss of N2. Ar–N2+ → Ar+ + N2 (a high-energy aryl cation: the unfavourable step that needs heat). N2 is a superb leaving group.
  • Step 2 – fluoride capture. The aryl cation abstracts F− from BF4− (BF4− → BF3 + F−) in an ion pair. Why BF4−: it is the least nucleophilic counter-ion that still carries a transferable F; in the presence of Cl− or H2O the cation would give ArCl or ArOH.

42.3 Hydrolysis to phenol and the unwanted by-product

Warm water: Ar–N2+ ⇌ Ar+ + N2 (slow, rate-determining; SN1-type) then Ar+ + H2O → ArOH2+ → ArOH + H+. In HCl solution Cl− competes (small amount of ArCl), so dilute H2SO4 is used. The phenol can couple with remaining diazonium ion (azo dye by-product), another reason to use dilute solutions and an excess of acid.

42.4 Reduction by H3PO2 (deamination)

The diazonium ion accepts an electron or a hydride from hypophosphorous acid (a radical chain): Ar• + H3PO2 → ArH + •PO2H2; the phosphorus radical reduces another diazonium ion to Ar• and continues the chain. Product: Ar–H; N2 lost.
