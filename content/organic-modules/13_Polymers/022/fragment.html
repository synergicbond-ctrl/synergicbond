18. Biodegradable polymers

Most addition polymers have a backbone of C–C bonds that no enzyme in soil or water cleaves, so they persist for centuries. A polymer is biodegradable when its backbone contains linkages that microbial enzymes can hydrolyse: ester (esterases, lipases) or amide/peptide (proteases) bonds, or glycosidic bonds (cellulases, amylases). Hydrolysis breaks the chain into small fragments that micro-organisms use as food (CO2, H2O, biomass).

IMP Rule of thumb: hetero-atom in the backbone (–CO–O–, –CO–NH–) and a flexible, modest-mass chain → biodegradable. Pure carbon backbone (PE, PP, PVC, PS, PTFE) → persists. Polyvinyl alcohol is a carbon-backbone exception that is degraded by specific oxidase enzymes in some bacteria.

18.1 PHBV: poly(β-hydroxybutyrate-co-β-hydroxyvalerate)

Copolymer of 3-hydroxybutanoic acid and 3-hydroxypentanoic acid, linked by ester bonds. Bacteria (Alcaligenes eutrophus) make it as an energy-storage granule when fed glucose and propionic acid. Brittle if pure PHB; incorporation of the C5 units (hydroxyvalerate) lowers crystallinity and gives toughness and flexibility. Uses: speciality packaging, orthopaedic devices, controlled drug release; it degrades by hydrolysis in the body or soil to monomers.

β-Hydroxy acids join head-to-tail: each unit is –O–CH(R)–CH2–CO–, with R = CH₃ (HB) or C₂H₅ (HV).

18.2 Polylactic acid (PLA) and polyglycolic acid (PGA)

lactic acid (2-hydroxypropanoic acid) PLA unit glycolic acid PGA unit
Both are poly(α-hydroxy acids); PLA has a methyl on each unit.

PLA is made either by direct polycondensation of lactic acid (low mass) or, for high mass, by ring-opening polymerisation of lactide (the cyclic dimer) with Sn(II) octoate and an alcohol initiator: the alkoxide attacks a lactide C=O (π*), the tetrahedral intermediate collapses and breaks the ring, giving a new alkoxide end that attacks the next lactide. Lactic acid is obtained by fermentation of corn starch or sugar cane. PLA from L-lactide is semi-crystalline (m.p. ≈ 175 °C); uses: compostable cups and packaging, 3D-printing filament, absorbable sutures and screws (hydrolysed in the body to lactic acid, which enters metabolism). PGA: even faster hydrolysis, more crystalline; dissolving surgical sutures (Dexon); copolymer with lactic acid (PLGA) for drug-delivery capsules.

Lactide (cyclic dimer of lactic acid, ring of 6) opens to the linear dimer; repeated opening builds PLA.

18.3 Nylon-2-nylon-6 and others

Nylon-2-nylon-6 (section 13.5) is a biodegradable alternating polyamide of glycine and 6-aminohexanoic acid. Polycaprolactone (PCL), polybutylene succinate and starch blends are further examples; cellulose and starch themselves are natural biodegradable polymers.

Exam Trap Nylon-6 and nylon-6,6 are not biodegradable in practice (long hydrophobic CH2 sequences, tight H-bonded crystals). Only the nylon-2-nylon-6 copolymer is the biodegradable nylon in the syllabus.

Why biodegradable?

Why is PHBV biodegradable while polythene is not?

Show answer

PHBV has ester links in the backbone that esterase enzymes hydrolyse; polythene has an all-carbon, non-polar backbone that enzymes cannot cleave.
