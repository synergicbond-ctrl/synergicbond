41. Mechanism: diazotisation, step by step

  • Step 1 – nitrosonium ion. NaNO2 + HCl → HNO2. H+ protonates HNO2 on OH to give H2O+–N=O, which loses H2O to form NO+. MO lens: NO+ is isoelectronic with N2 and CO; its LUMO is a π* orbital with its largest coefficient on N. Why acid is needed: HNO2 itself is a poor electrophile; protonation turns water into a leaving group.
  • Step 2 – N-nitrosation. The amine lone pair (HOMO) attacks N of NO+ (LUMO π*): Ar–N+H2–N=O. (Only a free amine can do this, so excess acid slows the reaction a little; but acid is needed to form NO+ – hence the working pH of about 1.)
  • Step 3 – proton loss. ArNH–N=O (N-nitrosamine).
  • Step 4 – tautomerism. ArNH–N=O ⇌ Ar–N=N–OH (diazohydroxide): a 1,3 proton shift that places the π bond between the nitrogens.
  • Step 5 – protonation and loss of water. The OH is protonated and leaves; the lone pair on the adjacent N forms the N≡N triple bond: Ar–N+≡N.
1. NO⁺ (from HNO₂, H⁺); 2. n(N) → π*(NO⁺)⟶ 3. −H⁺⟶ 4. tautomerise⟶ 5. H⁺, −H₂O⟶
The five steps from aniline to benzenediazonium

Why the aryl diazonium ion survives at 0–5 °C but the alkyl ion does not. In Ar–N+≡N the filled ring π orbitals mix with the π* orbitals of N≡N (conjugation), strengthening the C–N bond. Cleavage of Ar–N would give an aryl cation, whose empty orbital is an sp2 orbital in the plane of the ring, orthogonal to the π system and unstabilised – extremely high energy. An alkyl diazonium ion gives a stabilised sp2/p carbocation readily (σ-donation and hyperconjugation).
