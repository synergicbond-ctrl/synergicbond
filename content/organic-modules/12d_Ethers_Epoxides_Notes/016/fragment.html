Practice 1 L1 Foundation

What is the IUPAC name of the ether shown?

(A) ethoxymethane

(B) methoxyethane

(C) methoxypropane

(D) ethoxyethane

Show answer
  1. The molecule is CH3–O–CH2CH3. The longer alkyl group (two carbons) is the parent chain: ethane.
  2. The smaller group, CH3O–, is the methoxy prefix. The name is methoxyethane (ethyl methyl ether in common nomenclature).

Answer: B

Practice 2 L1 Foundation

Which compound has the highest boiling point?

(A) butan-1-ol

(B) ethoxyethane

(C) pentane

(D) methoxypropane

Show answer
  1. Butan-1-ol is the only compound here with an O–H group, so its molecules are held together by intermolecular hydrogen bonds.
  2. The ethers and pentane (all of similar molar mass) have only dipole–dipole and dispersion forces and boil at about 30–40 °C, compared with about 118 °C for butan-1-ol.

Answer: A

Practice 3 L1 Foundation

How many ethers (constitutional isomers containing a C–O–C unit) have the molecular formula C4H10O?

Show answer
  1. Split the four carbons between the two alkyl groups: (CH3, C3H7) and (C2H5, C2H5).
  2. With methyl there are two propyl groups (n-propyl and isopropyl), giving methoxypropane and 2-methoxypropane; the ethyl/ethyl split gives ethoxyethane. Total 3.

Answer: 3

Practice 4 L1 Foundation

Which combination is the best Williamson synthesis of tert-butyl methyl ether, (CH3)3C–O–CH3?

(A) (CH3)3CCl + CH3ONa

(B) (CH3)3CONa + CH3I

(C) (CH3)3CONa + C6H5Br

(D) (CH3)3COH + CH3OH, conc. H2SO4, 140 °C

Show answer
  1. The Williamson reaction is SN2, so the halide must be methyl or primary. A tertiary halide (option A) would undergo E2 to give 2-methylpropene.
  2. Sodium tert-butoxide with iodomethane puts the bulky group on the nucleophile and the unhindered methyl on the electrophile. Option C needs an aryl halide, which does not undergo SN2, and option D gives a mixture of ethers and alkene.

Answer: B

Practice 5 L1 Foundation

Dimethyl ether is heated with an excess of concentrated HI. The final organic product and by-product are:

(A) methanol only

(B) iodomethane and water

(C) methane and hypoiodous acid

(D) iodomethane and methanol, even with excess HI

Show answer
  1. The ether oxygen is protonated and iodide attacks a methyl group by SN2: CH3OCH3 + HI → CH3I + CH3OH.
  2. With excess HI the methanol formed is converted into iodomethane as well: CH3OH + HI → CH3I + H2O. Overall two CH3I and one H2O.

Answer: B

  2. The products are phenol and iodomethane. Phenol does not react further with HI.

Practice 7 L1 Foundation

Ethylene oxide is treated with CH3MgBr in dry ether and the mixture is then hydrolysed with dilute acid. The product is:

(A) propan-1-ol

(B) propan-2-ol

(C) ethanol

(D) butan-1-ol

Show answer
  1. The carbanion-like CH3 of the Grignard reagent attacks a ring CH2 carbon and opens the ring: CH3–CH2CH2–O−MgBr+.
  2. Hydrolysis gives CH3CH2CH2OH, propan-1-ol. Reaction with ethylene oxide lengthens the chain by two carbons.

Practice 8 L1 Foundation

Which reagent converts an alkene into an epoxide in one step?

(A) mCPBA (a peroxyacid)

(B) OsO4, NaHSO3

(C) O3, then Zn/H2O

(D) Hg(OAc)2, H2O, then NaBH4

Show answer
  1. Peroxyacids such as mCPBA transfer an oxygen atom across the C=C bond directly to give an oxirane.
  2. OsO4 gives a syn-diol, ozone with a reductive work-up cleaves the double bond, and oxymercuration–demercuration adds H and OH.

Practice 9 L1 Foundation

The C–O–C bond angle in dimethyl ether is closest to:

(A) 104.5°

(B) 111°

(C) 120°

(D) 180°

Show answer
  1. Oxygen is sp3 hybridised with two lone pairs, so the geometry is bent with an angle near the tetrahedral value.
  2. The angle is slightly larger than the 104.5° of water because the two methyl groups repel each other more than hydrogen atoms do, giving about 111°.

Answer: B

Which test detects peroxides in a sample of diethyl ether?

(A) Shaking with acidified aqueous KI, then adding starch (blue-black colour)

Practice 14 L1 Foundation

(A) 1-methoxypropane

(B) 2-methoxypropane

Practice 15 L1 Foundation

Ethanol is heated with excess ethanol and concentrated H2SO4 at 140 °C. The major organic product is:

(A) ethene

(B) ethoxyethane

(C) ethanal

(D) ethyl ethanoate

Show answer
  1. At about 140 °C with excess alcohol, one molecule of ethanol (protonated) is attacked by the oxygen of another by SN2, giving diethyl ether and water.

  2. The cyclohexadienone intermediate tautomerises to restore aromaticity, giving 2-allylphenol (2-(prop-2-en-1-yl)phenol).

Practice 17 L1 Foundation

Which protecting group for an alcohol is removed by catalytic hydrogenolysis (H2, Pd/C)?

(A) tetrahydropyranyl (THP) ether

(B) tert-butyldimethylsilyl (TBS) ether

Practice 18 L1 Foundation

Which statements about ethers are correct?

(A) Ethers boil at lower temperatures than isomeric alcohols.

(B) Ethers are good hydrogen-bond donors.

(C) Ethers behave as Lewis bases and dissolve in cold concentrated H2SO4.

(D) Ethers are rapidly hydrolysed by dilute aqueous NaOH at room temperature.

Show answer
  1. A is correct: ethers cannot form hydrogen bonds with each other, unlike alcohols.
  2. B is false: there is no O–H, so an ether cannot donate a hydrogen bond (it can only accept one). D is false: ethers are inert to bases.
  3. C is correct: the lone pairs on oxygen accept a proton to give an oxonium salt.

Answer: A,C

Practice 20 L1 Foundation

Grignard reagents are prepared in diethyl ether or THF rather than in hexane because:

(A) the ether oxygen lone pairs coordinate to magnesium and stabilise RMgX

(B) the ether supplies the protons needed to form RMgX

(C) ether reacts with RMgX to form the alkane R–H

Practice 21 L1 Foundation

tert-Butyl methyl ether is treated with one equivalent of HI at 0 °C. The main products are:

(A) 2-iodo-2-methylpropane and methanol

(B) iodomethane and 2-methylpropan-2-ol

(C) 2-iodo-2-methylpropane and iodomethane

(D) no reaction

Show answer
  1. After protonation the C–O bond to the tertiary carbon ionises easily to give the tert-butyl cation (SN1) and methanol.

Practice 22 L1 Foundation

Ordinary acyclic ethers are inert towards nucleophiles, but ethylene oxide reacts readily. The main reason is:

(A) ring strain in the three-membered ring

(B) the presence of an acidic hydrogen

(C) the higher electronegativity of oxygen in epoxides

(D) the presence of a carbonyl group

Show answer
  1. The bond angles in an oxirane ring are about 60° instead of about 109°, so the ring is strained.
  2. Ring opening relieves this strain, which compensates for the poor leaving-group ability of an alkoxide.

Practice 31 L2 Main

trans-2-Chlorocyclohexanol and cis-2-chlorocyclohexanol are separately treated with aqueous NaOH. Which statements are correct?

(A) The trans isomer forms cyclohexene oxide readily.

(B) The cis isomer forms cyclohexene oxide as readily as the trans isomer.

(C) Ring closure requires the alkoxide oxygen to attack the carbon from the side opposite the C–Cl bond (anti-periplanar arrangement).

(D) Cyclohexene oxide is an achiral (meso-type) compound.

Show answer
  1. Intramolecular SN2 needs the O− and the leaving group anti (both axial). This arrangement is possible in the diaxial conformer of the trans isomer, so A and C are correct.
  2. In the cis isomer one substituent is always axial and the other equatorial, so they are gauche and no backside attack is possible: B is false.
  3. Cyclohexene oxide has a mirror plane (cis-fused ring), so D is correct.

Answer: A,C,D

Practice 35 L3 Advanced

Styrene oxide (2-phenyloxirane) is opened (i) by methanol/H+ and (ii) by CH3O−/CH3OH. The products (i) and (ii) are, respectively:

(A) PhCH(OCH3)CH2OH and PhCH(OH)CH2OCH3

(B) PhCH(OH)CH2OCH3 and PhCH(OCH3)CH2OH

(C) PhCH(OCH3)CH2OH in both

(D) PhCH(OH)CH2OCH3 in both

Show answer
  1. Under acidic conditions the benzylic carbon carries the larger positive charge (benzylic stabilisation), so methanol attacks it.

Practice 36 L2 Main

Cyclohexene oxide is hydrolysed with dilute aqueous acid. How many stereoisomeric cyclohexane-1,2-diols are formed?

Show answer
  1. Water attacks one ring carbon from the face opposite to the oxygen (anti opening), giving the trans diol.
  2. Attack at either of the two equivalent carbons occurs equally, giving the two enantiomers (1R,2R) and (1S,2S) in equal amount (a racemate). The cis (meso) diol is not formed. Number of stereoisomers: 2.

Answer: 2

Practice 37 L3 Advanced

Which sequences convert but-2-ene into meso-butane-2,3-diol?

(A) cis-but-2-ene; mCPBA, then H3O+

(B) trans-but-2-ene; mCPBA, then H3O+

(C) cis-but-2-ene; OsO4, then NaHSO3

(D) trans-but-2-ene; cold dilute alkaline KMnO4

Show answer

  2. OsO4 and cold KMnO4 give syn dihydroxylation. Syn addition to the cis alkene gives the meso diol (C); syn addition to the trans alkene gives the racemic pair (D is wrong).

Practice 47 L3 Advanced

Allyl phenyl ether labelled with 14C at the O–CH2 carbon (PhO–14CH2CH=CH2) undergoes Claisen rearrangement. In the product 2-allylphenol, the 14C is located at:

(A) the benzylic CH2 (attached to the ring)

(B) the central CH= carbon

(C) the terminal =CH2 carbon

(D) equally distributed over all three side-chain carbons

Show answer
  1. The [3,3] shift forms the new C–C bond to the ring at the carbon that was the far end of the allyl group and breaks the O–14CH2 bond.

Practice 51 L2 Main

A mixture of three different primary alcohols is heated with concentrated H2SO4 at 140 °C. How many different ethers can be formed?

Show answer

  2. Each pair of different alcohols gives one mixed ether: 3 pairs. Total = 3 + 3 = 6, which equals n(n+1)/2 for n = 3.
