Predict the heating product

Give the product on heating: (a) glutaric acid, (b) adipic acid with barium hydroxide, (c) maleic acid, (d) 3,3-dimethylglutaric acid, (e) 2-methylsuccinic acid.

Show solution
  1. (a) Glutaric anhydride. (b) Cyclopentanone. (c) Maleic anhydride. (d) 3,3-Dimethylglutaric anhydride (the gem-dimethyl groups even assist ring closure: Thorpe–Ingold effect). (e) 2-Methylsuccinic (pyrotartaric) anhydride.

21.3 Oxalic acid

  • Preparation: heating sodium formate above 360 °C (2 HCOONa → (COONa)2 + H2), then acidification; or from sawdust and NaOH/KOH at 200 °C; industrially from CO + NaOH, or by oxidation of sugar with HNO3.
  • Reducing agent: oxalic acid is oxidised to CO2 by KMnO4, K2Cr2O7, HNO3, Cl2 and others. The titration with KMnO4: 2 MnO4− + 5 H2C2O4 + 6 H+ → 2 Mn2+ + 10 CO2 + 8 H2O. At room temperature the reaction is slow at first and autocatalysed by Mn2+; so the solution is warmed to 60–70 °C and the first drops decolourise slowly. No external indicator (KMnO4 self-indicates, faint pink at end-point). The equivalent weight of hydrated oxalic acid H2C2O4·2H2O (M = 126) against KMnO4 is 126/2 = 63 (n-factor 2, since C changes from +3 to +4 per carbon: 2 e− per molecule).
  • With conc. H2SO4: CO + CO2 + H2O (the CO2 is absorbed by KOH, leaving CO: a laboratory preparation of carbon monoxide). With glycerol: at 110 °C: formic acid (glyceryl monooxalate loses CO2); at 260 °C: allyl alcohol.
  • Calcium oxalate: oxalic acid and oxalates precipitate white CaC2O4 with CaCl2 (insoluble in acetic acid, soluble in strong mineral acids), the test for oxalate; the main component of kidney stones.
  • Forms a stable ferrioxalate complex [Fe(C2O4)3]3−; removes rust and ink stains.
Example 53 Main

KMnO4 titration

0.63 g of pure oxalic acid dihydrate is dissolved in 100 mL water. 20.0 mL of this solution, acidified and warmed, is titrated with 0.020 M KMnO4. Find the titre.

Show solution
  1. n(H2C2O4·2H2O) = 0.63/126 = 5.0 × 10−3 mol in 100 mL → 1.0 × 10−3 mol in 20 mL.
  2. Ratio 2 MnO4− : 5 H2C2O4: n(MnO4−) = 1.0 × 10−3 × 2/5 = 4.0 × 10−4 mol.
  3. Volume = 4.0 × 10−4/0.020 = 0.020 L = 20.0 mL.
Example 54 Adv

Mixture of acids

10.0 mL of a solution containing oxalic acid and sodium oxalate required 12.0 mL of 0.04 M KMnO4; the same volume required 15.0 mL of 0.10 M NaOH for phenolphthalein neutralisation (to the dibasic stage for the acid only). Find [H2C2O4] and [Na2C2O4].

Show solution
  1. The KMnO4 titration measures total oxalate: 12.0 × 0.04 = 0.48 mmol MnO4−; × 5/2 = 1.20 mmol oxalate in 10 mL (0.12 M).
  2. NaOH reacts only with the free acid H2C2O4: 15.0 × 0.10 = 1.50 mmol OH− = 2 × n(acid): n(acid) = 0.75 mmol in 10 mL → [H2C2O4] = 0.075 M.
  3. Oxalate salt = 1.20 − 0.75 = 0.45 mmol → [Na2C2O4] = 0.045 M.

21.4 Malonic, succinic, glutaric and adipic acids

  • Malonic acid is made from chloroacetic acid (NaCN, hydrolysis). It has an acidic CH2; with P2O5 it gives carbon suboxide O=C=C=C=O; in the Doebner condensation and with urea (→ barbituric acid) it shows its active-methylene character.
  • Succinic acid forms the five-membered succinic anhydride and succinimide (with NH3, Δ). In biochemistry, succinate is dehydrogenated enzymatically to fumarate.
  • Glutaric acid: forms the six-membered anhydride; related to the lactone/lactam chemistry of 5-carbon chain compounds.
  • Adipic acid is prepared industrially by oxidation of cyclohexane (to a cyclohexanol/cyclohexanone mixture) and then nitric acid. It is polycondensed with hexane-1,6-diamine to give nylon-6,6 (a polyamide); the Ba salt gives cyclopentanone.

21.5 Aromatic dicarboxylic acids

  • Phthalic acid (benzene-1,2-dicarboxylic) is made by oxidising o-xylene or naphthalene (V2O5, 400 °C gives phthalic anhydride). With NH3/Δ → phthalimide; phthalimide + NaOBr → anthranilic acid; phthalic anhydride + phenol/H2SO4 → phenolphthalein; with resorcinol → fluorescein.
  • Isophthalic (m-) and terephthalic acid (p-). Terephthalic acid, from p-xylene, with ethane-1,2-diol gives PET (terylene, dacron), a polyester.

21.6 Maleic and fumaric acids: a stereochemistry story

Maleic acid (cis-butenedioic acid) and fumaric acid (trans) are geometrical isomers; maleic is much more soluble and lower-melting (cis, intramolecular H-bond, polar), fumaric is the more stable isomer (≈ 22 kJ mol−1 lower enthalpy; higher mp 287 °C, sublimes). Maleic → fumaric: heat with a little HCl, bromine/hν, or I2/hν (radical or acid catalysed). Fumaric → maleic: not directly; fumaric acid on strong heating gives maleic anhydride.

Example 55 Main

Stereochemistry of addition

Which isomer of tartaric acid forms when (a) maleic acid is treated with cold dilute alkaline KMnO4; (b) fumaric acid is treated with peroxybenzoic acid then aqueous acid?

Show solution
  1. (a) KMnO4 (syn addition of two OH groups) to the cis alkene gives meso-tartaric acid (the two OH groups end on the same side in the eclipsed picture: internal mirror plane). (b) Epoxidation of fumaric acid retains the trans geometry (trans-epoxide); acid ring opening is anti (backside attack): anti addition to a trans alkene gives the meso acid.
  2. Both meso answers can be checked by the rule: syn + cis → meso; anti + trans → meso; syn + trans or anti + cis → racemic.
