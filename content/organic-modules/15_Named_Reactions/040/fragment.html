35. Finkelstein and Swarts reactions (halogen exchange)

Finkelstein: R–Cl or R–Br + NaI in dry acetone → R–I + NaCl/NaBr (precipitate). SN2 with inversion; the reaction is driven to the right because NaCl and NaBr are insoluble in acetone whereas NaI is soluble (Le Chatelier). Best for primary and secondary halides, allyl, benzyl. Swarts: R–Cl, R–Br + AgF, Hg2F2, CoF2 or SbF3 → R–F (the ordinary route to alkyl fluorides, since NaF/KF are poor sources in organic solvents, and direct fluorination is explosive). SbF3 with SbCl5 (a Lewis-acid catalyst) converts polychloro compounds: CCl4 → CCl2F2 (Freon-12); CHCl3 → CHClF2, then pyrolysis gives tetrafluoroethene (→ Teflon).

Trap Aryl and vinyl halides do not react in Finkelstein (no SN2 at sp2 carbon). Tertiary halides give elimination. The Swarts reaction does not make R–F from R–OH (use HF/pyridine or DAST); and alkyl iodides cannot be made from alkyl chlorides by NaI in a protic solvent, because there is no precipitate to drive the equilibrium.
Example 40 Main

Predict the product and stereochemistry

(S)-2-Chlorooctane is treated with NaI in acetone. Give the product's configuration and explain what happens if the mixture is left for a long time.

Show solution
  1. One SN2 inversion: (R)-2-iodooctane. The product itself can react with I− (I− is also a good leaving group), so on prolonged standing a second inversion returns the (S) isomer and in the end a racemic mixture of 2-iodooctane results (racemisation by repeated inversion). The rate of racemisation is twice the rate of substitution, since each substitution event inverts one molecule.
