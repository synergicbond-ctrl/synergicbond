13. The reactivity map of an amine

Every reaction of an amine uses the nitrogen lone pair (nucleophile/base) or the N–H bond (acidity, substitution on N). Two diagnostic questions sort the reactions.

  • How many N–H bonds? 1° (two) can be acylated, sulphonylated, and can react twice; 2° (one) can react once; 3° (none) cannot form stable neutral substitution products and only quaternises or oxidises.
  • Aliphatic or aromatic? Aryl–N compounds have a delocalised lone pair (weaker nucleophile) but an activated ring (electrophilic substitution) and form stable diazonium salts.
