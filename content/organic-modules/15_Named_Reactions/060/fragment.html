54. Hofmann bromamide (Hofmann degradation) rearrangement

Reagents: a primary amide R–CO–NH2 + Br2 + 4 NaOH (or NaOBr, from Br2/NaOH) in water, warm → primary amine R–NH2 (one carbon fewer) + Na2CO3. Mechanism: (1) amide N–H deprotonated; N-bromination gives R–CO–NHBr; (2) deprotonation again gives the bromoamide anion R–CO–N−Br; (3) R migrates from carbon to nitrogen with loss of Br− (concerted 1,2-shift), forming the isocyanate R–N=C=O; (4) OH−/H2O adds to the isocyanate giving carbamic acid R–NH–COOH, which loses CO2 (as carbonate in base) to give R–NH2. R keeps its configuration: retention at a chiral migrating carbon.

Acetamide → methyl isocyanate (R = CH3 migrates C → N)
H₂O; –CO₂⟶
Isocyanate hydrolysis → methylamine
Br₂, NaOH⟶
Benzamide → aniline (net: lose the carbonyl carbon as carbonate)
Key idea Count carbons: the amine has one carbon fewer than the amide, i.e. the same carbon count as the *carboxylic acid minus one*: CH3CH2CONH2 (3 C) → CH3CH2NH2 (2 C). Therefore Hofmann is the route "RCOOH → RNH2 with one carbon less" (via RCOCl/NH3).
Trap Needs an unsubstituted –CONH2 (two N–H available to be replaced/removed). N-methylamides do not do it. Secondary-alkyl and benzyl R groups migrate with retention (optically active 2-phenylpropanamide gives optically active 1-phenylethylamine). The isocyanate is an isolable intermediate under anhydrous conditions; with CH3OH instead of water the product is a carbamate R–NH–CO2CH3.
Example 48 Main

Hofmann sequences

Give the amine and its carbon count when (a) butanamide and (b) 2-phenylethanamide (PhCH2CONH2) are treated with Br2/NaOH.

Show solution
  1. (a) Butanamide (4 C) → propan-1-amine, CH3CH2CH2NH2 (3 C). (b) 2-Phenylethanamide (8 C) → benzylamine, PhCH2NH2 (7 C).
Example 49 Adv

Retention of configuration

(S)-2-Methylbutanamide, CH3CH2CH(CH3)CONH2, undergoes Hofmann degradation. What is the configuration of the amine?

Show solution
  1. The migrating carbon moves with its electron pair and keeps its three other substituents in the same spatial arrangement (retention). The product is butan-2-amine. The CIP priority of the substituents at the stereocentre changes: in the amide the priorities were C(=O)N > CH2CH3 > CH3 > H; in the amine they are N > CH2CH3 > CH3 > H. N now takes the position of C(=O)N and the order is unchanged, so the label stays (S): (S)-butan-2-amine.
