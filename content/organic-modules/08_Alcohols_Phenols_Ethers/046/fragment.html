8.2 Non-chromium oxidants

  • Swern oxidation: DMSO, (COCl)2 at −78 °C, then Et3N. Mechanism: DMSO + oxalyl chloride → chlorodimethylsulphonium chloride; the alcohol O attacks sulphur to give an alkoxysulphonium ion; the base removes the α-H through a five-membered ylide transition state, giving RCHO/R2CO, dimethyl sulphide (smell) and CO/CO2/Et3NH+Cl−. 1° → aldehyde without over-oxidation; 2° → ketone.
  • Dess–Martin periodinane (DMP): hypervalent iodine(V); 1° → aldehyde, 2° → ketone in CH2Cl2 at room temperature, neutral, mild.
  • MnO2: oxidises allylic and benzylic alcohols selectively to aldehydes/ketones (a heterogeneous reaction on the surface); saturated alcohols are unaffected.
  • Cu, 573 K (dehydrogenation): vapour of the alcohol passed over hot copper. 1° → aldehyde (+ H2); 2° → ketone (+ H2); 3° → alkene (dehydration, because there is no α-H). This is a standard way to tell the three classes apart.
  • Oppenauer oxidation: 2° alcohol + acetone (excess), Al(O-t-Bu)3 → ketone; the reverse reaction is the Meerwein–Ponndorf–Verley (MPV) reduction: ketone + Al(OCH(CH3)2)3/propan-2-ol → alcohol. The transfer of hydride goes through a six-membered cyclic transition state; C=C and other reducible groups are unaffected. Oppenauer is used for acid-sensitive allylic alcohols (e.g. in steroid chemistry).
  • Ceric ammonium nitrate (CAN): (NH4)2Ce(NO3)6 (yellow) forms a red complex with alcohols (qualitative test for –OH in compounds up to C10).
  • TEMPO/NaOCl: selective for primary alcohols → aldehydes (the oxoammonium ion is the oxidant).

Example 21 L1 Foundation

Products of oxidation

Give the product of oxidising each of the following with (i) PCC, (ii) hot acidified K2Cr2O7: (a) propan-1-ol, (b) butan-2-ol, (c) 2-methylpropan-2-ol, (d) phenylmethanol.

Show solution
  1. (a) PCC: propanal; K2Cr2O7: propanoic acid.
  2. (b) Secondary alcohol: butan-2-one with both reagents.
  3. (c) Tertiary alcohol: no reaction with either reagent under mild conditions (hot concentrated acid causes dehydration/cleavage, not a simple oxidation).
  4. (d) PCC: benzaldehyde; K2Cr2O7: benzoic acid.
Example 22 L2 Main

Chemoselective oxidation

Which reagent oxidises (E)-hex-2-en-1-ol to (E)-hex-2-enal without touching the double bond: (A) KMnO4/H+; (B) MnO2; (C) hot Jones reagent; (D) O3?

Show solution
  1. (A) oxidises C=C and takes the alcohol to acid; (C) takes the aldehyde to acid; (D) cleaves C=C.
  2. (B) MnO2 (also PCC, Swern, DMP) stops at the aldehyde and is selective for allylic alcohols.
Example 23 L3 Advanced

Oxidation puzzle

An alcohol A (C4H10O) is oxidised by PCC to B. B gives a positive Tollens test; the Victor Meyer test on A gives red colour; A gives no turbidity with Lucas reagent at room temperature. B, on warming with dilute NaOH, gives a condensation product C8H14O. Identify A, B and the product, and explain why the isomeric alcohol 2-methylpropan-1-ol is excluded.

Show solution
  1. Red in the Victor Meyer test = primary alcohol; Tollens-positive B = aldehyde; no Lucas turbidity = ordinary primary alcohol. Two primary C4H10O alcohols exist: butan-1-ol and 2-methylpropan-1-ol.
  2. The condensation product C8H14O = 2 C4H8O − H2O, so it is an aldol condensation (aldol addition followed by loss of water).
  3. Butanal has two α-hydrogens: the aldol, 3-hydroxy-2-ethylhexanal, has an α-H and loses water on warming to give a conjugated enal. 2-Methylpropanal has one α-H; its aldol, 3-hydroxy-2,2,4-trimethylpentanal, has no α-H (the α-carbon is quaternary), so it cannot dehydrate.
  4. A = butan-1-ol; B = butanal; product = 2-ethylhex-2-enal, CH3CH2CH2CH=C(C2H5)CHO (C8H14O).
