2.3 Complex (branched) substituents

Definition. A complex substituent is a branch that is itself branched. It is named as a substituted alkyl group and enclosed in brackets. Its attachment carbon is C-1 and its chain is the longest chain that starts at that carbon.

Example 7 Standard

Name the compound.

Show solution
  1. Longest chain through the CH(CH₂CH₂CH₂CH₃)₂ carbon: nine carbons (four + the centre + four). The branch on C-5 is –CH(CH₃)–CH(CH₃)₂.
  2. Name the branch from its attachment carbon: C1 (CH, attached), C2 (CH with methyl), C3 (CH₃) → propyl chain with methyls at 1 and 2 → 1,2-dimethylpropyl.
  3. The centre carbon is C-5 from either end, so no numbering choice remains.

5-(1,2-Dimethylpropyl)nonane. The whole expression "dimethylpropyl" stays inside the brackets, and for alphabetical order its first letter is d.

HYF A complex substituent is alphabetised under the first letter of its complete name, including any multiplying prefix inside it: (1,2-dimethylpropyl) is filed under d, whereas two simple methyl groups ("dimethyl") are filed under m.
