11F · High-yield facts

  • §2.3 A complex substituent is alphabetised under the first letter of its complete name, including any multiplying prefix inside it: (1,2-dimethylpropyl) is filed under d, whereas two simple methyl groups ("dimethyl") are filed under m.
  • §5.3 Oxo, formyl and acyl are decided by one question: is the carbonyl carbon part of the parent chain? If it is, the C=O is expressed as oxo on that chain carbon. If it is not, the carbonyl carbon hangs off the chain together with its H or R group and is named formyl or acyl.
  • §8.3 Rank the two groups on one carbon at a time, then decide whether the two higher-ranked groups lie on the same side. The usual error is to compare groups across the double bond.
  • §9.7 An acid chloride R–COCl is an acyl chloride (acetyl chloride, benzoyl chloride); an anhydride (RCO)₂O uses the acid name (acetic anhydride, phthalic anhydride). Acetyl, acetate and acetic belong to one family: the acyl group, the anion or ester, and the acid.
  • §10.2 When a question gives both an older-style and a 2013-style name among the options and the stem says "IUPAC name" without "preferred", pick the one that applies the chain-contains-the-multiple-bond convention (older). A well-set JEE question avoids conflicts, so if both options look right the stereodescriptor or the substituent order will be the real difference.
