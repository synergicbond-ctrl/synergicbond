31.5 Structure-elucidation and quantitative problems

Practice 290 L3 ELU

A secondary alcohol A (C4H10O) is dehydrated to B, which is ozonised (Zn/H2O) to give only one product C. Identify A, B and C.

Show answer
  1. A = butan-2-ol; B = but-2-ene; C = ethanal (two molecules, symmetrical alkene).
Practice 291 L3 ELU

An alcohol A (C5H12O) is oxidised to a ketone B that does not give the iodoform test. A is not optically active. Identify A and B.

Show answer
  1. Secondary alcohols of C5H12O: pentan-2-ol, pentan-3-ol, 3-methylbutan-2-ol. Ketone without CH3CO– and an achiral alcohol: A = pentan-3-ol, B = pentan-3-one.
Practice 292 L3 ELU

An alcohol C6H14O gives immediate turbidity with Lucas reagent. On dehydration it gives an alkene whose ozonolysis gives only propanone. Identify the alcohol and the alkene.

Show answer
  1. The alkene (CH3)2C=C(CH3)2 gives only propanone. A tertiary alcohol that gives it: 2,3-dimethylbutan-2-ol, (CH3)2C(OH)CH(CH3)2 (C6H14O ✓).
Practice 293 L3 ELU

Compound X (C7H6O2) gives a violet colour with FeCl3, reduces Tollens’ reagent, does not liberate CO2 from NaHCO3, and is steam volatile. Identify X.

Show answer
  1. CHO (Tollens), phenolic OH (FeCl3), not an acid, intramolecular H-bond (steam volatile): salicylaldehyde (2-hydroxybenzaldehyde).
Practice 294 L3 ELU

Compound Y (C7H6O3) gives violet colour with FeCl3 and CO2 with NaHCO3; on heating with soda lime it gives phenol. Identify Y.

Show answer
  1. Phenolic OH + COOH, decarboxylation to phenol: salicylic acid.
Practice 295 L3 ELU

A cyclic ether A (C4H8O) is heated with excess concentrated HI. The product is C4H8I2. Identify A and the product.

Show answer
  1. Both C–O bonds are cleaved (SN2 at each CH2): A = THF (oxolane); product = 1,4-diiodobutane.
Practice 296 L3 ELU

A tetrahydric alcohol C4H10O4 consumes 3 mol HIO4, giving 2 HCHO and 2 HCOOH. Identify it.

Show answer
  1. Three cleavages in a four-carbon chain: HOCH2–CH(OH)–CH(OH)–CH2OH, butane-1,2,3,4-tetrol (erythritol or threitol).
Practice 297 L3 ELU

A compound C2H6S reacts with NaOH and forms a disulphide on mild oxidation. Identify it and the oxidation product.

Show answer
  1. A thiol: ethanethiol, C2H5SH; oxidation gives diethyl disulphide, C2H5SSC2H5. (Dimethyl sulphide, the isomer, has no S–H and gives sulphoxide.)
Practice 298 L3 ELU

94 g of phenol is treated with excess bromine water. Calculate the mass of the precipitate (C = 12, H = 1, O = 16, Br = 80).

Show answer
  1. C6H3Br3O: M = 72 + 3 + 240 + 16 = 331. 94 g = 1.00 mol → 331 g of 2,4,6-tribromophenol.
Practice 299 L3 NUM

0.900 g of impure aspirin is boiled with 50.0 mL of 0.500 M NaOH. The unreacted NaOH requires 22.0 mL of 0.500 M HCl. Calculate the percentage purity (M of aspirin 180; the impurity consumes no alkali).

Show answer
  1. NaOH taken = 25.0 mmol; excess = 11.0 mmol; consumed = 14.0 mmol. Aspirin consumes 3 NaOH (COOH, ester hydrolysis, phenol): aspirin = 4.667 mmol = 0.840 g. Purity = 0.840/0.900 = 93.3 %.
Practice 300 L3 NUM

20.0 mL of 0.0250 M K2Cr2O7 (in acid) is just enough to oxidise a sample of ethanol completely to ethanoic acid (3C2H5OH + 2Cr2O72− + 16H+ → 3CH3COOH + 4Cr3+ + 11H2O). Find the mass of ethanol.

Show answer
  1. n(Cr2O72−) = 0.0200 × 0.0250 = 5.00 × 10−4 mol. n(ethanol) = 5.00 × 10−4 × 3/2 = 7.50 × 10−4 mol. Mass = 7.50 × 10−4 × 46 = 0.0345 g = 34.5 mg.
Practice 301 L3 NUM

Calculate the pH of 0.100 M aqueous phenol (pKa = 10.0), neglecting the ionisation of water.

Show answer
  1. [H+] = √(KaC) = √(10−10 × 0.100) = 3.16 × 10−6 M → pH = 5.50.
Practice 302 L3 NUM

Calculate the pH of 0.0100 M p-nitrophenol (pKa = 7.15).

Show answer
  1. Ka = 7.08 × 10−8; [H+] = √(7.08 × 10−8 × 0.0100) = 2.66 × 10−5 M → pH = 4.58.
Practice 303 L3 NUM

What percentage of phenol (pKa 10) is ionised in a buffer of pH 9.0?

Show answer
  1. [A−]/[HA] = 10pH − pKa = 10−1 = 0.10. Fraction ionised = 0.10/1.10 = 9.1 %.
Practice 304 L3 NUM

Estimate the equilibrium constant for C6H5OH + C2H5O− ⇌ C6H5O− + C2H5OH using pKa values 10 and 16. What does it tell you about ethoxide as a base towards phenol?

Show answer
  1. K = Ka(phenol)/Ka(ethanol) = 10−10/10−16 = 106. The reaction goes essentially to completion: ethoxide quantitatively deprotonates phenol.
Practice 305 L3 NUM

Why can NaOH (pKa of water 15.7) convert phenol almost completely into phenoxide but cannot do the same to ethanol?

Show answer
  1. For phenol: K = 1015.7 − 10 = 105.7 ≫ 1. For ethanol: K = 1015.7 − 16 = 0.5, so the equilibrium has substantial ethanol remaining; alcohols are only partly converted to alkoxides by hydroxide.
Practice 306 L3 NUM

State, with the numerical value of the ring strain, why the ring opening of ethylene oxide by water is strongly exothermic and essentially irreversible.

Show answer
  1. The three-membered ring carries about 114 kJ mol−1 of strain (C–O–C angle about 61° instead of 109.5°). Opening the ring releases this strain, and the O–H and C–O bonds formed in the diol are strong, so the reaction is exothermic and goes to completion.
Practice 307 L3 NUM

8.8 g of ethylene oxide reacts with an excess of CH3MgBr and water. Calculate the mass of propan-1-ol formed at 70 % yield (C2H4O = 44; C3H8O = 60).

Show answer
  1. 8.8 g = 0.20 mol → 0.20 mol propan-1-ol at 100 % = 12.0 g; at 70 %: 8.4 g.
Practice 308 L3 ELU

An optically active alcohol C6H12O (A) decolourises Br2, is hydrogenated to an optically inactive alcohol B (C6H14O), and gives immediate Lucas turbidity. Identify A.

Show answer
  1. Unsaturated, chiral, and hydrogenation removes the chirality: a tertiary allylic alcohol. Hydrogenation must create a symmetric carbinol carbon. 3-Methylpent-1-en-3-ol, CH2=CH–C(CH3)(OH)C2H5 (C6H12O), is chiral; hydrogenation gives 3-methylpentan-3-ol, (C2H5)2C(OH)CH3, which is achiral ✓. A = 3-methylpent-1-en-3-ol.
Practice 309 L3 ELU

Compound A (C9H12O) does not react with Na, but with HI gives phenol and an alkyl iodide B (C3H7I) that gives propene with alcoholic KOH. B is hydrolysed to an alcohol that does not give iodoform. Identify A and B.

Show answer
  1. A is an aryl propyl ether. If B were 2-iodopropane, the alcohol (propan-2-ol) would give iodoform; it does not, so B = 1-iodopropane; A = propoxybenzene (C6H5OCH2CH2CH3).
Practice 310 L3 ELU

An aromatic compound X (C7H8O) is insoluble in NaOH, gives no colour with FeCl3, and with HI gives phenol and CH3I. Identify X.

Show answer
  1. An aryl methyl ether: anisole (methoxybenzene), C7H8O.
Practice 311 L3 ELU

An aromatic compound X (C7H8O) is insoluble in NaOH, gives no colour with FeCl3, reacts with Na with evolution of H2, and is oxidised by PCC to a compound that gives a silver mirror. Identify X.

Show answer
  1. Alcohol (Na), primary (aldehyde on PCC), not phenolic: benzyl alcohol, C6H5CH2OH → benzaldehyde.
Practice 312 L3 ELU

Compound W (C3H6O) is a chiral epoxide. With NaSC2H5 it gives a thioether-alcohol V. State the structure of V and its iodoform behaviour.

Show answer
  1. W = 2-methyloxirane. Thiolate attacks CH2: V = 1-(ethylsulfanyl)propan-2-ol, CH3CH(OH)CH2SC2H5; it has CH3CH(OH)– so it gives a positive iodoform test (although the sulphide is also oxidised by NaOI).
Practice 313 L3 ELU

Explain the order of reactivity toward electrophilic bromination: phenol > anisole > toluene > benzene > chlorobenzene > nitrobenzene.

Show answer
  1. Activation order of substituents: OH (+M) > OCH3 (+M, slightly less hydrogen-bond assistance) > CH3 (+I, hyperconjugation) > H. Cl is deactivating (−I outweighs +M) but o,p-directing; NO2 is strongly deactivating (−M, −I). In water phenol is partly phenoxide, which is more reactive still.
Practice 314 L3 ELU

Arrange the following in increasing order of boiling point and justify: o-nitrophenol, p-nitrophenol, phenol, nitrobenzene.

Show answer
  1. Nitrobenzene (211 °C; dipolar, no H-bond) vs phenol (182 °C; H-bonded). Experimental bp: phenol 182, nitrobenzene 211, o-nitrophenol 216, p-nitrophenol decomposes (~279). Phenol < nitrobenzene < o-nitrophenol < p-nitrophenol. The para isomer has intermolecular H-bonding; the ortho isomer is intramolecularly bonded but is heavier and more polar than phenol.
Practice 315 L3 ELU

Why does 2,6-di-tert-butylphenol have very different reactivity from phenol (it is a poor acid, and the phenoxide does not do Kolbe–Schmitt)? Name the compound class it exemplifies.

Show answer
  1. Two bulky ortho tert-butyl groups hinder solvation of the O–H/O− and block attack at the ortho positions; the phenoxy radical is stabilised and persistent, so such hindered phenols (BHT is one) are radical-trapping antioxidants.
Practice 316 L3 ELU

Why is the Lucas test usable only for alcohols of up to about six carbon atoms?

Show answer
  1. The test relies on the alcohol dissolving in the aqueous Lucas reagent and the alkyl chloride separating as an insoluble second phase. Higher alcohols do not dissolve to begin with, so the observed cloudiness cannot be interpreted.
Practice 317 L3 ELU

How do you carry out the conversion of cyclohexene to cyclohexane-1,2-diol of (a) cis and (b) trans configuration, and which one gives an optically inactive, achiral (meso) product?

Show answer
  1. (a) cis-Diol: OsO4/NMO (or cold dilute alkaline KMnO4); the cis-1,2-diol is meso (achiral). (b) trans-Diol: mCPBA then H3O+; it is chiral, formed as a racemate (optically inactive overall but resolvable).
Practice 318 L3 ELU

Why is the order of acidity of the hydroxyl-substituted benzoic acids: ortho > meta > para? Give pKa values.

Show answer
  1. pKa: salicylic 2.97, m-hydroxybenzoic 4.08, p-hydroxybenzoic 4.54 (benzoic 4.20). Ortho: intramolecular H-bond stabilises the carboxylate and −I of OH is closest. Meta: only −I. Para: +M of OH dominates and reduces acidity below benzoic acid.
Practice 319 L3 ELU

Mixture X contains phenol (A), cyclohexanol (B) and benzoic acid (C) in ether. Describe the order in which they are extracted by (i) NaHCO3(aq), (ii) NaOH(aq), and what remains in the ether.

Show answer
  1. (i) NaHCO3: benzoic acid (C) as benzoate. (ii) NaOH: phenol (A) as phenoxide. Remaining in ether: cyclohexanol (B).
Practice 320 L3 ELU

Two compounds, C7H8O (M and N) have the following properties. M dissolves in NaOH and with Br2/H2O gives a precipitate. N does not dissolve in NaOH, gives H2 with sodium and gives benzaldehyde with PCC. Identify M and N and a test that distinguishes them directly.

Show answer
  1. M = a cresol (e.g. m-cresol gives a tribromide); N = benzyl alcohol. Test: neutral FeCl3 (M violet/blue, N none) or NaOH solubility.
Practice 321 L3 ELU

A diol C4H10O2 consumes 1 mol HIO4 and gives two molecules of the same aldehyde. Identify the diol and give the number of its stereoisomers.

Show answer
  1. Butane-2,3-diol: cleavage gives 2 CH3CHO. Three stereoisomers: (R,R), (S,S) and meso.
Practice 322 L3 ELU

Explain why tetrahydrofuran is miscible with water but diethyl ether is only slightly soluble. Both have O accepting H-bonds.

Show answer
  1. In THF the alkyl groups are tied back in a ring so the oxygen lone pairs are more exposed; THF has a smaller hydrophobic surface per O and a larger dipole moment (1.75 D vs 1.15 D). Diethyl ether has two free ethyl groups that hinder hydration and add hydrophobic surface.
Practice 323 L3 ELU

How can CH3COCH2CH2COOC2H5 be converted (a) selectively into the hydroxy ester CH3CH(OH)CH2CH2COOC2H5 and (b) into the diol?

Show answer
  1. (a) NaBH4/EtOH reduces the ketone and leaves the ester. (b) LiAlH4, then H3O+, reduces both groups to give pentane-1,4-diol (and ethanol). The hydroxy ester can lactonise to γ-valerolactone on standing in acid.
Practice 324 L3 ELU

Which carbonyl compound and Grignard reagent are needed to make 1-phenylethanol, and what happens if the Grignard is added to benzaldehyde in the presence of moisture?

Show answer
  1. CH3MgBr + C6H5CHO (or C6H5MgBr + CH3CHO), then H3O+. Moisture destroys the Grignard (CH3MgBr + H2O → CH4 + Mg(OH)Br), so the yield falls.
Practice 325 L3 ELU

Explain why the acid-catalysed hydration of 3,3-dimethylbut-1-ene gives 2,3-dimethylbutan-2-ol as the major product while oxymercuration gives 3,3-dimethylbutan-2-ol.

Show answer
  1. In acid hydration the 2° cation rearranges by methyl shift to the 3° cation, which captures water. In oxymercuration a cyclic mercurinium ion is attacked by water at the more substituted carbon without any free carbocation, so the skeleton is retained.
Practice 326 L3 ELU

Write the mechanism-based reason why SOCl2 with pyridine inverts and SOCl2 alone retains the configuration of a secondary alcohol.

Show answer
  1. Both give a chlorosulphite ROS(=O)Cl. Alone, it collapses as an ion pair (SNi) delivering Cl− from the same face (retention). With pyridine, the HCl is trapped as pyridinium chloride and free Cl− attacks the back side of the chlorosulphite (SN2, inversion).
Practice 327 L3 ELU

Why can the Williamson synthesis be used to make cyclic ethers like THF but intramolecular attempts to make a 4-membered oxetane are low-yielding?

Show answer
  1. 5-Membered ring closure is favoured both entropically and sterically (little strain, good effective molarity); a 4-membered ring has about 107 kJ mol−1 strain and poorer orbital alignment, and the intermolecular side reactions (oligomers, elimination) compete.
