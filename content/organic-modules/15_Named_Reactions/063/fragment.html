57. Baeyer–Villiger oxidation (ketone → ester, cyclic ketone → lactone)

Reagents: a peroxy-acid: m-chloroperbenzoic acid (mCPBA), peracetic acid, trifluoroperacetic acid (CF3CO3H, the most reactive), or H2O2 with an acid catalyst. A ketone R–CO–R′ gives the ester R–CO–O–R′ (oxygen inserted between the carbonyl carbon and one of the two carbon groups); a cyclic ketone gives a lactone. Mechanism: (1) the peracid adds to the protonated C=O giving the tetrahedral Criegee intermediate R(R′)C(OH)–O–O–C(=O)R″; (2) in a concerted step one group migrates from carbon to the adjacent oxygen as the carboxylate leaves (the migrating group must be anti-periplanar to the O–O bond); (3) loss of H+ gives the ester. The migrating group keeps its stereochemistry (retention). Migratory aptitude (which group moves, the group better able to bear positive charge): tertiary alkyl > cyclohexyl (secondary) > benzyl ≈ phenyl > primary alkyl > methyl. H is better than all alkyl groups in aldehydes, giving carboxylic acids (but electron-rich aryl aldehydes give aryl formates, see Dakin).

mCPBA or CH₃CO₃H⟶
Acetophenone → phenyl ethanoate (phenyl migrates, not CH3)
CF₃CO₃H⟶
Pinacolone → tert-butyl ethanoate (tertiary alkyl migrates)
mCPBA⟶
Cyclohexyl methyl ketone → cyclohexyl ethanoate (secondary > methyl); the ring carbon keeps its configuration
CH₃CO₃H⟶
Cyclohexanone → ε-caprolactone (7-membered)
mCPBA⟶
2-Methylcyclohexanone → 7-methyloxepan-2-one: the more substituted C-2 migrates, so O ends up beside the CH(CH3) carbon
H₂O₂ or peracid⟶
Cyclobutanone → γ-butyrolactone (strain relief makes cyclobutanone very reactive)
CF₃CO₃H⟶
Benzophenone → phenyl benzoate
Trap A substrate that also contains a C=C: peracids epoxidise alkenes faster than they oxidise ketones, so a keto-alkene gives the epoxy-ketone. Aromatic ring substituents change the aryl aptitude: p-methoxyphenyl migrates better than phenyl, p-nitrophenyl worse. The new C–O bond is to the group that migrated, never to the other group. If the product names ask "RCOOR′ or R′COOR", write the carbonyl on the side that did not migrate.
Example 53 Main

Baeyer–Villiger products

Give the main product when each ketone is treated with mCPBA: (a) CH3COCH2CH3, (b) CH3CH2COC(CH3)3, (c) (CH3)2CHCOCH3, (d) 4-methylcyclohexanone.

Show solution
  1. (a) Ethyl > methyl: ethyl ethanoate, CH3COOCH2CH3. (b) t-Butyl migrates: CH3CH2COOC(CH3)3 = tert-butyl propanoate. (c) Isopropyl (secondary) > methyl: isopropyl ethanoate, CH3COOCH(CH3)2. (d) 5-methyloxepan-2-one (4-methyl-ε-caprolactone): the ring bond to C-2 or C-6 migrates (equivalent), the oxygen is inserted into the ring, and the methyl is on C-5 of the seven-membered lactone (O is atom 1, C=O is atom 2).
Example 54 Adv

Chain of oxidations

Cyclohexanol is oxidised to A (PCC), then to B (peracid), then B is hydrolysed with aqueous NaOH to give C. Name A, B and C and the C6 difunctional product.

Show solution
  1. A = cyclohexanone; B = ε-caprolactone (Baeyer–Villiger); C = the hydrolysed lactone: 6-hydroxyhexanoate, HO(CH2)5COO− Na+; on acidification, 6-hydroxyhexanoic acid (which re-lactonises slowly). Further oxidation (CrO3) would give adipic acid HOOC(CH2)4COOH, the nylon-6,6 monomer.
