23. Oxidative cleavage of alkenes

23.1 Ozonolysis

Reagents: (1) O3, CH2Cl2 or MeOH, −78 °C; (2) work-up. Mechanism (Criegee): O3 adds to C=C by 1,3-dipolar cycloaddition to give the primary ozonide (molozonide), which fragments (retro-[3+2]) to a carbonyl compound + a carbonyl oxide; these recombine (in the opposite orientation) as the secondary ozonide (1,2,4-trioxolane); the work-up cleaves the O–O bonds. The key result: C=C is cut completely and each carbon of the double bond becomes a C=O (carbon skeleton preserved, no carbon is lost except in oxidative treatment of =CH2).

23.2 Counting rules (for the integer-type problems)

Formula number of carbonyl groups formed = 2 × (number of C=C). A ring alkene gives one molecule with two C=O; an acyclic alkene gives two molecules. An acyclic chain with n isolated C=C gives n + 1 fragments.
  • Cyclic alkene with one C=C → one dicarbonyl with the same number of carbons as the ring.
  • Terminal =CH2 → HCHO (reductive); with H2O2: CO2.
  • Isolated dienes: each C=C is cleaved independently; the carbons between the two double bonds end up in a dicarbonyl fragment.
  • Conjugated dienes: the central C–C is a σ bond between the two internal carbons; each internal carbon becomes a C=O, producing a 1,2-dicarbonyl (e.g. glyoxal OHC–CHO).
  • Alkynes: ozonolysis of RC≡CR′ gives RCOOH + R′COOH (via an α-dicarbonyl); terminal alkynes give RCOOH + CO2 (+ H2O).
Example 40 Integer

Ozonolysis structure elucidation

An alkene C7H14 on ozonolysis (Zn/H2O) gives propanone and butanal. Find its structure and name. How many moles of O3 would be consumed per mole?

Show solution
  1. Join the two carbonyl carbons by a C=C: (CH3)2C=CH–CH2CH2CH3: C3H6 (from propanone, 3 C) + C4 (butanal: CH3CH2CH2CHO, 4 C) = C7. Structure: (CH3)2C=CHCH2CH2CH3, i.e. 2-methylhex-2-ene. Formula C7H14 ✔.
  2. One C=C → 1 mol O3.
Example 41 Integer

Count the products

How many distinct organic products (counting each different compound once) are obtained on reductive ozonolysis of (a) 2,5-dimethylhexa-2,4-diene? (b) limonene? (c) hepta-1,6-diene? (d) cyclopentene?

Show solution
  1. (a) (CH3)2C=CH–CH=C(CH3)2 is symmetric: ends give 2 × propanone; centre CH–CH gives OHC–CHO. Distinct products: propanone, ethanedial = 2 (3 molecules).
  2. (b) Limonene (ring C=C plus isopropenyl C=C): methanal plus one ring-opened keto-aldehyde, CH3C(=O)CH2CH2CH(CH2CHO)C(CH3)=O (RDKit-checked): 2 distinct products.
  3. (c) Hepta-1,6-diene: 2 × HCHO (one compound) and pentanedial OHC(CH2)3CHO: 2 distinct products.
  4. (d) Cyclopentene → pentanedial (glutaraldehyde): 1 product.
Example 42 Integer

Hot KMnO4/H+ products

Predict the products of vigorous oxidation (hot conc. KMnO4, H+) of (a) 2-methylbut-2-ene (b) propene (c) 1-methylcyclopentene (d) cyclohexene (e) CH3CH=CHCH=CH2.

Show solution
  1. Rules: =CH2 → CO2 (+ H2O); RCH= → RCOOH; R2C= → ketone.
  2. (a) (CH3)2C=CHCH3 → CH3COCH3 (propanone) + CH3COOH (ethanoic acid).
  3. (b) CH3COOH + CO2.
  4. (c) 1-Methylcyclopentene → CH3CO–CH2CH2CH2–COOH (5-oxohexanoic acid): the ring opens to a keto-acid.
  5. (d) Cyclohexene → hexanedioic (adipic) acid HOOC(CH2)4COOH.
  6. (e) Penta-1,3-diene: CH3CH=CH–CH=CH2: CH3COOH + HOOC–COOH (oxalic acid, further to CO2) + CO2. Products: CH3COOH, (COOH)2, CO2 (oxalic acid is itself oxidised under hot permanganate to 2 CO2).
