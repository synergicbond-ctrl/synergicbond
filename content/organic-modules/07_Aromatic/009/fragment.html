7. Electrophilic aromatic substitution: the general mechanism

Benzene's π cloud is a good nucleophile but a poor one compared with an alkene: attack by an electrophile E+ costs the aromatic stabilisation (about 150 kJ mol-1), so benzene needs strong electrophiles (or catalysed generation of them). The reaction is a substitution rather than an addition because the second step, loss of H+, regenerates the aromatic ring and is much more favourable than adding a nucleophile to the cation.

Key idea Remember the geometry of the arenium ion: counting from the sp3 carbon (C1), positive charge sits on C2, C4 and C6. This single fact controls all of directing-group theory in section 10.

7.1 The energy profile

The reaction has two steps and two transition states. The first TS (formation of the σ-complex) is higher, so step 1 is rate-determining. The σ-complex is a genuine intermediate: it has been observed (heptamethylbenzenium ion, mesitylene–ethyl fluoride–BF3 adducts at low temperature) and in some cases isolated as a salt.

Key idea Rate = k[ArH][E+] for most EAS: the first step decides the rate, and the second step (deprotonation) is fast. Consequence: no primary kinetic isotope effect (kH/kD ≈ 1) in nitration, bromination, chlorination and Friedel–Crafts: the C–H bond is not broken in the slow step. Exceptions where kH/kD > 1 (deprotonation partly rate-limiting): sulphonation (≈ 1–2), iodination, azo coupling with hindered substrates, and sulphonation/nitration of very crowded arenes.
Example 8 Main

Isotope effect

C6H6 and C6D6 are nitrated in separate vessels (HNO3/H2SO4). What do you conclude from the observation that both react at the same rate, and what would a kH/kD near 7 have implied?

Show solution
  1. Equal rates mean the C–H(D) bond is not cleaved in the rate-determining step.
  2. Therefore the slow step is the attack of NO2+ on the π system to form the σ-complex; deprotonation (C–H cleavage) occurs after the slow step in a fast step.
  3. A kH/kD near 7 would show a normal primary isotope effect: C–H bond breaking in the slow step (deprotonation rate-limiting, or a concerted process), which is what is seen (small values) only in a few EAS such as sulphonation of hindered arenes.

7.2 Why substitution, not addition?

The σ-complex can in principle react in two ways: (a) lose H+ from the sp3 carbon: substitution, giving back the aromatic ring (stabilised by about 150 kJ mol-1); (b) add a nucleophile to a carbon bearing the charge: addition, giving a non-aromatic cyclohexadiene. Path (a) is overwhelmingly favoured thermodynamically and kinetically. Addition is seen only for the more easily dearomatised polycyclics (naphthalene, anthracene, phenanthrene), where less aromatic stabilisation is lost.

7.3 Reactivity–selectivity and the Hammond postulate

The TS of step 1 resembles the σ-complex (the cation is high in energy), so substituent effects on the stability of the arenium ion are felt almost fully in the TS. That is why groups that stabilise the cation (+M, +I, hyperconjugation) speed up the reaction and direct to the positions that place the charge next to them. A very reactive electrophile (e.g. Cl+-like, early TS) is less selective; a less reactive, more selective electrophile (Br2/FeBr3, NO2+ in weakly ionising solvents) discriminates more. Example: toluene with chlorine gives o : m : p ≈ 60 : 1 : 39 for chlorination (partial rate factor for p ≈ 820 relative to benzene) but bromination of toluene is far more selective for para (para : ortho ≈ 67 : 33, m ≈ 0.3 %).

7.4 Stoichiometry of ortho : para and the statistical factor

There are two ortho positions and one para position. A group with no steric or electronic preference would give ortho : meta : para = 2 : 2 : 1. Anything different from this is due to electronic or steric effects. Ortho is usually less than 2 × para when the group or electrophile is large (t-butyl, SO3H, COCH3) and the para isomer dominates. For small or specially directing groups (OH, NH2 under certain conditions, F), ortho can be competitive or even dominant.

Trap Many questions say "predict the major product" for a para-directing group and the answer key says para; but nitration of toluene gives more ortho than para (58 : 38) simply because two ortho positions are available. Read "major" as the statistically supported answer unless the electrophile or group is bulky.
