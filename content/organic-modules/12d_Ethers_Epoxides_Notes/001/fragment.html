2. Nomenclature: alkoxy, oxa and cyclic names

In substitutive IUPAC nomenclature an ether is not a principal characteristic group. It is named as an alkoxy derivative of the parent hydrocarbon. The larger or more substituted alkyl group is chosen as the parent chain and the smaller one becomes the alkoxy prefix (methoxy, ethoxy, propoxy, propan-2-yloxy, tert-butoxy). For example CH3OCH2CH3 is methoxyethane and (CH3)2CHOCH3 is 2-methoxypropane. Older common names list the two groups alphabetically followed by 'ether' (ethyl methyl ether).

Methoxyethane, 2-methoxypropane, 1,2-dimethoxyethane, methoxybenzene (anisole) and ethoxybenzene (phenetole).

When another group has higher priority the alkoxy group appears as a prefix: HOCH2CH2OCH3 is 2-methoxyethanol. In replacement ('oxa') nomenclature the oxygen is treated as an atom that replaces a CH2 group in the chain, so CH3OCH2CH2OCH3 can be called 2,5-dioxahexane. The same idea names the heterocycles.

  • Oxirane (3-membered, ethylene oxide); substituted forms such as 2-methyloxirane replace the old 'epoxy' prefix (1,2-epoxypropane).
  • Oxetane (4-membered).
  • Oxolane (5-membered, tetrahydrofuran, THF).
  • Oxane (6-membered, tetrahydropyran, THP).
  • 1,4-Dioxane and 1,3-dioxolane (two oxygens).

Oxirane, oxetane, oxolane, oxane and 7-oxabicyclo[4.1.0]heptane (cyclohexene oxide).

Numbering of a ring starts at the heteroatom (O = 1) and proceeds in the direction that gives the lowest locants to substituents. In a fused epoxide the bicyclic name locates oxygen as an 'oxa' bridge, as in 7-oxabicyclo[4.1.0]heptane.

Trap. The chain with more carbon atoms (or the more substituted one) is the parent. In 2-ethoxypropane the parent is propane, not ethane, because the propane chain is the larger alkyl portion.
