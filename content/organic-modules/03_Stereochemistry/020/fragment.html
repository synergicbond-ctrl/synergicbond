17. Ring strain and the shapes of small rings

17.1 Baeyer strain theory and its limits

Baeyer (1885) assumed all rings are planar and that strain is the deviation of the internal angle from the tetrahedral angle 109°28′. For a planar n-gon the internal angle is 180(n − 2)/n, and the deviation per bond is half the difference:

Formula Angle deviation d = ½ [109°28′ − 180(n − 2)/n]

Key idea Baeyer's theory is right for small rings (3 and 4) and wrong for rings of six or more: larger rings are puckered, not planar, and cyclohexane in the chair has bond angles of about 111°, so it is essentially strain-free. Sachse and Mohr proposed the puckered rings. Heats of combustion per CH2 confirm: cyclopropane 697, cyclobutane 686, cyclopentane 664, cyclohexane 659 kJ per CH2 (the open-chain value is about 659).
Trap Ring strain has three parts: angle strain, torsional strain (eclipsing) and steric strain (transannular H···H crowding in medium rings of 8–11 carbons). Do not explain cyclopentane's strain by angle strain alone: its angles are near perfect but the planar form has ten eclipsing H/H interactions; the envelope shape relieves part of that.

17.2 Cyclopropane, cyclobutane, cyclopentane

  • Cyclopropane: C–C–C = 60°. The C–C bonds are "bent" (banana bonds), with an inter-orbital angle of about 104°; the C–C bonds are weaker (about 270 kJ mol-1 against 347 for ethane) and the ring opens easily (hydrogenation, HBr, Br2 at room temperature, behaves like an alkene).
  • Cyclobutane: puckered "butterfly" with a fold of about 25–30°, which reduces eclipsing at the cost of slightly more angle strain.
  • Cyclopentane: the envelope (four carbons in a plane, the fifth out of the plane) rapidly pseudo-rotates.
