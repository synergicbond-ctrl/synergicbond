14. Michael addition (conjugate, 1,4-addition)

Reagents: a stabilised carbanion (the Michael donor: enolate of malonate, acetoacetate, a ketone, nitroalkane, cyanoacetate) + an α,β-unsaturated carbonyl, nitrile, nitro or sulfone (the Michael acceptor), catalytic base (NaOEt, piperidine). Product: a 1,5-dicarbonyl (or 1,5-dicarbonyl equivalent). Mechanism: the nucleophile attacks the β-carbon (the soft electrophilic site of the conjugated system), giving an enolate that is protonated at the α-carbon. The overall change is addition of Nu–H across C=C. 1,4 or 1,2? Soft nucleophiles (enolates, R2CuLi, amines, thiols, CN− under thermodynamic conditions) add 1,4. Hard nucleophiles (RLi, LiAlH4, most Grignard reagents, NaBH4 on enones, alone) add 1,2 to the C=O. Adding catalytic Cu(I) to a Grignard switches it to 1,4.

Trap Product-type question: "Which of the following is a Michael reaction?" Look for a stabilised carbanion adding to the β-carbon of a C=C–C=O/C≡N/NO2; the product has the two activating groups in a 1,5-relationship (in the malonate example: ester C=O and ketone C=O separated by three carbons).
