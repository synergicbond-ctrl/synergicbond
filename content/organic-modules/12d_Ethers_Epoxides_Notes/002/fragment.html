3. Physical properties

The most important physical property of ethers is their low boiling point relative to alcohols of the same molar mass. Alcohol molecules associate through O–H···O hydrogen bonds, which must be broken on boiling. Ether molecules cannot donate hydrogen bonds, so their intermolecular forces are limited to dipole–dipole and dispersion forces. The boiling point of an ether is therefore close to that of an alkane of comparable mass and far below that of an isomeric alcohol.

  • Ethoxyethane (diethyl ether): about 35 °C; pentane: about 36 °C; butan-1-ol: about 118 °C.
  • Dimethyl ether is a gas (about −25 °C) whereas its isomer ethanol boils near 78 °C.
  • Among isomeric ethers the boiling point falls as branching increases and the surface area decreases.

Solubility in water is governed by hydrogen bonding with water. Ether oxygen accepts hydrogen bonds from water, so low-mass ethers dissolve to a small extent: dimethyl ether is quite soluble, diethyl ether dissolves only slightly, and as the hydrocarbon portion grows the solubility drops. Cyclic ethers such as THF and 1,4-dioxane have the alkyl groups tied back and are miscible with water in all proportions. Ethers dissolve organic compounds well and are used as solvents, and diethyl ether is less dense than water.

Ranking rule. For isomers of C4H10O: butan-1-ol > butan-2-ol > 2-methylpropan-2-ol (alcohols, H-bonded, branching lowers the value) > methoxypropane > ethoxyethane > 2-methoxypropane (ethers, no H-bond donation).

Ethers are chemically inert towards bases, dilute acids, reducing agents and most oxidants, which makes them excellent solvents for Grignard reagents, organolithium compounds and metal hydrides. The solvent molecules solvate the metal cation through lone-pair donation and thereby stabilise the organometallic. They are, however, highly flammable and their vapours form explosive mixtures with air.

Trap. Do not argue that ethers have lower boiling points because they are 'non-polar'. They are polar; the reason is the absence of an O–H bond, hence no intermolecular hydrogen bonding.
