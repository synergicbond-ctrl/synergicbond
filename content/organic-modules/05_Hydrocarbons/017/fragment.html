16. Alkenes: structure, geometry and stability

Alkenes (olefins) CnH2n contain a C=C bond: one σ bond (sp2–sp2) and one π bond (side-on overlap of two p orbitals). The six atoms of ethene lie in one plane, with H–C=C ≈ 121° and H–C–H ≈ 118°. C=C length is 134 pm (shorter than C–C, 154 pm); bond enthalpy about 614 kJ mol-1, of which the π bond contributes about 260 kJ. Rotation about C=C would break the π bond, so cis/trans (E/Z) isomerism exists. The π electron cloud above and below the plane is exposed, so an alkene acts as a nucleophile (electron donor) towards electrophiles: that is the core of its chemistry.

16.1 E/Z nomenclature and counting geometrical isomers

Use CIP priority on each carbon of the double bond: Z (zusammen) if the two higher-priority groups are on the same side; E (entgegen) if on opposite sides. cis/trans refers to like groups, so cis ≠ Z in general.

Example 21 Integer

Count geometrical isomers

How many geometrical isomers does (a) hexa-2,4-diene, (b) octa-2,4,6-triene, (c) CH3–CH=CH–CH=CH–C2H5 have?

Show solution
  1. (a) Hexa-2,4-diene CH3CH=CH–CH=CHCH3 is symmetrical, n = 2 (even): 21 + 20 = 3 (E,E; Z,Z; E,Z).
  2. (b) Octa-2,4,6-triene CH3CH=CH–CH=CH–CH=CHCH3 is symmetrical, n = 3 (odd): 22 + 21 = 6.
  3. (c) Hepta-2,4-diene with ends CH3 and C2H5 is unsymmetrical: 22 = 4.

16.2 Stability of alkenes

Alkene stability is measured by the heat of hydrogenation (ΔH of the reaction with H2 to give the same alkane): smaller ΔH = more stable alkene. Three effects stack:

  • Alkyl substitution (hyperconjugation of C–H/C–C σ bonds into the π system and the greater strength of an sp2–sp3 bond than sp3–sp3): more alkyl groups on the C=C = more stable. Order: R2C=CR2 > R2C=CHR > R2C=CH2 ≈ RCH=CHR > RCH=CH2 > CH2=CH2.
  • Geometry: trans (E) is more stable than cis (Z): in cis the alkyl groups are close (steric strain ≈ 4 kJ).
  • Conjugation with another π system stabilises further (≈ 15 kJ).

Example 22 Advanced

Rank by stability

Arrange in order of decreasing stability: (A) 2-methylbut-2-ene (B) 2-methylbut-1-ene (C) pent-1-ene (D) (E)-pent-2-ene.

Show solution
  1. Classify by substitution: A is trisubstituted; D is 1,2-disubstituted (trans); B is 1,1-disubstituted; C is monosubstituted.
  2. More alkyl groups on C=C means more stability, and trans disubstituted lies a little below the trisubstituted alkene but above the 1,1-disubstituted and monosubstituted ones (heats of hydrogenation: A −113, D −115 to −116 for trans-2-pentene range, B ≈ −119, C ≈ −126 kJ mol-1).
  3. Order of stability: A > D > B > C.

16.3 Strain in cycloalkenes: trans only in large rings

A trans double bond requires at least eight ring carbons: trans-cyclooctene exists (chiral, and strained by about 40 kJ more than cis); trans-cycloheptene is a transient species; smaller rings contain only cis double bonds. Bredt's rule prevents a double bond at the bridgehead of a small bicyclic compound.
