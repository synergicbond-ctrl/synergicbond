51. Oxidation numbers and counting redox changes

To decide whether a step is an oxidation, a reduction, or neither, assign an oxidation number to the carbon concerned: +1 for each bond to O, N or halogen; −1 for each bond to H; 0 for each bond to C (a double bond counts twice).

Carbon inBonds to heteroatomsBonds to HOxidation number
CH3OH (carbinol C)13−2
RCH2OH (carbinol C)12−1
RCHO (C=O carbon)21+1
R2CHOH (carbinol C)110
R2C=O (C=O carbon)20+2
RCOOH (carboxyl C)30+3
HCHO220
CO240+4

So: RCH2OH → RCHO is an oxidation (−1 → +1, two electrons); RCHO → RCOOH is another oxidation (+1 → +3); R2CO → R2CHOH is a reduction (+2 → 0); C=O → CH2 (Clemmensen) is a reduction by four electrons (+2 → −2). In a Cannizzaro reaction, one carbon goes +1 → +3 and the other +1 → −1: a disproportionation (net electron count conserved).

Example 54 Adv

Disproportionation and oxidation states

(a) Identify the oxidation number of the carbonyl carbon in HCHO, CH3CHO, CH3COCH3. (b) Show that the Cannizzaro reaction of HCHO is a disproportionation. (c) How many electrons are transferred when 1 mol of ethanal is oxidised to ethanoic acid?

Show solution
  1. (a) HCHO: 2 bonds to O, 2 to H: +2 − 2 = 0. CH3CHO: 2 bonds to O, 1 to H, 1 to C: +2 − 1 = +1. CH3COCH3: 2 bonds to O, 2 to C: +2.
  2. (b) 2 HCHO → CH3OH + HCOO−: carbon goes 0 → −2 (reduction) in methanol and 0 → +2 in formate (the formate carbon has two bonds to O in C=O plus one in C–O−, three heteroatom bonds, one H: +3 − 1 = +2). Net electron change zero: disproportionation.
  3. (c) CH3CHO (+1) → CH3COOH (+3): 2 electrons per molecule.
