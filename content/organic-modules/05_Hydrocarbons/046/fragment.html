45. Problem bank, Level 1 (foundation and JEE Main): alkanes and cycloalkanes

Every problem has a worked answer. Attempt first, then open the solution. Level 1 problems test one idea each; Level 2 (JEE Advanced multi-correct, matrix, paragraph) and Level 3 (integer and multi-step) follow.

Practice 1 L1 Alkanes

Isomer count

How many structural isomers does C6H14 have, and which has the lowest boiling point?

Show answer

5 isomers: n-hexane, 2-methylpentane, 3-methylpentane, 2,2-dimethylbutane, 2,3-dimethylbutane. The most branched (most spherical, least surface contact), 2,2-dimethylbutane, has the lowest boiling point (49.7 °C).

Practice 2 L1 Alkanes

Boiling-point order

Arrange n-pentane, isopentane and neopentane in decreasing boiling point.

Show answer

n-Pentane (36 °C) > isopentane (28 °C) > neopentane (9.5 °C). Branching reduces surface area and dispersion forces.

Practice 3 L1 Alkanes

Melting point

Among the three pentane isomers which has the highest melting point and why?

Show answer

Neopentane (−17 °C vs −130 °C for n-pentane): its spherical symmetry packs well in the crystal.

Practice 4 L1 Alkanes

Wurtz reaction

Give the product of the Wurtz reaction of (a) bromoethane (b) 2-bromopropane (c) a mixture of CH3Br and C2H5Br.

Show answer

(a) n-butane. (b) 2,3-dimethylbutane. (c) A mixture of three alkanes: ethane, propane, butane.

Practice 5 L1 Alkanes

Wurtz failure

Why can neopentane not be made in good yield by a Wurtz reaction, and by what method can it be made?

Show answer

Neopentane (CH3)4C would need a methyl halide plus a tertiary t-butyl halide; t-BuX eliminates under sodium (isobutene forms). Use Corey–House: [(CH3)3C]2CuLi (from t-BuLi and CuI) + CH3I → (CH3)4C.

Practice 6 L1 Alkanes

Kolbe electrolysis

What alkane forms by electrolysis of aqueous sodium propanoate? What forms at each electrode?

Show answer

Butane. Anode: 2 CH3CH2COO− → CH3CH2CH2CH3 + 2 CO2 + 2e−. Cathode: 2 H2O + 2e− → H2 + 2 OH−.

Practice 7 L1 Alkanes

Soda lime

Sodium butanoate is heated with soda lime. Name the hydrocarbon. How would the product differ if Kolbe electrolysis were used?

Show answer

Soda lime: propane (one carbon less). Kolbe: hexane (R–R, 2 × 3 carbons).

Practice 8 L1 Alkanes

Corey–House

Write the reaction that gives 2-methylpentane from a Gilman reagent.

Show answer

(CH3)2CH–Li → [(CH3)2CH]2CuLi (with CuI). Then + CH3CH2CH2Br → (CH3)2CH–CH2CH2CH3 (2-methylpentane).

Practice 9 L1 Alkanes

Carbides

Name the gas liberated when water acts on (a) Al4C3 (b) CaC2 (c) Mg2C3.

Show answer

(a) methane. (b) ethyne. (c) propyne.

Practice 10 L1 Alkanes

Grignard hydrolysis

What is formed when CH3CH2MgBr reacts with D2O?

Show answer

CH3CH2D (deuterioethane) + Mg(OD)Br.

Practice 11 L1 Alkanes

Carbon-count bookkeeping

How many carbon atoms does the alkane have that is formed from (a) Wurtz of 1-bromobutane (b) soda lime on sodium pentanoate (c) Kolbe on sodium butanoate?

Show answer

(a) 8 (octane). (b) pentanoate has 5 C, loses one: 4 (butane). (c) 2 × 3 = 6 (hexane).

Practice 12 L1 Halogenation

Reactivity order

Arrange F2, Cl2, Br2, I2 in decreasing reactivity and in decreasing selectivity towards alkane C–H bonds.

Show answer

Reactivity: F2 > Cl2 > Br2 > I2. Selectivity: I2 > Br2 > Cl2 > F2 (Hammond postulate: the less exothermic abstraction has the later, more product-like TS).

Practice 13 L1 Halogenation

Why iodination fails

Why does direct iodination of methane not work?

Show answer

The H-abstraction by I• (ΔH = +34 kcal) and the overall reaction (ΔH = +13 kcal) are endothermic, and the HI formed reduces the alkyl iodide back to the alkane (RI + HI → RH + I2).

Practice 14 L1 Halogenation

Chain steps

Identify initiation, propagation and termination steps among: (i) Cl2 → 2Cl• (ii) CH3• + Cl2 → CH3Cl + Cl• (iii) CH3• + CH3• → C2H6 (iv) Cl• + CH4 → HCl + CH3•.

Show answer

(i) initiation; (ii) and (iv) propagation; (iii) termination.

Practice 15 L1 Halogenation

Number of monochloro products

How many monochlorination products (structural isomers only) does each alkane give: (a) n-butane (b) isobutane (c) neopentane (d) 2-methylbutane (e) 2,3-dimethylbutane?

Show answer

(a) 2. (b) 2. (c) 1. (d) 4. (e) 2.

Practice 16 L1 Halogenation

With stereoisomers

Repeat the previous problem counting stereoisomers.

Show answer

(a) 3 (2-chlorobutane is chiral). (b) 2. (c) 1. (d) 6. (e) 3 (1-chloro-2,3-dimethylbutane is chiral).

Practice 17 L1 Halogenation

Percentage distribution

Monochlorination of 2-methylpropane (relative rates 1° : 3° = 1 : 5). What fraction is tert-butyl chloride?

Show answer

9 primary H × 1 = 9; 1 tertiary × 5 = 5. Fraction = 5/14 = 35.7 %.

Practice 18 L1 Halogenation

Bromination selectivity

Monobromination of propane (1° : 2° = 1 : 82). Percentage of 2-bromopropane?

Show answer

2 × 82 = 164; 6 × 1 = 6. 164/170 = 96.5 %.

Practice 19 L1 Halogenation

Radical stability

Arrange the radicals in decreasing stability: CH3•, (CH3)2CH•, (CH3)3C•, CH3CH2•, CH2=CH–CH2•.

Show answer

Allyl > (CH3)3C• > (CH3)2CH• > CH3CH2• > CH3•.

Practice 20 L1 Halogenation

Which gives a single monochloride?

Which of the following gives only one monochloro derivative: neopentane, 2,2-dimethylbutane, cyclohexane, isopentane?

Show answer

Neopentane and cyclohexane (all H's equivalent).

Practice 21 L1 Halogenation

ΔH of bromination

Using BDE (kcal/mol): C–H 105, Br–Br 46, C–Br 70, H–Br 87, find ΔH for CH4 + Br2 → CH3Br + HBr and for the first propagation step.

Show answer

Overall: (105 + 46) − (70 + 87) = −6 kcal/mol. First propagation: 105 − 87 = +18 kcal/mol (endothermic, rate-determining).

Practice 22 L1 Halogenation

Sulphochlorination

What is the product of ethane + SO2 + Cl2, hν?

Show answer

Ethanesulphonyl chloride, CH3CH2SO2Cl (Reed reaction).

Practice 23 L1 Reactions

Nitration of propane

How many nitro compounds form in the vapour-phase nitration of propane?

Show answer

Four: 1-nitropropane, 2-nitropropane, nitroethane, nitromethane (C–C cleavage accompanies substitution).

Practice 24 L1 Reactions

Combustion

How many moles of O2 are needed to burn completely 1 mol of (a) C4H10 (b) C6H14? Volume of CO2 at STP from 11 g of propane?

Show answer

(3n+1)/2: (a) 6.5 mol; (b) 9.5 mol. 11 g C3H8 = 0.25 mol → 0.75 mol CO2 = 16.8 L.

Practice 25 L1 Reactions

Isomerisation and aromatisation

Name the reagents for (i) n-butane → isobutane (ii) n-hexane → benzene.

Show answer

(i) anhydrous AlCl3/HCl, ≈ 300 K. (ii) Cr2O3/Al2O3 (or Pt), 773 K, 10–20 atm (4 H2 lost).

Practice 26 L1 Reactions

Oxidation of isobutane

Which reagent oxidises isobutane to tert-butyl alcohol, and why does it selectively attack the tertiary position?

Show answer

KMnO4 (or O2/catalyst). The 3° C–H is weakest (BDE 96 kcal) and gives the most stable radical.

Practice 27 L1 Reactions

Hydrogenation route

Which alkenes give 2-methylbutane on hydrogenation?

Show answer

2-Methylbut-1-ene, 3-methylbut-1-ene, 2-methylbut-2-ene: three.

Practice 28 L1 Reactions

Reduction of RX

Name three reagents that convert CH3CH2CH2Br into propane.

Show answer

Zn/H+; LiAlH4; H2/Pd (also Mg then H2O).

Practice 29 L1 Conformations

Ethane

State the dihedral angle and approximate energy of the eclipsed form of ethane relative to staggered.

Show answer

0° dihedral; about 12 kJ mol-1 (2.9 kcal) higher: three H/H eclipsing interactions of about 4 kJ each.

Practice 30 L1 Conformations

Butane

Arrange anti, gauche, eclipsed (120°) and fully eclipsed conformers of butane in order of increasing energy, with values.

Show answer

Anti (0) < gauche (3.8) < eclipsed (16) < fully eclipsed (19 kJ mol-1).

Practice 31 L1 Conformations

Gauche more stable

In which of these is the gauche conformer more stable than anti? (a) butane (b) ethylene glycol (c) 1,2-dichloroethane in the gas phase

Show answer

(b) because of intramolecular hydrogen bonding. Butane: anti is more stable. 1,2-Dichloroethane in the gas phase also prefers anti (dipole and steric repulsion), so only (b).

Practice 32 L1 Conformations

Newman projection

How many distinct conformations (staggered + eclipsed) are conventionally named for ethane, and what is the relative number of H/H eclipsing interactions in the eclipsed conformer?

Show answer

Two named conformations: staggered and eclipsed. Eclipsed ethane has 3 H/H eclipsing interactions; staggered has none.

Practice 33 L1 Physical

Alkane solubility

Why are alkanes insoluble in water but soluble in benzene?

Show answer

They cannot hydrogen-bond to water, and the cost of breaking water's hydrogen-bond network is not repaid; with benzene, dispersion forces of similar magnitude replace those in the pure liquids.

Practice 34 L1 Alkanes

Cracking

Write the products of cracking of C10H22 into one alkane and one alkene of equal carbon number.

Show answer

C10H22 → C5H12 + C5H10 (pentane + pentene).

Practice 35 L1 Cycloalkanes

Baeyer angle strain

Calculate the Baeyer deviation for cyclobutane and cyclopentane.

Show answer

Cyclobutane: ½(109°28′ − 90°) = 9°44′. Cyclopentane: ½(109°28′ − 108°) = 0°44′.

Practice 36 L1 Cycloalkanes

Stability order

According to Baeyer strain theory which cycloalkane is most stable? Which is actually the most stable?

Show answer

Baeyer: cyclopentane (smallest deviation). In reality: cyclohexane (chair, strain-free), because rings larger than 5 are not planar.

Practice 37 L1 Cycloalkanes

Heats of combustion per CH2

Arrange cyclopropane, cyclobutane, cyclopentane, cyclohexane in decreasing −ΔHc per CH2.

Show answer

Cyclopropane (697) > cyclobutane (686) > cyclopentane (664) > cyclohexane (659 kJ mol-1).

Practice 38 L1 Cycloalkanes

Ring-opening

Predict the product of (a) cyclopropane + H2/Ni, 80 °C (b) cyclopropane + HBr (c) cyclobutane + H2/Ni, 200 °C (d) cyclopentane + HBr.

Show answer

(a) propane. (b) 1-bromopropane. (c) butane. (d) no reaction.

Practice 39 L1 Cycloalkanes

Preparation

How is cyclopropane prepared from 1,3-dibromopropane?

Show answer

Zn dust (or Na) in ethanol/ether: intramolecular Wurtz (Freund reaction); the two C–Br bonds are replaced by a C–C bond.

Practice 40 L1 Cycloalkanes

Regiochemistry of ring opening

Give the product of methylcyclopropane + HBr.

Show answer

2-Bromobutane (H adds to the less substituted ring carbon, Br to the more substituted carbon).

Practice 41 L1 Cycloalkanes

Isomer counts

How many isomers (including cyclic and stereo) does C4H8 have? And C5H10?

Show answer

C4H8: 5 structural (6 with the cis/trans pair of but-2-ene). C5H10: 10 structural; 13 including stereoisomers.

Practice 42 L1 Cycloalkanes

Axial and equatorial H

How many axial and equatorial hydrogens does cyclohexane have, and what happens on ring flip?

Show answer

Six axial, six equatorial. On ring flip each axial H becomes equatorial and vice versa (the ring flip barrier is about 45 kJ mol-1, so it is fast at 25 °C).

Practice 43 L1 Cycloalkanes

Boat versus chair

By how much is the boat higher than the chair and what are the two strain types in the boat?

Show answer

About 30 kJ mol-1 (twist-boat 23). Flagpole H···H steric repulsion and eclipsing (torsional) strain along the bottom C–C bonds.

Practice 44 L1 Cycloalkanes

Equatorial preference

Why is methylcyclohexane more stable with CH3 equatorial? Estimate the strain of the axial form.

Show answer

The axial CH3 has two gauche (1,3-diaxial) interactions with axial H's at C-3 and C-5: 2 × 3.8 ≈ 7.6 kJ mol-1 (A-value 1.74 kcal mol-1).

Practice 45 L1 Cycloalkanes

Conformational lock

Which substituent effectively locks the cyclohexane ring: CH3, OH, C(CH3)3, Cl?

Show answer

tert-Butyl (A-value > 4 kcal mol-1).

Practice 46 L1 Cycloalkanes

Cis/trans and ax/eq

For each, state whether the isomer is e,e / a,e: (a) trans-1,4-dimethyl (b) cis-1,3-dimethyl (c) cis-1,2-dimethyl (d) trans-1,3-dimethyl.

Show answer

(a) e,e (or a,a). (b) e,e (or a,a). (c) a,e. (d) a,e.

Practice 47 L1 Cycloalkanes

Stereoisomer count

How many stereoisomers does each have: 1,2-dimethylcyclohexane, 1,3-dimethylcyclohexane, 1,4-dimethylcyclohexane, 1,1-dimethylcyclohexane?

Show answer

3 (cis meso + trans pair); 3 (cis meso + trans pair); 2 (cis, trans: both achiral); 1.

Practice 48 L1 Cycloalkanes

More stable isomer

Which is more stable: (a) cis- or trans-1,2-dimethylcyclohexane (b) cis- or trans-1,3- (c) cis- or trans-1,4-?

Show answer

(a) trans (e,e). (b) cis (e,e). (c) trans (e,e). In each case the more stable isomer can place both CH3 equatorial; the other needs one axial CH3 (≈ 7.6 kJ).

Practice 49 L1 Cycloalkanes

Monochlorination of ring compounds

How many monochloro products (structural) does methylcyclopentane give? With stereoisomers?

Show answer

Structural: 4 (1-chloro-1-methyl, 1-chloro-2-methyl, 1-chloro-3-methyl, chloromethyl). With stereoisomers: 10.

Practice 50 L1 Cycloalkanes

Decalin

Which is more stable, cis- or trans-decalin? Which one can undergo ring flip?

Show answer

trans-Decalin is more stable by about 11 kJ mol-1; it is rigid. cis-Decalin can undergo ring flipping.

Practice 51 L1 Cycloalkanes

Bicyclic nomenclature

Give the number of carbons and formula of bicyclo[2.2.1]heptane, bicyclo[2.2.2]octane and spiro[4.5]decane.

Show answer

C7H12; C8H14; C10H18.

Practice 52 L1 Cycloalkanes

Preparation of cyclohexane

Name the industrial source of cyclohexane and the conditions.

Show answer

Hydrogenation of benzene: H2, Ni, 150–200 °C (or Pt at room temperature under pressure).

Practice 53 L1 Cycloalkanes

Bromine test

Which decolourises Br2/CCl4 quickly in the dark: propene, cyclopropane, cyclohexane?

Show answer

Propene only (cyclopropane reacts slowly/needs a Lewis acid; cyclohexane needs light for substitution).

Practice 54 L1 Cycloalkanes

Torsion angle

What is the C–C–C–C dihedral angle in a perfect chair cyclohexane and in a planar ring?

Show answer

Chair: about 55°–60° (staggered). Planar cyclohexane: 0° (eclipsed, 12 eclipsing interactions).

Practice 55 L1 Cycloalkanes

Adamantane

What is the molecular formula of adamantane and how many CH and CH2 groups are there?

Show answer

C10H16: four CH (bridgeheads) and six CH2.
