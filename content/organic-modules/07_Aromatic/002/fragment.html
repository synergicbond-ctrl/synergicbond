How to use this chapter

This chapter is written as a small book. Part A (sections 1–5) explains what makes benzene special and how to name, count and make aromatic compounds. Part B (sections 6–13) is the heart: the electrophilic aromatic substitution (EAS) mechanism, every named EAS reaction, the logic of directing and activating effects, and what to do when two substituents compete. Part C (sections 14–19) covers side-chain chemistry, ring addition and reduction, polycyclic arenes, heterocycles, nucleophilic aromatic substitution, benzyne and diazonium chemistry. Part D (sections 20–22) is synthesis planning and summary sheets, and Part E is a graded problem set of more than 300 questions with full solutions: Level 1 (Main), Level 2 (Advanced single and multiple correct), Level 3 (Advanced integer, matrix match and paragraph).

Shortcut Read each section in this order: rule → reason → exception → worked example. The reason (always an arenium-ion or resonance argument) is what lets you answer unseen questions; the rules alone only let you answer seen ones.
Key idea Almost every question in this chapter reduces to one picture: an electrophile attacks the π cloud, a cation (the arenium ion) forms, and the substituent either stabilises that cation (activating) or destabilises it (deactivating). Position of attack is decided by where the positive charge is placed relative to the substituent.
