Key idea Which aldehyde or halide for which amino acid? Strecker: the aldehyde carbon becomes C-α, and R is the aldehyde's substituent: acetaldehyde → alanine, isobutyraldehyde → valine, isovaleraldehyde → leucine, formaldehyde → glycine, PhCH2CHO → phenylalanine, 3-(methylthio)propanal → methionine. Acetamidomalonate: the halide RX supplies R: PhCH2Cl → phenylalanine; CH3I → alanine; (CH3)2CHCH2Br → leucine; CH3SCH2CH2Cl → methionine.
Trap Strecker needs NH3 + HCN with an aldehyde (or ketone → α,α-disubstituted amino acid). Starting with a carboxylic acid is HVZ. Aromatic aryl halides do not work in acetamidomalonate (no SN2 on sp2 carbon): phenylglycine can't be made that way.
<h4>Resolution of racemic amino acids</h4>
  • Diastereomeric salt: acetylate the amino group, then form salts with a chiral base (brucine, strychnine, (−)-quinine); separate by crystallisation.
  • Enzymatic: N-acetyl-DL-amino acid + acylase I → L-amino acid + unreacted N-acetyl-D-amino acid (the enzyme hydrolyses only the L-isomer). Separate; racemise the D-acetyl compound and recycle.
  • Max theoretical yield of the pure L-isomer from a racemate without recycling = 50 %.
