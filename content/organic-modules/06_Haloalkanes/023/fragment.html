22. Benzyne: elimination–addition

Chlorobenzene (no EWG) reacts with sodium amide in liquid ammonia at −33 °C to give aniline. The mechanism proceeds by an aryne:

  • 1. The strong base removes an ortho hydrogen (acidic because of the –I of X and the sp2 carbon).
  • 2. The resulting aryl anion expels X−: benzyne (1,2-didehydrobenzene), an unstable intermediate with a "triple bond" formed by in-plane overlap of two sp2 orbitals (poor overlap, very strained, very reactive; not a normal alkyne).
  • 3. The nucleophile (NH2−) adds to either end of the strained bond, generating an aryl anion.
  • 4. The aryl anion is protonated by the solvent (NH3).
NaNH2, liq. NH3⟶ NH2−, then NH3⟶
Chlorobenzene to aniline via benzyne (elimination of HCl, then addition of NH3).
Key idea Roberts' labelling experiment (1953): chlorobenzene with 14C at C-1 gives aniline with 14C at C-1 and C-2 in a 1:1 ratio. The nucleophile attacks both ends of the benzyne with equal probability, which is impossible in a direct SN2/SNAr path (where NH2 would sit only on C-1). This is the classic evidence for an aryne.
Unsymmetrical cases: the nucleophile adds so that the resulting carbanion is as stable as possible (an electron-withdrawing group close to the carbanion is better).
  • o-Chlorotoluene → o-toluidine + m-toluidine (≈ 1:1).
  • p-Chlorotoluene → m-toluidine + p-toluidine (≈ 1:1). m-Chlorotoluene → o-, m- and p-toluidine.
  • m-Bromoanisole (EWG by induction, OCH3) → m-anisidine only: the amide adds to put the carbanion adjacent to the C–OCH3 carbon (stabilised by the –I of oxygen).
NaNH2, NH3⟶ +
o-Chlorotoluene: o- and m-toluidine (amino group lands at either carbon of the benzyne triple bond).
NaNH2, NH3⟶ + +
m-Chlorotoluene: o-, m- and p-toluidine (two different arynes form).
Trap If both ortho positions are blocked there is no ortho H, no benzyne, no reaction (2,6-dimethylchlorobenzene is inert to NaNH2). The first step needs an ortho H.
Generating benzyne other ways, and trapping it: (i) o-fluorobromobenzene + Mg (via Grignard then loss of MgBrF); (ii) benzenediazonium-2-carboxylate (from anthranilic acid + isoamyl nitrite) on heating loses N2 and CO2. Benzyne is trapped as a dienophile in Diels–Alder reactions.
+ Diels–Alder⟶
1,4-Dihydro-1,4-epoxynaphthalene (7-oxabenzonorbornadiene): benzyne as the dienophile and furan as the diene.
Example 38 Adv

Product count

How many isomeric aromatic amines C7H9N form when m-chlorotoluene reacts with NaNH2/NH3? Name them.

Show solution
  1. m-Chlorotoluene (Cl at C1, CH3 at C3) has two ortho H: at C2 and at C6.
  2. Loss of H from C2 gives the 1,2-aryne; NH2− adds at C1 (→ m-toluidine) or C2 (→ o-toluidine).
  3. Loss of H from C6 gives the 1,6-aryne; NH2− adds at C1 (→ m-toluidine) or C6 (→ p-toluidine).
  4. Three different amines: o-, m- and p-toluidine (3).
Example 39 Adv

Why are halobenzenes with ortho-methyls inert?

Explain why 2-chloro-1,3-dimethylbenzene does not react with KNH2/NH3 while 2-chlorotoluene does.

Show solution
  1. Benzyne formation needs a hydrogen ortho to the halogen. In 2-chloro-1,3-dimethylbenzene both positions ortho to Cl carry CH3, so there is no ortho H: no benzyne can form. (Direct SNAr fails because there is no EWG, and the benzyne route is blocked.)
Example 40 Adv

Mechanism decision

Give the mechanism and product: (a) 4-chloronitrobenzene + CH3ONa (b) chlorobenzene + KNH2/NH3 (c) 1-bromo-4-methylbenzene + KNH2/NH3.

Show solution
  1. (a) SNAr (addition–elimination): 4-nitroanisole. (b) benzyne: aniline. (c) benzyne: a ≈ 1:1 mixture of m-toluidine and p-toluidine.
