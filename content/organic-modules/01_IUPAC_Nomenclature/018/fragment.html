2.6 Cycloalkanes

  • The ring is named cyclo + alkane: cyclopropane, cyclobutane, cyclopentane, cyclohexane.
  • One substituent: no locant is needed (methylcyclohexane).
  • Two or more substituents: number the ring so the locant set is lowest; if tied, the alphabetically first substituent gets the lower number. With a principal group (–OH, –COOH …) that carbon is C-1 (Part 3).
  • Ring or chain as the parent? The principal functional group decides first (cyclohexylmethanol has the OH on the chain, so the chain is the parent).
Compare & Distinguish With no principal group, the 2013 recommendations always make the ring the parent. Many textbooks instead make the larger part the parent (the ring if it has at least as many carbons as the chain). The two agree whenever the ring is at least as large as the chain.
Special Case A molecule with two separate rings and no principal group is named on the larger ring: the six-membered ring is the parent in 1-cyclopentyl-2,3-dinitrobenzene, and the cyclopentyl group is a substituent.

Example 9 Direct application

  1. Ring: six carbons; three substituents: two methyl, one ethyl. The ethyl carbon is between a methyl-carbon and a CH₂.
  2. Start at a methyl-bearing carbon that is next to the ethyl-bearing carbon and walk towards the ethyl carbon: C1 CH(CH₃), C2 CH(Et), C3 CH₂, C4 CH(CH₃) → {1,2,4}.
  3. Every other start/direction gives a higher set (for example {1,4,5} or {1,2,5}), so {1,2,4} is the lowest.

2-Ethyl-1,4-dimethylcyclohexane. Alphabetical order: ethyl before dimethyl, because di is ignored (e < m).
