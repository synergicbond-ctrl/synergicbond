Structure elucidation, synthesis and quantitative problems (Level 3 continued)

Practice 312 L3 · Elucidation

An ester A (C5H10O2) is hydrolysed to an acid X and an alcohol Y. X reduces Tollens’ reagent; Y gives a positive iodoform test. Identify A.

Show answer
  1. X reduces Tollens’ reagent: X = HCOOH. So A is a formate ester HCOOR with R = C4H9. Y = C4H9OH gives iodoform: it must contain CH3CH(OH)–: butan-2-ol.
  2. A = sec-butyl methanoate, HCOOCH(CH3)CH2CH3 (C5H10O2; it is also chiral).
Practice 313 L3 · Elucidation

A dibasic acid A (C4H6O4) on heating gives an anhydride B (C4H4O3). B with NH3, then heat, gives C (C4H5NO2). C with Br2/KOH gives D (C3H7NO2). Identify A–D.

Show answer
  1. A: succinic acid HOOC(CH2)2COOH (C4H6O4); B: succinic anhydride; C: succinimide (acidic N–H); D: Hofmann degradation of the ring-opened amide: H2NCH2CH2COOH, β-alanine (C3H7NO2).
Δ⟶ NH₃, Δ⟶ Br₂, KOH⟶
Practice 314 L3 · Elucidation

A dibasic acid E (C3H4O4) evolves CO2 on heating at 140 °C and leaves an acid F (C2H4O2). E with urea and POCl3 gives G. Identify E, F and G.

Practice 315 L3 · Elucidation

Salicylic acid (A) is treated with (i) (CH3CO)2O/H+ to give B; (ii) CH3OH/H+ to give C; (iii) soda lime to give D. Identify B, C, D and the FeCl3 behaviour of A–D.

Practice 316 L3 · Hunsdiecker

What is formed when silver succinate is treated with bromine in CCl4 (Hunsdiecker conditions)? And Kolbe electrolysis of sodium succinate?

Show answer
  1. Hunsdiecker: both CO2 groups replaced: 1,2-dibromoethane, BrCH2CH2Br (the textbook outcome for a dicarboxylate; practical yields are poor). Kolbe: ethene (CH2=CH2) (the intermediate diradical or radical anion expels the second CO2).
Practice 317 L3 · Kolbe stats

A 2 : 1 molar mixture of sodium acetate and sodium propanoate is electrolysed. Estimate the molar ratio ethane : propane : butane (assume random coupling).

Show answer
  1. Radical fractions: CH3· = 2/3, C2H5· = 1/3. Ethane (CH3–CH3): (2/3)² = 4/9. Propane (CH3–C2H5): 2 × (2/3)(1/3) = 4/9. Butane: (1/3)² = 1/9.
  2. Ratio 4 : 4 : 1.
Practice 318 L3 · Colligative

6.0 g of acetic acid is dissolved in 100 g of benzene (Kf = 5.12 K kg mol−1); the observed freezing-point depression is 2.82 K. Calculate the van’t Hoff factor and the degree of association into dimers.

Show answer
  1. Molality = (6.0/60)/0.100 = 1.00 m. ΔTf = i Kf m → i = 2.82/5.12 = 0.551.
  2. For 2 HA ⇌ (HA)2 with degree of association α: i = 1 − α/2 → α = 2(1 − 0.551) = 0.90 (90 % of the acid is dimerised).
Practice 319 L3 · Thermodynamics

For the esterification CH3COOH + C2H5OH ⇌ CH3COOC2H5 + H2O, Kc = 4.0 at 298 K. Find ΔG° and comment.

Show answer
  1. ΔG° = −RT ln K = −(8.314 J K−1 mol−1)(298 K) ln 4 = −3.43 kJ mol−1.
  2. It is only slightly negative (K is near 1), so the reaction does not go to completion: that is why excess alcohol or removal of water is needed. ΔH° is small, so the position hardly changes with temperature; heating just speeds up attainment of equilibrium.
Practice 320 L3 · Hammett

The saponification of ethyl benzoates follows log(k/k0) = ρσ with ρ = +2.5. Calculate the relative rates of ethyl p-nitrobenzoate (σ = +0.78) and ethyl p-methoxybenzoate (σ = −0.27).

Show answer
  1. p-NO2: k/k0 = 102.5 × 0.78 = 101.95 = 89. p-OCH3: k/k0 = 102.5 × (−0.27) = 10−0.675 = 0.21.
  2. A positive ρ means negative charge builds up in the transition state (tetrahedral intermediate); electron-withdrawing groups stabilise it and speed the reaction.
Practice 321 L3 · Back titration

A 0.600 g aspirin tablet is refluxed with 25.0 mL of 0.500 M NaOH. The excess alkali needs 35.1 mL of 0.100 M HCl. Find the percentage of aspirin (M = 180.16) in the tablet.

Show answer
  1. NaOH taken = 12.50 mmol; unused = 3.51 mmol; used = 8.99 mmol. Each aspirin consumes 3 NaOH: n(aspirin) = 8.99/3 = 2.997 mmol → 0.540 g.
  2. Percentage = 0.540/0.600 × 100 = 90.0 %.
Practice 322 L3 · Silver salt

The silver salt of a dibasic acid (0.500 g) leaves 0.3270 g of silver on ignition. Find the molar mass of the acid. If the acid forms an anhydride on heating at 160 °C, identify it.

Show answer
  1. n(Ag) = 0.3270/107.87 = 3.031 mmol; each formula unit Ag2A contains two Ag: n(salt) = 1.516 mmol. M(salt) = 0.500/1.516 × 10−3 = 329.9. M(acid) = M(salt) − 2 × 107.87 + 2 × 1.008 = 329.9 − 215.7 + 2.0 = 116.2.
  2. C4H4O4 (M = 116.07): maleic or fumaric acid. Formation of an anhydride at 160 °C identifies it as maleic acid.
Practice 323 L3 · Esters

Isoamyl acetate (banana oil) is prepared from acetic acid and an alcohol. Name the alcohol, give the formula and molar mass of the ester, and state two steps to raise the yield.

Show answer
  1. Alcohol: 3-methylbutan-1-ol (isoamyl alcohol). Ester: CH3COOCH2CH2CH(CH3)2, C7H14O2, M = 130.2 g mol−1. To raise the yield: use excess acetic acid (or alcohol) and remove water (Dean–Stark trap or conc. H2SO4).

What mass of 2-bromopropanoic acid is obtained from 74 g of propanoic acid in 100 % yield? What mass of alanine would form on ammonolysis in 70 % yield?

Practice 329 L3 · Coumarin

Salicylaldehyde is heated with acetic anhydride and sodium acetate. Predict the product, and explain the ring closure.

Show answer
  1. Perkin condensation gives o-hydroxycinnamic acid (cis geometry needed); the ortho OH attacks the acid (or the mixed anhydride) intramolecularly, closing a six-membered lactone: coumarin (2H-chromen-2-one, C9H6O2), after loss of H2O.

Practice 330 L3 · Benzilic acid

Benzil, PhCOCOPh, is boiled with aqueous KOH and then acidified. Name the product and the type of rearrangement.

Show answer
  1. Benzilic acid rearrangement: OH− adds to one C=O; a phenyl group migrates to the adjacent carbonyl carbon (1,2-shift); the product after acidification is benzilic acid (2-hydroxy-2,2-diphenylacetic acid), Ph2C(OH)COOH, C14H12O3.
1. KOH, H₂O 2. H₃O⁺⟶
Practice 331 L3 · Phthalein

Phenolphthalein is prepared from phthalic anhydride and phenol with H2SO4 or ZnCl2. How many carbon atoms does it contain, and what colour change occurs at pH ≈ 8.3–10?

Show answer
  1. Phthalic anhydride (C8H4O3) + 2 phenol (2 × C6H6O) − H2O → C20H14O4: 20 carbons. It is colourless in acid and pink-red in alkali (pH 8.3–10): opening of the lactone ring and quinoid formation.
Practice 332 L3 · Urea derivative

Urea reacts with concentrated nitric acid to give a white crystalline salt. Give its formula and the site of protonation.

Show answer
  1. Urea is a weak monoacidic base protonated on oxygen: urea nitrate, [H2NC(OH)=NH2]+ NO3−, formula CH5N3O4 (CO(NH2)2·HNO3, M = 123.1), sparingly soluble in cold conc. HNO3.
Practice 333 L3 · Extraction

A mixture of benzoic acid, phenol, aniline and naphthalene in ether is separated by successive extraction with (1) aq. HCl, (2) aq. NaHCO3, (3) aq. NaOH. State which compound ends up in each layer.

Show answer
  1. (1) HCl extracts aniline (as anilinium chloride). (2) NaHCO3 extracts benzoic acid (carboxylate). (3) NaOH extracts phenol (phenoxide). Naphthalene remains in the ether. Each aqueous layer is neutralised (NaOH or HCl) to regenerate the compound.
Practice 334 L3 · Mechanism

Write the mechanism of the reaction of benzoyl chloride with ethanol in pyridine, and say what role pyridine plays.

Show answer
  1. (1) Ethanol O attacks C=O (addition) giving the tetrahedral intermediate PhC(O−)(Cl)(O+HEt). (2) Chloride leaves (elimination) giving protonated ester. (3) Pyridine removes H+ (forming pyridinium chloride), pulling the equilibrium over and preventing acid-catalysed side reactions. Pyridine can also add first to give an N-acylpyridinium ion (a more reactive acylating agent).
+ pyridine⟶
Practice 335 L3 · Mechanism

Show why in Fischer esterification the oxygen of the alcohol is retained in the ester but in the acid-catalysed hydrolysis of tert-butyl acetate it is the tert-butyl–oxygen bond that breaks.

Show answer
  1. Fischer: the protonated carbonyl is attacked by the alcohol oxygen (best nucleophile) at the acyl carbon, so the C–O(alcohol) bond is formed and the C–OH bond of the acid is cleaved (acyl–O fission).
  2. tert-Butyl acetate: AAL1. After protonation of the carbonyl oxygen, C–O(alkyl) ionisation gives the very stable tertiary cation and acetic acid: the energy cost of forming the tetrahedral intermediate at a crowded acyl carbon is higher than the cost of ionising to (CH3)3C+.
Practice 336 L3 · Stereo

(R)-2-Phenylpropanoic acid is converted to the amine by Curtius (via the acyl azide). Give the product and its CIP descriptor.

Show answer
  1. The migrating group retains its configuration. The substituents on the stereocentre: COOH (C with O,O,O) > Ph (C,C,C) > CH3 > H in the acid; in the amine NH2 > Ph > CH3 > H: the same ranking, same spatial arrangement. Product: (R)-1-phenylethanamine (and for the (S)-acid, the (S)-amine).
1. SOCl₂ 2. NaN₃ 3. Δ 4. H₂O⟶
Practice 337 L3 · Rank

Rank in increasing rate of acid-catalysed esterification with methanol: acetic acid, propanoic acid, 2-methylpropanoic acid, 2,2-dimethylpropanoic acid, 2,6-dimethylbenzoic acid.

Show answer
  1. Steric hindrance at the carbonyl carbon is decisive: the more substituted the α-carbon, the slower the esterification. 2,6-Dimethylbenzoic acid is slowest (rule of six).
  2. 2,6-dimethylbenzoic < 2,2-dimethylpropanoic < 2-methylpropanoic < propanoic < acetic.
Practice 338 L3 · Rank

Rank in decreasing ease of base-catalysed hydrolysis: ethyl acetate, ethyl chloroacetate, ethyl pivalate, ethyl benzoate, phenyl acetate.

Show answer
  1. Chloro substituent speeds, phenoxide is a better leaving group, conjugation/steric slow the others.
  2. ethyl chloroacetate > phenyl acetate > ethyl acetate > ethyl benzoate > ethyl pivalate.
Practice 339 L3 · Rank

Rank by boiling point: ethanoic acid, ethyl ethanoate, ethanamide, N,N-dimethylethanamide, ethanenitrile.

Show answer
  1. Ethyl ethanoate (77 °C, no H-bond donor) < ethanenitrile (82 °C, large dipole) < ethanoic acid (118 °C, dimers) < N,N-dimethylethanamide (165 °C, strong dipole but no N–H) < ethanamide (221 °C, two N–H donors).
