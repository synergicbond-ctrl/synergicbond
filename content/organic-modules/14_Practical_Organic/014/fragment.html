4. Differential extraction

A compound dissolved in water (or in a reaction mixture) is shaken in a separating funnel with an immiscible organic solvent (ether, chloroform, ethyl acetate, benzene) in which it is far more soluble. The compound passes into the organic layer; the two layers are separated and the organic solvent is distilled off. It is governed by the distribution (partition) law: at constant temperature a solute distributes between two immiscible solvents so that the ratio of its concentrations in them is constant, provided the solute has the same molecular state in both:

Formula K = Corg/Caq (K is the partition coefficient, large for a good extraction)
Formula Fraction left in the aqueous layer after n extractions, each with a volume v of organic solvent: (V/(V + K v))n (V = volume of aqueous solution)
IMP For a fixed total volume of solvent, several extractions with small portions are more efficient than one extraction with the whole volume (see the curve below). The gain falls off rapidly: beyond 4 or 5 portions there is little improvement.
K = C(org)/C(aq) = 3, 100 mL aqueous layer, 100 mL ether in total: many small portions beat one large portion, with a limiting value.
HYF Practical points: the denser layer is at the bottom (ether is above water; chloroform is below water); shake gently with venting to release pressure; emulsions are broken by adding brine (salting out), which also lowers the solubility of the organic compound in the aqueous layer; the organic extract is dried over anhydrous Na2SO4 or MgSO4 or CaCl2 before the solvent is evaporated.
Example 6 Adv

Extraction numerical

100 mL of aqueous solution contains 5.0 g of a solute. The partition coefficient K = Cether/Cwater = 3. Calculate the mass left in water after (a) one extraction with 100 mL ether and (b) two extractions with 50 mL ether each.

Show solution
  1. (a) Fraction left = V/(V + Kv) = 100/(100 + 3 × 100) = 1/4. Mass left = 5.0 × 0.25 = 1.25 g.
  2. (b) Per extraction: 100/(100 + 3 × 50) = 100/250 = 0.4. Two extractions: 0.42 = 0.16. Mass left = 5.0 × 0.16 = 0.80 g.
  3. Two half-portions leave 0.80 g, one full portion leaves 1.25 g. Several small extractions win.
Validity Check K = Corg/Caq is constant only if the solute has the same molecular form in both layers and the solutions are dilute. Benzoic acid dimerises in benzene and ionises in water, so the simple constant fails (see Problem Set A: the constant becomes Caq2/Cbenzene).
Think Further

Is the gain from many small extractions unlimited?

Show solution

No. The limit as n → ∞ with total solvent volume V0 fixed is exp(−K V0/V) (for K = 3, V0 = V: 0.050), reached asymptotically; beyond 4 to 5 portions the improvement is small and not worth the time.
