2. Classification: five independent ways

A polymer can be placed in each of the five classes below at once. Nylon-6,6 is synthetic, linear, a condensation polymer, a fibre, and a copolymer (alternating) in the strict sense. Do not treat the schemes as exclusive.

2.1 By source

ClassDefinitionExamples
NaturalFound in plants and animalsCellulose, starch, proteins, natural rubber, nucleic acids, silk, wool, lignin, chitin
Semi-syntheticNatural polymer chemically modifiedCellulose acetate (acetate rayon), cellulose nitrate (celluloid with camphor, guncotton), viscose rayon (regenerated cellulose), vulcanised rubber
SyntheticMade in the laboratory/industry from small moleculesPolythene, PVC, nylon, terylene, Bakelite, Buna-S, Teflon

2.2 By structure of the chain

Formula Linear: closely packed, high density, high m.p. Branched: poor packing, low density, low m.p. Cross-linked: rigid, infusible, insoluble network
  • Linear polymers: long straight chains with no side branches, e.g. high-density polyethene (HDPE), nylon, PVC. Chains pack well → higher density, tensile strength and melting point.
  • Branched-chain polymers: main chain with side chains of the same monomer, e.g. low-density polyethene (LDPE), amylopectin, glycogen. Branches prevent close packing → lower density, lower melting point, lower tensile strength.
  • Cross-linked (network) polymers: bifunctional + polyfunctional monomers, or linear chains joined by covalent bridges, e.g. Bakelite, melamine–formaldehyde, vulcanised rubber (S–S bridges), urea–formaldehyde, glyptal on curing. Hard, brittle, do not melt.

Exam Trap Nylon-6 is made by ring-opening of caprolactam (a little water initiates), so no small molecule is lost in the net reaction. It is still a polyamide, with the same –CO–NH– links as nylon-6,6, and standard Class 12 treatments list it as a condensation polymer (they classify by the chemical nature of the link). If an option calls it an addition polymer, treat it as wrong.

2.4 By molecular forces (physical behaviour)

  • Elastomers: weak van der Waals forces, coiled chains, can be stretched many times their length and recover: natural rubber, Buna-S, Buna-N, neoprene. Light cross-linking (vulcanisation) prevents chains from slipping.
  • Fibres: strong intermolecular forces (hydrogen bonds, dipole–dipole), high tensile strength, high m.p., low solubility, crystalline: nylon-6, nylon-6,6, terylene (PET), Kevlar, polyacrylonitrile (Orlon), silk, wool, cotton.
  • Thermoplastics: intermolecular forces between those of elastomers and fibres; soften on heating, harden on cooling, can be re-moulded repeatedly: polythene, polystyrene, PVC, PMMA, Teflon.
  • Thermosetting polymers: fusible/semi-fluid in the first stage, then cured by heat (and pressure) into infusible, insoluble cross-linked solids that cannot be remoulded: Bakelite, melamine–formaldehyde, urea–formaldehyde, epoxy resins.
HYF Quick sort: soft stretchy → elastomer; thread, H-bonds, amide/ester/nitrile → fibre; bottle, wrap, pipe, linear/branched → thermoplastic; formaldehyde in the name, network → thermoset.

2.5 By monomer composition

  • Homopolymer: one type of monomer (polythene, PVC, PTFE, nylon-6, polystyrene).
  • Copolymer: two or more different monomers (Buna-S, Buna-N, PHBV, nylon-6,6, terylene, Bakelite). Nylon-6,6 and PET are strictly alternating copolymers but are also called condensation homopolymers in many books; the exam will tell you which view it wants.

Each * is an open valence. See Section 7 on copolymers for the four sequence types.
Exception & Its Reason Nylon-6 is a ring-opening product yet is called a condensation polymer. No atoms are lost in the net reaction, but the polymer has the same –CONH– links as nylon-6,6 and is classified by that chemical link. The classification is by the product’s link, not by the by-product.
Validity Check The five classifications are independent labels. “Thermoplastic” describes behaviour on heating, not the mechanism: both addition (PE) and condensation (nylon-6) polymers can be thermoplastic, and both addition (vulcanised rubber, after treatment) and condensation (Bakelite) polymers can form networks.
