3. Effect of substituents on the acidity of phenols

A substituent changes the acidity of phenol by changing the stability of the phenoxide ion relative to phenol. Electron-withdrawing groups (–I, –R) stabilise the anion and increase acidity; electron-releasing groups (+I, +R) destabilise the anion and decrease acidity. The effect depends on position because inductive effects fall off with distance, whereas resonance acts only from ortho and para positions.

  • p-Nitrophenol ≈ 7.2, o-nitrophenol ≈ 7.2, m-nitrophenol ≈ 8.4 (compare phenol ≈ 10)
  • 2,4-Dinitrophenol ≈ 4; 2,4,6-trinitrophenol (picric acid) < 1
  • p-Chlorophenol ≈ 9.4; p-cyanophenol ≈ 8.0
  • p-Cresol ≈ 10.3; p-methoxyphenol ≈ 10.2; m-methoxyphenol ≈ 9.7

The nitro group at ortho or para withdraws electrons both inductively and by resonance, because the negative charge on the ring carbon can be taken up by the nitro group as a nitronate-type form. At the meta position there is no such conjugation, so only the weaker inductive effect operates and m-nitrophenol is less acidic than the other two nitrophenols.

The methoxy group shows a position-dependent effect: at the para position its +R effect dominates (slightly lower acidity than phenol), but at the meta position only its –I effect operates (higher acidity). The methyl group lowers acidity through +I and hyperconjugation. A halogen withdraws inductively more than it donates by resonance, so p-chlorophenol is more acidic than phenol.

Trap. o-Nitrophenol forms a strong intramolecular hydrogen bond between OH and the adjacent NO2 group. This lowers its boiling point and water solubility, and makes it steam volatile, but it does not make it a much weaker acid than p-nitrophenol (the pKa values are nearly equal). Always argue about the property that the hydrogen bond actually changes: volatility, solubility and melting point.

Steric inhibition of resonance is a further subtle factor. In 3,5-dimethyl-4-nitrophenol the two methyl groups flank the nitro group and twist it out of the ring plane, so its –R effect is switched off and the compound is less acidic than p-nitrophenol. Use the criteria in this order: (1) nature of the group, (2) position (o, p versus m), (3) number of groups, (4) any steric twisting.
