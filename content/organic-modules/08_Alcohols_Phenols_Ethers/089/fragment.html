24. Epoxides (oxiranes)

An epoxide is a three-membered cyclic ether. The C–O–C angle is about 61° (against 109.5° for sp3), so the ring bonds are bent (“banana” bonds) and the ring strain is about 114 kJ mol−1 (about 27 kcal mol−1). The strain makes epoxides reactive towards nucleophiles under both acidic and basic conditions even though alkoxide is normally a very poor leaving group: relief of strain pays for it. Ordinary ethers are inert to bases; epoxides are not. Ethylene oxide (oxirane) is a colourless gas (bp 10.7 °C), toxic and carcinogenic; it is made industrially by direct oxidation of ethene with O2 over a silver catalyst at about 520–570 K and 10–20 atm. It is used for ethylene glycol, ethanolamines, ethoxylates (detergents), polyethylene glycols and as a sterilant. Propylene oxide and epichlorohydrin are made by halohydrin or oxidation routes; epichlorohydrin with bisphenol A gives epoxy resins.
