11. Ambident nucleophiles

An ambident nucleophile has two nucleophilic sites. The site of attack depends on the counter-ion, the substrate (SN1-like vs SN2-like) and the solvent.

!trap The JEE convention: **KCN → nitrile; AgCN → isonitrile; KNO_{2} → alkyl nitrite; AgNO_{2} → nitroalkane.** (Real systems give mixtures: e.g. KNO_{2} in DMF gives mostly the nitroalkane with some nitrite; the textbook rule is based on ionic versus covalent character.) For the answer in an exam, quote the convention. @sch CCBr + [C-]#N -[KCN, alcohol]-> CCC#N || Nitrile by C-attack (ionic cyanide). @sch CCBr + [Ag+].[C-]#N -[AgCN]-> CC[N+]#[C-] || Isonitrile by N-attack (covalent silver cyanide). Isocyanide: foul smell, hydrolysed by dilute acid to amine + HCOOH, stable to base. @sch CCBr + [Ag+].[O-][N+]=O -[AgNO_{2}]-> CC[N+](=O)[O-] || Nitroalkane (silver nitrite, covalent). @sch CCBr + [K+].[O-]N=O -[KNO_{2}]-> CCON=O || Alkyl nitrite (textbook rule for ionic nitrite). !tip Kornblum's rule (the modern version): a reaction proceeding by a pure S_{N}2 path puts the //more nucleophilic// (softer, less electronegative) atom of the ambident ion on carbon; a reaction with S_{N}1 character (carbocation-like TS, as when Ag^{+} pulls out the halide) puts the //more electronegative// end on carbon. In this chapter use the covalent/ionic textbook rule unless a question explicitly tests Kornblum's rule.
Example 24 Main

Ambident reagents

Write the major product: (a) ethyl bromide + KCN (b) ethyl bromide + AgCN (c) ethyl bromide + AgNO2 (d) ethyl bromide + KNO2. Name each.

Show solution
  1. (a) CH3CH2CN: propanenitrile (ethyl cyanide). (b) CH3CH2NC: ethyl isocyanide (ethyl isonitrile; carbylamine). (c) CH3CH2NO2: nitroethane. (d) CH3CH2ONO: ethyl nitrite.
Example 25 Adv

Product through NGP

(2R)-2-Bromopropanoic acid is treated with (i) concentrated (2 M) NaOH, (ii) Ag2O/H2O. Which lactic acid enantiomer (R or S) forms in each case?

Show solution
  1. First compute the geometric outcome: direct SN2 (high [OH−]) inverts; NGP route (Ag+ assists ionisation but carboxylate attacks from the back first; then water opens the α-lactone from the back) retains.
  2. (i) 2 M NaOH: strong nucleophile at high concentration: SN2 → inversion. Priorities Br > COOH > CH3 > H become OH > COOH > CH3 > H, same order: inversion flips the letter: (S)-lactic acid.
  3. (ii) Ag2O/H2O: no strong external nucleophile, and Ag+ assists loss of Br−; the carboxylate NGP operates (two inversions): retention, (R)-lactic acid.
