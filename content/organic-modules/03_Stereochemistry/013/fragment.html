10. The CIP system in depth: R/S and every tricky case

10.1 What the system does

Cahn–Ingold–Prelog (CIP) rules turn a three-dimensional arrangement about a stereogenic unit into a letter. They have two jobs: rank the four substituents (priority 1 to 4) and look at them in the right orientation. Ranking is the hard part; looking is mechanical.

  • Rule 1a: higher atomic number wins at the first point of difference.
  • Rule 1b: (rarely needed) a duplicated atom ranks by proximity to the root when sets are otherwise identical.
  • Rule 2: higher atomic mass number wins (isotopes): T > D > H; 13C > 12C. Used only when Rule 1 gives a tie.
  • Rule 3: Z > E for double-bond descriptors in the branches; then like pairs (R,R or S,S) rank above unlike pairs (R,S).
  • Rule 4: chiral stereogenic units rank over pseudoasymmetric units; r > s ... etc. (very rarely tested).
  • Rule 5: R > S, the final tie-breaker for pseudoasymmetric centres.
Key idea Priority is decided by the first point of difference, exploring sphere by sphere outward from the stereocentre. The decision is made as soon as a difference appears, no matter how large later spheres might be.

10.2 The procedure

  • 1. Identify the four atoms directly attached to the stereocentre. If they all differ in atomic number, rank them and stop.
  • 2. If two are the same element (usually C), look at the set of three atoms attached to each (written in decreasing atomic number order, e.g. (O, C, H)). Compare the sets atom by atom from the highest: the first difference decides.
  • 3. If the sets are identical, go one sphere outward. At each step compare the sets from the higher-ranking branch first; all branches in the same sphere are examined before moving further out.
  • 4. Multiple bonds are duplicated: C=O is treated as C bonded to (O, O-duplicate) and O bonded to (C, C-duplicate). The duplicate atoms carry only "phantom atoms" (atomic number 0) as their own substituents.
  • 5. Orient the molecule with priority 4 pointing away from you. If 1 → 2 → 3 runs clockwise: R (rectus). Anticlockwise: S (sinister).

The classic difficult comparisons:

  • Vinyl vs isopropyl: both (C, C, H). Next sphere: vinyl's =CH2 carries (duplicate C, H, H); each isopropyl CH3 carries (H, H, H); also vinyl's duplicate carbon carries phantoms (0,0,0). Compare in order: highest-ranking branch first: vinyl's real CH2 branch {C, H, H} versus isopropyl's branch {H, H, H}: vinyl wins. –CH=CH2 > –CH(CH3)2.
  • Phenyl vs tert-butyl: both (C, C, C). Next sphere: phenyl's ortho carbons each carry (C, C, H) [C-meta, duplicate, H]; tert-butyl's methyls (H, H, H): phenyl wins. –C6H5 > –C(CH3)3.
  • Ethynyl vs tert-butyl: (C,C,C) vs (C,C,C); next: ethynyl's carbons carry (C-dup, C-dup... ) {C, C, ...}: ethynyl has duplicate atoms with phantom substituents but also the terminal carbon with (C-dup, C-dup, H) i.e. (C, C, H) vs tert-butyl (H, H, H) → –C≡CH > –C(CH3)3.
  • Isopropyl vs CH2CH2Br: first sphere (C,C,H) vs (C,H,H): isopropyl wins at once; the Br two atoms away is never examined. –CH(CH3)2 > –CH2CH2Br.
Trap Do not be dazzled by a heteroatom further out. –CH2CH2OH versus –CH(CH3)2: first sphere (C,H,H) < (C,C,H), so isopropyl wins even though the other group has an oxygen one sphere further out. By contrast –CH2OH has the oxygen directly on the first carbon (O,H,H) and wins at once. Count spheres carefully.
Example 24 Adv

Rank

Rank the groups in each set by CIP priority (1 = highest): (a) –CH2OH, –CH(CH3)2, –CH=CH2, –H; (b) –C6H5, –CH(CH3)2, –CH2Cl, –CH3; (c) –COOH, –CH(OH)CH3, –CH2OH, –CHO.

Show solution
  1. (a) –CH2OH (O,H,H) first: O at the first point of difference. –CH=CH2 versus –CH(CH3)2: tie (C,C,H), vinyl wins on the next sphere. Order: CH2OH > CH=CH2 > CH(CH3)2 > H.
  2. (b) –CH2Cl (Cl,H,H) first. Phenyl (C,C,C) beats isopropyl (C,C,H). CH2Cl > C6H5 > CH(CH3)2 > CH3.
  3. (c) –COOH (O,O,O) > –CHO (O,O,H) > –CH(OH)CH3 (O,C,H) > –CH2OH (O,H,H). COOH > CHO > CH(OH)CH3 > CH2OH.
Example 25 Adv

Isotopes

Assign priorities to the substituents of a stereocentre carrying CH3, CD3, CH2CH3 and H.

Show solution
  1. At the first sphere all three carbon groups are carbon. Sets: CH3 (H,H,H); CD3 (D,D,D); CH2CH3 (C,H,H). Atomic number rules first (Rule 1): the set (C,H,H) has C at its first position, beating D (atomic number 1, the same as H). So ethyl is highest. CD3 versus CH3: all atomic numbers tie; Rule 2 (mass) then ranks D above H. Order: CH2CH3 > CD3 > CH3 > H.

10.5 Rings: ring closure and phantom atoms

In a ring, you follow each path round until it returns to the starting atom; the returned atom is entered as a duplicate with phantom substituents. Often the decision is already made before this matters. Let us look at one classic ring stereocentre.

(R)-3-methylcyclohexanone (S)-3-methylcyclohexanone
The ring carbon C-3 has H, CH3, C-2 (leading to C=O) and C-4 (leading to CH2)

10.6 Reading a wedge–dash structure

Wedge (solid): the bond points toward you; dash (hashed): away from you; plain line: in the plane of the paper. Procedure:

  • 1. Rank the four groups.
  • 2. If the lowest priority group (4) is on a dash, trace 1 → 2 → 3 as drawn: clockwise R, anticlockwise S.
  • 3. If group 4 is on a wedge (toward you), trace 1 → 2 → 3 and reverse the answer.
  • 4. If group 4 is in the plane, swap it with the group that is on the dash (or wedge) and reverse the answer; or tilt your head so that group 4 points away.
  • 5. Two swaps = no change; one swap = inversion.
Trap Never place group 4 on a wedge and read the sense as drawn. The commonest lost mark in this chapter: forgetting that H on a wedge reverses the apparent direction.

Example 26 Main

R or S?

Draw (S)-alanine and (S)-glutamic acid. Why does L-cysteine, the natural amino acid, have the label R although it has the same spatial arrangement as L-alanine?

L-alanine (S) L-cysteine (R) L-serine (S) L-glutamic acid (S)
Show solution
  1. In L-alanine the priorities are NH2 > COOH > CH3 > H. In L-cysteine, the side chain –CH2SH has (S,H,H) which beats –COOH (O,O,O) because S > O at the first point of difference. So the priorities become NH2 > CH2SH > COOH > H: two groups (2 and 3) have exchanged positions, which inverts the label. The molecule has the same spatial arrangement as L-alanine, but the label is R. In L-serine –CH2OH (O,H,H) loses to –COOH (O,O,O): same order as alanine: S.
Key idea The label R/S depends on priorities, so the same spatial arrangement can carry opposite labels in different molecules (L-cysteine R, L-serine S). D/L describes spatial arrangement relative to glyceraldehyde; R/S describes priorities. They are independent.

10.7 Four short methods to avoid mistakes

  • Method A (look from the opposite side of 4): if 4 points toward you, read 1 → 2 → 3 and reverse.
  • Method B (swap to put 4 at the back): if 4 is not on the dashed bond, swap 4 with the dashed group and reverse the final answer.
  • Method C (Fischer projection): if group 4 is on the vertical line (pointing away), read the sense directly; on the horizontal line, reverse.
  • Method D (permutation parity): write the groups in the order in which they appear going clockwise from a viewpoint opposite 4, count the transpositions needed to reach 1,2,3: even = same label, odd = opposite.
Example 27 Adv

Assign

Assign R/S to the stereocentres: (a) CH3CH(OH)COOH (L-lactic acid) drawn as C[C@H](O)C(=O)O; (b) the two centres of threonine (L-threonine: 2S,3R); (c) L-isoleucine (2S,3S).

L-lactic acid (S) L-threonine (S,R) L-isoleucine (S,S)
Show solution
  1. (a) C-2: OH > COOH > CH3 > H; as drawn → S (L-lactic acid is (S)-(+)).
  2. (b) In L-threonine C-2 (the α-carbon): –COOH (O,O,O) beats –CH(OH)CH3 (O,C,H), so NH2 > COOH > CH(OH)CH3 > H: S. C-3: OH > CH(NH2)COOH (N,C,H) > CH3 > H: R. So L-threonine is (2S,3R).
  3. (c) L-Isoleucine C-2: NH2 > COOH > CH(CH3)C2H5 > H: S. C-3: groups CH(NH2)COOH (N,C,H), C2H5 (C,H,H), CH3, H: the α-carbon (N,C,H) first, ethyl second, methyl third: S. So (2S,3S).

10.8 A reaction can change the label without changing the geometry

Replacing one substituent can reshuffle the priorities, so retention of geometry may show up as a changed letter, and inversion of geometry as an unchanged letter. The pair serine → cysteine is the classic case: the carbon skeleton and the 3-D arrangement are the same, yet the labels differ.

L-serine (S) L-cysteine (R)
Same arrangement, different letter: –CH₂OH (O,H,H) < –COOH (O,O,O) in serine, but –CH₂SH (S,H,H) > –COOH in cysteine
Example 28 Adv

Retention with change of label

(R)-Glyceraldehyde is oxidised (CHO → COOH) without touching the stereocentre. Give the label of the product, glyceric acid, and explain. Then do the same for (S)-serine → (R)-cysteine type changes.

(R)-glyceraldehyde (R)-glyceric acid
Show solution
  1. Glyceraldehyde: OH > CHO (O,O,H) > CH2OH (O,H,H) > H. Glyceric acid: OH > COOH (O,O,O) > CH2OH > H. The order of the groups is unchanged, and the spatial arrangement is unchanged, so the label stays R (D-glyceric acid is (R)).
  2. In contrast, in serine → cysteine, replacement of OH by SH moves the side chain above COOH in the priority list, which changes the label from S to R although no bond to the stereocentre is touched.
Example 29 Adv

Substitution with inversion

(R)-2-Bromobutane reacts with NaOH (SN2). Give the label of butan-2-ol formed.

(R)-2-bromobutane (S)-butan-2-ol
Show solution
  1. 2-Bromobutane: Br > C2H5 > CH3 > H. Butan-2-ol: OH > C2H5 > CH3 > H. The priority order is exactly parallel, so SN2 inversion of geometry gives the opposite letter: S.

D/L and R/S together

Natural (L)-alanine is (S); natural L-cysteine is (R); natural L-serine is (S). Which is true about L-threonine and L-isoleucine, whose second centres were assigned above? Answer using the labels computed.

Show solution

10.10 Summary sheet: CIP

  • Rank: higher atomic number first; at ties go outward sphere by sphere, comparing the highest branch first.
  • Double bond: duplicate atom. Triple bond: two duplicates. Ring closure: duplicate with phantom substituents.
  • Isotopes only after atomic number ties: T > D > H, 13C > 12C.
  • Order of important groups: –I > –Br > –Cl > –SH > –F > –OH > –NH2 > –COOH > –CHO > –CH2OH > –CH3 > –H (these were verified pairwise in 10.3).
  • Orient 4 away; 1 → 2 → 3 clockwise R, anticlockwise S. If 4 is toward you, reverse.
  • The label changes if the priority order of the groups changes, even when the geometry is the same.
