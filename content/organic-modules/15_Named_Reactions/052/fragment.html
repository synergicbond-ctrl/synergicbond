47. Schotten–Baumann reaction (acylation of amines, alcohols, phenols)

Reagents: RNH2, R2NH, ROH or ArOH + an acid chloride (RCOCl, ArCOCl) or anhydride, in aqueous NaOH or pyridine. The base neutralises the HCl formed (otherwise it protonates the amine, killing it) and in the case of phenols forms the phenoxide, a far better nucleophile. The reaction is an addition–elimination at the acyl carbon. Benzoyl chloride is the standard reagent (hence "benzoylation").

+ aq. NaOH⟶
Aniline → benzanilide (N-phenylbenzamide)
+ aq. NaOH⟶
Phenol → phenyl benzoate
Shortcut Protecting an aromatic amine: acetylation (CH3COCl or (CH3CO)2O) turns the very activating –NH2 into the moderately activating –NHCOCH3, preventing over-halogenation, oxidation, and (in nitration) protonation to the meta-directing –NH3+. Remove it later with aqueous acid or base.
