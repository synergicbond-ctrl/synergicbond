3.5 Industrial and biochemical routes (frequently asked as statements)

  • Methanol: CO + 2H2 → CH3OH (synthesis gas, ZnO/Cr2O3 catalyst at about 300–400 °C and 200–300 atm in the old process; Cu/ZnO/Al2O3 at about 50–100 atm and 250 °C in the modern process). Older source: destructive distillation of wood (“wood spirit”).
  • Ethanol: (i) hydration of ethene (H3PO4 on SiO2, 300 °C, 60–70 atm); (ii) fermentation of sugars: C12H22O11 + H2O →(invertase) C6H12O6 + C6H12O6; C6H12O6 →(zymase) 2C2H5OH + 2CO2. Starch is first hydrolysed by diastase and maltase. Fermentation stops at about 12–15 % ethanol because yeast is killed.
  • Isopropyl alcohol: hydration of propene (cumene-type acid catalyst).
  • Ethylene glycol: ethene oxide hydration (aqueous acid, 100 °C, or 200 °C pressure without acid); used as antifreeze and in terylene (PET).
  • Glycerol: by-product of the saponification of fats and oils; also from propene via allyl chloride/epichlorohydrin.
Example 9 L1 Foundation

Fermentation arithmetic

What mass of ethanol can be obtained from 90 g of glucose by fermentation (100 % yield)? What volume of CO2 at STP is released?

Show solution
  1. C6H12O6 (180 g mol−1) → 2C2H5OH + 2CO2.
  2. 90 g glucose = 0.5 mol → 1.0 mol ethanol = 46 g and 1.0 mol CO2 = 22.4 L at STP (using 22.4 L mol−1).
  3. Answer: 46 g ethanol, 22.4 L CO2.
