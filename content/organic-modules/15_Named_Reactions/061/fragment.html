55. Curtius, Schmidt and Lossen rearrangements

All three give the same type of intermediate, an isocyanate R–N=C=O (or its equivalent), by 1,2-migration of R from carbon to nitrogen with loss of a leaving group; they differ in how the nitrogen leaving group is installed.

Key idea Chain length for all three: R–CO–X → R–N: lose the carbonyl carbon. If the question asks "the amine has n − 1 carbons", the choice among Hofmann/Curtius/Schmidt/Lossen is made by the *starting functional group*: amide, acyl azide, acid + HN3, hydroxamic acid. Contrast: Gabriel keeps the carbon count of the alkyl halide; reduction of nitrile adds one carbon (RX → RCN → RCH2NH2).
Example 50 Main

Identify the starting material

An amine A with formula C4H11N is made from an acid by a rearrangement that loses one carbon. A is optically active. Identify A and the acid, given that the acid, after treatment with SOCl2, NaN3, heat, H3O+, gives A.

Show solution
  1. A optically active C4H11N amine = butan-2-amine (CH3CH2CH(NH2)CH3), the only chiral C4H11N amine (a primary amine with the carbon of N attached to four different groups: N, H, CH3, C2H5).
  2. The acid has one more carbon and the same R group: R = sec-butyl, so 2-methylbutanoic acid, CH3CH2CH(CH3)COOH (RCOOH → RCOCl → RCON3 → RNCO → RNH2, Curtius). The route proceeds with retention.
