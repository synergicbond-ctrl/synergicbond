35. Mechanism: SN2 alkylation of an amine (ammonolysis; exhaustive methylation)

Reaction: RNH2 + CH3–I → RNH2+CH3 I− → RNHCH3.

  • Step 1 – backside attack. The N lone pair (HOMO nN) approaches carbon from the side opposite to iodine, where the large lobe of σ*(C–I) points. Overlap nN → σ*(C–I) forms the N–C bond while the C–I bond breaks in the same step; the carbon passes through a trigonal-bipyramidal transition state with inversion (Walden). Why: the filled nN is the best donor available; C–I is weak and I− is a stable leaving group.
  • Step 2 – deprotonation. The ammonium ion R(H)2N+CH3 is a strong acid relative to RNH2 and loses H+ to the base (a second molecule of amine or NH3). Why: it regenerates a neutral amine with a free lone pair.
  • Step 3 – the product competes. RNHCH3 is more nucleophilic than RNH2 (a higher-energy nN because of one more +I group), so it reacts faster with the remaining CH3I. Continue until R4N+ has no lone pair and the sequence stops.
+ SN2: n(N) → σ*(C–I)⟶ −H⁺ (base)⟶
Methylamine + methyl iodide: one SN2 step then deprotonation
Shortcut Over-alkylation is thus an orbital result: each alkyl group raises the HOMO. Reductive amination, Gabriel and the azide route avoid it by making the nitrogen non-nucleophilic after the first alkylation (an amide-like phthalimide N, or a non-basic imine).
