27. Chapter tests

Two tests of twelve questions each. Atomic masses: H 1.008, C 12.011, N 14.007, O 15.999, Cl 35.45. R = 0.0821 L atm K-1 mol-1. Suggested time: 45 minutes each. Detailed solutions follow each question.

Test 1

Q214 Test 1

Neoprene

Which statement about neoprene is correct? (A) It is made from CH2=CHCl. (B) It consists mainly of trans-1,4 units of 2-chlorobuta-1,3-diene. (C) It has no C=C bond. (D) It is made by cationic polymerisation at −100 °C.

Show answer

(B). Neoprene is poly(2-chlorobuta-1,3-diene), formed by radical emulsion polymerisation mainly by 1,4-addition, so each unit keeps one in-chain C=C, mostly trans. (A) is the monomer of PVC; (C) is false because 1,4-addition leaves one C=C per unit; (D) describes butyl rubber.

Q215 Test 1

Mass of a hydroxy-acid polyester

A linear polyester forms from 25 molecules of a hydroxy acid HO–R–COOH of molar mass 90.0 g mol-1. Find the molar mass of the chain, nearest whole number.

Show answer
  1. Given 25 monomers, M = 90.0. Required: chain mass.
  2. 25 residues are joined by 24 ester links, so 24 H2O are lost (18.015 each).
  3. M = 25 × 90.0 − 24 × 18.015 = 2250.0 − 432.4 = 1818 g mol-1.
  4. Check: the chain mass is slightly below the sum of monomer masses, as expected.
Q216 Test 1

Step-growth statements

Select all correct statements about step-growth polymerisation. (A) High molar mass needs a very high extent of reaction. (B) Removing the small molecule drives the reaction forward. (C) Chains grow only by adding monomers to one active chain end. (D) Monomer is consumed early, while chains are still short.

Show answer

A, B, D. (A) Xn = 1/(1 − p) needs p > 0.99 for Xn ≥ 100. (B) The couplings are equilibria. (C) false: it describes chain growth; in step-growth any two molecules with reactive ends can join. (D) true: monomers become oligomers early.

Q217 Test 1

Osmometry

A polymer solution of 2.00 g L-1 has an osmotic pressure of 1.00 × 10-3 atm at 300 K. Find Mn to two significant figures.

Show answer
  1. Principle: π = cRT/Mn.
  2. Mn = 2.00 × 0.0821 × 300/1.00 × 10-3 = 49.26/1.00 × 10-3 = 4.926 × 104.
  3. Mn ≈ 4.9 × 104 g mol-1. Reasonable for a high polymer; osmometry gives the number-average mass.

Q219 Test 1

Passage: identify a monomer

A monomer M (C4H6O2) decolourises bromine water; its polymer P on boiling with aqueous NaOH gives a water-soluble polymer Q and sodium ethanoate. (i) Identify M. (ii) Identify Q. (iii) Why can Q not be made by polymerising its “own” monomer?

Show answer

(i) M = vinyl acetate, CH2=CHOCOCH3 (C4H6O2, one C=C). P = poly(vinyl acetate). (ii) Q = poly(vinyl alcohol); the acetate ester is saponified to –OH and sodium ethanoate. (iii) The monomer vinyl alcohol, CH2=CHOH, is an enol that tautomerises at once to acetaldehyde.

Which sample has the largest Mw/Mn? (A) a monodisperse polymer; (B) 1 mol of mass 10 000 + 1 mol of mass 30 000; (C) 1 mol of mass 10 000 + 1 mol of mass 90 000; (D) a living anionic polymer with PDI 1.02.

Q223 Test 1

Mass of butadiene

A Buna-S contains 3 butadiene molecules per styrene molecule. How many kilograms of butadiene are needed for 5.00 kg of Buna-S? (butadiene 54.09, styrene 104.15)

Show answer
  1. Mass fraction of butadiene = 3 × 54.09/(3 × 54.09 + 104.15) = 162.27/266.42 = 0.6091.
  2. Mass of butadiene = 0.6091 × 5.00 kg = 3.05 kg (styrene 1.95 kg).
  3. Check: 3.05 + 1.95 = 5.00 kg.
Q224 Test 1

Descriptive: tacticity

Explain why isotactic polypropene is crystalline and strong, whereas atactic polypropene is soft and sticky.

Show answer

In the isotactic chain all CH3 groups lie on one side of the zig-zag, so every unit has the same geometry and the chains can fold or coil into regular helices that pack into crystals; the many chain–chain contacts raise the melting point (≈ 165 °C) and strength. In the atactic chain the CH3 groups are placed at random, so no regular packing is possible; it remains amorphous, with weak forces, soft and sticky.

Show answer

Which statement about vulcanisation is correct? (A) It removes all C=C bonds. (B) Sulphur bridges between chains increase strength and the elastic range. (C) Tyre rubber contains about 50 % sulphur. (D) The cross-links are broken reversibly by ordinary heating.

Q227 Test 2

Living polymerisation yield

0.500 g of methyl methacrylate (100.12 g mol-1) is polymerised completely with 2.50 × 10-4 mol of a monofunctional anionic initiator. Find Xn and Mn.

Show answer
  1. Moles monomer = 0.500/100.12 = 4.994 × 10-3 mol.
  2. Xn = 4.994 × 10-3/2.50 × 10-4 = 20.0.
  3. Mn = 20.0 × 100.12 = 2.00 × 103 g mol-1 (the initiator end group is a small correction).

Q230 Test 2

Imbalanced step-growth

1.00 mol of a diacid and 1.01 mol of a diamine react until 99.5 % of the acid groups (the limiting group) have reacted. Find Xn.

Show answer
  1. r = 1.00/1.01 = 0.990099; p = 0.995.
  2. Xn = (1 + r)/(1 + r − 2rp) = 1.990099/(1.990099 − 2 × 0.990099 × 0.995) = 1.990099/(1.990099 − 1.970297) = 1.990099/0.019802 = 100.5 (≈ 100).
  3. Check: without imbalance Xn would be 200 (1/(1 − 0.995)); the 1 % excess halves it.
Q231 Test 2

Passage: a diene rubber

A rubber S gives only butanedial (OHC–CH2CH2–CHO) on ozonolysis with reductive work-up. (i) Identify S. (ii) Give its monomer. (iii) Can ozonolysis tell whether the C=C bonds are cis or trans? (iv) Name a catalyst type that gives the all-cis polymer.

Show answer

(i) S = 1,4-polybutadiene: each in-chain C=C is cleaved and the –CH2–CH2– between two double bonds becomes OHC–CH2CH2–CHO. (ii) Buta-1,3-diene. (iii) No: cleavage destroys the C=C geometry, so cis and trans chains give the same product. (iv) Ziegler–Natta or alkyl-lithium initiators (stereospecific coordination/anionic routes).

Q236 Test 2

Descriptive: acid chloride vs acid

Using orbital reasoning, explain why a primary amine reacts with an acid chloride at room temperature but forms an amide with a carboxylic acid only on heating.

Show answer

The amine nitrogen lone pair (HOMO) must donate into the π* (LUMO) of C=O. In an acid chloride Cl withdraws electrons, lowering π* and making the carbon more electrophilic, and Cl− is a good leaving group. In a carboxylic acid the OH lone pair donates into the carbonyl (raising π*), and the acid first protonates the amine to an unreactive ammonium carboxylate; amide formation needs heat to regenerate free amine and to drive out water.
