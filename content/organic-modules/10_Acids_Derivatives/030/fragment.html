29. Acid derivatives in nature and medicine

  • Peptide bond: an amide formed between the α-COOH of one amino acid and the α-NH2 of another. Its planarity (amide resonance) and trans geometry fix protein secondary structure. Peptides are hydrolysed by boiling 6 M HCl (to amino acids) or by proteases at body temperature; ordinary amide hydrolysis needs the harsh conditions of Section 12.3 because the C–N bond is resonance-stabilised.
  • Thioesters (acetyl-CoA): RCOSR′ are more reactive acyl donors than ordinary esters (the C–S bond has weaker overlap, giving less resonance stabilisation, and RS− is a better leaving group than RO−); biology uses them to transfer acyl groups and to generate enolates for C–C bond formation (a biological Claisen condensation builds fatty acids from acetyl-CoA and malonyl-CoA, followed by decarboxylation: the same logic as the malonic ester synthesis).
  • β-Lactam antibiotics (penicillins, cephalosporins): the four-membered lactam is strained, so its amide carbon is much more electrophilic than in a normal amide; it acylates a serine residue of the bacterial transpeptidase.
  • Aspirin (acetylsalicylic acid, Section 22.4): an ester made from salicylic acid and acetic anhydride; it acetylates a serine of cyclooxygenase. Paracetamol is N-(4-hydroxyphenyl)ethanamide: the amide, not an ester, so it does not hydrolyse easily.
  • Ibuprofen and naproxen are 2-arylpropanoic acids: chiral acids whose (S)-enantiomers are the active forms. Their acid group makes them soluble as salts.
  • Local anaesthetics (procaine, benzocaine) are esters of p-aminobenzoic acid; lidocaine is an amide of 2,6-dimethylaniline (the amide is slower to hydrolyse than the ester, and the 2,6-methyl groups hinder it further: longer duration of action).
  • Barbiturates: cyclic ureides from malonic esters and urea (Section 18.3).
  • Fats, oils and waxes: triglycerides are esters of glycerol; waxes are esters of long-chain acids with long-chain alcohols (beeswax is mainly C15H31COOC30H61).
  • Nylon and polyesters: step-growth polymers of diacids with diamines or diols (Section 21); Kevlar is a poly(p-phenylene terephthalamide) from terephthaloyl chloride and p-phenylenediamine.
  • Nitrile-containing compounds: acrylonitrile (polyacrylonitrile fibre, Orlon), cyanoacrylate glues (alkyl 2-cyanoacrylates polymerise by anionic mechanism on contact with moisture).
