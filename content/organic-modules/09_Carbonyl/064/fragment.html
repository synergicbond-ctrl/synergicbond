55. Hydration, hemiacetals and ring-chain tautomerism

A 4- or 5-hydroxyaldehyde (or ketone) exists mostly as a cyclic hemiacetal because the OH and C=O are held close in a 5- or 6-membered ring (an intramolecular, entropy-favoured addition). This is why glucose (5-hydroxy aldehyde in its open form) is more than 99 % cyclic in water, and why it still gives a (slow) Tollens' test through the small amount of open-chain aldehyde.

intramolecular⟶
5-Hydroxypentanal → 2-hydroxytetrahydropyran (six-membered ring)
intramolecular⟶
4-Hydroxybutanal → 2-hydroxytetrahydrofuran (five-membered ring)
