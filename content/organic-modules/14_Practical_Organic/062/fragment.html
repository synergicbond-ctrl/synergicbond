21. Infrared spectroscopy for structure deduction

Principle. A bond vibrates (stretches, bends) at a characteristic frequency. When infrared radiation of the same frequency falls on the molecule, the energy is absorbed only if the vibration changes the dipole moment of the molecule. The spectrum is plotted as % transmittance against the wavenumber ν̃ (cm-1), conventionally from 4000 to 400 cm-1. Hooke's law model. The bond behaves like a spring joining two masses:

Formula ν̃ = (1/2πc) √(k/μ) μ = m1m2/(m1 + m2) (reduced mass), k = force constant (N m-1) ≈ 500 (single), 1000 (double), 1500 (triple)

Consequences, all used in the exam: (1) stronger bond ⇒ higher ν̃ (C≡C > C=C > C–C); (2) lighter atom ⇒ higher ν̃ (C–H near 3000 cm-1, C–C near 1000-1200 cm-1); (3) heavier isotope lowers ν̃ (C–D at about 2200 vs C–H 3000: ratio √(μCH/μCD) ≈ 0.73).

Factors that move the C=O stretch (a favourite theory question):

  • Conjugation with C=C or an aromatic ring lowers ν̃ by 20-40 cm-1 (more single-bond character through resonance): acetone 1715, acetophenone 1685, benzaldehyde ~1700.
  • Ring strain raises it: cyclohexanone 1715, cyclopentanone ~1745, cyclobutanone ~1780.
  • Electron-withdrawing group on the carbonyl carbon (–I) raises it: acid chloride (Cl) > ester > ketone; lone-pair donation (+M) lowers it: amide (N) < acid < ester.
  • Intramolecular H-bond (e.g., salicylaldehyde, o-hydroxyacetophenone) lowers it. Intermolecular H-bond broadens the O–H band.
MOT Lens The orbital reasons behind the carbonyl shifts. (1) *Conjugation:* the C=O π orbital overlaps with the π system of a C=C or ring, forming delocalised π molecular orbitals; the resonance picture C=C–C=O ↔ ⁺C–C=C–O− shows that the carbonyl π bond order falls below 2, the force constant k decreases, and ν̃ falls. (2) *Amides:* the nitrogen lone pair (nN) donates into π*(C=O); the C–N bond gains double-bond character and C=O loses it, so amide C=O is the lowest. (3) *Acid chlorides:* chlorine is strongly σ-withdrawing but its 3p lone pair overlaps poorly with the carbon 2p, so little donation occurs and C=O stays strong and fast. (4) *Ring strain:* in a small ring the internal C–C–C angle is compressed, so the ring bonds use more p character, leaving more s character in the carbon orbital that bonds the exocyclic oxygen: a stronger, stiffer C=O and a higher ν̃ (cyclobutanone 1780 > cyclohexanone 1715).
Exam Trap The broad O–H band of a carboxylic acid (2500-3300) is much broader and extends lower than the alcohol O–H (3200-3550). A band at ~1715 with no O–H is a ketone (or aldehyde with the 2720 doublet); with a broad O–H it is an acid. Ester C=O is above the ketone.
Example 46 Adv

Hooke's law

Using ν̃ = (1/2πc)√(k/μ), estimate ν̃ for C–H (k = 500 N m-1), C≡C (k = 1500 N m-1) and C–D (same k as C–H). Compare the C–D value with that of C–H.

Show solution
  1. μ(C–H) = (12 × 1)/13 = 0.923 u = 1.533 × 10-27 kg ⇒ √(500/1.533 × 10-27) = 5.71 × 1014 s-1 (angular); divide by 2πc = 1.884 × 1011 cm s-1: ν̃ ≈ 3030 cm-1.
  2. μ(C≡C) = 6 u ⇒ ν̃ ≈ 2060 cm-1 (observed 2100-2260).
  3. μ(C–D) = (12 × 2)/14 = 1.714 u ⇒ ν̃(C–D) = 3030 × √(0.923/1.714) = 2230 cm-1: lower than C–H by a factor of 0.73.
Example 47 Main

Reading a spectrum

A compound C4H8O2 shows a very broad band 2500-3300 and a sharp strong band at 1710 cm-1. Another isomer shows a strong band at 1740 and no band above 3000. Identify the functional groups.

Show solution
  1. The first has –COOH (broad O–H and C=O at 1710): butanoic or 2-methylpropanoic acid.
  2. The second has an ester (C=O at 1740, no O–H): e.g. ethyl acetate, methyl propanoate, propyl formate.
  3. DBE = 1 for both (C=O).
Exception & Its Reason A symmetrical internal alkyne (but-2-yne) shows no C≡C band: a vibration is IR-active only if it changes the dipole moment, and the symmetrical stretch does not.
Concept check

Place in order of decreasing C=O stretching frequency: CH3CONH2, CH3COCl, CH3COCH3.

Show answer

CH3COCl (~1800) > CH3COCH3 (~1715) > CH3CONH2 (~1650).
