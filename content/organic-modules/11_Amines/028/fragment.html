28. Synthetic planning with diazonium salts

The amino group behaves as a "master switch": the order of steps for substituted benzenes follows three rules.

  • Rule 1 – introduce NH2 (from NO2) when its directing power is wanted; remove or replace it after. NO2 is meta-directing; NH2 (after reduction) o/p.
  • Rule 2 – ArN2+ gives the substituents that cannot be put on directly: F, I, CN, OH (clean), H.
  • Rule 3 – protect NH2 (acetyl) when oxidising or nitrating, and do not use diazonium salts above 278 K.
Example 28 Adv

m-Bromophenol from benzene

Outline the synthesis of m-bromophenol from benzene.

Show solution
  1. Benzene → HNO3/H2SO4 → PhNO2 (meta director) → Br2/Fe → m-bromonitrobenzene → Sn/HCl, then NaOH → m-bromoaniline → NaNO2/HCl, 273 K → diazonium → H2O, warm (or H2SO4, boil) → m-bromophenol.
  2. The nitro group is used only to place bromine meta; it is then converted to NH2 and finally to OH.
Example 29 Adv

o- and p-Bromotoluene

How would you prepare (a) o-bromotoluene, (b) p-bromotoluene, (c) m-bromotoluene from toluene?

Show solution
  1. (a, b) Nitration of toluene gives o- (≈ 58 %) and p-nitrotoluene (≈ 38 %) – separable by distillation/crystallisation. Each is reduced (Sn/HCl), diazotised and treated with CuBr/HBr: o- and p-bromotoluene respectively (Sandmeyer).
  2. (c) Meta cannot be obtained directly: p-nitrotoluene → reduce → p-toluidine → Ac2O → Br2 (ortho to NHAc, i.e. meta to CH3) → hydrolyse → 2-bromo-4-methylaniline → NaNO2/HCl → H3PO2 → m-bromotoluene.
Example 30 Adv

Sandmeyer + hydrolysis

Convert aniline to benzoic acid, and to benzoyl chloride.

Show solution
  1. Aniline → NaNO2/HCl, 273 K → CuCN/KCN → benzonitrile → H3O+, heat → benzoic acid → SOCl2 → benzoyl chloride.
Example 31 Main

Identify the reagent

Fill in X, Y, Z: benzenediazonium chloride → X (C6H5F), → Y (C6H5I), → Z (C6H6).

Show solution
  1. X: HBF4, then heat (Balz–Schiemann). Y: aqueous KI (no catalyst). Z: H3PO2 + H2O (or ethanol).
