Practice 191 L2 Multi-correct

Which of the following reagents convert aniline into acetanilide rapidly at room temperature? (A) acetic anhydride (B) acetyl chloride (C) acetaldehyde (D) acetone

Show answer
  1. Acid chlorides and anhydrides acylate the amine by nucleophilic acyl substitution (addition–elimination). Acetaldehyde and acetone give imines (Schiff bases), not amides.

A and B.

Practice 192 L2 Multi-correct

Which of the following can be used to prepare aniline? (A) nitrobenzene with Sn/HCl (B) benzamide with Br2/KOH (C) nitrobenzene with H2/Pd (D) benzoic acid with NH3, heat

Show answer
  1. (A) and (C) reduce NO2 → NH2; (B) is the Hofmann bromamide reaction (loss of the carbonyl carbon). (D) gives benzamide (or ammonium benzoate), not aniline.

Practice 193 L2 Multi-correct

Which of the following statements about diazonium salts are correct? (A) Benzenediazonium chloride is stable in cold aqueous solution. Practice 194 L2 Multi-correct

Which of the following cannot be prepared in good yield by Gabriel's phthalimide synthesis? (A) benzylamine (B) tert-butylamine (C) aniline (D) propylamine

Show answer

Practice 195 L2 Multi-correct

Which of the following evolve N2 when treated with NaNO2/HCl in the cold? (A) glycine (B) urea (C) aniline (D) acetamide (E) methylamine

Show answer
  1. Glycine (an aliphatic 1° amine) gives hydroxyacetic acid + N2 (Van Slyke); methylamine gives methanol + N2; primary amides and urea also react with HNO2 to evolve N2 (RCONH2 → RCOOH; H2NCONH2 → CO2). Aniline gives a stable diazonium salt in the cold.

A, B, D and E.

Practice 196 L2 Multi-correct

1-Chloro-2,4-dinitrobenzene reacts readily with: (A) aqueous NaOH (B) NH3 (C) CH3ONa (D) benzene/AlCl3

Show answer
  1. These are nucleophilic aromatic substitutions (SNAr) activated by o/p nitro groups. The ring is far too deactivated for Friedel–Crafts alkylation by an aryl chloride, and an aryl halide with AlCl3 does not generate a usable electrophile.

A, B and C.

Practice 197 L2 Multi-correct

Practice 199 L2 Multi-correct

Which statements about the Hofmann elimination are correct? (A) It proceeds by an E2 pathway with anti-periplanar geometry. (B) The major alkene is the more substituted one. (C) A quaternary hydroxide is heated. (D) Tetramethylammonium hydroxide gives an alkene.

Practice 201 L2 Multi-correct

Which of these reactions increase the number of carbon atoms in the chain? (A) RBr → RCN (B) RCN → RCH2NH2 (C) RCONH2 → RNH2 (D) ArN2+ → ArCN

Practice 212 L2 Fischer–Hepp

N-Methylaniline reacts with NaNO2/HCl in the cold to give a yellow oil X (C7H8N2O). On treatment with dry HCl in ethanol X rearranges to a green compound Y (an isomer). Identify X and Y.

X: N-nitroso-N-methylaniline Y: N-methyl-4-nitrosoaniline
Show answer
  1. Under acid conditions the nitroso group moves from N to the para carbon of the ring (Fischer–Hepp rearrangement), giving a C-nitroso compound; with N,N-dimethylaniline the same C-nitroso product forms directly.

X = N-nitroso-N-methylaniline; Y = 4-nitroso-N-methylaniline (Fischer–Hepp rearrangement).

Practice 215 L2 Reasoning

Aniline reacts with bromine water to give a tribromo compound but 4-aminoacetophenone gives a different result. Using the product of mono-bromination of 4-aminoacetophenone, predict the position of Br and justify.

4-aminoacetophenone 3-bromo-4-aminoacetophenone
Show answer
  1. The para position of NH2 is blocked by the acetyl group; NH2 (strong activator) controls the orientation, and the positions ortho to NH2 are meta to the C=O (deactivating group): both groups direct to the same carbons.

Bromine enters ortho to NH2 (position 3 of the acetophenone ring): 4-amino-3-bromoacetophenone (excess Br2 gives the 3,5-dibromo compound).

Practice 216 L2 Reasoning

Why is nitrobenzene a good solvent for the Friedel–Crafts alkylation of toluene but aniline is not?

Show answer

Nitrobenzene's ring is strongly deactivated, so it does not itself undergo Friedel–Crafts reaction, and its nitro group complexes AlCl3 only weakly, so the catalyst stays active. Aniline's N lone pair binds AlCl3 strongly, destroying the catalyst.

Practice 217 L2 Reasoning

Diazoaminobenzene (from benzenediazonium chloride and aniline in weakly acidic or neutral solution) is warmed with aniline and aniline hydrochloride. What is formed, and why does the reaction go to the C-coupled product?

4-aminoazobenzene
Show answer

4-Aminoazobenzene (aniline yellow). N-Coupling is fast but reversible; in the presence of acid the triazene splits back into PhN2+ and PhNH2, and the slower, irreversible C-coupling at the para position of aniline eventually traps the material as the thermodynamically stable aminoazo compound.

Practice 218 L2 Reasoning

Explain why the reaction of aniline with nitrous acid in the presence of excess aniline and little acid gives a yellow solid, diazoaminobenzene.

diazoaminobenzene
Show answer

With little acid the diazonium ion forms in the presence of unprotonated aniline; the nucleophilic N of aniline attacks the terminal N of ArN2+ (N-coupling), giving PhN=N–NHPh (1,3-diphenyltriazene). Excess mineral acid protonates the free amine and prevents this.

Practice 219 L2 Reasoning

Phenylhydrazine is obtained by reducing benzenediazonium chloride with SnCl2/HCl. How does its reaction with acetone proceed, and what class of product is formed?

acetone phenylhydrazone
Show answer

PhNHNH2 + (CH3)2C=O → (CH3)2C=N–NHPh (phenylhydrazone) + H2O. The NH2 terminus is the nucleophile (α-effect); acid catalysis as in imine formation.
