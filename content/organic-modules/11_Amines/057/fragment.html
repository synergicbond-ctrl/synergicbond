57. Mechanism: Curtius and Lossen — the same migration without a base

  • Curtius. RCON3 (acyl azide) on heating loses N2 while R migrates from C to the neighbouring N: the isocyanate RN=C=O is formed in one concerted step (no free acyl nitrene is needed). Driving force: release of N2, the most stable small molecule; it requires no base and no Br2.
  • Lossen. An O-activated hydroxamic acid RCO–NH–OX (X = acyl, sulphonyl) is deprotonated at N; R migrates to N as the O-leaving group (carboxylate/sulphonate) departs: R–N=C=O again.
  • Common features. (i) Retention at the migrating carbon. (ii) The isocyanate is a hungry electrophile (carbon between two electronegative atoms; the LUMO is π* of N=C=O with the largest coefficient on carbon): water gives carbamic acid → amine + CO2; alcohols give urethanes (carbamate esters); amines give ureas.
