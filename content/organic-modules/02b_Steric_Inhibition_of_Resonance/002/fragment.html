3. Consequences for acidity and basicity

Acidity: a donating or accepting group that conjugates with the carboxylate or phenoxide changes pKₐ. Twisting it out of the ring removes that conjugation, so the effect on pKₐ changes sign or shrinks.

Basicity: a lone pair conjugated with a ring is less available for protonation. Twisting the NR₂ group restores the lone pair to the nitrogen, so the amine becomes more basic than its conjugated analogue. This is also why basicity in water and in the gas phase can order differently: solvation of the ammonium ion is part of the aqueous order.
