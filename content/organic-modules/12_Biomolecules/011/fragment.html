1.9 Polysaccharides

Polysaccharides are polymers of monosaccharides joined by glycosidic links: homopolysaccharides (starch, glycogen, cellulose, chitin, inulin) and heteropolysaccharides (hyaluronic acid, agar). They are non-sweet, mostly insoluble, and are only weakly or not at all reducing (a single reducing end per chain).

Key idea Why we digest starch but not cellulose. Humans have α-amylase (cleaves α-1,4 links, giving maltose and dextrins) and maltase, but not cellulase (β-1,4). The α-link makes the chain coil (helix) and the β-link makes it extended and straight, so cellulose packs into rigid fibres. Ruminants and termites depend on gut micro-organisms that make cellulase.
<h4>Hydrolysis sequence of starch</h4>
  • starch → (amylase, saliva/pancreas) → dextrins → maltose → (maltase) → D-glucose
  • In acid: starch + H2O → glucose (complete); n(C6H10O5) + n H2O → n C6H12O6.
  • Formula and mass: repeating unit C6H10O5 = 162 g mol−1; the free glucose is 180. 162 g of starch gives 180 g of glucose.

Methylation analysis of polysaccharides (end-group assay)

Exhaustively methylate the polymer (all free OH → OMe), hydrolyse, identify the methyl sugars.

Formula In a branched polymer with b branches: number of non-reducing ends = b + 1; number of branch units (dimethyl) = b. For a very large polymer, mol tetra-OMe ≈ mol di-OMe. Average chain length (ACL) = total units / number of ends ≈ units per tetramethyl molecule.
Shortcut In a methylation result a tetra-O-methyl product is always the signature of a chain end, and each end is a measure of the extent of branching. The ratio tetra : di : tri tells you both the branch frequency and the chain length.

Example 26 Adv

Degree of polymerisation

A sample of cellulose has molar mass 4.86 × 105 g mol−1. (a) How many glucose units per chain? (b) How many mmol of 2,3,6-tri-O-methylglucose does 1.00 g give on exhaustive methylation and hydrolysis (assume a linear chain, ignore ends)?

Show solution
  1. (a) n = 486 000 / 162 = 3000.
  2. (b) Each unit gives one trimethyl glucose; 1.00 g contains 1.00/162 = 6.17 × 10−3 mol units → 6.17 mmol (ends ignored).
Example 27 Adv

Branching in amylopectin

Methylation of 1.62 g of a glycogen-type polysaccharide (units of 162 g mol−1) followed by hydrolysis gives 11.8 mg of 2,3,4,6-tetra-O-methylglucose (M = 236). Find the average chain length and the number of units per branch point.

Show solution
  1. Total units = 1.62/162 = 1.00 × 10−2 mol.
  2. Tetra-O-methyl = 11.8 × 10−3/236 = 5.0 × 10−5 mol = number of chain ends.
  3. Units per end = 1.00 × 10−2/5.0 × 10−5 = 200.
  4. Number of branches = ends − 1 ≈ ends, so there is one branch for every ≈ 200 units (a lightly branched polymer; real glycogen has far more).
Example 28 Adv

Three methylation fragments

Hydrolysis of fully methylated amylopectin gives 2,3,4,6-tetra-O-methyl-, 2,3,6-tri-O-methyl- and 2,3-di-O-methyl-glucose in the mole ratio 1 : 18 : 1. Interpret this and calculate the average chain length.

Show solution
  1. Tetra-OMe = non-reducing end; tri-OMe = 1→4 linked chain unit; di-OMe = branch point. 1 : 1 for end : branch is exactly as expected.
  2. Total units = 1 + 18 + 1 = 20. Number of ends = 1 per 20 units, so ACL ≈ 20 (one branch per 20 units).
Example 29 Main

Starch/glycogen/cellulose

Which statements are correct: (a) amylose gives blue colour with I2 (b) glycogen has more branching than amylopectin (c) cellulose has α-1,4 links (d) cellulose is hydrolysed by emulsin to some extent? Give brief reasons.

Show solution
  1. (a) ✓ the helix traps I3−. (b) ✓ branch every 8–12 vs 24–30 units. (c) ✗ β-1,4. (d) Cellulose is not hydrolysed by maltase; emulsin acts on the disaccharide cellobiose (β-glucosidase) but not effectively on the crystalline polymer; it needs cellulase. So (d) is ✗.
