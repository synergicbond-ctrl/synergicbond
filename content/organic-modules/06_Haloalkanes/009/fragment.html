!tip Decision recipe: (1) Is the carbon sp3? (2) Is it methyl/primary: SN2. (3) Tertiary: SN1 (or E2 with strong base). (4) Secondary: look at the nucleophile/solvent. Strong/aprotic → SN2; weak/protic → SN1. (5) Check the extra stabilisation (allyl, benzyl, α-O) and extra hindrance (neopentyl).
Example 18 Main

Rate law from data

For R–Br + OH− the initial rate is measured:

[RBr] 0.1, [OH−] 0.1, rate 2.0 × 10−4; [RBr] 0.2, [OH−] 0.1, rate 4.0 × 10−4; [RBr] 0.1, [OH−] 0.3, rate 2.0 × 10−4. Deduce mechanism.

Show solution
  1. Doubling [RBr] doubles the rate: first order in RBr.
  2. Tripling [OH−] leaves the rate unchanged: zero order in hydroxide.
  3. rate = k[RBr]: first-order, unimolecular: SN1 (tertiary or otherwise stabilised substrate). k = 2.0 × 10−4/0.1 = 2.0 × 10−3 s−1.
Example 19 Main

Predict the mechanism

Which mechanism (SN1 or SN2) is expected for: (a) CH3CH2CH2Br + NaCN in DMSO (b) (CH3)3CBr in ethanol, heat (c) C6H5CH2Cl + NaN3 in DMF (d) (CH3)2CHBr + CH3COOH, heat.

Show solution
  1. (a) 1°, strong Nu, aprotic: SN2. (b) 3°, protic, weak Nu: SN1 (with E1). (c) benzylic 1°, strong Nu, aprotic: SN2. (d) 2°, weak Nu, protic acid solvent, heat: SN1/E1 (mixed); the major pathway is SN1.
Example 20 Adv

Rearranged product

Predict the major substitution product on heating 2-chloro-3,3-dimethylbutane in methanol, and write the sequence.

Show solution
  1. Ionisation gives the secondary cation (CH3)3C–CH+–CH3.
  2. 1,2-Methyl shift from the quaternary carbon gives the tertiary cation (CH3)2C+–CH(CH3)2 (2,3-dimethylbut-2-yl).
  3. Methanol captures it: 2-methoxy-2,3-dimethylbutane; loss of H+ gives 2,3-dimethylbut-2-ene (major alkene) and 2,3-dimethylbut-1-ene.
CH3OH, Δ⟶
Methyl shift then capture.
Example 21 Adv

Stereochemical outcome in a ring

cis-1-Bromo-4-methylcyclohexane reacts with NaI in acetone (SN2), while 1-bromo-1-methylcyclohexane is solvolysed in water. Describe the stereochemistry of each product.

Show solution
  1. The first is SN2: Br is replaced by I from the opposite face: the product is trans-1-iodo-4-methylcyclohexane (inversion).
  2. The second is a tertiary halide: the planar cation is attacked from either face: 1-methylcyclohexan-1-ol has no stereocentre (C1 carries OH, CH3 and two identical ring arms), so there is a single product, with no stereochemical question.
NaI, acetone⟶
Inversion at C–Br changes cis to trans.
Example 22 Adv

Which is more reactive in SN1?

Order by SN1 solvolysis rate: (a) C6H5CH2Cl (b) (C6H5)2CHCl (c) (C6H5)3CCl (d) CH3CH2Cl (e) (CH3)3CCl.

Show solution
  1. The rate follows the stability of the carbocation formed: trityl > benzhydryl > t-butyl ≈ benzyl > ethyl.
  2. Benzyl (1°, but resonance over one ring) is slower than benzhydryl but the comparison with t-butyl depends on solvent; both lie far above ethyl.
  3. (c) > (b) > (e) ≈ (a) > (d).
Example 23 Adv

Allylic rearrangement

Write the products of hydrolysis of 3-chloro-3-methylbut-1-ene (CH2=CH–CCl(CH3)2) in water and explain why 1-chloro-3-methylbut-2-ene gives the same mixture.

Show solution
  1. Ionisation gives the tertiary allylic cation (CH3)2C+–CH=CH2 ↔ (CH3)2C=CH–CH2+.
  2. Water attacks the tertiary end to give 2-methylbut-3-en-2-ol, or the primary end to give 3-methylbut-2-en-1-ol (prenol).
  3. The isomeric chloride ionises to the same cation, hence the same two alcohols (the thermodynamically favoured primary alcohol with the more substituted double bond usually predominates).
