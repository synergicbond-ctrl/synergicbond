24. Haloform reaction (iodoform and chloroform tests)

Reagents: a methyl ketone CH3–CO–R, acetaldehyde, ethanol, or a secondary alcohol CH3–CH(OH)–R, with X2 + NaOH (or NaOX, hypohalite). Product: CHX3 + the carboxylate RCOO− (one carbon fewer than the methyl ketone). With I2/NaOH, CHI3 is a yellow precipitate (m.p. 119 °C, characteristic antiseptic odour): the iodoform test. Mechanism: (1) base-catalysed halogenation of the CH3 three times; each halogen makes the next H more acidic, so substitution runs to completion (CX3–CO–R); (2) OH− attacks the carbonyl, (3) CX3− (a good leaving group, stabilised by three halogens) is expelled, giving RCOOH + CX3−; (4) proton transfer gives CHX3 + RCOO−. Alcohols CH3CH(OH)R are first oxidised to CH3COR by the hypohalite.

Trap The test needs CH3–C=O or CH3–CH(OH)– attached to carbon or H. Acetic acid, esters and amides give no iodoform. Ethanol is the only primary alcohol that responds, because it is oxidised to CH3CHO. Pentan-3-one (no CH3CO group) does not respond, while pentan-2-one does.
Example 33 Main

Iodoform responders

Which of these give a yellow precipitate with I2/NaOH: (a) propan-2-ol, (b) pentan-3-one, (c) 1-phenylethanol, (d) ethyl ethanoate, (e) butan-2-one, (f) ethanal, (g) 2-phenylethanol? State the other organic product for those that respond.

Show solution
  1. (a) propan-2-ol → CH3COO− (oxidised to acetone, then CHI3 + CH3COO−); (c) PhCH(OH)CH3 → PhCOO−; (e) butanone → CH3CH2COO−; (f) ethanal → HCOO−. Not responders: (b) no CH3CO; (d) ester; (g) PhCH2CH2OH, a primary alcohol other than ethanol.
Example 34 Adv

Mass of iodoform

4.4 g of ethanal (M = 44) is converted to iodoform in 80 % yield by I2/NaOH. What mass of CHI3 (M = 394) forms?

Show solution
  1. n(CH3CHO) = 4.4/44 = 0.100 mol, giving 0.100 mol CHI3 at 100 %. At 80 %: 0.080 mol × 394 = 31.5 g.
