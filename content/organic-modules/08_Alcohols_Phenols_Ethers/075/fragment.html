Phenol at the centre of its chemistry (from left: salt, ether, ester, EAS, Kolbe–Schmitt, Reimer–Tiemann, hydrogenation, reduction)
Key idea Rules to remember: (1) OH is a strongly activating o,p-director; use mild conditions (low temperature, non-polar solvent) for mono-substitution. (2) Phenoxide is a better nucleophile at carbon than phenol: Kolbe–Schmitt, Reimer–Tiemann, coupling, Lederer–Manasse all go through it. (3) Phenols do not undergo SN at the ring carbon. (4) Acidity: −M/−I groups at o/p raise it, +M/+I groups lower it; meta shows only −I; steric inhibition of resonance switches off −M. (5) Ortho H-bonding gives volatility in steam; para H-bonding is intermolecular.
Example 53 L3 Advanced

Multi-step identification

Compound A (C7H8O) dissolves in NaOH and gives a violet colour with FeCl3. With excess Br2/H2O it gives a tribromo derivative C7H5Br3O. In the Reimer–Tiemann reaction A gives a major product B that is volatile in steam and gives a colour with FeCl3. Identify A and B.

Show solution
  1. C7H8O soluble in NaOH with a FeCl3 colour is a cresol (benzyl alcohol and anisole are neither acidic nor coloured by FeCl3).
  2. Three Br atoms must go to positions 2, 4 and 6 relative to OH. In o-cresol positions 2 and 6 are not both free (one carries CH3); in p-cresol position 4 is blocked. Only m-cresol (3-methylphenol) has all of positions 2, 4, 6 free: 2,4,6-tribromo-3-methylphenol, C7H5Br3O ✓.
  3. Reimer–Tiemann on m-cresol: the CHO enters ortho to OH at C-6 (C-2 is hindered between OH and CH3). The product is 2-hydroxy-4-methylbenzaldehyde; its OH···O=C intramolecular hydrogen bond makes it steam-volatile.
  4. A = m-cresol; B = 2-hydroxy-4-methylbenzaldehyde.
Example 54 L3 Advanced

Which is a stronger acid, and why?

Arrange in decreasing acidity with reasons: (a) phenol, salicylic acid (COOH), benzoic acid, acetic acid; (b) p-nitrophenol, p-chlorophenol, p-cresol, p-methoxyphenol, p-cyanophenol.

Show solution
  1. (a) Salicylic acid (2.97) > benzoic acid (4.20) > acetic acid (4.76) > phenol (10.0). Salicylic acid is strongest because its carboxylate is stabilised by an intramolecular hydrogen bond from the ortho OH. Benzoic acid is stronger than acetic acid because the sp2 carbon of the ring is electron-withdrawing relative to CH3.
  2. (b) p-Nitrophenol (7.15) > p-cyanophenol (7.95) > p-chlorophenol (9.4) > p-methoxyphenol (10.2) ≈ p-cresol (10.3). The NO2 and CN groups withdraw by −M and −I; Cl withdraws by −I (stronger than its +M); OCH3 donates by +M at para (stronger than its −I), and CH3 donates by +I.
