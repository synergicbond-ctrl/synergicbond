23. Unsaturated acids, fatty acids, fats and soaps

23.1 Acrylic, methacrylic, crotonic and cinnamic acid

  • Acrylic acid CH2=CH–COOH (pKa 4.25): from propene oxidation (via acrolein); polymerises to polyacrylates; methyl methacrylate (from acetone cyanohydrin, H2SO4, CH3OH) polymerises to PMMA (Perspex, plexiglass). Acrylic acid adds HBr anti-Markovnikov (in the dark and without peroxide!) to give 3-bromopropanoic acid, because the carboxyl group is a –M/–I group: the carbocation at the α-carbon (adjacent to COOH) is strongly destabilised, so H+ adds at the α carbon and Br− at the β-carbon (conjugate addition).
HBr⟶
Acrylic acid + HBr → 3-bromopropanoic acid (conjugate addition, “anti-Markovnikov”)
  • Crotonic acid (E-but-2-enoic acid, mp 72 °C) and isocrotonic acid (Z-, mp 15 °C): geometrical isomers; hydrogenation gives butanoic acid.
  • Cinnamic acid (E-3-phenylprop-2-enoic acid, mp 133 °C): from the Perkin reaction; hydrogenation → 3-phenylpropanoic acid; KMnO4 cleaves the C=C to PhCOOH (+ glyoxylic/oxalic acid); Br2 adds to the double bond → 2,3-dibromo-3-phenylpropanoic acid.

Unsaturated acids

Conjugate addition. In α,β-unsaturated acids the C=C is conjugated with C=O: nucleophiles (CN−, amines, enolates, HX) add at the β-carbon (Michael). Hydration of acrylic acid gives 3-hydroxypropanoic acid; HCN adds to give 3-cyanopropanoic acid.

Trap α,β-Unsaturated acids are less reactive than isolated alkenes toward electrophilic reagents (Br2, HBr by the polar mechanism) because C=O is electron-withdrawing; but they are more reactive toward nucleophiles at the β-carbon.

Oleic acid reactions: H2/Ni → stearic acid (hardening of oils to vanaspati ghee); Br2 adds (iodine value); hot KMnO4 cleaves at C9=C10 to nonanoic acid (pelargonic) + nonanedioic acid (azelaic acid); cold alkaline KMnO4 gives the dihydroxystearic acid; ozonolysis (oxidative work-up) gives the same acid pair; isomerises to elaidic acid with HNO2.

23.3 Fats and oils; soaps

Fats and oils are triglycerides (triesters of glycerol with three fatty acids). Saponification: (RCOO)3C3H5 + 3 NaOH → 3 RCOONa (soap) + glycerol. Soap is the sodium or potassium salt of a long-chain fatty acid; it cleans by forming micelles (hydrophobic tails dissolving grease, carboxylate heads into water). Hard water (Ca2+, Mg2+) precipitates insoluble calcium and magnesium salts (scum); detergents (sodium dodecylbenzenesulfonate, sodium alkyl sulfates) give soluble Ca/Mg salts, so they work in hard water. Branched alkylbenzenesulfonates are not biodegradable; linear ones are.

Example 59 Main

Saponification and iodine values

Calculate the saponification value of tristearin (M = 891.5) and the iodine value of triolein (M = 885.4).

Show solution
  1. SV = 3 × 56.1 × 1000/891.5 = 188.8 mg KOH per g (three ester groups per molecule, one KOH each).
  2. Triolein has 3 C=C: each adds one I2 (253.8): 3 × 253.8 = 761.4 g I2 per mole of fat; per 100 g fat: IV = 761.4 × 100/885.4 = 86.0.
Example 60 Adv

Composition from constants

A triglyceride has an iodine value of 86.0 (to within rounding) and a saponification value of 190. Is it consistent with triolein? What is the molar mass implied by the SV?

Show solution
  1. M = 3 × 56.1 × 1000/190 = 885.8 g mol−1: matches triolein (885.4). Number of C=C = IV × M/(100 × 253.8) = 86.0 × 885.8/25380 = 3.0: three double bonds, so each acyl chain has one C=C: the triglyceride is triolein-like (or a mixed triglyceride of C18 monounsaturated chains).
Example 61 Main

Products of oxidative cleavage

Predict the products from hot concentrated KMnO4 on (a) oleic acid, (b) cinnamic acid, (c) sorbic acid (hexa-2,4-dienoic acid).

Show solution
  1. (a) CH3(CH2)7COOH (nonanoic acid) + HOOC(CH2)7COOH (azelaic acid).
  2. (b) PhCOOH (benzoic acid) from the ring-CH= end, and HOOC–COOH (oxalic acid) from the =CH–COOH end (oxalic acid itself is oxidised further to CO2 under forcing conditions).
  3. (c) CH3CH=CH–CH=CH–COOH: cleavage of C2=C3 and C4=C5 gives CH3COOH (from C5–C6) and two HOOC–COOH fragments (from C1–C2 and C3–C4), which are further oxidised to CO2 under forcing conditions.
