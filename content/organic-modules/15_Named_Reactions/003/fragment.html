1. Wurtz reaction (alkane synthesis)

Reagents: 2 RX + 2 Na, dry ether (no trace of water: Na and the organosodium would be destroyed). Result: R–R, the carbon count doubles. Works best with R–I and R–Br, primary alkyl. Mechanism (two views are both acceptable at JEE level): (a) R–X + 2 Na → R−Na+ (organosodium, a carbanion source) + NaX; then R− attacks a second R–X by SN2: R–R + X−. (b) Single-electron transfer from Na gives the radical R•, and two radicals couple. Side products come from the same intermediates: the carbanion is a strong base, so with secondary and tertiary halides it causes E2 elimination (alkene + alkane), which is why Wurtz fails for 3° halides.

Key idea Wurtz is only useful for symmetrical alkanes with an even number of carbons made from a primary halide. Mixed halides RX + R′X give a statistical 1 : 2 : 1 mixture R–R, R–R′, R′–R′ that cannot be separated, so a "preparation of an unsymmetrical alkane by Wurtz" is a classic wrong choice.
+ 2 Na, dry ether⟶
Bromoethane → n-butane (C2 + C2 = C4)
Na, dry ether⟶
Intramolecular Wurtz: 1,4-dibromobutane → cyclobutane
Zn (or Na)⟶
1,3-Dibromopropane → cyclopropane (Zn/NaI is better than Na)
Trap Intramolecular Wurtz works for 3-, 4- (and 5-, 6-) membered rings, but 1,2-dibromides simply eliminate: BrCH2CH2Br + Zn → CH2=CH2. The ring size is (number of carbons between the halogens) + 2.
Example 1 Main

Product count in a mixed Wurtz

Methyl iodide and ethyl iodide are heated with sodium in dry ether. How many different alkanes can form, and what is their ratio if the two halides react at equal rates?

Show solution
  1. Possible couplings: CH3–CH3, CH3–CH2CH3, CH3CH2–CH2CH3: three alkanes (ethane, propane, butane).
  2. Each radical/carbanion pairs at random: P(Me–Me) = ¼, P(Me–Et) = ½, P(Et–Et) = ¼. Ratio ethane : propane : butane = 1 : 2 : 1.
Example 2 Main

Which dihalide gives which ring

Predict the hydrocarbon from (i) 1,3-dibromo-2,2-dimethylpropane with Zn, (ii) 1,4-dibromobutane with Na, (iii) 1,3-dibromocyclobutane with Na.

Show solution
  1. (i) Three-membered ring: 1,1-dimethylcyclopropane. (ii) Cyclobutane. (iii) the two carbons that carry bromine in the four-membered ring bond across the ring: bicyclo[1.1.0]butane (the most strained C4H6 hydrocarbon).
