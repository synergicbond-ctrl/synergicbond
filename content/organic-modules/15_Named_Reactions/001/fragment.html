How to use this chapter

This chapter is a working reference of more than 90 named reactions and rearrangements that appear in JEE Advanced, followed by a reagent-by-reagent table, a set of multi-step road-map syntheses and a graded problem bank (Foundation, Main, Advanced) with complete solutions. Every structure and every atom balance in the schemes was machine-checked (RDKit) while this chapter was built: in each scheme the atoms lost or gained between two structures are exactly the atoms named in the text. For every reaction you meet, force yourself to answer the same six questions. They are the whole of reaction-problem technique.

  • 1. What is the functional group change? (C–C bond made, group converted, skeleton rearranged, chain shortened or lengthened.)
  • 2. What is the key intermediate? (carbocation, carbanion/enolate, carbene, nitrene-like N, radical, pericyclic transition state.)
  • 3. Where does it go? (regiochemistry: Markovnikov or not, which carbon is attacked, which group migrates.)
  • 4. In which geometry? (stereochemistry: syn/anti, inversion/retention, E/Z, cis/trans.)
  • 5. What stops it? (limitation: substrate class that fails, competing reaction, over-reaction.)
  • 6. What is the carbon count? (chain length before and after, since JEE loves "how many carbons does the product have").
Shortcut Read a reagent as a verb. NaBH4 reduces, LDA deprotonates, PCC oxidises mildly, SOCl2 chlorinates. If you cannot say what a reagent does in one verb you have not learnt it yet. Part G of this chapter is exactly that verb table.
Trap Never memorise only the product. JEE Advanced asks "which of the following is formed", "how many stereoisomers", "which migrates" and "what happens if the substituent is changed". These need the mechanism, so every reaction below is given with its mechanism.
