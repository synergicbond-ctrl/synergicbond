8. Family 3: partial reduction of acid derivatives

8.1 Rosenmund reduction Acid chloride + H2 over Pd/BaSO4 (poisoned with quinoline or sulphur, to prevent over-reduction of aldehyde to alcohol) gives an aldehyde. It cannot be used for formaldehyde (HCOCl is unstable) and not for ketones.

H₂, Pd/BaSO₄, quinoline⟶
Ethanoyl chloride → ethanal
H₂, Pd/BaSO₄⟶
Benzoyl chloride → benzaldehyde

8.2 Stephen reduction Nitrile + SnCl2/HCl (dry HCl in ether) gives the aldimine hydrochloride (RCH=NH2+Cl− as the tin complex); hydrolysis (H3O+) gives the aldehyde. Works best for aryl nitriles and short-chain alkyl nitriles.

(i) SnCl₂, HCl (ii) H₃O⁺⟶
Ethanenitrile → ethanal (via the aldimine)
(i) SnCl₂, HCl (ii) H₃O⁺⟶
Benzonitrile → benzaldehyde

8.3 DIBAL-H Di-isobutylaluminium hydride, (iBu)2AlH, a bulky electrophilic hydride donor, at −78 °C reduces nitriles and esters to aldehydes (after H3O+ work-up). At that temperature the tetrahedral aluminium intermediate does not collapse until work-up, so no second hydride adds. At room temperature, esters would be reduced to the alcohol.

(i) DIBAL-H, −78 °C (ii) H₂O⟶
Methyl butanoate → butanal
(i) DIBAL-H (ii) H₃O⁺⟶
DIBAL on a nitrile (like Stephen but more general)

8.4 LiAlH(OtBu)3 This hindered, less reactive hydride (lithium tri-tert-butoxyaluminium hydride) reduces acid chlorides to aldehydes at low temperature and does not touch the aldehyde formed.

Trap LiAlH4 and NaBH4 (when it reacts at all with acid chlorides) overshoot to the alcohol. A question that says "stop at the aldehyde" is asking for Rosenmund, DIBAL-H, Stephen or LiAlH(OtBu)3.
