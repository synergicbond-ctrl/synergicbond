8.3 E/Z for double bonds

  • Take each end of the double bond, rank its two substituents with the CIP rules.
  • Z (zusammen): the higher-priority groups are on the same side. E (entgegen): on opposite sides.
  • E/Z isomerism requires each sp² carbon to carry two different groups; a =CH₂ or =C(CH₃)₂ end cannot show it.
Why It Happens Rotation about a C=C bond is blocked because it would break the π overlap, so the substituents stay on fixed sides of the double bond. If one end carries two identical groups, exchanging them gives the same molecule, so no pair of isomers arises.

Example 51 Standard

Assign E/Z to both drawings of 2-bromobut-2-ene, and state which one is "cis" by the methyl groups.

a b
Show solution
  1. On C2 the groups are Br and CH₃: Br > CH₃ (atomic number). On C3 the groups are CH₃ and H: CH₃ > H.
  2. Drawing (a): the two methyl groups are on opposite sides (trans-methyls). Then Br (on C2) and the C4 methyl (on C3) are on the same side → Z.
  3. Drawing (b): the methyls are on the same side (cis-methyls), so Br and the C4 methyl are on opposite sides → E.

(a) is (Z)-2-bromobut-2-ene (trans-methyls); (b) is (E)-2-bromobut-2-ene (cis-methyls). Here cis ≠ Z: the geometric word depends on which groups you compare, the CIP descriptor on priorities.
