18. Organometallic compounds: Grignard and organolithium reagents

18.1 Preparation

Grignard reagent: R–X + Mg → R–Mg–X in dry diethyl ether or THF (the ether oxygen lone pairs coordinate to Mg and solvate the reagent). Reactivity of halide: RI > RBr > RCl ≫ RF; aryl chlorides need THF. Trace of I2 (or 1,2-dibromoethane) activates the Mg surface. Strictly dry conditions: every H–O, H–N, H–S bond destroys the reagent. In solution RMgX is in equilibrium with R2Mg and MgX2 (Schlenk equilibrium); it is represented as R–MgX. Organolithium: R–X + 2 Li → R–Li + LiX (ether or hexane). Stronger bases/nucleophiles than Grignards; also from Li–halogen exchange (n-BuLi + ArBr) and from ArH by deprotonation.

Mg, dry ether⟶
Ethylmagnesium bromide: C–Mg bond is polar covalent with carbon δ−: the carbon behaves as a carbanion (R−).
Key idea A Grignard reagent is simultaneously a very strong base (pKa of conjugate alkane ≈ 50) and a strong nucleophile. It is quenched by anything with an acidic proton, even weakly acidic: water, alcohols, phenols, carboxylic acids, amines, terminal alkynes (pKa 25), amides, and even the N–H/O–H/S–H protons in the same molecule. Such compounds cannot be protected only by dryness; the molecule itself must not carry O–H etc. when making its own Grignard.
Trap A halide containing an –OH, –COOH, –NH2, C≡C–H or a carbonyl cannot be turned into its Grignard (BrCH2CH2OH + Mg fails). Also, ethers are fine, tertiary amines are fine, and the Grignard from a halide with a nitro group fails (the nitro group reacts).
