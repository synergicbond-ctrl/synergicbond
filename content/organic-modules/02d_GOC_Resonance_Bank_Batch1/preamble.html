RM-002 [Foundation] · Master sheet Q2 · Key: c (disagrees) · Status: Verified (independent solution)

Correct stability order of carbocations: (a) CH₂=CH–CH₂⁺ > CH₃CH₂⁺ > CH₂=CH⁺ (b) CH₃CH₂⁺ > CH₂=CH–CH₂⁺ > CH₂=CH⁺ (c) CH₂=CH⁺ > CH₃CH₂⁺ > CH₂=CH–CH₂⁺ (d) CH₂=CH–CH₂⁺ > CH₂=CH⁺ > CH₃CH₂⁺

Full solution
  1. Allyl cation is resonance-stabilised over three carbons.
  2. Primary ethyl cation is stabilised by hyperconjugation (3 α-H).
  3. Vinyl cation has an sp-type empty orbital in the plane: least stable.
  4. Order: allyl > ethyl > vinyl = option (a).

Answer: (a)

RM-003 [Foundation] · Master sheet Q3 · Key: d (disagrees: 14 cannot be reached) · Status: Verified

Number of hyperconjugative structures of (CH₃)₂CH–C⁺H–CH(CH₃)₂. (a) 2 (b) 6 (c) 12 (d) 14

Full solution
  1. The cation carbon is bonded to two CH carbons, each with one H.
  2. α-H = 1 + 1 = 2. Methyl H are β to the cation and do not count.
  3. Answer (a) = 2.

Answer: (a)

RM-004 [Foundation] · Master sheet Q4 · Key: a (disagrees) · Status: Verified

Maximum hyperconjugation is shown by: (a) CH₃CH=CH₂ (b) (CH₃)₂C=CH₂ (c) (CH₃)₂C=C(CH₃)₂ (d) CH₃CH=CHCH₃

RM-005 [Application] · Master sheet Q5 · Key: a (disagrees) · Status: Verified in part (cis / 2-methylpropene numbers not in opened sources)

Lowest heat of hydrogenation (to alkane) among 1-butene, cis-2-butene, trans-2-butene and 2-methylpropene: (a) 1-butene (b) cis-2-butene (c) trans-2-butene (d) 2-methylpropene

Full solution
  1. A more stable alkene releases less heat on hydrogenation (smaller magnitude).
  2. Ref. [2], p. 344: but-1-ene −125.9, trans-but-2-ene −114.6 kJ/mol. trans-but-2-ene is smaller than but-1-ene.
  3. Among the four, the most stable (smallest ΔH) is trans-but-2-ene (c). The cis and 2-methylpropene values are not in the opened text; the choice rests on trans < but-1-ene and the disubstituted rule.

Answer: (c)

RM-006 [Foundation] · Master sheet Q6 · Key: d (agrees) · Status: Verified

Which compound has no hyperconjugation? (a) CH₃–CH=CH₂ (b) CH₃–C≡CH (c) (CH₃)₃C• (d) (CH₃)₃C–CH=CH₂

Full solution
  1. (a) three α-H on the sp² carbon.
  2. (b) three α-H on CH₃ attached to the sp carbon.
  3. (c) nine α-H on the radical carbon.
  4. (d) the quaternary carbon next to C=C has no hydrogen: no α-H, so no hyperconjugation through C–H.

Answer: (d)

RM-007 [Application] · Master sheet Q7 · Key: b (disagrees) · Status: Verified

Which carbocation cannot undergo a 1,2-shift to a more stable ion? (a) (CH₃)₃C–CH₂⁺ (b) CH₃CH₂CH₂⁺ (c) (CH₃)₂CH–C⁺H–CH₃ (d) (CH₃)₃C⁺

Full solution
  1. (a) 1° → 3° by methyl shift.
  2. (b) 1° → 2° by hydride shift.
  3. (c) 2° → 3° by hydride shift.
  4. (d) tertiary: no more stable ion accessible by a 1,2-shift.

Answer: (d)

RM-008 [Application] · Master sheet Q8 · Key: c (disagrees) · Status: Verified

On rearrangement the 3,3-dimethylbutan-2-yl cation (CH₃)₃C–C⁺H–CH₃ gives: (a) (CH₃)₂C⁺–CH(CH₃)₂ (b) (CH₃)₂CH–C⁺H–CH₃ (c) CH₃–C⁺(CH₃)–CH₂CH₂CH₃ (d) (CH₃)₃C–CH₂⁺

Full solution
  1. A methyl migrates from the quaternary carbon to the cation carbon with its bonding pair.
  2. The cation moves to the former quaternary carbon, which now bears two methyls.
  3. Product: (CH₃)₂C⁺–CH(CH₃)₂ = 2,3-dimethylbutan-2-yl cation, option (a).

Answer: (a)

RM-009 [Application] · Master sheet Q9 · Key: d (disagrees) · Status: Verified

The neopentyl cation (CH₃)₃C–CH₂⁺ rearranges to: (a) (CH₃)₂C⁺–CH₂CH₃ (b) (CH₃)₂CH–C⁺H–CH₃ (c) no rearrangement (d) CH₂=C(CH₃)₂

RM-011 [Foundation] · Master sheet Q11 · Key: c (disagrees) · Status: Verified (standard definition; Ref. [2] Ch.1 text)

Which statement about the resonance hybrid is correct? (a) its energy is lower than that of the most stable contributor (b) it is a rapidly interconverting mixture (c) it is the arithmetic mean of bond lengths only for non-equivalent structures (d) it has the geometry of the least stable contributor

Full solution
  1. A resonance hybrid is a single species; its energy is lower than any contributor.
  2. Option (a) is the defining statement. (b) describes a tautomeric/exchanging equilibrium, not resonance.

Answer: (a)

RM-012 [Foundation] · Master sheet Q12 · Key: d (disagrees) · Status: Verified in substance; note the sign convention of option (a)

Resonance energy of benzene is calculated as: (a) ΔH(hyd, observed) − calculated for cyclohexatriene (b) calculated − observed for cyclohexatriene (c) ΔH(combustion) observed − 2 × ΔH(combustion) of cyclohexene (d) ΔH(formation) of benzene

Full solution
  1. Resonance energy = (hypothetical, from three isolated C=C) − (observed) = calculated − observed.
  2. With hydrogenation values: 360 − 208 = 152 kJ mol⁻¹ (Ref. [2] gives 151).
  3. Option (a) as written ("observed − calculated") gives a negative number, so its sign is reversed; the correct sign is option (b) in the formula reading.

Answer: (a)

RM-013 [Application] · Master sheet Q13 · Key: d (agrees) · Status: Verified

Which statement about hyperconjugation is incorrect? (a) it is delocalisation of σ(C–H) electrons into an adjacent p or π orbital (b) it shortens the C(sp³)–C(sp²) bond in propene (c) it lengthens the C–H bonds that take part (d) it makes the participating C–H bonds stronger

Full solution
  1. (a), (b), (c) are the standard effects (Ref. [3] Ch.1 on σ–π interaction; Ref. [2], p. 222).
  2. (d) is wrong: the participating C–H bonds are weakened and lengthened.

Answer: (d)

RM-015 [Application] · Master sheet Q15 · Key: a (agrees) · Status: Verified

Most stable resonance contributor of acrylamide CH₂=CH–C(=O)–NH₂: (a) CH₂=CH–C(=O)–NH₂ (b) CH₂=CH–C(O⁻)=N⁺H₂ (c) ⁺CH₂–CH=C(O⁻)–NH₂ (d) CH₂=CH–C⁺(O⁻)–NH₂

Full solution
  1. (a) has all atoms with octets and no formal charge.
  2. (b) separates charge, (c) and (d) place charge on carbon and an unstable O⁻/C⁺ pattern.
  3. Most stable: (a).

Answer: (a)

RM-016 [Foundation] · Master sheet Q16 · Key: b (agrees) · Status: Verified

Which pair are resonance structures? (a) CH₃–CO–CH₃ and CH₃–C(OH)=CH₂ (b) CH₂=CH–CH₂⁺ and ⁺CH₂–CH=CH₂ (c) chair and boat cyclohexane (d) CH₃CH₂CH₂⁺ and (CH₃)₂CH⁺

Full solution
  1. Resonance: same atoms, positions of π/lone-pair electrons differ.
  2. (a) moves an H (tautomers). (c) conformers. (d) moves an H (constitutional isomers).
  3. (b) is the allyl cation pair.

Answer: (b)

RM-018 [Application] · Master sheet Q18 · Key: (not in key list) · Status: Verified

Heterolytic cleavage of (CH₃)₃C–Br in a polar solvent is faster than for CH₃CH₂–Br because: (a) (CH₃)₃C⁺ is stabilised by nine hyperconjugative structures and +I (b) Br⁻ leaves more easily from 1° (c) only sterics, no electronic reason (d) CH₃CH₂⁺ is planar
