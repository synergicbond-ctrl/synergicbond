24.3 Stereochemistry

Because attack is from the back and at one carbon only, cyclic epoxides give trans products, and epoxides of defined alkene geometry give predictable diastereomers.

H₃O⁺⟶
Cyclohexene oxide + H₃O⁺ → trans-cyclohexane-1,2-diol (racemic): the two OH groups are anti
1. NaOCH₃, CH₃OH 2. H₂O⟶
Cyclohexene oxide + methoxide → trans-2-methoxycyclohexanol (racemic; one enantiomer drawn)
HBr⟶
Cyclohexene oxide + HBr → trans-2-bromocyclohexanol
1. C₆H₅MgBr 2. H₃O⁺⟶
Grignard reagent attacks from the back: trans-2-phenylcyclohexanol

Acyclic epoxides and diols: an anti opening of a meso (cis) epoxide by water or hydroxide gives the racemic diol; anti opening of a trans epoxide (a racemic pair) gives the meso diol. This is why peracid/H3O+ and OsO4 give opposite diastereomers (section 9.1).

cis-2,3-Dimethyloxirane (meso) → (2S,3S)/(2R,3R)-butane-2,3-diol (racemic; one enantiomer drawn)
1. HO⁻/H₂O or H₃O⁺⟶
trans-2,3-Dimethyloxirane → meso-butane-2,3-diol

Chiral epoxide with a stereocentre carbon. In base the stereocentre (if it is the more substituted carbon) is not attacked, so its configuration is retained; in acid it is attacked and inverted.

CH₃O⁻, CH₃OH⟶
(R)-Propylene oxide + methoxide: attack at CH₂, stereocentre untouched → (R)-1-methoxypropan-2-ol
CH₃OH, H⁺⟶
(R)-Propylene oxide in acid: attack at the stereocentre with inversion → (S)-2-methoxypropan-1-ol
Trap A label can change without any bond to the stereocentre being broken, and can stay the same when the centre is inverted. Always decide the geometry (retention/inversion) first and then work out R/S from the new priorities. In the pair above the priorities at the stereocentre are O > CH2O > CH3 > H in both products, so inversion changes R to S and retention keeps R.
Fürst–Plattner (trans-diaxial) rule. A substituted cyclohexene oxide opens to the trans-diaxial product (the nucleophile enters axially, through a chair-like transition state), not to the diequatorial isomer, which would require a twist-boat transition state. In 1-methylcyclohexene oxide under basic conditions the nucleophile still attacks the secondary carbon (less hindered), and in acid the tertiary carbon, in both cases giving the trans relationship between Nu and OH.

Example 66 L1 Foundation

Opening of ethylene oxide

Write the products of ethylene oxide with (a) H2O/H+; (b) NH3; (c) CH3MgBr then H3O+; (d) HCl; (e) HCN; (f) C2H5OH/H+; (g) LiAlH4.

Show solution
  1. (a) HOCH2CH2OH (ethylene glycol). (b) HOCH2CH2NH2 (2-aminoethanol; further reaction gives di- and tri-ethanolamine). (c) CH3CH2CH2OH (propan-1-ol: two carbons added to the Grignard carbon). (d) ClCH2CH2OH (2-chloroethanol, ethylene chlorohydrin). (e) HOCH2CH2CN (ethylene cyanohydrin). (f) C2H5OCH2CH2OH (2-ethoxyethanol, a “cellosolve”). (g) CH3CH2OH (ethanol).
Example 67 L2 Main

Regiochemistry

Predict the major product and name it: (a) 2-methyloxirane + CH3CH2SNa; (b) 2-methyloxirane + HBr; (c) 2,2-dimethyloxirane + C2H5OH/H2SO4; (d) 2,2-dimethyloxirane + NaCN/H2O; (e) 2-phenyloxirane + CH3OH/H+.

Show solution
  1. (a) Strong nucleophile, less hindered CH2: 1-(ethylsulfanyl)propan-2-ol, CH3CH(OH)CH2SC2H5.
  2. (b) Acid: Br− attacks the more substituted carbon: 2-bromopropan-1-ol, CH3CHBrCH2OH (the exam answer; both carbons are similar, so some 1-bromopropan-2-ol also forms in practice).
  3. (c) Acid, tertiary carbon: 2-ethoxy-2-methylpropan-1-ol, (CH3)2C(OC2H5)CH2OH.
  4. (d) Cyanide is a strong nucleophile and attacks CH2: (CH3)2C(OH)CH2CN, 3-hydroxy-3-methylbutanenitrile.
  5. (e) Acid, benzylic carbon (stabilised cation character): 2-methoxy-2-phenylethanol, PhCH(OCH3)CH2OH.
Example 68 L2 Main

Stereochemical outcome

Give the product and its stereochemistry when (i) cyclopentene oxide reacts with NaOH/H2O; (ii) cis-2,3-diethyloxirane (meso) is hydrolysed with dilute H2SO4.

Show solution
  1. (i) Cyclopentene oxide opens anti: trans-cyclopentane-1,2-diol, formed as a racemic mixture (attack at either carbon is equally likely and gives the two enantiomers).
  2. (ii) cis-2,3-Diethyloxirane is meso. Anti hydrolysis gives the racemic (3R,4R)/(3S,4S)-hexane-3,4-diol; attack at either carbon of the symmetrical epoxide gives the two enantiomers in equal amounts.
Example 69 L3 Advanced

Acid versus base, same epoxide

2,2,3-Trimethyloxirane, (CH3)2C–CH–CH3 (epoxide), is treated with (A) CH3OH/H+ and (B) CH3O−/CH3OH. Name the products and explain the difference.

Show solution
  1. The epoxide has one tertiary carbon (C bearing two CH3) and one secondary carbon.
  2. (A) Acid: methanol attacks the tertiary carbon, which carries more positive character in the protonated epoxide: CH3CH(OH)–C(CH3)2OCH3, 3-methoxy-3-methylbutan-2-ol.
  3. (B) Base: methoxide attacks the secondary, less hindered carbon: (CH3)2C(OH)–CH(OCH3)CH3, 3-methoxy-2-methylbutan-2-ol.
  4. The two are regioisomers: electronic control under acid, steric control under base.
