21.1 Claisen rearrangement of allyl aryl ethers

An allyl phenyl ether heated to about 470 K rearranges to an o-allylphenol. It is a concerted, thermal [3,3]-sigmatropic shift through a six-membered chair-like transition state: the O–C(allyl) bond breaks while a new C–C bond forms between the terminal carbon of the allyl group and the ortho carbon. The immediate product is a non-aromatic cyclohexa-2,4-dienone, which tautomerises (enolises) to restore aromaticity.

Δ, ≈ 470 K ([3,3] shift)⟶
Allyl phenyl ether → 2-allylphenol (via the 6-allylcyclohexa-2,4-dienone)
  • Allyl inversion: a crotyl ether (O–CH2–CH=CH–CH3) gives the ortho product attached through the *other* end of the allyl unit, 2-(1-methylprop-2-enyl)phenol (a branched chain). This shows the allylic transposition characteristic of [3,3] shifts.
  • Both ortho positions blocked: the allyl group cannot tautomerise at carbon, so a second [3,3] (Cope) shift moves it to the para position: allyl 2,6-dimethylphenyl ether → 4-allyl-2,6-dimethylphenol (the para-Claisen rearrangement).
  • No catalyst is needed, and the reaction is intramolecular and stereospecific; compare with the Fries rearrangement (intermolecular, Lewis acid).
Δ⟶
Crotyl phenyl ether → 2-(but-3-en-2-yl)phenol (branched)
Δ, then Cope⟶
Both ortho positions blocked: para-Claisen product
Example 48 Adv

Claisen

Predict the products on heating (a) allyl phenyl ether (b) allyl 2,6-dimethylphenyl ether (c) prop-2-enyl 4-methylphenyl ether. Why does (b) not give an ortho product?

Show solution
  1. (a) 2-Allylphenol.
  2. (b) 4-Allyl-2,6-dimethylphenol. The first [3,3] shift would place the allyl group on a ring carbon that already carries CH3: this dienone cannot tautomerise (no H to lose from a quaternary carbon), so a second [3,3] (Cope) shift carries the allyl group to the para carbon, where enolisation is possible.
  3. (c) The ortho position is free: 2-allyl-4-methylphenol.

21.2 The nitroso group and other special substituents

The –N=O group is an unusual substituent: it is deactivating (strongly –I and –M) but, because of the nitrogen lone pair, it is ortho/para-directing, a second class of "deactivating o/p" group together with the halogens. Nitrosobenzene is made by oxidation of phenylhydroxylamine, and aromatic nitroso compounds are also formed by nitrosation of activated rings (phenol, N,N-dimethylaniline) with NO+.

21.3 More isotope effects

Isotopic substitution distinguishes mechanisms: nitration of C6H5CD3 and C6H5CH3 proceeds at the same rate (ring C–H not involved, and CH3/CD3 not cleaved), but side-chain radical bromination (Br· abstracting H or D) is slower for CD3 (primary isotope effect, kH/kD of about 5 for Br·). Benzene with a deuterated ring (C6D6) is nitrated and brominated at the same rate as C6H6 (no isotope effect) but sulphonated slightly more slowly.

Key idea Summary of isotope effects in EAS: kH/kD ≈ 1 (nitration, halogenation, Friedel–Crafts); small values (up to ≈ 2–3) for sulphonation and iodination; larger values for azo coupling with hindered substrates; large (≈ 4–8) for radical benzylic hydrogen abstraction.

21.4 Chlorine addition and the isomers of BHC

Free-radical addition of Cl2 to benzene gives 1,2,3,4,5,6-hexachlorocyclohexane, C6H6Cl6. Because the six C–Cl bonds can be up or down on the ring, there are eight diastereomers; one of them (the α-isomer) is chiral and exists as a pair of enantiomers, so the total is nine stereoisomers (verified by computer enumeration). The γ-isomer (lindane) is the insecticide. Some textbooks quote 'eight isomers', meaning the eight diastereomers; read the question to see which is asked.

21.5 Benzyne with an oxyanion substituent

If the substituent on the benzyne is an anion such as O− (from a bromophenol with excess amide), the nucleophile adds meta to it: the new carbanion then sits as far as possible from the negative charge already present. This is a way to make 3-aminophenols. For a neutral –I substituent such as OCH3 the nucleophile also ends up meta (section 17). A para substituent such as alkyl is too distant to bias the addition (≈ 50 : 50).

Example 49 Adv

Practice with the extras

(a) How many diastereomers and how many stereoisomers does hexachlorocyclohexane (from benzene + 3 Cl2) have? (b) Which ring of benzanilide, PhCONHPh, is nitrated, and where? (c) A student claims that nitration of toluene-d3 (C6H5CD3) will be slower than that of toluene because of the C–D bond. Comment.

Show solution
  1. (a) 8 diastereomers, 9 stereoisomers in all (cis/trans arrangements of the six Cl atoms; the α-isomer is chiral).
  2. (b) The ring attached to the nitrogen (the N lone pair still activates it, though less than in aniline; the C=O-bearing ring is deactivated by the carbonyl): para-nitration gives N-(4-nitrophenyl)benzamide.
  3. (c) Wrong: the CD3 bonds are not broken in ring nitration, so there is no primary isotope effect; the rate is essentially unchanged (a tiny secondary effect on hyperconjugation is not significant).
