7. Family 2: hydration of alkynes

7.1 Mercury(II)-catalysed (Kucherov) hydration Alkynes add H2O in dilute H2SO4 with HgSO4. Markovnikov addition of water gives an enol that tautomerises at once to the carbonyl compound. Ethyne gives ethanal (the only aldehyde obtained this way); every terminal alkyne gives a methyl ketone; an unsymmetrical internal alkyne gives a mixture of two ketones; a symmetrical internal alkyne gives one ketone.

7.2 Hydroboration–oxidation (anti-Markovnikov) A terminal alkyne reacts with a bulky borane (disiamylborane or 9-BBN; ordinary BH3 would add twice) at the terminal carbon; oxidation with H2O2/NaOH gives the enol and then the aldehyde RCH2CHO. This is the standard way to convert a terminal alkyne into an aldehyde with the same carbon number.

(i) Sia₂BH or 9-BBN (ii) H₂O₂, NaOH⟶
Pent-1-yne → pentanal (anti-Markovnikov); compare Hg²⁺/H₃O⁺ → pentan-2-one
Key idea Terminal alkyne: Hg2+/H3O+ → methyl ketone; R2BH then H2O2/OH− → aldehyde. The two reactions are complementary.
