6. Tautomerism: keto-enol and why the keto form usually wins

Tautomers are constitutional isomers that interconvert rapidly, usually by the migration of a hydrogen atom (a proton) together with a shift of a double bond. Unlike ordinary isomers, tautomers exist in a mobile equilibrium and normally cannot be separated. The commonest case is keto-enol tautomerism: a carbonyl compound with at least one hydrogen on the carbon next to the carbonyl (an α-hydrogen) is in equilibrium with its enol, a compound with OH on a C=C carbon.

Reactant:

Reagent: trace acid or base; rapid equilibrium

Product:

Propanone (keto form) and prop-1-en-2-ol (enol form). The equilibrium lies overwhelmingly on the keto side; the enol fraction is of the order of 10-8.

Why does the keto form dominate? Compare the bonds that change. Going from keto to enol, a C=O π bond and a C-H σ bond are traded for a C=C π bond and an O-H σ bond. The C=O π bond is much stronger than a C=C π bond, and this outweighs the gain from forming the stronger O-H bond in place of C-H, so the keto form is lower in energy by roughly 40-50 kJ mol-1 in simple aldehydes and ketones.

Enol content can rise greatly when something stabilises the enol. (i) Conjugation plus intramolecular hydrogen bonding: in pentane-2,4-dione (acetylacetone) the enol has a six-membered, hydrogen-bonded, conjugated chelate ring, and the enol form is about 80% of the neat liquid and higher in non-polar solvents. (ii) Aromaticity: the keto form of phenol (cyclohexa-2,4-dienone) would destroy the aromatic sextet, so phenol exists almost entirely as the enol. (iii) Two carbonyl groups flanking one CH2 (1,3-dicarbonyls such as ethyl acetoacetate) increase the acidity of that CH2 and the extent of enolisation.

Key idea. Keto-enol tautomerism requires an α-hydrogen. Benzaldehyde, formaldehyde, benzophenone and 2,2-dimethylpropanal have no α-hydrogen and cannot enolise. The tautomers differ in the position of an atom (H) as well as of electrons; resonance structures differ only in the position of electrons.
Trap. Tautomers are real, separate species in equilibrium and are connected by equilibrium arrows (⇌). Resonance forms are not real species and are connected by the double-headed arrow (↔). Do not interchange the two.
