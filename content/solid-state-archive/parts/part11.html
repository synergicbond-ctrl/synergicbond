Source pages: pp. 15–17

◆Concept

This part counts mirror planes, rotational axes and the centre of inversion of a cube, giving the classroom total of 23 geometrical symmetry elements.

1. Planes of Symmetry (POS): 9

◆Concept

Take a cube 0≤x,y,z≤1. There are 3 central rectangular mirror planes and 6 diagonal mirror planes.
  • Central planes: x=1/2, y=1/2, z=1/2.
  • Diagonal planes: x=y, x+y=1, y=z, y+z=1, z=x, z+x=1.

∑Formula

Total planes of symmetry = 9.

2. Axes of Symmetry (AOS): 13

Axis typeGeometrical lineCount
C₄ / tetradThrough centres of opposite faces3
C₃ / triadAlong body diagonals joining opposite corners4
C₂ / diadThrough midpoints of opposite parallel edges6

∑Formula

Total rotational axes = 13.

3. Centre of Symmetry

◆Concept

The inversion centre is the geometric centre of the cube: (1/2,1/2,1/2). Every point r relative to the centre maps to −r.

∑Formula

Elementary geometrical-element count used in the notes: 9 + 13 + 1 = 23.

4. Coordinate Blueprint

⚠JEE Advanced traps & edge cases

  • The “23” is an elementary count of geometrical symmetry elements used in the source, not the order (number of operations) of the full cubic point group.
  • A body diagonal joins two opposite corners, so 8 corners give 4 body diagonals, not 8.
  • A C₄ axis is counted as one axis even though 90°, 180° and 270° rotations are possible about it.

▶Solved examples

Worked example 1

Example 1 — Integer

How many C₃ axes does a cube possess?

  1. C₃ axes lie along body diagonals.
  2. 8 vertices pair into 4 opposite-vertex pairs.

4 C₃ axes.

Worked example 2

Example 2 — Total

Using the standard geometrical-element convention, find the total symmetry elements.

  1. Mirror planes = 9.
  2. Rotational axes = 13.
  3. Inversion centre = 1.

Total = 23.
