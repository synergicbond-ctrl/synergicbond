4. Nucleophilic substitution at sp3 carbon: the big picture

A nucleophilic substitution replaces a leaving group (LG, here X) on carbon by a nucleophile (Nu): R–X + Nu− → R–Nu + X−. Two limiting mechanisms were identified by Hughes and Ingold in the 1930s, differing in when the C–X bond breaks relative to the C–Nu bond forming:

SN2 (bimolecular)SN1 (unimolecular)
StepsOne concerted stepTwo (or more) steps; carbocation intermediate
Rate lawrate = k[RX][Nu]; second orderrate = k[RX]; first order, independent of [Nu]
Rate-determining stepNu attacks, X leaves simultaneouslyIonisation R–X → R+ + X−
StereochemistryComplete inversion (Walden inversion)Racemisation (often with slight excess of inversion)
Substrate orderCH3 > 1° > 2° ≫ 3°3° > 2° ≫ 1° (allyl, benzyl high)
NucleophileStrong, high concentrationWeak or neutral is fine
SolventPolar aprotic best (DMSO, DMF, acetone, MeCN)Polar protic best (H2O, ROH, HCOOH)
RearrangementNoneCommon (1,2-H, 1,2-alkyl shifts)
ΔS‡Negative (two particles become one ordered TS)Near zero or slightly positive
α-Deuterium effect kH/kD≈ 0.95–1.05≈ 1.10–1.25 (normal)

Real reactions lie on a continuum: a secondary substrate in a moderately ionising solvent with a moderate nucleophile can show mixed SN1/SN2 behaviour, with partial inversion and partial racemisation. In JEE problems, the conditions are chosen to place the reaction clearly at one end.
