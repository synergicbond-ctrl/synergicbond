Practice 35 Main

Rank SN2

Arrange in decreasing order of SN2 reactivity with NaI in acetone: (a) CH3CH2CH2Br (b) (CH3)2CHBr (c) CH3Br (d) (CH3)3CBr.

Show answer
  1. Steric hindrance at the α-carbon controls SN2: methyl > primary > secondary ≫ tertiary.
  2. (c) > (a) > (b) > (d).
Practice 36 Main

Rank SN1

Arrange in decreasing order of SN1 reactivity (solvolysis in aqueous ethanol): (a) CH3Br (b) CH3CH2Br (c) (CH3)2CHBr (d) (CH3)3CBr.

Show answer
  1. SN1 rate follows carbocation stability: 3° > 2° > 1° > methyl. (d) > (c) > (b) > (a). (For (a) and (b) the actual mechanism is SN2 with solvent, but the ordering remains.)
Practice 37 Main

Leaving group

Rank the rates of SN2 of ethyl X with a given nucleophile: X = Cl, Br, I, F, OTs.

Show answer
  1. A good leaving group is the conjugate base of a strong acid. Order: OTs ≈ I > Br > Cl ≫ F. (Tosylate is comparable with or better than iodide.)
Practice 38 Main

Rate law

The reaction CH3CH2Br + OH− → CH3CH2OH + Br− was followed at 25 °C. Initial rates: ([RBr], [OH−], rate) = (0.1, 0.1, 1.0 × 10−4), (0.2, 0.1, 2.0 × 10−4), (0.1, 0.2, 2.0 × 10−4). Write the rate law and calculate k.

Show answer
  1. Doubling [RBr] doubles the rate (first order). Doubling [OH−] doubles the rate (first order).
  2. rate = k[RBr][OH−]. k = 1.0 × 10−4/(0.1 × 0.1) = 1.0 × 10−2 L mol−1 s−1; second-order, so SN2.
Practice 39 Main

Rate law

For (CH3)3CBr + H2O the rate is found to be independent of the concentration of other nucleophiles and first order in the halide. If [RBr] is tripled, by what factor does the rate change? What if the volume of the solution is halved?

Show answer
  1. rate = k[RBr]. Tripling [RBr]: rate × 3. Halving the volume doubles [RBr]: rate × 2.
Practice 40 Main

Stereochemistry

(R)-2-Bromobutane reacts with NaSH in acetone/water by SN2. Give the configuration of the product butane-2-thiol.

Show answer
  1. Backside attack inverts the spatial arrangement. Priorities: Br > C2H5 > CH3 > H for the bromide; SH > C2H5 > CH3 > H for the thiol (S outranks C): same order. So inversion changes the letter: (S)-butane-2-thiol.
+ SN2⟶
(R)-2-Bromobutane gives (S)-butane-2-thiol: inversion.
Practice 41 Main

Stereochemistry

Optically active 2-bromooctane is converted into 2-iodooctane with NaI/acetone, and the product's rotation decreases with time and finally reaches zero. Explain.

Show answer
  1. The first SN2 step inverts the configuration. Iodide, however, is a good nucleophile and a good leaving group; the product 2-iodooctane undergoes further SN2 with iodide, which inverts again. Repeated inversion drives the sample to a 50:50 mixture of R and S: racemisation by successive SN2 reactions.
Practice 42 Main

Solvent effect

The reaction CH3I + Cl− → CH3Cl + I− is about 106 times faster in DMF than in methanol. Why?

Show answer
  1. In methanol Cl− is strongly solvated by hydrogen bonding to O–H, which stabilises it and lowers its nucleophilicity. DMF (polar aprotic) solvates Na+/K+ through the carbonyl oxygen but has no O–H or N–H to hydrogen-bond to the anion: the "naked" chloride is far more nucleophilic.
Practice 43 Main

Nucleophilicity

Arrange in decreasing nucleophilicity towards CH3Br in ethanol: I−, Br−, Cl−, F−. Why does the order reverse in DMSO?

Show answer
  1. In protic ethanol: I− > Br− > Cl− > F− (larger, more polarisable, less tightly solvated). In DMSO the anions are not hydrogen-bonded, so nucleophilicity follows basicity: F− > Cl− > Br− > I−.

Which reacts faster with CH3ONa/CH3OH by SN2? (a) CH3CH2CH2Cl or CH3CH2CH2I (b) CH3CH2CH2CH2Br or (CH3)2CHCH2Br (c) CH2=CH–CH2Br or CH3CH2CH2Br.

Practice 45 Main

Williamson synthesis

Suggest the preparation of (a) ethyl isopropyl ether (b) tert-butyl methyl ether (c) benzyl ethyl ether, giving the best alkoxide and halide.

Show answer
  1. The halide must be primary or methyl (SN2); the bulky group should be on the alkoxide.
  2. (a) (CH3)2CHONa + CH3CH2Br (the reverse, CH3CH2ONa + (CH3)2CHBr, gives much propene).
  3. (b) (CH3)3CONa + CH3I (not CH3ONa + (CH3)3CBr, which eliminates).
  4. (c) C6H5CH2Cl + C2H5ONa (or C6H5CH2ONa + C2H5Br): both are primary.
+ ⟶
Williamson synthesis of ethyl isopropyl ether: the bulky group is carried by the alkoxide.
Practice 46 Main

Chain extension

Starting from CH3CH2Br, how would you prepare (a) propanoic acid (b) propan-1-amine?

Show answer
  1. (a) CH3CH2Br + KCN → CH3CH2CN (propanenitrile); H3O+, heat → CH3CH2COOH.
  2. (b) CH3CH2CN + LiAlH4 (or H2/Ni) → CH3CH2CH2NH2. Both routes add one carbon (CnX → Cn+1).
Practice 47 Main

Amines from halides

Why does the reaction of ethyl bromide with ammonia not stop at ethylamine? How can pure primary amine be obtained?

Show answer
  1. Ethylamine is more nucleophilic than ammonia (electron-donating ethyl group), so it reacts further to give diethylamine, triethylamine and tetraethylammonium bromide. Use a large excess of NH3 to favour mono-alkylation, or better the Gabriel synthesis (potassium phthalimide, then hydrazine) or the azide route (NaN3, then LiAlH4).
Practice 48 Main

Gabriel synthesis

Write the Gabriel synthesis of butan-1-amine. Why does the method fail with chlorobenzene?

Show answer
  1. Potassium phthalimide + CH3CH2CH2CH2Br (SN2) → N-butylphthalimide; hydrazinolysis (or NaOH/H2O) → butylamine + phthalhydrazide. With chlorobenzene the SN2 does not occur (aryl halide, sp2 C–X): aromatic primary amines cannot be made by Gabriel's method. Also fails with 2° and 3° halides (elimination).
Practice 49 Main

Silver salts

Why does the reaction of CH3CH2Br with moist Ag2O give ethanol, with little ethene?

Show answer
  1. Moist Ag2O is AgOH: a source of OH− that is nucleophilic but much less basic in this form (insoluble), and Ag+ helps to pull out Br− (AgBr precipitates). The conditions favour substitution; strongly basic alcoholic KOH would give more elimination.
Practice 50 Main

Ambident

What is formed when ethyl bromide reacts with (a) KCN (b) AgCN (c) AgNO2 (d) KNO2? Which of (a)–(d) gives a foul-smelling product, and what is it called?

Show answer
  1. (a) C2H5CN, propanenitrile. (b) C2H5NC, ethyl isocyanide. (c) C2H5NO2, nitroethane. (d) C2H5ONO, ethyl nitrite.
  2. The foul-smelling compound is the isocyanide (isonitrile) from (b).
Practice 51 Main

Neighbouring group

(S)-2-Bromopropanoic acid is converted to lactic acid with Ag2O/H2O (retention), but to the opposite enantiomer with concentrated NaOH. Explain.

Show answer
  1. With Ag2O/H2O the carboxylate group attacks the α-carbon from the back, displacing Br− (first inversion) to form a strained α-lactone; water then attacks the α-carbon from the back (second inversion). Net retention.
  2. In concentrated NaOH the high concentration of OH− attacks directly by SN2 (single inversion), giving the enantiomer.
Practice 52 Main

SNi

What is the stereochemical outcome in the reaction of (S)-butan-2-ol with (a) SOCl2 alone (b) SOCl2/pyridine? Name the mechanisms.

Show answer
  1. (a) SNi (substitution nucleophilic internal): retention: (S)-2-chlorobutane (via the chlorosulfite and an intimate ion pair). (b) Pyridine intercepts HCl and releases free Cl− that attacks from the back (SN2): inversion: (R)-2-chlorobutane.
Practice 53 Main

Rearrangement

What is formed when neopentyl bromide, (CH3)3CCH2Br, is warmed with aqueous ethanol? Why is there no direct SN2 product?

Show answer
  1. Back-side attack is blocked by the three methyl groups on the β-carbon (neopentyl: practically inert to SN2). Ionisation is accompanied by a 1,2-methyl shift giving the tertiary cation (CH3)2C+CH2CH3, which gives 2-ethoxy-2-methylbutane, 2-methylbutan-2-ol and 2-methylbut-2-ene (major alkene).
Practice 54 Main

Allylic substitution

Hydrolysis of CH3CH=CHCH2Cl gives two isomeric alcohols. Name them and explain.

Show answer
  1. Ionisation gives the resonance-stabilised cation CH3CH=CH–CH2+ ↔ CH3CH+–CH=CH2. Water attacks at either terminus: but-2-en-1-ol (crotyl alcohol) and but-3-en-2-ol.

Practice 57 Main

Common ion

The solvolysis of benzhydryl chloride, (C6H5)2CHCl, in aqueous acetone is slowed when LiCl is added. Explain.

Show answer
  1. The rate-determining ionisation is reversible: R–Cl ⇌ R+ + Cl−. Added chloride (a common ion) pushes the equilibrium toward R–Cl and captures R+ before it reacts with water; hence the net reaction slows (mass-law effect). Observed for stable cations; indicative of SN1.
Practice 58 Main

Which gives a precipitate?

With alcoholic AgNO3, which compounds give an immediate precipitate at room temperature? (A) CH3CH2CH2Cl (B) (CH3)3CCl (C) C6H5Cl (D) C6H5CH2Cl (E) CH2=CHCl

Show answer
  1. Immediate precipitation indicates facile ionisation (stable carbocation): tertiary and benzylic. (A) needs heat; (C) and (E) never react. (B), (D).
Practice 59 Main

Tosylates

Why is the conversion of an alcohol to its tosylate (TsCl, pyridine) said to proceed with retention of configuration, and what is the stereochemical result of then treating the tosylate of (R)-butan-2-ol with NaBr in DMF?

Show answer
  1. In ROH + TsCl the oxygen attacks sulfur; the C–O bond of the alcohol is never broken, so the configuration at carbon is unchanged (retention). The tosylate is then displaced by Br− in SN2: inversion, so (R)-butan-2-ol gives (S)-2-bromobutane. Overall: one inversion.

Intramolecular

Predict the product when (A) ClCH2CH2CH2CH2OH (4-chlorobutan-1-ol) and (B) ClCH2CH2OH are heated with NaOH. Name the cyclic products.

Show answer
  1. Intramolecular SN2 by the alkoxide. (A) Five-membered ring: tetrahydrofuran (oxolane). (B) Three-membered ring: ethylene oxide (oxirane).
Practice 61 Main

Intramolecular

Why does 2-chloroethanol with aqueous NaOH give mainly ethylene oxide rather than ethylene glycol, while chloroethane gives ethanol?

Show answer
  1. In the alkoxide of 2-chloroethanol, the nucleophile O− and the leaving group Cl are on the same molecule; the intramolecular reaction (3-exo-tet) is faster than the intermolecular attack by OH− (effective molarity ≫ [OH−]); ethylene glycol results only if the epoxide is subsequently opened by aqueous hydroxide.

tert-Butyl bromide is treated with NaCN in DMSO. What is the major product?

Practice 66 Main

Exchange

Why does an optically active 2-iodobutane racemise on standing with NaI in acetone, but 2-chlorobutane under similar conditions with NaCl (acetone) does not noticeably?

Show answer
  1. Iodide is a much better nucleophile and leaving group, so repeated SN2 inversions occur at a measurable rate. NaCl is insoluble in acetone (very low [Cl−]) and Cl− is a poorer nucleophile; the exchange is far slower.
Practice 67 Main

Product of epoxide-forming reaction

Predict the product of trans-2-chlorocyclohexanol with NaOH. Why does cis-2-chlorocyclohexanol not give the same product?

Show answer
  1. The alkoxide must attack the carbon bearing Cl from the back, anti to the leaving group. In the trans isomer, OH and Cl can be both axial (trans-diaxial): the intramolecular SN2 gives cyclohexene oxide (7-oxabicyclo[4.1.0]heptane). In the cis isomer, O− and Cl are always gauche (one axial, one equatorial), so back-side attack is geometrically impossible and the epoxide does not form (the ketone or other products form instead).
