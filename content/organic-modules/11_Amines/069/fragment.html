Practice 76 L1 Aniline

Why does direct nitration of aniline with HNO3/H2SO4 give a considerable amount of m-nitroaniline? How is p-nitroaniline obtained pure?

Show answer
  1. Also, HNO3 oxidises free aniline. The acetamido group is o/p-directing, is not protonated, and its bulk favours para.

In the acid medium aniline is protonated to anilinium (–NH3+), a meta-directing group (≈ 47 % meta). Pure p-nitroaniline: acetylate (acetanilide) → nitrate → hydrolyse.

Practice 77 L1 Aniline

Why does aniline not undergo Friedel–Crafts alkylation or acylation?

Show answer

The basic –NH2 group reacts with the Lewis acid AlCl3 (an acid–base adduct), giving a positively charged N that strongly deactivates the ring and consumes the catalyst.

Practice 78 L1 Aniline

What happens when aniline is heated with conc. H2SO4 at 453–473 K? Write the structure of the product and explain its high melting point.

sulphanilic acid (zwitterion)
Show answer

p-Aminobenzenesulphonic acid (sulphanilic acid). It exists as a dipolar ion (H3N+–C6H4–SO3−), an ionic lattice with a very high melting point and low solubility in organic solvents.

Practice 79 L1 Aniline

Outline the conversion of aniline to p-bromoaniline.

Show answer
  1. Direct bromination of aniline would give 2,4,6-tribromoaniline; the acetyl group reduces the activation and is removed at the end.

Aniline → (CH3CO)2O → acetanilide → Br2/CH3COOH → p-bromoacetanilide → H3O+/heat (or NaOH) → p-bromoaniline.

Practice 80 L1 Aniline

How is benzene converted into aniline in two steps?

Show answer

Benzene → conc. HNO3/conc. H2SO4, 323–333 K → nitrobenzene → Sn/HCl (or Fe/HCl, or H2/Pd), then NaOH → aniline.

Practice 81 L1 Diazotisation

Write the equation for the diazotisation of aniline and state (i) the temperature, (ii) why it must be maintained.

Show answer

C6H5NH2 + NaNO2 + 2HCl → C6H5N2+Cl− + NaCl + 2H2O at 273–278 K. Above about 283 K the diazonium salt decomposes: C6H5N2+ + H2O → C6H5OH + N2 + H+.

Practice 82 L1 Diazonium

Give the product of benzenediazonium chloride with (a) CuCl/HCl, (b) CuBr/HBr, (c) CuCN/KCN, (d) KI, (e) HBF4 then heat, (f) H3PO2, (g) H2O warm.

Show answer
  1. (a)–(c) are Sandmeyer reactions; (e) is Balz–Schiemann; (f) is deamination.

(a) chlorobenzene, (b) bromobenzene, (c) benzonitrile, (d) iodobenzene, (e) fluorobenzene, (f) benzene, (g) phenol.

Practice 83 L1 Coupling

Write the product of coupling of benzenediazonium chloride with phenol and state the pH required.

4-hydroxyazobenzene
Show answer

4-Hydroxyazobenzene (p-hydroxyazobenzene), an orange dye; pH 9–10 (alkaline), where the phenoxide ion is the reactive coupling partner.

Practice 84 L1 Coupling

What is the product of benzenediazonium chloride + β-naphthol in NaOH at 273 K? State its colour.

1-phenylazo-2-naphthol
Show answer

1-(Phenylazo)-2-naphthol, an orange-red dye (Sudan I). The coupling position is C1 (alpha, ortho to the OH).

Practice 85 L1 Coupling

Why is coupling with aniline carried out at pH 4–5 and not in strong acid or alkali?

Show answer

In strong acid aniline is protonated (no lone pair, no activation); in alkali the diazonium ion is converted into the inactive diazotate ion. Weak acid keeps free aniline and the diazonium ion.

Practice 86 L1 Diazonium

Aniline is converted to benzoic acid via benzonitrile. Give the reagents of each step.

Show answer
  1. The CuCN step adds one carbon to the ring system.

Aniline → NaNO2/HCl, 273 K → PhN2+Cl− → CuCN/KCN → PhCN → H3O+, heat → PhCOOH.

Practice 87 L1 Diazonium

How would you convert aniline into (a) phenol, (b) benzene, (c) fluorobenzene?

Show answer

(a) NaNO2/HCl, 273 K, then warm H2O; (b) diazotise, then H3PO2; (c) diazotise with HBF4, isolate ArN2+BF4−, heat.

Practice 88 L1 Diazonium

What is the purpose of starch–iodide paper in diazotisation?

Show answer

It detects excess nitrous acid (nitrite oxidises iodide to iodine → blue-black starch complex); the endpoint of diazotisation is a faint persistent positive test. Excess HNO2 is then destroyed with urea or sulfamic acid before coupling.

Practice 89 L1 Diazonium

Why are benzenediazonium salts more stable than alkyl diazonium salts?

Show answer

Resonance: the π electrons of the ring delocalise into the N≡N+ group, giving partial C=N character; moreover loss of N2 would produce an unstable aryl cation. Alkyl diazonium ions lose N2 instantly to give carbocations.

Practice 90 L1 Dye

Name the dye obtained by coupling diazotised sulphanilic acid with N,N-dimethylaniline and give the colour in acid and alkali.

Show answer

Methyl orange; red in acid, yellow-orange in alkali/neutral (indicator range pH 3.1–4.4).

Practice 91 L1 Synthesis

Show how to prepare 1,3,5-tribromobenzene from aniline.

Show answer
  1. Direct bromination of benzene cannot give this isomer; the NH2 group directs all three Br atoms and is then removed by deamination.

Aniline → Br2/H2O → 2,4,6-tribromoaniline → NaNO2/HCl, 273 K → diazonium salt → H3PO2 → 1,3,5-tribromobenzene.

Practice 92 L1 Nitro

Nitromethane dissolves in aqueous NaOH. Why? Write the structure of the ion formed.

nitronate (aci-nitro) anion
Show answer

The α-H of nitromethane is acidic (pKa 10.2) because the conjugate base (the nitronate ion CH2=N+(O−)O−) is resonance stabilised with the charge on oxygen.

Practice 93 L1 Nitro

Write the conditions of conversion of benzene into m-dinitrobenzene.

Show answer
  1. The first nitro group deactivates the ring and directs meta; harsher conditions are needed.

Benzene → conc. HNO3/conc. H2SO4, 323–333 K → nitrobenzene → fuming HNO3/conc. H2SO4, 373 K → m-dinitrobenzene.

Practice 94 L1 Nitro

What are the products of the reaction of ethyl bromide with (a) AgNO2, (b) KNO2?

Show answer
  1. AgNO2 is covalent (N-attack); KNO2 is ionic (O-attack).

(a) nitroethane (CH3CH2NO2), (b) mainly ethyl nitrite (CH3CH2ONO).

Practice 95 L1 Nitro

What do 1° and 2° nitroalkanes give with HNO2/NaOH?

Show answer

1°: nitrolic acid (red solution in NaOH); 2°: pseudonitrole (blue colour); 3°: no reaction.

Practice 96 L1 SNAr

Why is p-chloronitrobenzene much more reactive toward aqueous NaOH than chlorobenzene? Write the product.

Show answer

The o/p nitro group stabilises the anionic intermediate (Meisenheimer complex) by resonance and −I effects. Product: p-nitrophenol (after acidification).

Practice 97 L1 Cyanide

Which is formed from CH3Br with (a) KCN, (b) AgCN? Name both products.

Show answer

(a) CH3CN (acetonitrile, ethanenitrile); (b) CH3NC (methyl isocyanide), the major product with covalent AgCN.

Practice 98 L1 Isocyanide

Write the products of the acid hydrolysis of (a) CH3CN, (b) CH3NC.

Show answer
  1. Isocyanides are stable to alkali but hydrolysed by dilute acid; the carbon attached to N becomes the formic acid carbon.

(a) CH3COOH + NH4+; (b) CH3NH2 + HCOOH.

Practice 99 L1 Isocyanide

What is the product of catalytic reduction (H2/Ni) of (a) CH3CN, (b) CH3NC?

Show answer
  1. The nitrile carbon becomes CH2; the isocyanide carbon becomes a methyl on N.

(a) CH3CH2NH2 (a 1° amine, ethylamine); (b) (CH3)2NH (a 2° amine, dimethylamine).

Practice 100 L1 Cyanide

How can ethanamide be converted into ethanenitrile? What is the one-carbon difference route to methylamine from the amide?

Show answer

Ethanamide + P2O5 (or SOCl2, POCl3) heat → CH3CN (dehydration). Ethanamide + Br2/KOH → CH3NH2 (Hofmann: loss of one C).

Practice 101 L1 Nitrile

Give the product of CH3CN with (a) CH3MgBr then H3O+, (b) H2O2/NaOH.

Show answer
  1. Grignard adds once to the nitrile carbon giving the imine salt (no further addition), which hydrolyses to the ketone.

(a) acetone (CH3COCH3); (b) acetamide (partial hydrolysis to the amide).

Practice 102 L1 Mixed

Which is formed when C6H5N2+Cl− is treated with SnCl2/HCl?

phenylhydrazine
Show answer

Phenylhydrazine, C6H5NHNH2 (reduction with retention of both N atoms).

Practice 103 L1 Mixed

Arrange in increasing order of acidic strength: phenol, o-nitrophenol, 2,4-dinitrophenol, picric acid.

Show answer
  1. Each nitro group at ortho/para delocalises the phenoxide charge.

Phenol < o-nitrophenol < 2,4-dinitrophenol < picric acid (pKa 10.0, 7.2, 4.1, 0.4).
