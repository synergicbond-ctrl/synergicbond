12. Relationships between stereoisomers

12.1 The four possible relationships

Take any two molecules with the same molecular formula. Ask the questions in order:

  • 1. Same connectivity? If not, they are constitutional (structural) isomers.
  • 2. Same connectivity: superimposable (after rotations about single bonds)? If yes, they are the same compound (identical).
  • 3. Not superimposable: are they mirror images? If yes: enantiomers.
  • 4. Not mirror images: diastereomers (including cis/trans isomers, epimers, anomers, meso versus chiral forms).
Key idea Enantiomers have opposite descriptors at every stereogenic unit. Diastereomers have the opposite descriptor at some but not all units. Identical compounds have the same descriptors at all units (or the descriptor set maps to itself by symmetry, as for meso forms).
(2R,3R)
(2S,3S)
meso (2R,3S)

The first and second are mirror images (every label inverted): enantiomers. The meso form is a diastereomer of each of them.

12.2 Turning the rule into an algorithm

For two isomers with n stereocentres and the same ordering of centres:

  • All n labels differ → enantiomers (unless the molecule is meso, in which case inversion gives the same compound).
  • Some but not all differ → diastereomers.
  • None differ → identical.
  • If the two molecules have different numbers of labels, or the numbering of equivalent centres is ambiguous (symmetrical molecules), compare after picking the numbering that maps one onto the other.
Trap In a symmetrical molecule the descriptor set (R,S) and (S,R) describe the same meso compound (renumber from the other end), whereas (R,R) and (S,S) are different. Never conclude "enantiomers" for (2R,3S) versus (2S,3R) of a symmetrical compound: they are identical.
meso (R,S) (S,S) (S,S) (R,R) (R,R)

12.3 The vocabulary of diastereomers

  • Epimers: diastereomers differing at exactly one stereocentre (D-glucose / D-mannose, C-2 epimers; D-glucose / D-galactose, C-4 epimers).
  • Anomers: epimers differing at the anomeric (hemiacetal) carbon of a cyclic sugar (α- and β-D-glucopyranose).
  • Meso compound: an achiral stereoisomer that has stereocentres and an internal mirror plane (or inversion centre).
  • Geometrical (cis/trans, E/Z) isomers: diastereomers.
  • Racemic mixture: an equimolar mixture of two enantiomers, not a single compound.
Example 38 Main

Epimers or enantiomers?

Name the relationship: (a) D-glucose and L-glucose; (b) D-glucose and D-mannose; (c) D-glucose and D-galactose; (d) D-glucose and D-fructose; (e) (R)-CHFClBr and (S)-CHFClBr.

Show solution
  1. (a) All four centres inverted: enantiomers. (b) C-2 only: C-2 epimers (diastereomers). (c) C-4 only: C-4 epimers. (d) Different functional groups (aldose versus ketose, C6H12O6 both): constitutional (functional group) isomers. (e) Enantiomers.
Example 39 Adv

Relationship pairs by computer-checked drawing

State the relationship of each pair: (i) (2S,3S)- and (2R,3S)-2-bromo-3-chlorobutane; (ii) (R,R)- and (S,S)-tartaric acid; (iii) (R,R)-tartaric acid and meso-tartaric acid.

Show solution
  1. (i) Only one of the two centres is inverted: diastereomers. (ii) Every centre inverted: enantiomers. (iii) The meso form is neither the same as nor the mirror image of (R,R): diastereomers.
