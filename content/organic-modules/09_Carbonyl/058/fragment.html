49. β-Keto acids, decarboxylation and the acetoacetic ester synthesis

A carboxylic acid with a C=O group β to the carboxyl (a β-keto acid) loses CO2 on gentle heating (about 373–423 K). The reaction goes through a six-membered cyclic transition state in which the carboxyl O–H hydrogen is transferred to the ketone oxygen as the C–C bond breaks; the immediate product is an enol, which tautomerises to the ketone. The same applies to malonic acids (the second carboxyl plays the role of the carbonyl).

Δ, −CO₂⟶ tautomerism⟶
3-Oxobutanoic acid → propanone. The enol is the first product
Key idea The geometry requirement is that the C=O and the C–COOH bond can be aligned so that the enol C=C can form. In a bicyclic β-keto acid whose carboxyl group sits on a bridgehead, the enol would need a double bond at the bridgehead (anti-Bredt) and decarboxylation does not occur under mild conditions.
2-oxocyclohexane-1-carboxylic acid (decarboxylates) 3-oxobutanoic acid 2-oxobicyclo[2.2.2]octane-1-carboxylic acid (resists)
A β-keto acid with its carboxyl on a bridgehead cannot form the bridgehead enol, so it does not lose CO₂ easily

Acetoacetic ester synthesis. Ethyl 3-oxobutanoate (pKa of the central CH2 about 11) is converted into its enolate by NaOEt; an alkyl halide alkylates the central carbon; hydrolysis of the ester and decarboxylation give a methyl ketone CH3COCH2R. A second alkylation before hydrolysis gives CH3COCHRR′.

(i) NaOEt (ii) CH₃CH₂Br (iii) NaOH(aq), then H₃O⁺, Δ⟶
Ethyl acetoacetate → pentan-2-one (the new carbon chain is CH₃CO–CH₂–CH₂CH₃)
(i) NaOEt, CH₃I (ii) NaOEt, CH₃CH₂Br (iii) NaOH, then H₃O⁺, Δ⟶
Two different alkyl groups: 3-methylpentan-2-one
Shortcut Reading an acetoacetic-ester product: the ketone is always CH3CO–CH(R)(R′): the CH3CO– group and the α-carbon come from ethyl acetoacetate; R and R′ come from the halides. Count carbons accordingly. With concentrated alkali, cleavage ("acid hydrolysis") instead gives two carboxylic acids, CH3COOH and RR′CHCOOH.
Malonic ester synthesis (the same idea for acids): diethyl malonate is alkylated, then hydrolysed and decarboxylated to RCH2COOH.
Example 50 Adv

Acetoacetic ester

Using ethyl acetoacetate, outline syntheses of (a) butanone, (b) hexan-2-one, (c) 3-methylbutan-2-one. Why is route (c) not done with isopropyl bromide?

Show solution
  1. The product is always CH3CO–CH(R)(R′): the acetyl group and the α-carbon come from ethyl acetoacetate, R and R′ from the alkyl halides.
  2. (a) One methylation (CH3I) then hydrolysis/decarboxylation: CH3COCH2CH3, butanone.
  3. (b) Hexan-2-one is CH3CO–CH2–CH2CH2CH3: R = n-propyl, so alkylate once with 1-bromopropane.
  4. (c) 3-Methylbutan-2-one is CH3CO–CH(CH3)2: two methyl groups on the α-carbon, so methylate twice (CH3I, NaOEt, then again) before hydrolysis. Isopropyl bromide is a secondary halide, and the enolate is also a base: elimination to propene competes strongly with SN2.
(i) NaOEt, n-C₃H₇Br (ii) NaOH, H₃O⁺, Δ⟶
(i) NaOEt, CH₃I (ii) NaOEt, CH₃I (iii) NaOH, H₃O⁺, Δ⟶
Example 51 Adv

Decarboxylation

Which of these β-keto acids lose CO2 readily on heating: (a) 3-oxopentanoic acid, (b) 2-oxocyclopentane-1-carboxylic acid, (c) 2-oxobicyclo[2.2.2]octane-1-carboxylic acid, (d) 2,2-dimethyl-3-oxobutanoic acid?

Show solution
  1. (a) Yes (gives butanone). (b) Yes (gives cyclopentanone). (c) No: the carboxyl is at a bridgehead; the enol would place C=C at a bridgehead of a small bicycle. (d) Yes: the six-membered transition state does not need an α-hydrogen on the acid carbon; the product is 3-methylbutan-2-one, (CH3)2CH–COCH3.
  2. Answer: a, b, d.
(a) (b) (c) (d)
