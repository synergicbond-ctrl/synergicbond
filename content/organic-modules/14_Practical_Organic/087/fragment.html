Practice 1 Foundation · MCQ

A mixture of naphthalene and sodium chloride is best separated by: (A) crystallisation from water (B) sublimation (C) steam distillation (D) distillation under reduced pressure

Show answer
  1. Naphthalene sublimes on gentle heating; NaCl does not. The vapour condenses on a cold surface as pure naphthalene crystals.
  2. Crystallisation from water would dissolve NaCl but naphthalene is insoluble; this also works as a filtration/wash but the standard answer for a solid mixture of this kind is sublimation.

Answer: (B)

Practice 2 Foundation · multi-correct

Which of the following can be purified by steam distillation? (A) aniline (B) o-nitrophenol (C) p-nitrophenol (D) nitrobenzene (E) glycerol

Show answer
  1. Steam distillation needs a liquid (or low-melting solid) that is immiscible with water and steam-volatile.
  2. Aniline, nitrobenzene and o-nitrophenol (intramolecular H-bond, hence volatile) qualify. p-Nitrophenol is held by intermolecular H-bonds and is not volatile in steam. Glycerol is miscible with water and decomposes: it is purified by vacuum distillation.

Answer: A, B, D

Practice 3 Foundation

In TLC the solvent front moved 12.0 cm and a compound moved 3.0 cm from the origin. Calculate Rf.

Show answer
  1. Rf = 3.0/12.0 = 0.25.

Answer: 0.25

Practice 4 Foundation

The Rf of a dye in a given solvent on silica is 0.40. How far does the dye travel when the solvent front has moved 7.5 cm?

Show answer
  1. Distance = Rf × (front distance) = 0.40 × 7.5 = 3.0 cm.

Answer: 3.0 cm

Practice 5 Application · integer

On a TLC plate a 10 cm run separates compounds P and Q whose Rf values are 0.35 and 0.55. What is the distance in mm between the centres of the two spots?

Show answer
  1. P: 0.35 × 10 = 3.5 cm; Q: 0.55 × 10 = 5.5 cm.
  2. Separation = 2.0 cm = 20 mm.

Answer: 20

Practice 6 Foundation

100 mL of an aqueous solution contains 10.0 g of a solute (K = Cether/Cwater = 4). Find the mass left in the aqueous layer after shaking with (a) 100 mL ether once; (b) 2 × 50 mL ether.

Show answer
  1. (a) Fraction left = 100/(100 + 4 × 100) = 0.200 ⇒ 2.00 g.
  2. (b) Per extraction: 100/(100 + 4 × 50) = 0.3333; two extractions: 0.1111 ⇒ 1.111 g.

Answer: (a) 2.00 g; (b) 1.11 g

Practice 7 Application · integer

A solute has K = Corg/Caq = 2.5. How many extractions with 25 mL portions of the organic solvent are needed to remove at least 99 % of the solute from 100 mL of its aqueous solution?

Show answer
  1. Fraction left per extraction = 100/(100 + 2.5 × 25) = 0.6154.
  2. Need (0.6154)n ≤ 0.01: n ≥ ln 0.01/ln 0.6154 = 9.49, so n = 10.
  3. Check: n = 10 leaves 0.0078; n = 9 leaves 0.0127.

Answer: 10

Practice 8 Application

Chlorobenzene (M = 112.5) steam-distils at 363 K (90 °C) under 760 mm Hg; the vapour pressure of water at 363 K is 526 mm Hg. Calculate (a) the vapour pressure of chlorobenzene at this temperature, (b) the mass of chlorobenzene collected per gram of water, (c) the mole fraction of chlorobenzene in the vapour.

Show answer
  1. (a) porg = 760 − 526 = 234 mm Hg.
  2. (b) worg/ww = (234 × 112.5)/(526 × 18) = 2.78 g per g water.
  3. (c) Mole fraction in vapour = porg/P = 234/760 = 0.308.

Answer: (a) 234 mm Hg; (b) 2.78 g; (c) 0.308

Practice 9 Application

Using the data of the previous problem, what mass of water must be evaporated to carry over 20.0 g of chlorobenzene?

Show answer
  1. 1 g water carries 2.78 g chlorobenzene.
  2. Water needed = 20.0/2.78 = 7.2 g.

Answer: 7.2 g

Practice 10 Application

KNO3 (50.0 g) is contaminated with 5.0 g NaCl. It is dissolved in the minimum amount of boiling water (solubility of KNO3 at 373 K: 246 g per 100 g water) and cooled to 273 K (KNO3: 13.3 g per 100 g water; NaCl: 35.7 g per 100 g). Calculate the mass of pure KNO3 crystals obtained, and show that NaCl stays in solution.

Show answer
  1. Water needed = 50.0 × 100/246 = 20.3 g.
  2. At 273 K this water holds 2.70 g KNO3, so crystals = 50.0 − 2.70 = 47.3 g (recovery 94.6 %).
  3. NaCl: this water can dissolve 7.26 g at 273 K > 5.0 g, so all NaCl stays in the mother liquor.

Answer: 47.3 g

Practice 11 Foundation · MCQ

Which statement about a TLC experiment is incorrect? (A) The origin line is drawn in pencil. (B) The solvent level in the jar is below the origin. (C) Rf can be greater than 1 if the compound is very polar. (D) A more polar eluent usually increases Rf values on silica.

Show answer
  1. Rf = distance travelled by compound / distance travelled by solvent front; the compound cannot overtake the solvent, so Rf ≤ 1 always. (A), (B), (D) are correct practice.

Answer: (C)

Practice 12 Foundation · MCQ

In paper chromatography of amino acids the stationary phase is: (A) the cellulose fibres (B) water held on cellulose (C) the organic solvent (D) silica gel

Show answer
  1. Cellulose adsorbs about 20 % water; this bound water is the stationary liquid and the separation is by partition between water and the moving organic phase.

Answer: (B)

Practice 13 Foundation · MCQ

Glycerol is purified by distillation under reduced pressure because: (A) it is immiscible with water (B) it decomposes at its normal boiling point (C) it is volatile in steam (D) it forms an azeotrope with water

Show answer
  1. Under reduced pressure it boils at a much lower temperature than its normal b.p. (563 K) at which it decomposes.

Answer: (B)

Practice 14 Application

Four compounds: toluene, acetophenone, benzyl alcohol, benzoic acid are run on silica gel with 9 : 1 hexane–ethyl acetate. Arrange them in decreasing Rf and give the reason.

Show answer
  1. Silica is polar; polar compounds are held more strongly and move less.
  2. Polarity increases: toluene (hydrocarbon) < acetophenone (ketone, H-bond acceptor) < benzyl alcohol (H-bond donor and acceptor) < benzoic acid (strongest H-bonding; also tends to streak).

Answer: toluene > acetophenone > benzyl alcohol > benzoic acid

Practice 15 Application · matching

Match the mixture (P to S) with the separation method (1 to 4). P: camphor + sand; Q: aniline + tar; R: acetone + methanol (treat as an ideal mixture); S: KNO3 + NaCl. 1: fractional crystallisation; 2: steam distillation; 3: sublimation; 4: fractional distillation.

Show answer
  1. Camphor sublimes, sand does not: 3. Aniline is steam-volatile, tar is not: 2. Acetone (329 K) and methanol (338 K) have close b.p.: 4. Two salts of very different temperature coefficient of solubility: 1.

Answer: P–3, Q–2, R–4, S–1

Practice 16 Advanced

Benzoic acid is distributed between benzene and water. It exists as a monomer in water and as a dimer in benzene. When Cbenzene = 0.200 M the aqueous concentration is 0.0100 M. (a) Show that the constant is K = Caq2/Cbenzene. (b) Predict Caq when Cbenzene = 0.800 M.

Show answer
  1. (a) In benzene 2A ⇌ A2; the monomer concentration is proportional to √Cbenzene (nearly all solute is dimer). Distribution of the monomer: Caq/[A]benzene = constant ⇒ Caq ∝ √Cbenzene ⇒ Caq2/Cbenzene = K.
  2. K = (0.0100)2/0.200 = 5.0 × 10−4.
  3. (b) Caq = √(K × 0.800) = √(5.0 × 10−4 × 0.800) = 0.0200 M.

Answer: Caq = 0.020 M (quadrupling Cbenzene only doubles Caq)

Practice 17 Application

A mixture of two immiscible liquids, A (M = 78) and water, boils at 342 K at 760 mm Hg. If p°water = 224 mm Hg at 342 K, find (a) the vapour pressure of A, (b) the ratio of masses of A to water in the distillate.

Show answer
  1. (a) pA = 760 − 224 = 536 mm Hg.
  2. (b) wA/ww = (536 × 78)/(224 × 18) = 10.37.

Answer: (a) 536 mm Hg; (b) 10.37

Practice 18 Foundation

Why must the origin line in TLC or paper chromatography be drawn with a pencil rather than a pen?

Show answer
  1. Ink contains dyes that dissolve in the eluent and would themselves run up the plate, giving spurious spots. Graphite is insoluble and inert.

Answer: ink dyes move with the solvent

Practice 19 Application · multi-correct

Which statements about fractional distillation are correct? (A) It is used when the b.p. of two miscible liquids differ little. (B) Each theoretical plate in the column corresponds to one vaporisation–condensation cycle. (C) A mixture forming an azeotrope can be completely separated into pure components by repeated fractionation. (D) The vapour reaching the top of the column is richer in the lower-boiling component.

Show answer
  1. (A), (B) and (D) are correct. (C) is false: at the azeotropic composition liquid and vapour have the same composition, so no further enrichment is possible.

Answer: A, B, D

Practice 20 Application

An organic liquid X (M = 123, b.p. 484 K, insoluble in water) is steam-distilled at 372.5 K. At that temperature p°water = 733 mm Hg. Calculate the mass ratio X : water in the distillate, and comment.

Show answer
  1. pX = 760 − 733 = 27 mm Hg.
  2. wX/ww = (27 × 123)/(733 × 18) = 0.252 g per g water.
  3. Only about a quarter of a gram of X per gram of water: although the process is mild, it needs a lot of steam; this is characteristic of compounds with low vapour pressure at 373 K (here nitrobenzene).

Answer: 0.25

Practice 21 Application · integer

How many of the following are purified by sublimation? naphthalene, camphor, anthracene, benzoic acid, NaCl, iodine, NH4Cl, sucrose

Show answer
  1. Sublimable solids: naphthalene, camphor, anthracene, benzoic acid, iodine, NH4Cl: six. NaCl and sucrose (decomposes) do not sublime.

Answer: 6

Practice 22 Foundation · MCQ

Mother liquor is: (A) the solvent used for washing the crystals (B) the filtrate left after crystals are separated, containing soluble impurities (C) the first fraction of a distillation (D) the eluate in column chromatography

Show answer
  1. The saturated solution left after the crystals have formed.

Answer: (B)

Practice 23 Application

A student recrystallises 2.00 g of impure benzoic acid (1.80 g pure) from 30 mL of water. Benzoic acid dissolves to the extent of 0.17 g per 100 mL at 273 K and 6.8 g per 100 mL at 368 K. (a) Is 30 mL enough to dissolve the benzoic acid at 368 K? (b) Maximum yield on cooling to 273 K?

Show answer
  1. (a) Capacity at 368 K = 30 × 6.8/100 = 2.04 g ≥ 1.80 g: yes.
  2. (b) Left in solution at 273 K = 30 × 0.17/100 = 0.051 g; crystals ≤ 1.80 − 0.051 = 1.75 g; recovery 97.2 %.

Answer: (a) yes; (b) 1.75 g

Practice 24 Advanced · paragraph

A student is given a brown, oily mixture from the nitration of toluene containing o-nitrotoluene, p-nitrotoluene, unreacted toluene and acidic tar. State a logical sequence of purification steps and the principle of each.

Show answer
  1. Wash with aq. NaHCO3/NaOH: removes acidic tar (as salts) and residual acid.
  2. Distil off toluene (b.p. 384 K; far below the nitrotoluenes, b.p. 495 K and 511 K): simple distillation.
  3. The nitrotoluenes (o-: liquid, m.p. 264 K; p-: solid, m.p. 325 K) can be separated by cooling: p-nitrotoluene crystallises first (fractional crystallisation), or by fractional distillation/vacuum distillation since the b.p. differ by about 16 K.
  4. Final purity check: sharp m.p. of p-nitrotoluene (325 K) and a single TLC spot.

Answer: wash → distil → crystallise

Practice 25 Application · MCQ

Two compounds have Rf 0.62 and 0.58 on silica in hexane–ethyl acetate 4 : 1. Which change is most likely to improve resolution? (A) a more polar solvent mixture (B) a less polar solvent mixture (C) heating the plate (D) a thicker spot

Show answer
  1. Both spots are near the middle; lowering the eluent polarity moves both to lower Rf and typically increases the difference in retention. A more polar eluent would push both towards the front, compressing differences. (D) would worsen resolution.

Answer: (B)

Practice 26 Foundation

State two differences between adsorption (column) chromatography and partition (paper) chromatography.

Show answer
  1. Adsorption: stationary phase is a solid (silica, alumina) and separation depends on differential adsorption; partition: stationary phase is a liquid held on a solid support (water on cellulose) and separation depends on relative solubility in the two liquids.
