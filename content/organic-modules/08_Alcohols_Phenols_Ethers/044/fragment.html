8. Oxidation of alcohols

Oxidation of an alcohol removes the hydrogen on the carbinol carbon (the α-H) together with the O–H hydrogen. The result depends on how many α-hydrogens the alcohol has.

  • Primary (two α-H): RCH2OH → RCHO → RCOOH. The aldehyde is an intermediate and can be isolated only with a reagent that does not oxidise aldehydes (anhydrous conditions, so no aldehyde hydrate) or by removing the aldehyde as it forms.
  • Secondary (one α-H): R2CHOH → R2C=O. The ketone resists further oxidation under normal conditions.
  • Tertiary (no α-H): no oxidation under mild conditions. Under forcing conditions (hot acidic KMnO4 or K2Cr2O7) dehydration to an alkene occurs first, then C=C cleavage gives a mixture of ketones and acids with fewer carbons.
