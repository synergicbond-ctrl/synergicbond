6. Polymerisation of dienes and stereochemistry of the unit

A conjugated diene such as buta-1,3-diene or isoprene can add in three ways: 1,2- (vinyl pendant), 3,4- (isoprene only) or 1,4-. In 1,4-addition one C=C is left in the chain between the new σ bonds, and it can be cis (Z) or trans (E).

buta-1,3-diene cis-1,4-polybutadiene unit trans-1,4-polybutadiene unit 1,2-polybutadiene (vinyl) unit
1,4-addition leaves an in-chain C=C (cis or trans); 1,2-addition leaves a pendant vinyl group.
isoprene cis-1,4-polyisoprene (natural rubber) trans-1,4-polyisoprene (gutta-percha, balata)
Same constitution, different geometry, very different properties.
IMP Natural rubber is cis-1,4-polyisoprene (Hevea brasiliensis latex), M ≈ 105–106; gutta-percha/balata is the trans isomer: hard, non-elastic (golf-ball covers, cable insulation, dental fillings). Ziegler–Natta (TiCl4 + AlR3) or Li-based catalysts make synthetic cis-polyisoprene, essentially identical to natural rubber.

Why does cis give a rubber but trans a hard plastic? The cis chain is kinked at every double bond, so chains cannot align and crystallise; they stay coiled and amorphous, with only weak van der Waals forces, and snap back when stress is removed. The trans chain is nearly straight, packs into crystallites, and is stiff.

Exam Trap Natural rubber is not made by free-radical addition of isoprene in the laboratory. Radical polymerisation of isoprene gives a mixture of 1,4-cis, 1,4-trans, 1,2 and 3,4 units (a poor rubber). The all-cis structure needs a stereospecific catalyst (Z–N or alkyl-Li) or the plant's enzymes.
