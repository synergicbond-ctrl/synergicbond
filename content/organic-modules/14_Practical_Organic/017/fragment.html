5.2 Thin layer chromatography and Rf

A small spot of the solution is applied on the origin line (marked in pencil, not ink, which would dissolve) near the bottom of the plate. The plate stands in a closed jar containing a shallow layer of solvent so that the origin is above the liquid; the solvent rises by capillary action. When it nears the top the plate is removed and the solvent front is marked at once. The spots are made visible, and each one's retention factor is measured:

Formula Rf = (distance travelled by the compound from the origin to the centre of its spot)/(distance travelled by the solvent front from the origin) always 0 < Rf < 1
Spot B is less polar (travels further, higher Rf = 0.55) than A (Rf = 0.25) on silica. The mixture lane resolves both.
  • Colourless spots are shown by UV light (a fluorescent indicator plate shows dark spots), iodine vapour (brown spots), a ninhydrin spray (amino acids: purple/blue; heat), 2,4-DNP spray (carbonyl compounds: yellow-orange), neutral FeCl3 spray (phenols), or a KMnO4 dip (oxidisable groups: yellow spots on purple).
  • Rf depends on the compound, the adsorbent, the solvent, the plate thickness and the temperature; it does not depend on the amount of compound or on how far the front travelled. It is characteristic only under identical conditions, which is why a known sample is run in a neighbouring lane ("co-spotting").
  • A more polar solvent raises the Rf of all compounds (the polar solvent displaces the compounds from the silica). If all spots are at the origin, increase the solvent polarity; if they are all at the front, decrease it.
  • The same quantity for paper chromatography is also called Rf; in paper chromatography the more water-soluble amino acids (polar side chains, such as serine) move less in the organic mobile phase than the less polar ones (leucine, valine), since water is the stationary phase.
Example 7 Main

Rf values

In a TLC experiment the solvent front moved 9.0 cm from the origin. Compound X moved 2.7 cm and compound Y moved 6.3 cm. (a) Find Rf of X and Y. (b) How far from the origin would the solvent front be when Y has moved 4.2 cm? (c) Which is the more polar compound if the plate was silica gel?

Show solution
  1. (a) Rf(X) = 2.7/9.0 = 0.30; Rf(Y) = 6.3/9.0 = 0.70.
  2. (b) Rf is constant, so front = 4.2/0.70 = 6.0 cm.
  3. (c) X (lower Rf) sticks more strongly to polar silica, so X is more polar.
Example 8 Adv

Choosing the eluent

A mixture of fluorene (hydrocarbon), fluorenone (ketone) and fluorenol (alcohol) is placed on a silica column. In what order do they elute with hexane gradually enriched with ethyl acetate?

Show solution
  1. Elution order follows polarity (least polar first): the hydrocarbon fluorene, then the ketone fluorenone, then the alcohol fluorenol, which hydrogen bonds most strongly to silica.
  2. The Rf on TLC follows the same order: fluorene > fluorenone > fluorenol.
  • Gradient elution. The polarity of the eluent is raised in stages (hexane → hexane/ethyl acetate → ethyl acetate → methanol), so that progressively more strongly adsorbed components are brought off the column one after another.
  • Two-dimensional chromatography. After the first development the paper is turned through 90° and developed again in a second solvent; components that overlapped in the first run (complex amino-acid mixtures) separate in the second.
  • Binder. A TLC plate holds its thin layer of silica or alumina on the glass by a binder such as calcium sulphate (plaster of Paris; "silica gel G").
  • Identity by chromatography. Equal Rf in one solvent is necessary but not sufficient to prove two compounds identical; agreement in several solvent systems, or co-spotting (a lane in which both are applied on top of each other showing a single round spot), is the evidence.
Exam Trap In paper chromatography the stationary phase is the water adsorbed in the cellulose, not the paper itself; the mobile phase is the organic solvent. Many students write "paper is the stationary phase": true for the physical support, but the separation is by partition.
Why It Happens On silica the surface Si–OH groups hydrogen-bond to polar groups of the solute. A compound with –OH, –NH2 or –COOH is held strongly and moves slowly; a hydrocarbon interacts only weakly and travels with the solvent.
Compare & Distinguish Adsorption chromatography separates by strength of binding to a solid surface; partition chromatography separates by relative solubility in two liquid phases (water held on cellulose versus the moving organic solvent). TLC on silica and column chromatography are adsorption; paper chromatography is partition.
Concept check

A spot has Rf 0.10 in hexane. How should the eluent be changed to move it to about Rf 0.4?

Show answer

Increase the polarity of the eluent (for example hexane/ethyl acetate), because a more polar eluent displaces the polar solute from silica.
