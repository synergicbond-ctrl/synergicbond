22. Kinetic and thermodynamic enolates

An unsymmetrical ketone with α-hydrogens on two different carbons gives two regioisomeric enolates.

  • The kinetic enolate forms faster: the less hindered, usually the less substituted one (bulky base, low temperature, aprotic solvent, strong base in slight excess ketone, short time, no equilibration).
  • The thermodynamic enolate is the more stable: usually the more substituted C=C (small base, higher temperature, protic solvent or excess ketone to allow proton exchange).
LDA, THF, −78 °C⟶
Kinetic enolate of 2-methylcyclohexanone: deprotonation at C-6 (less substituted)
NaH or Et₃N/TMSCl, 298 K, equilibrating⟶
Thermodynamic enolate: tetrasubstituted C=C toward the methyl-bearing carbon

Trapping the enolates with alkyl halides gives the corresponding alkylation products:

(i) LDA, −78 °C (ii) CH₃I⟶
(i) kinetic enolate alkylated at C-6: 2,6-dimethylcyclohexanone
(i) NaH, equilibrating (ii) CH₃I⟶
(ii) thermodynamic: 2,2-dimethylcyclohexanone
Shortcut Remember the pairing: LDA / −78 °C / THF → kinetic → less substituted site. NaOEt or NaH / room temperature or heat / protic or equilibrating → thermodynamic → more substituted site. A question with LDA and an unsymmetrical ketone nearly always tests this point.
22.1 C-alkylation versus O-alkylation
The enolate is an ambident nucleophile: it can react at carbon or at oxygen. Soft electrophiles (alkyl iodides and bromides, Michael acceptors) react at carbon; hard electrophiles (chlorosilanes, acid chlorides, sulfonates) react at oxygen. Polar aprotic solvents free the oxygen and favour O-attack somewhat.
(i) LDA (ii) Me₃SiCl⟶
Silyl enol ether (O-attack): a stable, isolable enolate equivalent, used in Mukaiyama aldol and enolate regeneration (MeLi or F⁻)
(i) LDA (ii) CH₃CH₂I⟶
C-alkylation with a soft electrophile: acetone → pentan-2-one

22.2 Alkylation limits Only primary alkyl halides and methyl halides work well (SN2 on a hindered halide competes with E2 because the enolate is also a base). Poly-alkylation is possible with weak bases: LDA (complete conversion) prevents it.

Trap Aryl halides and vinyl halides do not alkylate enolates by SN2. Tertiary halides give alkenes (E2).
