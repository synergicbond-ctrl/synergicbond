9.3 The pinacol rearrangement

A vicinal diol treated with acid (H2SO4, H3PO4, or a Lewis acid) loses water and rearranges to a carbonyl compound with a new carbon skeleton. The prototype: pinacol (2,3-dimethylbutane-2,3-diol) → pinacolone (3,3-dimethylbutan-2-one). Pinacol itself is made by the bimolecular reduction of acetone with magnesium amalgam followed by water (2 CH3COCH3 + Mg(Hg) → pinacolate; H2O → pinacol). Mechanism (four steps).

  • 1. One OH is protonated.
  • 2. Water leaves to give a carbocation (the rate-determining step). If the two OH groups are not equivalent, the OH that leaves is the one that gives the more stable carbocation (tertiary > secondary, benzylic/diaryl best).
  • 3. A group on the neighbouring carbinol carbon migrates with its electron pair to the cationic carbon (1,2-shift). The positive charge is thereby moved onto the carbon that carries the OH, and it is stabilised as a protonated carbonyl (oxocarbenium ion), which makes the shift highly favourable.
  • 4. Loss of H+ from the oxygen gives the carbonyl compound.

Migratory aptitude (which group moves): roughly H ≈ aryl (electron-rich aryl best: p-methoxyphenyl > phenyl) > tertiary alkyl > secondary alkyl > primary alkyl > CH3. Aryl groups migrate through a bridged phenonium-like transition state; H and aryl compete, and the observed product also depends on the geometry (the migrating group should be anti to the leaving water). In JEE problems the usual rules are: (1) form the more stable cation, (2) the group that can better bear a partial positive charge migrates (aryl > alkyl; H often easiest), (3) the product is a ketone unless H migrates from a CH(OH)–R terminus or a terminal CH2OH is left, which gives an aldehyde.

Trap Do not apply Zaitsev-type thinking to pinacol: the driving force is the formation of the protonated carbonyl (C=O). A tertiary carbocation next to a carbon carrying OH will always rearrange (never lose a proton to give an alkene) because the shift yields the very stable oxocarbenium ion. Also remember that in the symmetrical diol the first cation is the same whichever OH leaves.
Example 28 L2 Main

Pinacol products

Give the major product of the acid-catalysed rearrangement of (a) 2,3-dimethylbutane-2,3-diol, (b) 2,3-diphenylbutane-2,3-diol, (c) 2-methylpropane-1,2-diol.

Show solution
  1. (a) Pinacol → pinacolone, (CH3)3C–CO–CH3.
  2. (b) Either OH may leave (both carbons are tertiary and benzylic). On the cation, the neighbouring carbon offers CH3 and Ph; phenyl migrates better: 3,3-diphenylbutan-2-one, CH3CO–C(CH3)Ph2.
  3. (c) HOCH2–C(OH)(CH3)2: the OH on the tertiary carbon leaves to give the 3° cation; H migrates from CH2OH, leaving a protonated aldehyde: 2-methylpropanal, (CH3)2CH–CHO.
Example 29 L3 Advanced

Which OH leaves?

In 1,1-diphenylpropane-1,2-diol, Ph2C(OH)–CH(OH)CH3, which hydroxyl is lost first and what is the major product?

Show solution
  1. The OH on C-1 leaves because it gives the diaryl-stabilised (benzhydryl) tertiary cation Ph2C+, far more stable than the secondary cation that loss of the C-2 OH would give.
  2. The groups on C-2 that can migrate are H and CH3; hydrogen migrates more readily. C-2 becomes the carbonyl carbon: Ph2CH–CO–CH3, 1,1-diphenylpropan-2-one.
Example 30 L2 Main

Migration versus ring contraction

Predict the product when 1,2-dimethylcyclopentane-1,2-diol is treated with H2SO4.

Show solution
  1. Both ring carbons C-1 and C-2 are tertiary and carry CH3 and OH, so the cation forms at C-1 (either OH may leave; the diol is symmetrical).
  2. From C-2 either a methyl group or a ring C–C bond could migrate. A ring bond shift would contract the five-membered ring to a strained cyclobutane (with an acetyl group), so it is not favoured; the methyl group migrates.
  3. After the methyl shift C-1 carries two methyl groups and C-2 is the carbonyl carbon: 2,2-dimethylcyclopentanone (C7H12O).
