9. Side-chain and aromatic oxidation: benzylic oxidation, quinones, Dakin and cumene

The benzene ring resists oxidation by KMnO4 and K2Cr2O7, but an alkyl side chain is oxidised at the benzylic carbon. Hot alkaline or acidic KMnO4 (or hot acidified dichromate) converts any alkyl side chain bearing at least one benzylic hydrogen into a –COOH group, regardless of the chain length: toluene, ethylbenzene, isopropylbenzene (cumene) and butylbenzene all give benzoic acid, the remaining carbons being lost as CO2. The first step is abstraction of the weak benzylic C–H bond to form a resonance-stabilised benzylic radical. A group such as tert-butyl, which has no benzylic hydrogen, is not oxidised.

Reactant:

Reagent: Hot KMnO4, OH−; then H3O+

Product:

The benzylic CH2 becomes the carboxyl carbon; the terminal CH3 is lost as CO2.

Reactant:

Reagent: Hot KMnO4, OH−; then H3O+

Product:

The methyl group has benzylic hydrogens; the tert-butyl group has none and survives.

Stopping at the aldehyde is possible with chromyl chloride (Étard reaction: CrO2Cl2 in CS2 or CCl4, then H2O), which converts toluene to benzaldehyde. Phenols and hydroquinones are oxidised on the ring: phenol is converted to p-benzoquinone by acidified dichromate, and hydroquinone ⇌ p-benzoquinone is a reversible two-electron, two-proton redox couple. Catechol similarly gives o-benzoquinone.

Reactant:

Reagent: Na2Cr2O7, H2SO4 (or Ag2O)

Product:

Hydroquinone to p-benzoquinone: loss of 2 H (2 e−).

In the Dakin reaction an o- or p-hydroxybenzaldehyde (or hydroxyaryl ketone) reacts with alkaline H2O2: hydroperoxide adds to the carbonyl, the aryl group migrates to oxygen (as in Baeyer–Villiger) and hydrolysis of the aryl formate gives the dihydric phenol (catechol from salicylaldehyde, hydroquinone from the p-isomer). The industrial cumene process uses radical autoxidation: cumene reacts with O2 at the tertiary benzylic C–H to give cumene hydroperoxide, and acid causes migration of phenyl to oxygen, giving phenol and propanone (acetone).

Reactant:

Reagent: (i) O2, heat (radical chain); (ii) dil. H2SO4

Product:

Cumene process: phenol + propanone.

Key idea. Benzylic oxidation requires at least one H on the benzylic carbon. Whatever the chain length, the product on the ring is –COOH.
Trap. tert-Butylbenzene is inert to KMnO4 because it has no benzylic hydrogen, whereas cumene (one benzylic H) is oxidised both by KMnO4 (to benzoic acid) and by O2 (to the hydroperoxide). The order of steps matters in synthesis: nitration of toluene followed by oxidation gives 4-nitrobenzoic acid (after separation from the ortho isomer), whereas oxidation first makes the ring carry a meta-directing –COOH group and nitration then gives 3-nitrobenzoic acid.
