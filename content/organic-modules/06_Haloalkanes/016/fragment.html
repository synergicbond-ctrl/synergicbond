15. The E1cB reaction

In E1cB (elimination, unimolecular, conjugate base) the base first removes the proton to give a carbanion (the conjugate base of the substrate), which then expels the leaving group in a second step. The mechanism is favoured when:

  • the β-hydrogen is acidic (next to C=O, NO2, CN, SO2R, or in a fluorene-type, or an ester) so that the carbanion (enolate) is stabilised, and
  • the leaving group is poor (–OH, –OR, –F, –OCOR), so it does not leave while the β C–H is still attached.
NaOH, Δ⟶
Dehydration of the β-hydroxy ketone (aldol) through its enolate: E1cB. Product α,β-unsaturated ketone, stabilised by conjugation (that is why this elimination is driven forward).
Key idea Aldol condensation = aldol addition + E1cB dehydration. Poor LG (HO−) leaves only because the enolate pushes it out (negative charge on the α-carbon, expelling the β-leaving group).
Kinetics: rate = k[substrate][base] (like E2: second order in the irreversible case), but the deuterium-exchange experiment distinguishes them: in E1cB(R) the carbanion forms reversibly, so substrate recovered from D2O/OD− has D on the α-carbon (or at least rate of exchange > rate of elimination).
