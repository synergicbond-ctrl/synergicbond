Practice 303 Adv

Carius estimation of bromine

0.400 g of an organic bromo compound is heated with fuming nitric acid and silver nitrate in a sealed Carius tube; 0.376 g of AgBr is obtained. Calculate the percentage of bromine. (Ag = 108, Br = 80.)

Show answer
  1. M(AgBr) = 188 g mol−1. n(AgBr) = 0.376/188 = 2.00 × 10−3 mol = n(Br). Mass of Br = 2.00 × 10−3 × 80 = 0.160 g.
  2. % Br = 0.160/0.400 × 100 = 40 %.
Practice 304 Adv

Carius estimation of chlorine

0.200 g of an organic chloro compound gives 0.287 g of AgCl in a Carius estimation. Calculate the percentage of chlorine. (Cl = 35.5, AgCl = 143.5.)

Show answer
  1. n(AgCl) = 0.287/143.5 = 2.00 × 10−3 mol. Mass of Cl = 2.00 × 10−3 × 35.5 = 0.071 g. % Cl = 0.071/0.200 × 100 = 35.5 %.
Practice 305 Adv

Precipitate

4.625 g of 1-chlorobutane (C4H9Cl, M = 92.5) is completely converted into chloride ion by prolonged heating with alcoholic KOH/NaOH, and the solution is acidified and treated with excess AgNO3. What mass of AgCl is formed?

Show answer
  1. n(C4H9Cl) = 4.625/92.5 = 0.0500 mol, giving 0.0500 mol Cl− and 0.0500 mol AgCl. Mass = 0.0500 × 143.5 = 7.175 g.
Practice 306 Adv

Yield of Williamson ether

2.30 g of sodium is dissolved in excess ethanol, and 10.9 g of bromoethane (M = 109) is added and heated. 5.18 g of diethyl ether (M = 74) is isolated. What is the percentage yield? (Na = 23.)

Show answer
  1. n(Na) = 0.100 mol → 0.100 mol NaOEt; n(EtBr) = 10.9/109 = 0.100 mol. Both react 1:1 → 0.100 mol ether = 7.40 g (theoretical).
  2. Yield = 5.18/7.40 × 100 = 70 %.
Practice 307 Adv MCQ

Aryl halide statements

Which statements are correct for chlorobenzene? (A) It undergoes nitration more slowly than benzene. (B) Its C–Cl bond is shorter than the C–Cl bond in ethyl chloride. (C) It is hydrolysed easily by aqueous NaOH at 25 °C. (D) It is an ortho/para director. (E) Its dipole moment is larger than that of CH3Cl.

Show answer
  1. (A) True (–I effect dominates the rate). (B) True (≈ 1.70–1.74 Å against 1.77 Å). (C) False: it needs 623 K and 300 atm. (D) True. (E) False: 1.69 D against 1.87 D.
  2. A, B, D.
Practice 308 Adv MCQ

SNAr statements

Select the correct statements about nucleophilic aromatic substitution of 1-halo-2,4-dinitrobenzenes: (A) The rate-determining step is the addition of the nucleophile. (B) The fluoro compound reacts fastest. (C) A meta nitro group activates as strongly as a para nitro group. (D) A resonance-stabilised carbanion (cyclohexadienyl anion) is the intermediate. (E) The rate depends on the nucleophile concentration.

Show answer
  1. (A) True. (B) True (F ≫ Cl ≈ Br > I). (C) False: meta nitro cannot delocalise the charge by resonance. (D) True (Meisenheimer complex). (E) True: bimolecular overall.
  2. A, B, D, E.
Practice 309 Adv MCQ

E2 in cyclohexane

Which statements are correct about E2 elimination of menthyl chloride and neomenthyl chloride? (A) H and Cl must be trans-diaxial. (B) An equatorial Cl eliminates faster than an axial Cl. (C) The isopropyl group keeps the ring conformation locked in the stable chair of neomenthyl chloride. (D) Menthyl chloride gives only one alkene. (E) Neomenthyl chloride reacts faster than menthyl chloride.

Show answer
  1. (A) True. (B) False: axial Cl eliminates faster (it already has axial H partners). (C) True for the reasoning (bulky isopropyl and methyl equatorial; Cl axial in neomenthyl). (D) True (2-menthene only, since the C4-H is equatorial in the reactive conformer). (E) True.
  2. A, C, D, E.
Practice 310 Adv MCQ

Alkynes by dehydrohalogenation

Which compounds give an alkyne (possibly after work-up) when treated with excess NaNH2 in liquid ammonia? (A) CH3CHBrCH2Br (B) CH3CBr2CH3 (C) CH3CH2CHBr2 (D) CH2=CHBr (E) CH3CH2CH2Br.

Show answer
  1. (A) vic-dibromide → propyne. (B) gem-dibromide → propyne. (C) gem-dibromide → but-1-yne. (D) vinyl bromide loses HBr → ethyne. (E) only one halogen: gives propene, not an alkyne.
  2. A, B, C, D.
Practice 311 Adv MCQ

Organometallic statements

Which statements about Grignard reagents are correct? (A) They are destroyed by water. (B) They react with CO2 to give carboxylic acids. (C) Ethers stabilise them. (D) They add twice to esters. (E) They react with nitriles to give amines directly.

Show answer
  1. (A) True. (B) True. (C) True (Lewis-base coordination of oxygen to Mg). (D) True (two equivalents, tertiary alcohol). (E) False: nitriles give ketones after hydrolysis.
  2. A, B, C, D.

Counting isomers

How many isomers of C5H11Br are primary alkyl bromides, how many secondary and how many tertiary?

Show answer
  1. Primary: 1-bromopentane, 1-bromo-2-methylbutane, 1-bromo-3-methylbutane, 1-bromo-2,2-dimethylpropane: 4. Secondary: 2-bromopentane, 3-bromopentane, 2-bromo-3-methylbutane: 3. Tertiary: 2-bromo-2-methylbutane: 1. (Total 8.)
Practice 313 Adv

Counting chiral halides

How many chiral isomers are there among the four constitutional isomers of C4H9Cl, and how many chiral isomers among the five constitutional isomers of C3H5Cl3?

Show answer
  1. C4H9Cl: only 2-chlorobutane is chiral: 1. C3H5Cl3: 1,1,1-, 1,1,2-, 1,1,3-, 1,2,2- and 1,2,3-trichloropropane; only 1,1,2-trichloropropane (CHCl2–CHCl–CH3) has a stereocentre (C2: H, Cl, CH3, CHCl2): 1. (In 1,2,3-trichloropropane C2 carries two identical CH2Cl groups.)
Practice 314 Adv

Hydrolysis products

Which isomer of C5H11Cl would give a tertiary alcohol on hydrolysis with water (SN1) with no rearrangement needed, and how many such isomers exist?

Show answer
  1. Only a tertiary chloride gives a tertiary alcohol directly: 2-chloro-2-methylbutane. Number: 1. (Secondary chlorides such as 3-chloro-2-methylbutane would give the tertiary alcohol after a hydride shift, but that is not "directly".)
Practice 315 Adv

Cross-check of answers

Without using the table of data, estimate the relative amounts of 1-bromo-2-methylpropane and 2-bromo-2-methylpropane in the radical bromination of isobutane, and explain why the tertiary product dominates.

Show answer
  1. Nine primary H, one tertiary H; the tertiary C–H is weakest (BDE ≈ 400 kJ mol−1 against ≈ 420 for primary) and the tertiary radical is most stable. Bromine atoms are very selective (late TS): ratio ≈ 1640 : 9, i.e. > 99 % tert-butyl bromide.
Practice 316 Adv

Rate of SN2 and chain length

The relative SN2 rates of RBr with I− are CH3 30, C2H5 1, n-C3H7 0.4, i-C4H9 0.03, neopentyl 10−5. Compute the ratio of rates (methyl : neopentyl) and comment.

Show answer
  1. 30 : 10−5 = 3 × 106. Increasing β-branching (n-propyl → isobutyl → neopentyl) slows SN2 by roughly one order of magnitude per additional methyl on the β-carbon, then drastically at the quaternary β-carbon, reflecting hindrance to back-side attack.
Practice 317 Adv

Product of Hunsdiecker on a diacid

Predict the product from the disilver salt of succinic acid (butanedioic acid, AgOOC–CH2CH2–COOAg) with two equivalents of Br2. How many moles of CO2 form per mole of salt?

Show answer
  1. Each carboxylate undergoes Hunsdiecker decarboxylation: the product is 1,2-dibromoethane, BrCH2CH2Br, with 2 mol CO2 (and 2 AgBr) per mole of salt.
Practice 318 Adv

Alkyl halide and amine

Why does isopropyl bromide with ammonia give mainly propene as a by-product but ethyl bromide does not?

Show answer
  1. Isopropyl bromide is secondary: the amine/ammonia is a base as well as a nucleophile, and for 2° halides E2 competes (more strongly with heat). Ethyl bromide is a primary halide with no steric hindrance: SN2 dominates.

Identify the reagent

Identify the reagent in each: (a) RCH2OH → RCH2Cl with SO2 and HCl as by-products (b) RBr → RMgBr (c) ArNH2 → ArCl via a diazonium salt (d) RCOOAg → RBr (e) R–Br → R–I.

Show answer
  1. (a) SOCl2. (b) Mg in dry ether. (c) NaNO2/HCl (0–5 °C) then CuCl/HCl (Sandmeyer). (d) Br2/CCl4 (Hunsdiecker). (e) NaI in dry acetone (Finkelstein).
