6.3 Osazone formation, step by step

1. PhNHNH2⟶ 2. tautomerise⟶
Aldose → phenylhydrazone → ene-hydrazine (enol at C-2)
3. N–N cleavage, − PhNH2⟶ 4. PhNHNH2 (2 more)⟶
α-Imino-ketone → osazone, yellow, crystalline
  • Step 1. The nucleophilic –NH2 of phenylhydrazine (an α-effect nucleophile) adds to C=O (π*) and loses water: phenylhydrazone.
  • Step 2. The C-2–H next to the C=N is acidic; tautomerisation gives an enehydrazine, a conjugated 1,2-enol. MO view: the C2–H σ bond overlaps with the C=N π* (hyperconjugation) which is why only the α-carbon is activated.
  • Step 3. The enehydrazine N–N bond is weak (low-lying σ*NN); the electrons of the enol O–H (pushing from the enol oxygen through the C=C) drive cleavage of N–N, expelling aniline and forming the α-imino ketone: this step oxidises C-2 to a carbonyl while reducing the second phenylhydrazine molecule to aniline + NH3.
  • Step 4. The new C-2 carbonyl, and the C-1 imine (by exchange), react with 2 further PhNHNH2. The osazone is stabilised by intramolecular N–H···N hydrogen bonding and extended conjugation; this is why the reaction stops at C-2 and does not proceed to C-3.
