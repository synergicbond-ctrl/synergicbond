Alphabetical order matters in two places: (a) in writing the prefixes of every name, and (b) in numbering, but only when the locant sets from the two directions are identical (Example 3). It never overrides a lower locant at an earlier point.

Validity Check The tie-break compares locants in the order in which the prefixes will be cited. If the first-cited prefix has the same locant in both numberings, the comparison moves to the second-cited prefix, and so on (Example 6).

  1. Chain: six carbons. Locants from the left: bromo 2, chloro 4, methyl 4 → {2,4,4}. From the right: methyl 3, chloro 3, bromo 5 → {3,3,5}.
  2. First point of difference: 2 < 3, so the left numbering wins. (Only the first point of difference matters, not the sum of the locants.)
  3. Alphabetical order: bromo, chloro, methyl.

2-Bromo-4-chloro-4-methylhexane. (Halogens and alkyl groups are all just prefixes, ranked together by locant, then alphabetically.)
