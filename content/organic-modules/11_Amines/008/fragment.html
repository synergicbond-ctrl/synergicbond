8. Ammonolysis of alkyl halides (Hofmann's method)

Alkyl halides undergo SN2 attack by NH3 (alcoholic, sealed tube, 373 K) to give RNH3+X−, and a base (excess NH3) frees RNH2. The product is itself a better nucleophile than NH3, so it competes for RX, giving a mixture of 1°, 2°, 3° amines and R4N+X−.

NH₃⟶ CH₃Br, base⟶ CH₃Br, base⟶ CH₃Br⟶
Polyalkylation: each product is more nucleophilic than NH₃, so the reaction runs on to the quaternary salt
  • A large excess of NH3 favours the primary amine; excess RX favours the quaternary salt.
  • Reactivity of the halide: RI > RBr > RCl; 1° > 2°. Tertiary halides give elimination. Aryl and vinyl halides do not react (no SN2, and the C–X bond has partial double-bond character).
  • The mixture is separated by fractional distillation or by Hinsberg's reagent.
Trap tert-Butyl bromide with NH3 gives mainly isobutene (E2), not tert-butylamine. A 1° or 2° halide is required. Chlorobenzene reacts with NH3 only under drastic conditions (Cu catalyst, high pressure) or when activated by o/p nitro groups (SNAr).
