10. Perkin reaction

Reagents: ArCHO + (RCH2CO)2O + sodium or potassium salt of the same acid RCH2COOH (as base), 453 K (180 °C), then acid work-up. Product: an α,β-unsaturated acid (cinnamic acid from benzaldehyde and ethanoic anhydride), E. Mechanism: acetate removes an α-H from the anhydride (the anhydride is the only α-H acid that is acidic enough); the anhydride enolate attacks the aldehyde, giving a β-acetoxy intermediate; acyl transfer and E1cB-type elimination of acetate give the mixed anhydride of cinnamic acid, which hydrolyses to the acid.

+ CH₃COONa, 453 K⟶ +
Benzaldehyde → E-cinnamic acid + ethanoic acid
+ CH₃CH₂COONa, Δ⟶ +
With propanoic anhydride: α-methylcinnamic acid (the α-carbon of the anhydride supplies the new C=C carbon)
Trap Perkin works with aromatic aldehydes (aliphatic aldehydes polymerise under the conditions); the acid anhydride must have at least two α-H on one side (so that after condensation the α-carbon still bears one H for the E1cB step): (CH3CO)2O and (CH3CH2CO)2O work, [(CH3)2CHCO]2O stops at the β-substituted adduct, because after addition the α-carbon has no hydrogen left for the elimination. With an o- or p-NO2 group on the aldehyde the reaction is faster; with an electron-donating group (p-OCH3) it is slower.
