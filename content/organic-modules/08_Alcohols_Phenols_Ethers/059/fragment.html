13.2 Other preparations

(a) Dow process (hydrolysis of chlorobenzene). C6H5Cl + NaOH (aqueous, about 623 K and 300 atm) → C6H5ONa → H+ → phenol. The conditions are severe because the C–Cl bond is strengthened by resonance with the ring and an SN2 attack is impossible on an sp2 carbon. The mechanism at high temperature is elimination–addition through benzyne (the strong base removes the ortho H; Cl− leaves; OH− adds to the triple bond). The evidence for benzyne comes from Roberts’s experiment: 14C-labelled chlorobenzene with KNH2 in liquid NH3 gives aniline labelled equally at C-1 and C-2. If an electron-withdrawing group (NO2) is ortho or para to Cl, simple nucleophilic aromatic substitution occurs at 368 K (addition–elimination, Meisenheimer complex).

(b) Fusion of sodium benzenesulphonate with NaOH (about 573 K): C6H5SO3Na + 2NaOH → C6H5ONa + Na2SO3 + H2O; then acidification. This is the old industrial route, and the only route available to naphthols from naphthalenesulphonic acids. (c) Hydrolysis of benzenediazonium salts. C6H5N2+Cl− + H2O, warm (about 283 → 330 K) → C6H5OH + N2 + HCl. The best laboratory route (from aniline: diazotise at 273–278 K, then warm the solution; the ice-cold salt is stable). Keep the solution acidic to avoid azo coupling of phenol with unreacted diazonium salt.

(d) Oxidation methods. Dakin oxidation: o- or p-hydroxybenzaldehyde (or a ketone) with H2O2/NaOH gives catechol/hydroquinone. Arylboronic acids with H2O2/NaOH give phenols. Baeyer–Villiger oxidation of an aryl ketone gives an aryl ester that is hydrolysed. (e) From aryl ethers: HI, HBr or BBr3 cleaves anisole to phenol (Part III). (f) From coal tar: the fraction distilling at 443–503 K (“middle oil” or “carbolic oil”) contains phenols, extracted with aqueous NaOH, then acidified (CO2) to liberate the phenols.

Example 37 L2 Main

Choose the method

Which is the best way to prepare (a) phenol from benzene on an industrial scale; (b) p-nitrophenol from chlorobenzene; (c) catechol from salicylaldehyde?

Show solution
  1. (a) The cumene process (benzene + propene, O2, then H3O+), producing acetone as a valuable by-product.
  2. (b) Nitrate chlorobenzene (HNO3/H2SO4) and separate the para isomer (the major one); then aq. NaOH at 368 K (SNAr, activated by the para NO2) and acidify. Hydrolysing first and nitrating phenol afterwards is inferior: the nitration of phenol gives an o/p mixture and some oxidation.
  3. (c) Dakin oxidation: o-hydroxybenzaldehyde + H2O2/NaOH gives catechol (the aryl group migrates to oxygen, the formate ester is hydrolysed).
