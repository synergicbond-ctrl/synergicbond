37. Mechanism: Hinsberg's reagent (sulphonylation)

Sulphur here is S(VI) in a tetrahedral sulphonyl chloride. The amine attacks S in an SN2-like (addition–elimination at sulphur) step; the LUMO is σ*(S–Cl).

  • Step 1 – attack at sulphur. nN → σ*(S–Cl); Cl− leaves in a concerted or very short-lived trigonal-bipyramidal process. Why: S is electron-poor (two O, one Cl, one aryl) and Cl− is an excellent leaving group.
  • Step 2 – deprotonation. The N–H+ of the sulphonylammonium ion loses H+ to OH−, giving the neutral sulphonamide RNHSO2Ph (from a 1° amine).
  • Step 3 – the acidic N–H (1° only). Excess OH− removes the remaining N–H: pKa ≈ 10–12, because the anion is stabilised by the strongly electron-withdrawing SO2 group (inductive effect and negative hyperconjugation of the N lone pair into the σ*(S–O) and σ*(S–C) orbitals). The anion is soluble. A 2° amine has no N–H after step 2, hence an insoluble product.

Trap Textbooks sometimes explain the acidity of sulphonamides by "d-orbital participation". Modern bonding descriptions use strong −I and negative hyperconjugation instead; for exam purposes the safe statement is "the SO2 group is strongly electron withdrawing and stabilises the conjugate base".
