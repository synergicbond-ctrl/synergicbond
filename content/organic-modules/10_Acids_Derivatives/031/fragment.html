30. Rapid-fire facts (read the night before)

Acidity and physical properties

  • Acid strength: carboxylic acids ≫ phenols ≫ alcohols; NaHCO3 separates the first from the others.
  • pKa: CF3COOH 0.5 < CCl3COOH 0.7 < Cl2CHCOOH 1.3 < ClCH2COOH 2.9 < HCOOH 3.75 < C6H5COOH 4.2 < CH3COOH 4.76 < CH3CH2COOH 4.87.
  • Halogen –I order: F > Cl > Br > I. Distance order: α ≫ β > γ. Additivity: roughly 1.4–1.5 pKa units per extra Cl on the same carbon.
  • ortho-substituted benzoic acids are stronger than the parent, whatever the group (ortho effect).
  • meta: –I only; para: –I and ±M. p-CH3, p-OCH3, p-NH2 weaken; p-NO2 strengthens.
  • Salicylic acid (2.97) ≫ p-hydroxybenzoic (4.58); the ortho isomer is volatile with steam.
  • Diacids: pKa1 < pKa2; closer COOH groups → stronger first ionisation and a larger gap (oxalic 1.25/4.27; maleic 1.90/6.07; fumaric 3.03/4.44).
  • Boiling points: amide > acid > alcohol > ester ≈ acyl chloride; dimerisation in non-polar solvents halves the apparent molar mass (i ≈ 0.5).
  • C=O IR: acyl chloride 1800 > anhydride 1820/1760 > ester 1740 > acid 1710 > amide 1670.

Preparation

  • 1° alcohol/aldehyde → acid (oxidation); alkylbenzene → benzoic acid (needs benzylic H; tert-butylbenzene is inert).
  • Grignard + CO2, or RX + CN− then hydrolysis: +1 carbon. Haloform: −1 carbon. Arndt–Eistert: +1 carbon. Malonic ester: +2 carbons from RX.
  • Kolbe–Schmitt: phenoxide + CO2 → salicylic acid (Na+, 125 °C); K+ at 200 °C → para.

Reactions of the acid

  • SOCl2: gaseous by-products (SO2, HCl). PCl5: POCl3 + HCl.
  • Fischer: acyl–O fission, Kc ≈ 4 (EtOH/AcOH), 67 % at 1 : 1, 90 % at 1 : 3, 97 % at 1 : 10.
  • LiAlH4: RCH2OH; NaBH4: no reaction; B2H6: selective for –COOH.
  • HVZ: α-bromination, needs α-H; products: α-amino, α-hydroxy, α,β-unsaturated acids.
  • Soda lime: RH (−1 C). Kolbe electrolysis: R–R. Hunsdiecker: RBr (−1 C). β-Keto acids and malonic acids decarboxylate by a cyclic TS.
  • HCOOH is a reducing acid (Tollens, Fehling, HgCl2, KMnO4); conc. H2SO4 dehydrates it to CO.

Derivatives

  • Reactivity: RCOCl > (RCO)2O > RCOOH ≈ RCOOR′ > RCONH2; only “downhill” conversions work directly.
  • Rosenmund: H2, Pd/BaSO4, quinoline → RCHO. LiAlH(OtBu)3: RCHO. R2CuLi: ketone. Excess RMgX: 3° alcohol.
  • Anhydrides: Perkin (ArCHO + (RCH2CO)2O + RCH2COONa → cinnamic acids); Fries (aryl ester + AlCl3 → hydroxyaryl ketone).
  • Esters: BAC2 (OH−, irreversible); AAC2 (acid, reversible); AAL1 (t-butyl, benzhydryl). LiAlH4: two alcohols; DIBAL: aldehyde. Formates + excess RMgX: 2° alcohol. Ester pyrolysis: syn elimination, alkene + acid.
  • Amides: planar, neutral, protonate at O; hydrolysis hard; LiAlH4: amine (same C); P2O5: nitrile; Br2/NaOH: amine (−1 C); HNO2: acid + N2.
  • Nitriles: RCN + RMgX → ketone (stops); RCN + LiAlH4 → RCH2NH2; DIBAL → RCHO; isonitrile: foul smell, carbylamine reaction, RNHCH3 on reduction.
  • Hofmann/Curtius/Lossen/Schmidt: isocyanate, −1 C, retention of configuration; Beckmann: anti group migrates.

Condensations

  • Claisen: ≥ 2 α-H, ≥ 1 equiv. alkoxide matched to the ester; driven by deprotonation of the product. Dieckmann: 1,6 → 5-ring; 1,7 → 6-ring.
  • Crossed Claisen with a non-enolisable ester (HCOOEt, PhCOOEt, (COOEt)2, EtOCOOEt). Two enolisable esters: four products.
  • EAA: dilute base → ketone; conc. base → two acids. Malonic: → RCH2COOH. 3° and aryl halides do not alkylate.

Dicarboxylic and hydroxy acids

  • Oxalic → HCOOH + CO2; malonic → CH3COOH + CO2; succinic/glutaric/phthalic/maleic → anhydrides; adipic → cyclopentanone; pimelic → cyclohexanone.
  • α-Hydroxy → lactide; β-Hydroxy → α,β-unsaturated acid; γ/δ → lactones.
  • Maleic is the cis acid (stronger pKa1, weaker pKa2, forms anhydride at 160 °C); fumaric is trans.
  • Tartaric: three stereoisomers; citric: achiral; lactic: chiral, iodoform positive.
  • Oleic acid (cis-9): hot KMnO4 → nonanoic acid + azelaic acid; hydrogenation → stearic; saponification value ∝ 1/M; iodine value ∝ number of C=C.
