11. Phenol–formaldehyde resins and other condensation products

Phenol reacts with formaldehyde at the ortho and para positions, so phenol has a functionality of three toward formaldehyde (two ortho and one para). Under basic conditions with excess formaldehyde, phenol is converted into o- and p-hydroxymethylphenols (Lederer–Manasse), and further to di- and tri-hydroxymethylphenols; on heating, these condense with each other, with loss of water, into a cross-linked network. Acid catalysis with less than one equivalent of formaldehyde gives linear or lightly branched chains joined by CH2 bridges.

Reactant:

Reagent: base (NaOH), excess HCHO, mild heating

Product:

Hydroxymethylphenols; these condense on heating, forming –CH2– bridges.

  1. Step 1: phenoxide attacks the carbonyl carbon of HCHO through its o/p carbon, giving a hydroxymethylphenol after tautomerism.
  2. Step 2 (basic): a hydroxymethyl group of one ring is attacked by the o/p carbon of another phenoxide with loss of water, linking two rings with a CH2 bridge.
  3. Step 3: repetition at all three activated positions gives a cross-linked, three-dimensional thermosetting polymer, Bakelite.
  • Resol: base catalyst, excess HCHO; contains hydroxymethyl groups; cures on heating without additives.
  • Novolac: acid catalyst, excess phenol; linear; contains no hydroxymethyl groups; cured by adding formaldehyde source such as hexamethylenetetramine.

Condensation of two molecules of phenol with one molecule of a ketone gives a bisphenol. Phenol with acetone under acid catalysis gives bisphenol A, a monomer of polycarbonates and epoxy resins. Heating phenol with phthalic anhydride and a dehydrating acid gives phenolphthalein, an indicator which is colourless in acid and pink in base.

Reactant:

Reagent: H+ (2 phenol : 1 acetone)

Product:

Bisphenol A, formed by substitution at the para position.

Reactant:

Reagent: conc. H2SO4, heat

Product:

Phenolphthalein (2 phenol + phthalic anhydride, with loss of one H2O).

Key idea. Hydroxymethylation uses the o/p positions only. Blocking one of them (p-cresol) reduces the functionality to two, so the product is linear and thermoplastic; m-cresol keeps all three positions and cross-links more rapidly than phenol.
