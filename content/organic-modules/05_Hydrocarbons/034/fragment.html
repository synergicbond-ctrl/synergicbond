33. Addition reactions of alkynes

33.1 Halogens and hydrogen halides

Alkynes add X2 and HX less readily than alkenes: the intermediate vinyl cation (sp-hybridised carbon) is less stable than an alkyl cation, and the π cloud is held more tightly. (However, conjugation or an adjacent alkyl group helps.)

  • Br2 (1 eq.) → trans-dibromoalkene (anti addition through a bridged bromonium ion); a second equivalent gives the tetrabromoalkane.
  • HX (1 eq.) → vinyl halide (Markovnikov); 2 eq. → geminal dihalide (both halogens on the same carbon, the more substituted one in terminal alkynes). The second addition is faster because the halogen lone pair stabilises the cation.
  • HBr + peroxide: radical, anti-Markovnikov, gives mostly the (Z) bromoalkene.

33.2 Hydration: Kucherov reaction and keto–enol tautomerism

Alkyne + H2O, dil. H2SO4, HgSO4 (Hg2+ catalyst) → enol → ketone (Markovnikov). The enol is unstable and tautomerises to the carbonyl form (keto–enol tautomerism). Ethyne gives ethanal (the only aldehyde by this route); every other terminal alkyne gives a methyl ketone; a symmetrical internal alkyne gives one ketone; an unsymmetrical internal alkyne gives a mixture of two ketones. Mechanism: Hg2+ adds to the π bond (mercurinium-type); water attacks the more substituted carbon; protonolysis gives the enol; tautomerisation.

33.3 Hydroboration–oxidation: aldehyde from a terminal alkyne

A terminal alkyne with a hindered dialkylborane (disiamylborane Sia2BH, or 9-BBN; BH3 would add twice) adds H–B across the triple bond: syn, B on the terminal carbon (anti-Markovnikov). Oxidation (H2O2, NaOH) converts the vinylborane to an enol, which tautomerises to an aldehyde. Internal alkynes give ketones.

Key idea Terminal alkyne: HgSO4/H2O/H+ → methyl ketone (Markovnikov); R2BH then H2O2/OH− → aldehyde (anti-Markovnikov). This pair is a favourite for a "convert the ketone to the aldehyde" question.

33.4 Oxidative cleavage

Ozonolysis or hot KMnO4 cleaves C≡C completely: RC≡CR′ → RCOOH + R′COOH; terminal alkyne → RCOOH + CO2. Mild KMnO4 (neutral, cold) can give an α-diketone (R–CO–CO–R′).

Example 50 Integer

Alkyne structure from products

An alkyne C6H10 gives only propanoic acid on ozonolysis with oxidative work-up and does not react with ammoniacal AgNO3. Identify it. Another alkyne C5H8 gives CH3COOH and CH3CH2COOH. Identify it.

Show solution
  1. Only CH3CH2COOH: symmetrical alkyne CH3CH2–C≡C–CH2CH3 = hex-3-yne (no ≡C–H, no silver precipitate ✔).
  2. For the second: join the two acyl carbons: CH3–C≡C–CH2CH3 = pent-2-yne; (the formula is then C5H8).
