24.3 Quantitative methods

  • Equivalent weight of an acid: titrate with standard NaOH; equivalent weight = (mass/V × N); for a dibasic acid M = 2 × equivalent weight. Silver salt method: ignite the silver salt of the acid to metallic silver: for a monobasic RCOOAg: M(acid) = M(salt) − 107.9 + 1.0.
  • Estimation of –COOH on a compound: NaHCO3 back-titration or direct titration with phenolphthalein; ester value (saponification equivalent): heat with a known excess of standardised KOH, back-titrate the unused KOH.
  • Degree of unsaturation: DoU = (2C + 2 + N − H − X)/2; a C=O, a ring, or a C=C each count 1; –C≡N counts 2. For an acid CnH2nO2 the DoU is 1 (C=O), for a diacid CnH2n−2O4 it is 2.
Example 62 Main

Silver salt analysis

0.500 g of the silver salt of a monobasic acid leaves 0.2981 g of silver on ignition. Find the molar mass of the acid and identify it.

Show solution
  1. RCOOAg → Ag. Moles of Ag = 0.2981/107.87 = 2.763 × 10−3 mol = moles of salt.
  2. M(salt) = 0.500/2.763 × 10−3 = 180.9 g mol−1. M(acid) = M(salt) − M(Ag) + M(H) = 180.9 − 107.87 + 1.008 = 74.0 g mol−1.
  3. CnH2nO2 with M = 74 gives n = 3: propanoic acid.

24.4 Spectral identification examples

Example 63 Adv

Spectral puzzle

A compound C4H8O2 shows IR bands at 1740 cm−1 (strong), no O–H band; 1H NMR: δ 1.2 (t, 3H), 2.0 (s, 3H), 4.1 (q, 2H). Identify it. What would a carboxylic acid isomer of the same formula show?

Show solution
  1. C=O at 1740 with no O–H: an ester. The ethyl pattern (t 1.2, q 4.1, with the q at 4.1 = OCH2) and a methyl singlet at 2.0 (CH3C=O): ethyl ethanoate (ethyl acetate).
  2. A carboxylic acid isomer (butanoic acid) would show a broad O–H (2500–3300 cm−1), C=O at about 1710 cm−1, and a one-proton singlet at δ 10–12 that disappears on D2O shake.
Example 64 Adv

Two isomers C3H5ClO2

Two isomers of C3H5ClO2: A (NMR: δ 1.7, d, 3H; 4.4, q, 1H; 11, s, 1H) and B (δ 2.9, t, 2H; 3.8, t, 2H; 11, s, 1H). Identify A and B and say which is the stronger acid.

Show solution
  1. Both are chloropropanoic acids (COOH at δ 11, DoU = 1). A: CH3–CH(Cl)–COOH (doublet for CH3, quartet for CHCl): 2-chloropropanoic acid. B: ClCH2CH2COOH (two triplets): 3-chloropropanoic acid.
  2. A is the stronger acid (pKa 2.83 against 4.08) because Cl is closer to the carboxyl group (–I effect decreases with distance).
