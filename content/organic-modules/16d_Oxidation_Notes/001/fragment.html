2. Oxidation of alcohols by chromium(VI)

Primary and secondary alcohols are oxidised by Cr(VI) reagents. Jones reagent (CrO3 in aqueous H2SO4, added to the alcohol in acetone) and acidified K2Cr2O7 are strong, aqueous oxidants; pyridinium chlorochromate (PCC, C5H5NH+ ClCrO3−), pyridinium dichromate (PDC) and Collins reagent (CrO3·2 pyridine) are used in anhydrous dichloromethane. All share one mechanism: formation of a chromate ester followed by removal of the carbinol hydrogen.

  1. The alcohol oxygen attacks Cr(VI) and, after proton transfer, a chromate ester R2CH–O–CrO3H (or its chloro or pyridinium analogue) forms.
  2. A base (water or pyridine) removes the hydrogen on the carbinol carbon; the C–H electrons form the C=O π bond while the O–Cr bond breaks and the electron pair goes to chromium.
  3. Chromium has gone from Cr(VI) to Cr(IV); Cr(IV) is reduced further to Cr(III) by disproportionation or by reaction with more alcohol. In acid the orange Cr(VI) solution therefore turns green (Cr3+).

The carbinol hydrogen is essential. Tertiary alcohols have none and are not oxidised; in hot acid they dehydrate instead. Secondary alcohols stop at the ketone. A primary alcohol first gives an aldehyde. In aqueous acid this aldehyde is in equilibrium with its hydrate RCH(OH)2, which carries a carbinol hydrogen and forms a second chromate ester, so oxidation continues to the carboxylic acid. In anhydrous PCC/CH2Cl2 there is no water, no hydrate is formed, and the reaction stops at the aldehyde.

Reactant:

Reagent: PCC, CH2Cl2 (anhydrous)

Product:

Dry conditions: the aldehyde cannot form a hydrate and survives.

Reactant:

Reagent: Jones reagent (CrO3, H2SO4, H2O, acetone), excess

Product:

Aqueous acid: aldehyde hydrate is oxidised further.

Reactant:

Reagent: Jones reagent or PCC

Product:

A secondary alcohol gives the ketone with either reagent; the ketone resists further mild oxidation.

Key idea. The distinction between PCC and Jones is a question of water, not of oxidising strength. The identical chromate-ester step operates in both.
Trap. 2-Methylpropan-2-ol and other tertiary alcohols give no colour change with acidified dichromate at room temperature. Isolated C=C bonds are not attacked by PCC or Jones reagent under the usual conditions, so a molecule such as an alkenol can be oxidised at the alcohol carbon alone.
