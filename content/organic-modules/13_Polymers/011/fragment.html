7. Copolymerisation

When two monomers M1 and M2 polymerise together, the sequence of units depends on the cross-propagation rates. With reactivity ratios r1 = k11/k12 and r2 = k22/k21 (kij = rate constant for a chain ending in Mi• adding Mj), the composition of the copolymer being formed at any instant is given by the Mayo–Lewis equation:

Formula d[M1]/d[M2] = ([M1]/[M2]) × (r1[M1] + [M2]) / (r2[M2] + [M1])
  • r1 ≈ r2 ≈ 0 → alternating (each radical prefers the other monomer): styrene + maleic anhydride, giving –[S–MA–S–MA]–.
  • r1r2 ≈ 1 → ideal random (statistical): composition of polymer equals that of feed when r1 = r2 = 1.
  • r1 > 1, r2 > 1 → tendency to blocks (rare).
  • r1 > 1, r2 < 1 → polymer is richer in M1 than the feed; the feed drifts, so composition drifts with conversion.

Example 7 Adv

Copolymer composition

Two monomers are fed 1:1 by moles with r1 = r2 = 1. What is the polymer composition? If r1 = 2.0 and r2 = 0.5, find d[M1]/d[M2] for an equimolar feed.

Show solution
  1. r1 = r2 = 1: d[M1]/d[M2] = 1 × (1 + 1)/(1 + 1) = 1 (copolymer composition = feed, ideal random).
  2. r1 = 2.0, r2 = 0.5, [M1] = [M2]: ratio = (2.0 + 1)/(0.5 + 1) = 3/1.5 = 2.0. The polymer has twice as many M1 as M2 units, though the feed was 1:1.
