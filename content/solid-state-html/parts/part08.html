◆Concept

This part moves from one-dimensional repetition to five two-dimensional lattice geometries and then to three-dimensional crystal systems described by six lattice parameters.

1. One-Dimensional Lattice

∑Formula

R = n a

2. Two-Dimensional Lattice Geometries

2D geometryMetric condition
Squarea = b, θ = 90°
Rectanglea ≠ b, θ = 90°
Hexagonal / triangular neta = b, θ = 60° or equivalently 120° by basis choice
Rhombic / centred-rectangular primitive forma = b, θ ≠ 90°
Oblique / parallelograma ≠ b, θ ≠ 90°

3. Three-Dimensional Description

◆Concept

A 3D unit cell requires three edge lengths and three interaxial angles.

∑Formula

Six lattice parameters: a, b, c, α, β, γ.
  • α = angle between b and c.
  • β = angle between a and c.
  • γ = angle between a and b.

4. Standard Result

★Exam-standard statement

Three-dimensional crystals are classified into 7 crystal systems and 14 Bravais lattices. The handwritten source uses a classroom heuristic before arriving at the same standard 7-system/14-lattice result; use the standard crystallographic result in problems.

⚠JEE Advanced traps & edge cases

  • The 2D hexagonal net may be described with a primitive angle of 60° or 120°; do not treat those as different lattices.
  • The six lattice parameters describe cell geometry, not the chemical basis.
  • Do not confuse “crystal system” with “Bravais lattice”.
