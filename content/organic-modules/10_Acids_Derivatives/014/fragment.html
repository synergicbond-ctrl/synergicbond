14. Rearrangements that convert carbonyl derivatives into amines

Hofmann, Curtius, Lossen and Schmidt all pass through an isocyanate R–N=C=O formed by a 1,2-shift of R from the carbonyl carbon to a nitrogen that is losing a leaving group. Hydrolysis of the isocyanate gives the unstable carbamic acid, which loses CO2 to give RNH2. The carbon of the original carbonyl is lost as CO2 (or carbonate in alkali).

Key idea Common features: (1) one carbon fewer than the starting acid derivative; (2) the migrating group R moves with its electron pair, retaining configuration (if R is a stereocentre); (3) the isocyanate can be trapped by water (amine), alcohol (carbamate/urethane), or amine (urea).

14.1 Hofmann bromamide (hypobromite) degradation

RCONH2 + Br2 + 4 NaOH → RNH2 + Na2CO3 + 2 NaBr + 2 H2O. Equivalent: NaOBr from Br2 + 2 NaOH.

OH⁻, Br₂ (N-bromination)⟶ OH⁻ (deprotonate N–H)⟶ − Br⁻, R migrates⟶ H₂O⟶ − CO₂⟶
Hofmann mechanism: N-bromoamide → bromoamide anion → isocyanate → carbamic acid → amine
  • 1. NaOBr brominates the amide N (RCONHBr). 2. Base removes the second N–H (anion). 3. The R group migrates from C to N with loss of Br− (concerted): isocyanate. 4. OH−/H2O adds to RN=C=O: carbamate/carbamic acid; 5. decarboxylation: RNH2; the CO2 is trapped as Na2CO3.
  • R must not be sensitive to NaOBr (no unprotected C=C, no easily brominated phenol or amine).
  • The migrating group retains its configuration: (S)-2-phenylpropanamide gives (S)-1-phenylethanamine (CIP descriptor unchanged because NH2 ranks above C like the CONH2 carbon it replaces).
  • Electron-rich aryl groups migrate faster: p-methoxybenzamide reacts faster than p-nitrobenzamide.
  • Chain-length rule: the amine has n − 1 carbons if the amide had n. Aliphatic: CH3CH2CH2CONH2 → CH3CH2CH2NH2 (propan-1-amine).
  • Gives only primary amines, with amine-N coming from the amide N. The product amine is not contaminated by 2° or 3° amines (unlike alkylation of NH3).
Br₂, NaOH⟶
2,2-Dimethylpropanamide (pivalamide) → tert-butylamine: a 3° alkyl group migrates with retention and no rearrangement
Br₂, NaOH⟶
Retention of configuration at the migrating stereocentre
Example 36 Main

Hofmann degradations

Give the products: (a) butanamide, (b) benzamide, (c) 3-phenylpropanamide, (d) hexanediamide (adipamide) with excess NaOBr, (e) CH3CH2CONH2 with Br2/NaOMe in methanol (the isocyanate is trapped by solvent).

Show solution
  1. (a) propan-1-amine, CH3CH2CH2NH2. (b) aniline. (c) 2-phenylethanamine (PhCH2CH2NH2). (d) both CONH2 groups degrade: butane-1,4-diamine (putrescine) H2N(CH2)4NH2. (e) the isocyanate EtNCO adds methanol: methyl N-ethylcarbamate, CH3CH2NHCOOCH3.
Example 37 Adv

Hofmann vs reduction

Compare the carbon number and products for butanamide with (i) Br2/NaOH and (ii) LiAlH4.

Show solution
  1. (i) CH3CH2CH2NH2 (3 carbons). (ii) CH3CH2CH2CH2NH2 (4 carbons: butan-1-amine). Hofmann shortens the chain, LiAlH4 does not.

14.2 Curtius rearrangement

RCOCl + NaN3 → RCON3 (acyl azide) → heat (80 °C, toluene) → RN=C=O + N2; then H2O (RNH2), R′OH (carbamate), R′NH2 (urea). Alternative acyl azide sources: hydrazide + HNO2; acid + diphenylphosphoryl azide (DPPA). Migration is concerted, with retention. Isocyanate can be isolated (aprotic solvent). The sequence RCOOH → RCOCl → RCON3 → RNCO → RNH2 loses one carbon (the carbonyl).

NaN₃⟶ Δ, − N₂⟶ H₂O⟶
Curtius: acyl chloride → acyl azide → isocyanate → methylamine
HNO₂, 0 °C⟶
Acyl azide from a hydrazide (from ester + N₂H₄) and nitrous acid

14.3 Lossen rearrangement

An O-acylated or O-sulfonylated hydroxamic acid RCONHOCOR′, on treatment with base (or heat), loses R′COO− while R migrates: RNCO → RNH2. Hydroxamic acids RCONHOH are made from esters + NH2OH.

base (OH⁻) or Δ⟶ H₂O⟶
Lossen: O-acyl hydroxamate loses acetate

14.4 Schmidt reaction

Hydrazoic acid HN3 (toxic, explosive) with conc. H2SO4. (i) Carboxylic acids RCOOH → RNH2 + CO2 + N2. (ii) Ketones → N-substituted amides (like Beckmann); cyclohexanone → ε-caprolactam; acetophenone → acetanilide; the bulkier or more electron-rich group migrates, usually aryl > alkyl. (iii) Aldehydes → nitriles/formamides.

HN₃, conc. H₂SO₄⟶
Benzoic acid → aniline (loses CO₂ and N₂)
HN₃, H₂SO₄⟶
Cyclohexanone → ε-caprolactam (six-membered ring expands to seven)
HN₃, H₂SO₄⟶
Acetophenone → acetanilide (aryl migrates)

14.5 Beckmann rearrangement (for completeness)

Oximes treated with H2SO4, PCl5 or SOCl2 give amides; the group anti to the OH (leaving group) migrates. Cyclohexanone oxime → caprolactam, the monomer of nylon-6. An E/Z-oxime of an unsymmetrical ketone therefore gives a single amide; acetophenone gives acetanilide because the phenyl is anti to OH in the more stable (E) oxime.

H₂SO₄⟶
Cyclohexanone oxime → caprolactam
PCl₅ or H₂SO₄⟶
(E)-Acetophenone oxime (OH anti to phenyl) → acetanilide
Example 38 Adv

Identify the amide

A compound X, C8H9NO, on treatment with Br2/KOH gives Y, C7H9N. Y, on diazotisation at 0 °C and coupling with β-naphthol, gives a red azo dye. Which is X? (A) C6H5CH2CONH2 (B) 4-CH3C6H4CONH2 (C) C6H5CONHCH3 (D) C6H5CH2NHCHO

Show solution
  1. The dye test shows that Y is a primary aromatic amine (benzenediazonium-type salts are stable at 0 °C and couple; an aliphatic diazonium would lose N2 at once).
  2. (A) would give benzylamine: aliphatic, no coupling. (C) and (D) are N-substituted amides: Hofmann needs a primary amide (N–H2). (B) is a primary aroyl amide: Hofmann gives p-toluidine (C7H9N), which diazotises and couples.
  3. Answer: (B).
Example 39 Adv MCQ

Carbon count in rearrangements

For the same acid RCOOH, which of the following give an amine with one fewer carbon? (A) RCOCl → RCON3 → heat → hydrolysis (B) RCONH2, Br2/KOH (C) RCOOH, HN3/H2SO4 (D) RCOCl, CH2N2, Ag2O/H2O (E) RCONH2, LiAlH4

Show solution
  1. (A) Curtius, (B) Hofmann, (C) Schmidt each give RNH2, one carbon fewer. (D) Arndt–Eistert gives the homologated acid RCH2COOH. (E) gives RCH2NH2, the same number of carbons as the acid. Answer A, B, C.
