10. Arynes

10.1 Structure

Benzyne (1,2-didehydrobenzene, C6H4) is benzene with two adjacent hydrogens removed and an extra bond between those two carbons. It is the simplest aryne. The ring keeps its normal six π electrons. The extra bond is an abnormal π bond formed by overlap of two sp2 orbitals that lie in the plane of the ring and point outwards, outside the ring; it is perpendicular to the normal π system. This overlap is poor because the two orbitals do not point at one another as in a normal alkyne, so the extra bond is very weak and benzyne is a very unstable, strained species. It is electrophilic because of the weak extra bond, and its LUMO (the in-plane π* orbital) is low in energy, which makes it a good dienophile.

IMP Benzyne: planar C6H4; the aromatic sextet is intact; the extra weak bond lies in the plane of the ring from two sp2 orbitals, perpendicular to the aromatic π cloud. Electrophilic, reacts with nucleophiles in the plane of the ring and as a dienophile. It cannot be isolated; it can be observed in the gas phase by mass spectrometry (a peak at m/e 76, with the dimer at 152), where its lifetime is at least 2 × 10−8 s.
MOT Lens The extra bond of benzyne is formed by overlap of two sp2 orbitals that point outward in the plane of the ring and are almost parallel, so overlap is poor. The bonding combination is therefore only slightly stabilised and the antibonding combination (the LUMO) lies low in energy: that makes benzyne an excellent electrophile and dienophile. Because this LUMO lies in the ring plane, nucleophiles attack in the plane and are sensitive to hindrance from an ortho substituent.
Bonding Insight The aromatic sextet of benzyne is a delocalised π system perpendicular to the ring plane and is unaffected by the extra bond; the extra "triple bond" is a localised in-plane interaction. Drawing a triple bond in the ring is a localised-bond convention and does not mean that the ring becomes an alkyne with linear geometry.

10.3 The elimination–addition mechanism

Elimination–addition: ortho-H removed, Cl⁻ lost, NH₂⁻ adds to benzyne, aryl anion protonated by NH₃
  • Step 1: the strongly basic amide ion removes the proton ortho to the halogen. This proton is the most acidic because the electronegative halogen withdraws electron density through the σ framework; the carbanion lies in an sp2 orbital in the plane of the ring and has no connection with the π system.
  • Step 2: the carbanion expels halide ion. The elimination is syn-periplanar rather than anti-periplanar (both orbitals are in the ring plane), which is unusual, but it occurs. This step gives benzyne.
  • Step 3: the nucleophile adds to one carbon of the weak extra bond, in the plane of the ring, to give a new aryl carbanion on the other carbon.
  • Step 4: the aryl anion is protonated by the solvent (NH3), which gives the aniline.

Evidence for benzyne.

  • Labelling. Chlorobenzene labelled with 14C at C-1 gives aniline in which half the label is at C-1 and half at the two equivalent ortho positions (the aniline was degraded to locate each carbon). Direct displacement would place all the label at C-1; the 50 : 50 result requires the symmetrical intermediate. This was the first convincing evidence, obtained in 1953.
  • Trapping. Benzyne generated in the presence of a diene gives Diels–Alder adducts: furan gives 1,4-dihydro-1,4-epoxynaphthalene, and anthracene gives the symmetrical cage hydrocarbon triptycene.
  • Regiochemistry. Substituted halobenzenes give "cine" substitution (see below), which is hard to explain without benzyne.
  • No reaction without an ortho hydrogen. A halobenzene with both ortho positions blocked, such as 2-bromo-1,3-dimethylbenzene, cannot form benzyne and is inert towards sodium amide in liquid ammonia.
Benzyne as dienophile: furan gives the epoxynaphthalene, anthracene gives triptycene
Why It Happens The nucleophile attacks the aryne carbon that places the developing carbanion adjacent to an inductively electron-withdrawing substituent (OMe), because the anion lies in the ring plane and cannot be delocalised into the π system; oxygen can act only inductively. Attack alongside the substituent is also hindered, so both electronic and steric factors favour attack meta to OMe.
Exception & Its Reason When the substituent is distant (para-alkyl) or purely electron-releasing and sterically small, neither factor operates and about equal amounts of two isomers form (p-chlorotoluene gives m- and p-toluidine, about 1 : 1). When the substituent is an anion (O−) the meta product forms exclusively because the two negative charges are placed as far apart as possible.

Show answer
Three (o-, m- and p-), through 3-methylbenzyne and 4-methylbenzyne.
Concept check. Why does 2-bromo-1,3-dimethylbenzene not react with sodium amide?
Show answer
Both positions ortho to bromine are substituted by methyl groups, so there is no ortho hydrogen to remove and no aryne can form.

10.4 Regiochemistry with substituted arynes

The nucleophile can add to either carbon of an unsymmetrical aryne, so cine substitution occurs: the entering group may take the position adjacent to the one the leaving group vacated. Two factors decide the site: an electronic one and a steric one.

  • Electronic. A substituent that is inductively electron-withdrawing (OMe, for example) makes the carbanion formed on the adjacent carbon more stable. The nucleophile therefore adds to the aryne carbon that is farther from the substituent, so that the anion forms next to it. The same effect also makes the proton next to the halogen easier to remove. The anion is in the ring plane, so it does not interact with the π system; oxygen acts only inductively.
  • Steric. The attack occurs in the plane of the ring, so it is very sensitive to a substituent on the neighbouring carbon, which hinders attack alongside it.

A single product results from o-chloroanisole (and also from the m-isomer): the amine group ends up meta to OMe. The method makes meta-substituted anilines whose substituents are both ortho/para directing. A simple alkyl substituent in the para halide is too far from the triple bond to have much effect, and mixtures of about 50 : 50 are formed (p-chlorotoluene gives m- and p-toluidine). If the substituent is an anion (O−) the meta product is formed exclusively, because the incoming anion is put as far from the existing anion as possible.

Example 35 Adv

How many products?

How many different toluidines can form from (a) o-, (b) m-, (c) p-chlorotoluene with sodium amide in liquid ammonia? Name the arynes.

Show solution
  1. Number the carbon bearing CH3 as C-1.
  2. (a) o-Chlorotoluene (Cl at C-2): the only hydrogen ortho to Cl is at C-3, so the aryne is 3-methylbenzyne (C-2≡C-3). Amide adds at C-2 (o-toluidine) or C-3 (m-toluidine): 2 products.
  3. (b) m-Chlorotoluene (Cl at C-3): ortho hydrogens at C-2 and C-4 give 3-methylbenzyne (C-2≡C-3: o- and m-toluidine) and 4-methylbenzyne (C-3≡C-4: m- and p-toluidine). Products o-, m-, p-: 3 products.
  4. (c) p-Chlorotoluene (Cl at C-4): the ortho hydrogens at C-3 and C-5 are equivalent, giving 4-methylbenzyne only (m- and p-toluidine): 2 products.
Example 36 Adv

Benzyne or SNAr?

(i) 1-Chloro-2,4-dinitrobenzene reacts with aqueous hydroxide at room temperature, but chlorobenzene needs sodium amide in liquid ammonia. (ii) 4-Chloronitrobenzene gives 4-nitroaniline with ammonia. (iii) 2-Chloro-1,3-dimethylbenzene is unreactive towards sodium amide. Explain each.

Show solution
  1. (i) The two nitro groups (ortho and para) stabilise the negative charge of the addition intermediate by delocalising it onto oxygen, so the addition–elimination (SNAr) mechanism is easy even with a weak nucleophile. Chlorobenzene has no such group, so only the elimination–addition route is open, and it needs a very strong base.
  2. (ii) The nitro group activates the ring towards addition of the nucleophile at the carbon that carries chlorine, so substitution occurs at that carbon (ipso) with no aryne.
  3. (iii) Both ortho positions carry methyl groups, so there is no ortho hydrogen to remove and no benzyne can form.
Example 37 Adv

Trapping benzyne

What are the products when benzyne is generated in the presence of (a) furan, (b) anthracene?

Show solution
  1. (a) Furan is the 4π diene and benzyne the 2π component: the [4+2] adduct 1,4-dihydro-1,4-epoxynaphthalene, C10H8O.
  2. (b) Anthracene reacts as a diene across its central ring (C-9 and C-10): the cage hydrocarbon triptycene, C20H14.
  3. In the orbital description the two flat molecules approach in orthogonal planes: the in-plane π bond of benzyne interacts with the π orbitals of the diene.
