1. Structure, classification and nomenclature

An alcohol is a compound in which an OH group is attached to an sp3 carbon. The oxygen is sp3 hybridised with two lone pairs; the C–O–H angle in methanol is about 109°, close to tetrahedral (water is 104.5°, because the larger alkyl group opens the angle slightly). The C–O bond is about 142 pm and is polar (O is more electronegative), so alcohols have a dipole moment of about 1.7 D (methanol 1.7 D, ethanol 1.7 D). Classification by the carbon bearing OH

  • Primary (1°): the carbinol carbon carries one carbon substituent (CH3OH is usually grouped here too): ethanol, butan-1-ol.
  • Secondary (2°): two carbon substituents: propan-2-ol.
  • Tertiary (3°): three carbon substituents: 2-methylpropan-2-ol.

Special classes

  • Allylic alcohol: OH on the sp3 carbon next to C=C (prop-2-en-1-ol, CH2=CH–CH2OH). Ionises easily because the allyl cation is resonance stabilised.
  • Benzylic alcohol: OH on the sp3 carbon next to a benzene ring (phenylmethanol, C6H5CH2OH).
  • Vinylic alcohol (enol): OH on an sp2 carbon, C=C–OH. Almost always unstable; it tautomerises to the carbonyl compound (keto–enol tautomerism). Vinyl alcohol is not isolable; it becomes ethanal.
  • Phenols: OH on an aromatic ring carbon (Part II). They are not “alcohols” in behaviour.
  • Monohydric, dihydric, trihydric: one, two or three OH groups. Dihydric alcohols are glycols (vicinal if the OH groups are on adjacent carbons). Two OH groups on the same carbon (gem-diol) are normally unstable and lose water to give C=O, unless the carbon also bears strong electron-withdrawing groups: chloral hydrate CCl3CH(OH)2, ninhydrin hydrate and the hydrate of mesoxalic ester are isolable.

Trap Do not call an alcohol “allylic” only because it has a double bond somewhere. The OH-bearing carbon must be directly attached to a C=C carbon. But-3-en-1-ol, CH2=CH–CH2–CH2OH, is a homoallylic (ordinary 1°) alcohol and gives a slow Lucas test.
