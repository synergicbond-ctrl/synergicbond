13. Chemical tests that distinguish functional isomers

Because functional isomers have different groups, simple test-tube reactions separate them. The key tests are listed below, together with a few drawn examples.

  • Tollens' test (ammoniacal silver nitrate, warm): aldehydes give a silver mirror; ketones do not. Methanoic acid also reduces it because it retains an H-C=O unit (formate esters are usually reported to do so too, after hydrolysis in the alkaline reagent). Distinguishes ethanal from propanone, and aldehydes from isomeric ketones.
  • Fehling's test: aliphatic aldehydes give a red precipitate; benzaldehyde and ketones do not.
  • Iodoform test (I2/NaOH): positive for CH3CO-R or CH3CH(OH)-R (and ethanal/ethanol), giving yellow CHI3. Distinguishes butan-2-one from pentan-3-one, propan-2-ol from propan-1-ol.
  • Lucas test (ZnCl2/conc. HCl): tertiary alcohols turbid at once, secondary in about 5 minutes, primary only on heating. Separates isomeric butanols.
  • NaHCO3: effervescence with carboxylic acids only (not with phenols, alcohols or esters).
  • Hinsberg test (C6H5SO2Cl, aqueous NaOH): a primary amine gives a sulfonamide that dissolves in alkali; a secondary amine gives an insoluble sulfonamide; a tertiary amine does not react.
  • Bromine water: alkenes, alkynes decolourise it; phenols give a white precipitate of 2,4,6-tribromophenol; alkanes, larger cycloalkanes and benzene do not react in the dark.
  • Neutral FeCl3: phenols and enols give a violet, blue or green colour. 2,4-DNP: yellow-orange precipitate with aldehydes and ketones.

Trap. Ketones such as propanone give the iodoform test but not Tollens'; ethanal gives both. Benzaldehyde gives Tollens' but not Fehling's. Phenol dissolves in NaOH but gives no effervescence with NaHCO3.
