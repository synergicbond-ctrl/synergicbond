49. Mechanism: the reduction ladder of nitrobenzene (6 electrons, 6 protons)

Each two-electron step is the transfer of electrons from the metal (Sn, Fe, Zn) and protons from the medium:

  • PhNO2 → (2 e−, 2 H+, −H2O) → PhN=O → (2 e−, 2 H+) → PhNHOH → (2 e−, 2 H+, −H2O) → PhNH2.
  • In acid the last step is fast (protonation of the N–OH group makes water a leaving group), so no intermediate accumulates. In neutral NH4Cl the reduction of PhNHOH is slow and it can be isolated. In alkali PhNHOH is not protonated, and its nucleophilic N attacks the electrophilic N=O of nitrosobenzene: PhN(OH)–N(O−)Ph → loses water → azoxybenzene PhN(O)=NPh, then further 2 e− steps give azobenzene and hydrazobenzene.
+ alkaline: N attacks N=O, −H₂O⟶
Condensation of nitrosobenzene with phenylhydroxylamine to azoxybenzene (basic medium)

Why does acidic medium give a complete reduction? In acid, PhNHOH is protonated on O and ionises (nitrenium-like character), and the products (aniline) are removed as anilinium salt, so no nucleophilic N is left to condense with PhNO.
