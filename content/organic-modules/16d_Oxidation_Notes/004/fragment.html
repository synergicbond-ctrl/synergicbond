5. Baeyer–Villiger oxidation: migratory aptitude and regiochemistry

A ketone is converted into an ester, and a cyclic ketone into a lactone, by a peroxy acid such as mCPBA, peroxyacetic acid or trifluoroperoxyacetic acid (or H2O2 with a Lewis acid). One oxygen atom is inserted between the carbonyl carbon and one of its two substituents. The reaction is an oxidation of 2 e− per ketone.

  1. The peracid adds to the carbonyl group to give the tetrahedral Criegee intermediate R–C(OH)(R′)–O–O–C(=O)R″.
  2. In the rate-determining step one substituent migrates with its bonding pair from carbon to the adjacent oxygen as the O–O bond breaks and a carboxylate leaves; the group that best stabilises positive charge migrates.
  3. Loss of the proton from the hydroxyl gives the ester R–C(=O)–OR′, in which the migrated group is now bonded to oxygen.

Migratory aptitude follows the ability to stabilise positive charge: tertiary alkyl > cyclohexyl > secondary alkyl ≈ benzyl > phenyl > primary alkyl > methyl, with hydrogen usually migrating best of all. This is a general trend, not an exact ranking. The migrating carbon retains its configuration, so a stereocentre in the migrating group is unchanged. In an unsymmetrical ketone, the oxygen is therefore inserted next to the group of higher aptitude. For most aldehydes the hydrogen migrates and the carboxylic acid is formed (electron-rich aryl aldehydes can instead give aryl formates or phenols).

Reactant:

Reagent: mCPBA, CH2Cl2

Product:

Ring expansion to ε-caprolactone (seven-membered ring).

Reactant:

Reagent: CF3CO3H or mCPBA

Product:

Phenyl migrates in preference to methyl: phenyl acetate (not methyl benzoate).

Reactant:

Reagent: mCPBA

Product:

Tertiary alkyl migrates: tert-butyl acetate.

Key idea. To predict a Baeyer–Villiger product: rank the two groups on the carbonyl carbon, put the oxygen between the carbonyl carbon and the higher-ranked group, and keep the stereochemistry of the migrating carbon.
Trap. In cycloalkanones the oxygen is inserted into the ring so that the ring grows by one atom. The migrating group is not always the larger group; compare the substitution at the α-carbons, for instance in 2-methylcyclohexanone the more substituted α-carbon CH(CH3) migrates.
