21. Wittig reaction (C=O → C=C)

Reagents: a phosphonium ylide Ph3P=CR2 + an aldehyde or ketone → alkene + Ph3P=O. Making the ylide: Ph3P + R–CH2–X (SN2, primary halide) → Ph3P+–CH2R X− → strong base (n-BuLi, NaH, NaNH2, t-BuOK) removes the acidic α-H (pKa ≈ 22) → ylide Ph3P+–C−HR ↔ Ph3P=CHR. Mechanism: the nucleophilic ylide carbon attacks the carbonyl carbon; the betaine collapses to a four-membered oxaphosphetane, which fragments (retro-[2+2]) to alkene + Ph3P=O. The very strong P=O bond is the driving force. The C=C forms exactly where the C=O was: this is the Wittig's great advantage over dehydration or elimination (no regio-mixture; no migration of the double bond).

Stereochemistry: non-stabilised ylides (R = alkyl, H) with aldehydes give mainly the Z-alkene (kinetic, salt-free). Stabilised ylides (R = CO2Et, COR, CN) are less reactive but give the E-alkene (thermodynamic control, reversible betaine). The Horner–Wadsworth–Emmons modification (phosphonate carbanion, easier removal of the water-soluble phosphate) also gives E-alkenes.

Shortcut Retro-design: break the target alkene C=C and decide which carbon is the ylide carbon. Prefer the ylide from the less hindered primary (or methyl) halide and the more hindered side as the carbonyl. Methylenecycloalkanes and terminal alkenes (R2C=CH2) are made from the ketone + Ph3P=CH2, the only way to put the double bond exo to a ring with certainty.
Example 30 Main

Retrosynthesis

Show how (a) 2-methylbut-1-ene and (b) 1-phenylpropene can each be made by a Wittig reaction.

Show solution
  1. (a) CH2=C(CH3)CH2CH3: break at the C=CH2 bond: butanone (CH3COC2H5) + Ph3P=CH2 (from CH3Br + PPh3, then base).
  2. (b) PhCH=CHCH3: break at the C=C: PhCHO + Ph3P=CHCH3 (from bromoethane) → mainly the Z isomer with a non-stabilised ylide under salt-free conditions.
Example 31 Adv

Wittig vs elimination

Treatment of 2-methylcyclohexanone with Ph3P=CH2 gives one alkene only. Name it. Why would acid-catalysed dehydration of 1,2-dimethylcyclohexanol give a mixture?

Show solution
  1. Wittig gives 1-methyl-2-methylenecyclohexane (2-methyl-1-methylenecyclohexane): C=O is replaced by C=CH2; the double bond cannot migrate.
  2. Dehydration of the tertiary alcohol goes through the tertiary cation and produces the more substituted alkenes (1,2-dimethylcyclohexene) and the exocyclic isomers: a mixture, with the position of the double bond decided by stability, not by design.
