◆Concept

This part next develops the fractional contribution of boundary particles. This is the counting language used throughout unit-cell stoichiometry.

1. Sharing Fractions

PositionNumber of cells sharing the pointContribution to one cell
Corner81/8
Edge centre41/4
Face centre21/2
Body-centre / any strictly interior point11

◆Concept

The fraction is determined by how many adjacent unit cells share that geometrical point.

2. Representative Fractional Coordinates

  • Corner: (0,0,0) and translated equivalents.
  • Edge centre: (1/2,0,0), (0,1/2,0), (0,0,1/2), etc.
  • Face centre: (1/2,1/2,0), (1/2,0,1/2), (0,1/2,1/2), etc.
  • Body centre: (1/2,1/2,1/2).

3. General Counting Rule

∑Formula

N_eff = Σ (number at a position × sharing fraction).

◆Concept

Apply this species-by-species. Never reduce the chemical formula until every contribution and every removal has been accounted for.

⚠JEE Advanced traps & edge cases

  • A visible corner sphere is not one full atom of that unit cell.
  • A face-centred particle contributes 1/2, while an edge-centred particle contributes 1/4.
  • A particle lying strictly inside the cell contributes 1 even if it lies on a body diagonal.

▶Solved examples

Worked example 1

Example 1 — Mixed positions

A unit cell has atoms at 8 corners, all 12 edge centres and one body centre. Find the effective number.

  1. Corners: 8×1/8 = 1.
  2. Edges: 12×1/4 = 3.
  3. Body centre: 1.

Total effective particles = 5.

Worked example 2

Example 2 — Two species

A occupies all corners and B all face centres. Find the simplest formula.

  1. A = 8×1/8 = 1.
  2. B = 6×1/2 = 3.

Formula = AB₃.
