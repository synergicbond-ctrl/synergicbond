RM-020 [Application] · Master sheet Q20 · Key: a · Status: Partially verified (values for fused systems not in opened text)

Correct order of resonance energy (kcal mol⁻¹): (a) phenanthrene > anthracene > naphthalene > benzene (b) benzene > naphthalene > anthracene > phenanthrene (c) naphthalene > phenanthrene > anthracene > benzene (d) anthracene > phenanthrene > naphthalene > benzene

Full solution
  1. Benzene ≈ 36 kcal mol⁻¹ (Ref. [2], p. 351, opened).
  2. Fused systems have larger total resonance energy: naphthalene > benzene, and the sheet’s values rise through anthracene and phenanthrene.
  3. Option (a) lists these from largest to smallest, which matches the increasing trend with fusion. The fused-system values are not in the opened text, so this item is only partially verified.

Answer: (a)

RM-021 [Foundation] · Master sheet Q21 · Key: (see sheet key) · Status: Verified (counting; standard resonance)

C–O bond order in the carbonate ion CO₃²⁻: (a) 1.00 (b) 1.33 (c) 1.50 (d) 2.00

Full solution
  1. Three equivalent resonance contributors; four C=O units share among three bonds.
  2. Bond order = 4/3 ≈ 1.33.

Answer: (b)

RM-023 [Application] · Master sheet Q23 · Key: (see sheet key) · Status: Verified

Which carbocation is stabilised by both resonance and hyperconjugation? (a) CH₂=CH–CH⁺–CH₃ (b) C₆H₅–CH₂⁺ (c) CH₃⁺ (d) CH₂=CH⁺

Full solution
  1. (a) is an allylic cation (resonance over three carbons) and carries a CH₃ with α-H (hyperconjugation).
  2. (b) has resonance but its α-carbon is sp²: no α-H.
  3. (c) and (d) have neither effect.

Answer: (a)

RM-024 [Foundation] · Master sheet Q24 · Key: (see sheet key) · Status: Verified (standard terminology)

Hyperconjugation is also known as: (a) Baker–Nathan effect (b) electromeric effect (c) inductomeric effect (d) steric inhibition of resonance

Full solution
  1. The Baker–Nathan effect is the hyperconjugative electron-release order named after its discoverers.
  2. Electromeric is a different, temporary effect; (d) is a separate phenomenon.

Answer: (a)

RM-026 [Application] · Master sheet Q26 · Key: (see sheet key) · Status: Verified (standard definitions; Ref. [2] Ch. 1)

Correct statement(s) about resonance (one or more correct): (a) contributors have the same number of paired electrons (b) all contributors have the same atomic positions (c) the hybrid is always more stable than every contributor (d) equivalent contributors make equal contributions

Full solution
  1. Resonance moves only electrons: paired-electron count and nuclear positions are constant, so (a) and (b) are true.
  2. The hybrid is lower in energy than each contributor (c) is true.
  3. Equivalent contributors have equal weight (d) is true.

Answer: a, b, c, d

RM-027 [Application] · Master sheet Q27 · Key: (see sheet key) · Status: Verified

Which carbocation(s) are stabilised by resonance with a lone pair? (a) CH₃–O–CH₂⁺ (b) H₂N–CH₂⁺ (c) CH₃–CH₂⁺ (d) CH₂=CH–O–CH₂⁺

Full solution
  1. Each of (a), (b), (d) has an O or N lone pair on the atom attached to the cationic carbon, so resonance (octet-completing) is possible.
  2. (c) is an alkyl cation: hyperconjugation only, no lone pair.

Answer: a, b, d

RM-028 [Application] · Master sheet Q28 · Key: (see sheet key) · Status: Verified

Which species show a +M effect? (a) –OCH₃ (b) –NHCOCH₃ (c) –Cl (d) –CN

Full solution
  1. Lone-pair donors onto the ring: OCH₃, NHCOCH₃ (amide N shares with C=O but still donates), and Cl (weak +M).
  2. –CN is −M.

Answer: a, b, c

RM-031 [Application] · Master sheet Q31 · Key: (see sheet key) · Status: Verified

Correct statement(s) about homolytic cleavage: (a) it needs heat or light and gives radicals (b) the bond that gives the more stable radical pair has the lower BDE (c) it is favoured in non-polar solvents (d) each atom takes both electrons

Full solution
  1. (a) true: heat, light or peroxide initiate radical homolysis.
  2. (b) true: weaker bond → more stable radicals (Ref. [2], Table 4-2 BDEs).
  3. (c) true: non-polar media favour homolysis over ionisation.
  4. (d) false: each atom takes one electron.

Answer: a, b, c

Correct order(s) of C–H BDE: (a) CH₃–H > (CH₃)₃C–H (b) CH₂=CH–H > CH₃CH₂–H (c) CH₂=CH–CH₂–H < CH₃CH₂–H (d) C₆H₅CH₂–H < CH₃–H

RM-034 [Application] · Master sheet Q34 · Key: (see sheet key) · Status: Verified

Which processes start with heterolysis? (a) SN1 hydrolysis of t-BuCl (b) E1 elimination of t-BuBr (c) photochlorination of CH₄ (d) peroxide-initiated addition of HBr to propene

Full solution
  1. SN1 and E1 both begin with ionisation of the C–X bond to R⁺ + X⁻ (heterolysis).
  2. Photochlorination and peroxide-initiated HBr addition are radical chains starting with homolysis.

Answer: a, b
