A. Carbocations (Questions 95 to 112)

Question 95 Advanced MCQ, one or more

More stable than isopropyl

The gas-phase hydride affinity of the isopropyl cation is 247 kcal mol−1. Which of the following cations have a smaller hydride affinity (are more stable)? (A) benzyl, 233 (B) ethyl, 274 (C) 1,1-dimethylallyl, 225 (D) vinyl, 287 (E) tropylium, 200 (F) allyl, 256.

Show answer

A, C and E. A smaller hydride affinity means a more stable cation. Benzyl (233), 1,1-dimethylallyl (225) and tropylium (200) are below 247. Ethyl (274), vinyl (287) and the unsubstituted allyl cation (256) are above it: the primary allyl cation is less stable than isopropyl, though methyl substituents on the allyl system make it more stable.

Question 96 Advanced

Dehydration

2-Methylcyclohexanol is heated with H3PO4. Name the major alkene and explain which cations and eliminations are involved.

Show answer

Protonation and loss of water give the secondary cation at C-1 (E1). A 1,2-hydride shift from C-2 (the carbon bearing CH3) gives the tertiary 1-methylcyclohexyl cation; loss of H+ gives the most stable alkene, 1-methylcyclohexene (major, Zaitsev); 3-methylcyclohexene and methylenecyclohexane are minor.

H₃PO₄, Δ⟶
1-Methylcyclohexene (Zaitsev, trisubstituted)
Question 97 Advanced

Three hydrations

3-Methylbut-1-ene is converted into an alcohol by (i) H2O/H2SO4; (ii) Hg(OAc)2/H2O then NaBH4; (iii) BH3·THF then H2O2/NaOH. Name the major alcohol in each case.

Show answer

(i) 2-Methylbutan-2-ol (secondary cation → 1,2-H shift → tertiary cation). (ii) 3-Methylbutan-2-ol (Markovnikov, mercurinium ion does not rearrange). (iii) 3-Methylbutan-1-ol (anti-Markovnikov, concerted).

Question 98 Advanced

Neighbouring group

Neopentyl bromide in hot ethanol gives 2-ethoxy-2-methylbutane, never (CH3)3CCH2OEt. Why does the unrearranged ether not form?

Show answer

The primary neopentyl cation is too unstable to form, and SN2 is blocked by the quaternary β-carbon. Ionisation occurs with simultaneous methyl migration (assisted by the C–C σ bond), giving the tertiary cation (CH3)2C+CH2CH3; ethanol captures that ion, so only the rearranged ether results.

Question 99 Advanced

Product count

3-Methylbut-1-ene + HBr. How many different products (counting enantiomers separately) are expected from the carbocation pathway?

Show answer

Unrearranged: 2-bromo-3-methylbutane (stereocentre at C-2: 2 enantiomers). Rearranged: 2-bromo-2-methylbutane (achiral: 1). Total 3 products (2 constitutional isomers).

Question 101 Advanced MCQ, one or more

Statements on cations

Which statements are correct?

(A) The cyclobutylcarbinyl cation rearranges to the cyclopentyl cation, relieving ring strain. (B) The tricyclopropylmethyl cation (pKR+ −2.3) is more stable than the triphenylmethyl cation (pKR+ −6.63). (C) Neopentyl bromide ionises to a free primary carbocation that is captured by solvent. (D) 1-Bromoadamantane undergoes SN2 substitution readily.

Show answer

A and B. (C) is false: ionisation is accompanied by methyl migration, so the product has the tert-pentyl skeleton. (D) is false: backside attack is impossible at a bridgehead; 1-bromoadamantane reacts only by SN1, and slowly because the 1-adamantyl cation cannot become fully planar.

Question 102 Advanced

Stereochemistry

(S)-2-Bromooctane is treated with NaOH in aqueous acetone (i) at high [OH−] and (ii) after solvolysis in a polar protic solvent without added nucleophile, as (S)-3-bromo-3-methylhexane. Predict optical activity of the alcohols.

Show answer

(i) 2-Bromooctane is a secondary halide with strong nucleophile: SN2 gives (R)-octan-2-ol, inversion, optically active. (ii) 3-Bromo-3-methylhexane ionises: planar cation, racemic 3-methylhexan-3-ol (optically inactive; slight excess of inversion may remain).

Allylic cation

Buta-1,3-diene reacts with HCl at −78 °C. Write the cation, and list both products.

3-Chlorobut-1-ene (1,2) and 1-chlorobut-2-ene (1,4)

Question 104 Advanced

Aromatic cation

Cycloheptatriene loses hydride to a hydride acceptor to give a salt of formula C7H7+X−. Name the cation and explain why this C–H bond is so easily cleaved heterolytically, using the Hückel energies of section 4.6.

Show answer

The tropylium (cycloheptatrienyl) cation. Removing hydride from the sp3 CH2 leaves a planar ring of seven p orbitals with 6 π electrons. The Hückel energies show that the ring cation is more stable than the open-chain heptatrienyl cation by 0.93β (aromatic); the cation has the lowest hydride affinity in the data set (200 kcal mol−1) and a pKR+ of +4.7.

Question 105 Advanced

Pinacol regiochemistry

2,3-Diphenylbutane-2,3-diol is treated with acid. Which group migrates, and what is the product?

H⁺⟶
3,3-Diphenylbutan-2-one
Show answer

Question 106 Advanced

Cation sequence

A student writes: "1-pentanol + HBr (heat) gives 2-bromopentane via a primary cation." Criticise.

Show answer

A primary cation is too unstable to form; the reaction proceeds mainly by SN2 on the protonated alcohol, giving 1-bromopentane as the major product (little rearrangement). Only with rearrangement-prone substrates (neopentyl, or when a concerted shift can accompany ionisation) are skeletal changes seen.

Question 107 Advanced

Dienone

4,4-Dimethylcyclohexa-2,5-dienone in dilute acid gives a phenol. Name it and give the migrating group.

Show answer

3,4-Dimethylphenol, formed by 1,2-methyl shift of a methyl from C-4 to C-3 in the protonated dienone (a cation delocalised at C-3/C-5), followed by loss of H+ to aromatise.

Question 108 Advanced

Pinacol-type ring expansion

1-Cyclobutylethanol with HBr gives 1-bromo-1-methylcyclopentane (see text). By what mechanistic step does the ring expand, and give two reasons for the driving force.

Show answer

1,2-Alkyl (ring-carbon) shift to the adjacent cationic carbon. Drivers: relief of the ring strain of cyclobutane (about 26 kcal mol−1) in forming cyclopentane (about 6), and, after a further 1,2-hydride shift, conversion of the secondary ring-expanded cation into a tertiary cation.

Question 109 Advanced

Carbocation versus SN2

Which of these substrates will give predominantly rearranged product with ethanol under solvolysis conditions? (A) 3-bromo-2,2-dimethylbutane (B) 2-bromo-2-methylbutane (C) bromocyclohexane (D) (bromomethyl)cyclobutane

Show answer

A and D. (A) the secondary cation next to a quaternary C(CH3)3 rearranges by methyl shift to a tertiary cation; (D) ring expansion (cyclobutylcarbinyl → cyclopentyl). (B) tertiary cation: no better option; (C) secondary cyclohexyl: no better cation.

Question 110 Advanced

Friedel–Crafts

Benzene is alkylated with 2-methylpropan-1-ol and H2SO4. Predict the main product.

Show answer

tert-Butylbenzene: protonated alcohol loses water with hydride shift to give the tertiary cation (CH3)3C+, which attacks benzene (isobutyl cation → t-butyl by 1,2-H shift).

Question 111 Advanced

Ion pair

Optically active 1-phenylethyl chloride is dissolved in aqueous acetone. Its optical rotation is lost faster than chloride is liberated as free Cl− (the titrimetric solvolysis rate). What does this tell you about the mechanism?

Show answer

Racemisation (polarimetric rate) faster than solvolysis (titrimetric rate) shows internal return: the contact ion pair R+Cl− often collapses back to the covalent chloride, with the chloride returning to either face of the planar cation, before the nucleophile (solvent) can capture the cation. The intermediate is a planar (nearly) carbocation in an ion pair.

Question 112 Advanced

Kinetics of SN1

If the concentration of t-BuBr is tripled and [OH−] doubled, by what factor does the rate of SN1 hydrolysis change?

Show answer

Rate = k[t-BuBr]; tripled concentration gives a factor of 3; [OH−] has no effect.

Question 114 Advanced

Order of acidity

Arrange in decreasing acidity (DMSO pKa in brackets): toluene (43), diphenylmethane (32.3), triphenylmethane (30.6), cyclopentadiene (18.1), fluorene (22.6), diethyl malonate (16.4). Explain the trend within the hydrocarbons.

Show answer

Diethyl malonate (16.4) > cyclopentadiene (18.1) > fluorene (22.6) > triphenylmethane (30.6) > diphenylmethane (32.3) > toluene (43). Each additional phenyl group delocalises the negative charge further (toluene, diphenylmethane and triphenylmethane); cyclopentadiene and fluorene give anions with an aromatic cyclopentadienide ring (6 π electrons); malonate delocalises the charge onto the oxygen atoms of two carbonyl groups.

Question 122 Advanced

Alkylation

Acetone is converted into its enolate with one equivalent of LDA and then treated with benzyl bromide. Name the product. Why is LDA preferred to NaOEt for clean mono-alkylation?

Show answer

4-Phenylbutan-2-one. LDA is a much stronger base than the enolate (diisopropylamine is a far weaker acid than a ketone), so deprotonation is essentially complete and irreversible: no free ketone remains for the enolate to attack (no aldol) and the product is not deprotonated again by an excess of base. With NaOEt, whose conjugate acid has a pKa of about 16 against about 20 for the ketone, only a small equilibrium concentration of enolate is present together with the ketone, so aldol addition and further alkylation compete.

Question 123 Advanced

Cross Cannizzaro

Formaldehyde and benzaldehyde are heated with conc. NaOH. Predict the products and explain which is oxidised.

Show answer

Sodium formate and benzyl alcohol. Formaldehyde (more reactive carbonyl, forms the hydrate anion and then the hydride donor) is oxidised; benzaldehyde accepts the hydride and is reduced.

Question 124 Advanced

Benzilic acid

Predict the product of benzil with KOH followed by acidification. What migrates?

Benzilic acid, (C6H5)2C(OH)COOH. HO− adds to one C=O; a phenyl group migrates (1,2-shift) to the adjacent carbonyl carbon as the alkoxide pushes electrons back; the resulting carboxylate is protonated on work-up.

Question 125 Advanced

Phenylmagnesium bromide is added to ethylene oxide and the mixture is hydrolysed. What is formed and how many carbons were added to the Grignard carbon chain?

Show answer

Question 126 Advanced

Carbonyl reactivity

Why does the Grignard reagent not add to the ester group of a molecule when the Reformatsky zinc reagent is used instead?

Show answer

RZnX is far less basic and nucleophilic than RMgX: the zinc enolate of α-bromo ester adds to aldehyde or ketone C=O but does not attack the less electrophilic ester C=O, so ester groups (including its own) survive.

Question 132 Advanced

Bond energies

Estimate ΔH for C2H6 + Cl2 → C2H5Cl + HCl using bond dissociation enthalpies (kcal mol−1): C2H5–H 101, Cl–Cl 57, C2H5–Cl 85, H–Cl 103. Calculate also ΔH of the first propagation step.

Show answer

Question 137 Advanced

Ether peroxides

Why do ethers such as diethyl ether form hydroperoxides in air, and at which carbon?

Show answer

Question 157 Advanced

Methoxy aryne

o-Chloroanisole is treated with NaNH2 in liquid ammonia and gives a single methoxyaniline. Name it, name the aryne, and explain (electronic and steric reasons) why only one isomer forms.

Show answer

m-Anisidine (3-methoxyaniline), through 3-methoxybenzyne. The amide ion could attack either carbon of the aryne. Attack at the carbon meta to OMe places the new carbanion on the carbon next to the oxygen-bearing carbon, where the inductive electron withdrawal of oxygen stabilises it (the carbanion is in the ring plane and cannot interact with the π system, so oxygen acts only inductively); the same effect favours removal of the proton next to chlorine in the first step. Sterically, attack away from the OMe group is easier because the nucleophile must approach in the plane of the ring alongside the substituent.

Question 158 Advanced

Inductive direction in an aryne

Using the argument for 3-methoxybenzyne, predict the regiochemistry of addition of amide ion to 3-(trifluoromethyl)benzyne and name the aniline expected.

Show answer

The trifluoromethyl group withdraws electrons inductively, as does OMe. The nucleophile is expected to add to the aryne carbon meta to CF3, so that the developing carbanion lies on the carbon adjacent to the CF3-bearing carbon, where it is stabilised inductively. The expected product is 3-(trifluoromethyl)aniline. (The argument is a prediction by analogy; the observed ratio depends on the substrate and conditions.)

Calculate the rate constant and half-life of a first-order reaction with ΔG‡ = 75 kJ mol−1 at 298 K. (kBT/h = 6.21 × 1012 s−1.)

Question 162 Advanced

Selectivity from ΔΔG‡

By how much must the transition state leading to the desired product be lower in free energy (298 K) than the competing one for a 99 : 1 product ratio under kinetic control?

Show answer

ΔΔG‡ = RT ln 99 = 8.314 × 298 × 4.595 = 11 385 J = 11.4 kJ mol−1 (2.7 kcal mol−1).

Question 163 Advanced

Reading a profile

A reaction profile has these relative energies (kJ mol−1): reactant 0, TS1 +80, intermediate +30, TS2 +60, product −40. Find (a) the rate-determining step, (b) Ea for the reverse of step 1, (c) overall ΔH, (d) ΔH of step 1.

Show answer

(a) Step 1: barrier 80 versus 30 for step 2 (from the intermediate). (b) 80 − 30 = 50 kJ mol−1. (c) −40 − 0 = −40 kJ mol−1. (d) +30 − 0 = +30 kJ mol−1 (endothermic). The highest TS relative to the reactant is TS1, so it is the rate-determining step.

Question 164 Advanced

Curtin–Hammett

Conformers A (0) and B (+8 kJ mol−1) interconvert rapidly. A reacts via a TS at +60 and B via a TS at +55 kJ mol−1. What is the product ratio PB/PA at 298 K?

Show answer

By Curtin–Hammett the ratio depends only on the difference in TS energies: exp(5000/(8.314 × 298)) = 7.5 : 1 in favour of the product from the less stable conformer B (despite its small population).

Question 167 Advanced

Diels–Alder endo

Cyclopentadiene + maleic anhydride at 25 °C gives the endo adduct; heating the adduct to 190 °C gives the exo adduct. Interpret.

Show answer

Endo is kinetic (secondary orbital overlap lowers the TS) and exo is thermodynamic (less steric strain). At 190 °C the retro-Diels–Alder makes the reaction reversible, converting endo into the more stable exo adduct.

The reverse Ea also falls by 20 kJ mol−1; K is unchanged (ΔG° unchanged).
