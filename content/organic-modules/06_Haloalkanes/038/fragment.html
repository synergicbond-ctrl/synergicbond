37. Energy profiles, the Hammond postulate and what they predict

Reaction-coordinate diagrams are the quickest way to compare mechanisms. In each diagram the horizontal axis is the progress of the reaction, the vertical axis the free energy; a maximum is a transition state, a valley an intermediate.

Key idea The SN2 profile has one barrier, so any change in the stability of the transition state (sterics, nucleophile, leaving group) changes the rate directly. The SN1 profile has two barriers; the first (ionisation) is higher, so the rate depends only on the substrate, leaving group and solvent, and not on the nucleophile that is only involved in the second step.
Hammond postulate: the transition state of a step resembles the species (reactant or product/intermediate) closer to it in energy. An endothermic step has a late (product-like) TS; an exothermic step has an early (reactant-like) TS. Consequences in this chapter:
  • Ionisation in SN1 is highly endothermic: its TS resembles the carbocation, so cation stability (3° > 2° > 1°) controls the rate.
  • Radical bromination (H-abstraction endothermic) has a late TS resembling the alkyl radical, so it is selective for 3° C–H; chlorination (exothermic) has an early TS and low selectivity.
  • E2 with a poor leaving group (F, N+R3) has a TS with carbanion character (the proton is mostly transferred first): Hofmann orientation. E2 with a good leaving group (I, Br) has an alkene-like TS: Zaitsev.

The variable E2 spectrum: at one extreme the C–H bond breaks well before C–X (E1cB-like); in the middle it is synchronous; at the other the C–X bond breaks first (E1-like). Poor LG + strong base moves toward E1cB-like; good LG + weak base toward E1-like.
