3.3 Enzymes

Enzymes are biological catalysts, almost all proteins (some RNA: ribozymes). They are highly specific (the substrate fits the active site: lock-and-key, refined to induced fit), extremely efficient (106–1017-fold acceleration), act under mild conditions (≈ 37 °C, pH 7), and are inhibited by specific molecules. Optimum pH and temperature: pepsin pH ≈ 2, trypsin ≈ 8, salivary amylase ≈ 6.8; most human enzymes denature above ≈ 45–50 °C.

  • Cofactors: metal ions (Zn2+ in carbonic anhydrase, Mg2+ in kinases) and coenzymes (organic: NAD+, FAD, coenzyme A, thiamine pyrophosphate, pyridoxal phosphate, biotin), many derived from vitamins. Apoenzyme + cofactor = holoenzyme.

Kinetics (Michaelis–Menten)

Formula v = Vmax[S]/(KM + [S]); at [S] = KM, v = Vmax/2; Lineweaver–Burk: 1/v = (KM/Vmax)(1/[S]) + 1/Vmax; kcat = Vmax/[E]total
  • Competitive inhibitor (resembles substrate, binds the active site): raises apparent KM, Vmax unchanged; overcome by more substrate. Examples: malonate on succinate dehydrogenase; sulfanilamide vs p-aminobenzoic acid; ethanol vs methanol for alcohol dehydrogenase (ethanol is given to treat methanol poisoning).
  • Non-competitive inhibitor (binds elsewhere): lowers Vmax, KM unchanged; irreversible inhibitors (organophosphates on acetylcholinesterase, penicillin on transpeptidase) bind covalently.
  • Many enzymes are regulated allosterically; first step of a pathway is often feedback-inhibited by its end product.
Trap Enzyme action lowers the activation energy; it does not change the equilibrium constant or ΔG of the reaction. An enzyme-catalysed reaction speeds up with temperature only until the enzyme denatures (a bell curve).
Example 56 Main

α-Helix dimensions

An α-helical segment contains 72 residues. Calculate (a) the number of turns, (b) the length along the axis, (c) the number of intra-chain hydrogen bonds approximately.

Show solution
  1. (a) 72/3.6 = 20 turns.
  2. (b) Rise 1.5 Å/residue: 72 × 1.5 = 108 Å = 10.8 nm (check: 20 × 5.4 Å = 108 Å).
  3. (c) Each residue (except the last four) donates one N–H to the C=O four residues earlier: 72 − 4 = 68 H-bonds.
Example 57 Adv

Fully extended β-strand

How long is a fully extended polypeptide of 300 residues (3.5 Å per residue)? Compare with the same chain as an α-helix.

Show solution
  1. β-strand: 300 × 3.5 = 1050 Å; α-helix: 300 × 1.5 = 450 Å. The helix is ≈ 2.3 times shorter.
Example 58 Adv

Minimum molar mass

Analysis of a protein shows 0.34 % Fe by mass (one Fe per haem). Find (a) the minimum molar mass, (b) the actual molar mass if each protein molecule has 4 Fe.

Show solution
  1. (a) 55.85 / 0.0034 = 16 400 g mol−1 (minimum: one Fe per molecule).
  2. (b) With four Fe: 4 × 16 400 = 65 700 g mol−1, close to the actual value for haemoglobin (64 500).
Example 59 Adv

Michaelis–Menten numbers

An enzyme has KM = 2.0 mM and Vmax = 80 µM s−1. Find v at [S] = 6.0 mM, and the substrate concentration at which v = 0.9 Vmax.

Show solution
  1. v = 80 × 6.0/(2.0 + 6.0) = 60 µM s−1.
  2. v = 0.9 Vmax → 0.9 = S/(KM + S) → S = 9 KM = 18 mM.
Example 60 Main

Identify the test

(a) A tripeptide gives violet colour with CuSO4/NaOH. (b) A protein turns yellow with conc. HNO3. (c) A protein gives black precipitate with Pb(OAc)2 after boiling with NaOH. Name the tests and what each shows.

Show solution
  1. (a) Biuret: peptide bonds. (b) Xanthoproteic: aromatic amino acid residues (nitrated Tyr/Phe/Trp). (c) Lead sulphide test: cysteine/cystine sulfur.
Example 61 Adv

Denaturation

Which are correct? (a) Boiling an egg changes its primary structure. (b) Denaturation by urea breaks H-bonds and hydrophobic interactions. (c) Casein curdles at pH ≈ 4.6. (d) Heavy metal ions denature proteins by binding to –SH groups.

Show solution
  1. (a) ✗ Denaturation leaves the primary structure intact. (b) ✓ (c) ✓ casein pI ≈ 4.6. (d) ✓ (Hg2+, Pb2+).
Example 62 Adv

Enzyme inhibition

Two inhibitors are tested on an enzyme. Inhibitor I raises the apparent KM from 2 mM to 5 mM without changing Vmax; inhibitor II lowers Vmax to half without changing KM. Classify them and say which can be overcome by raising the substrate concentration.

Show solution
  1. I: competitive (KM up, Vmax same): overcome by excess substrate.
  2. II: non-competitive (Vmax down, KM same): not overcome by substrate.
Example 63 Adv

Sickle-cell mutation

The change Glu → Val in haemoglobin β-chain corresponds to which single-base change in the DNA coding strand if Glu is GAG and Val is GUG (RNA codons)? How does the net charge of the protein at pH 7.4 change per β-chain?

Show solution
  1. mRNA GAG → GUG: the middle base A → U in RNA, i.e. A → T at the DNA coding strand (GAG → GTG; template strand CTC → CAC).
  2. Glu (−1 at pH 7.4) is replaced by neutral Val: each β-chain loses one negative charge (net +1 change); haemoglobin S has two fewer negative charges per tetramer and migrates more slowly toward the anode in electrophoresis at pH 8.6 than HbA.
