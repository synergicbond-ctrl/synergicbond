4.1 Acidity

ROH ⇌ RO− + H+. The strength depends on the stability of the alkoxide ion, which in water is governed mainly by solvation and the inductive effect of R.

Order of acidity (standard JEE statement): H2O > CH3OH > primary > secondary > tertiary alcohols. (Numerical tables disagree about whether methanol is slightly more or less acidic than water: it depends on whether 14.0 or 15.7 is used for water; the examination answer is that water is more acidic than alcohols and methanol is the most acidic alcohol.) Why the order falls from 1° to 3°. Alkyl groups are electron donating (+I): they push electron density onto oxygen and destabilise RO−. The larger the alkyl group, the more hindered the oxygen is to solvation by water molecules, so the anion is less stabilised. tert-Butoxide has the bulkiest hydrocarbon shield, hence the weakest acid, the strongest conjugate base.

Trap In the gas phase the order reverses: (CH3)3COH > (CH3)2CHOH > C2H5OH > CH3OH > H2O. Without solvent, big alkyl groups stabilise the anion by polarisability (dispersion). The solution order is a solvation effect, not an inductive effect alone. A question that says “in the gas phase” reverses the answer.
Substituent effects: an electron-withdrawing group close to OH (−I) increases acidity (CF3CH2OH, ClCH2CH2OH, HOCH2CH2OH is slightly more acidic than ethanol); the effect decreases with distance. Alkenyl/aryl groups on the carbinol carbon (allylic, benzylic) make the alcohol a little more acidic (pKa of benzyl alcohol 15.4). Ethylene glycol, glycerol and 1,2-diols are more acidic than monohydric alcohols (pKa of glycol 14.2).
Reaction with bases: alcohols are too weakly acidic to react appreciably with NaOH (the equilibrium RO− + H2O ⇌ ROH + OH− lies to the right), but they react with Na, K, NaH, NaNH2 and Grignard reagents (stronger bases than RO−). Phenols dissolve in NaOH, alcohols do not (see Part II).
Example 10 L1 Foundation

Order of acidity

Arrange in decreasing acidity: CH3OH, (CH3)3COH, H2O, CF3CH2OH, C2H5OH, HC≡CH.

Show solution
  1. CF3CH2OH (12.4) is the strongest: −I of CF3.
  2. Then H2O (15.7/14) > CH3OH (15.5) > C2H5OH (15.9) > (CH3)3COH (18) in the usual (solution) order.
  3. Ethyne (25) is the weakest here.
  4. Order: CF3CH2OH > H2O > CH3OH > C2H5OH > (CH3)3COH > HC≡CH.
Example 11 L2 Main

Acidity and structure

Which is more acidic: (a) ethanol or ethane-1,2-diol; (b) 2-chloroethanol or 3-chloropropan-1-ol; (c) propan-2-ol or prop-2-en-1-ol (allyl alcohol)?

Show solution
  1. (a) In HOCH2CH2OH the second OH is electron-withdrawing (−I) and stabilises the alkoxide, and an intramolecular H-bond to the O− also helps: glycol is more acidic (14.2 vs 15.9).
  2. (b) The −I effect of Cl falls rapidly with distance (each CH2 cuts it roughly in third): 2-chloroethanol is more acidic.
  3. (c) The sp2 carbon is more electronegative than sp3 and exerts −I through CH2: allyl alcohol is slightly more acidic than propan-2-ol (which also has two +I methyl groups).
