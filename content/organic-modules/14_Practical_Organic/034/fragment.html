Why the Lucas test works. HCl/ZnCl2 converts ROH to RCl through an SN1 pathway; the alkyl chloride is insoluble in the aqueous reagent and makes it turbid. The rate follows the stability of the carbocation: 3° (hyperconjugation, +I) > 2° > 1°. Allyl and benzyl cations are stabilised by resonance.

HYF Lucas observations: tert-butyl alcohol turbid immediately; isopropyl alcohol after about 5 minutes; n-butanol: clear at room temperature (turbid only on heating). Mnemonic: 3° immediate, 2° five minutes, 1° nothing.

Victor Meyer test in detail. 1° alcohol → RCH2I → RCH2NO2. HNO2 gives a nitrolic acid RC(=NOH)NO2, which in NaOH gives a red sodium salt. 2° nitroalkane R2CHNO2 gives a pseudonitrole R2C(NO)NO2, blue in NaOH-ether. 3° nitroalkane has no α-hydrogen and no reaction occurs; the solution is colourless.

Example 17 Adv

Distinguishing three isomeric alcohols

Three isomers of C4H10O (butan-1-ol, butan-2-ol, 2-methylpropan-2-ol) are labelled X, Y, Z. Z is turbid immediately in Lucas reagent; Y becomes turbid in 5 minutes and gives a yellow ppt with I2/NaOH; X shows no change. Identify X, Y, Z.

Show solution
  1. Z: immediate turbidity ⇒ tertiary: 2-methylpropan-2-ol.
  2. Y: turbid in 5 min ⇒ secondary; and yellow CHI3 ⇒ CH3CH(OH)–: butan-2-ol.
  3. X: no reaction at room temperature ⇒ primary: butan-1-ol (no iodoform because no CH3CH(OH) unit).
Exception & Its Reason Lucas test fails for methanol and ethanol (primary, no carbocation route) and gives no visible turbidity for alcohols above about C6, because their solubility in the reagent is poor and the layer is already cloudy. Allyl and benzyl alcohols behave like 3° because their cations are resonance-stabilised.
Concept check

Predict the Lucas result for tert-butyl alcohol, butan-2-ol and butan-1-ol at room temperature.

Show answer

tert-Butyl alcohol: turbid at once; butan-2-ol: turbid in about 5 min; butan-1-ol: no turbidity.
