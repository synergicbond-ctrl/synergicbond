15. Acylation and sulphonylation

15.1 Acylation

1° and 2° amines are acylated by acyl chlorides, acid anhydrides and (slowly) esters; 3° amines are not. The N–H is replaced by N–COR (nucleophilic acyl substitution). A base (pyridine, aqueous NaOH – Schotten–Baumann conditions, or excess amine) is added to remove HCl and keep the amine free; otherwise half of the amine is lost as RNH3+Cl−.

CH₃COCl, pyridine⟶
Acetylation of aniline: acetanilide (protects the NH₂ group)
C₆H₅COCl, aq. NaOH⟶
Benzoylation (Schotten–Baumann): N-methylbenzamide
  • The product amide is less basic and less nucleophilic than the amine (lone pair delocalised into C=O), so acylation stops at one acyl group; it also deactivates the ring in aromatic amines compared with free –NH2 (but –NHCOCH3 is still an o/p director).
  • Acylation is used to (i) protect –NH2 during nitration/oxidation, (ii) separate/identify amines (crystalline derivatives with sharp m.p.), (iii) moderate the reactivity of aniline toward electrophiles.
  • Reactivity of acylating agents: acyl chloride > anhydride >> ester ≈ carboxylic acid (forms a salt).
Shortcut Amides are not basic: the hydrolysis of an amide gives back the amine as its salt (acid hydrolysis) or the free amine (base).
15.2 Hinsberg's test (benzenesulphonyl chloride)

Hinsberg's reagent, benzenesulphonyl chloride C6H5SO2Cl (or p-toluenesulphonyl chloride), reacts with amines in aqueous alkali:

  • 1° amine → N-alkylbenzenesulphonamide, RNH–SO2C6H5. The remaining N–H is acidic (the sulphonyl group stabilises the anion) so the product dissolves in KOH/NaOH as the salt [RN–SO2Ph]−K+. Acidification reprecipitates the sulphonamide.
  • 2° amine → N,N-dialkylbenzenesulphonamide R2N–SO2Ph. No N–H, so it is insoluble in alkali (stays as a precipitate/oil), and it is insoluble in acid too.
  • 3° amine → no stable product (sulphonylammonium forms but is hydrolysed by the alkali back to the amine and benzenesulphonate). The amine remains as an oil, insoluble in alkali but dissolves when acid is added; the sulphonyl chloride is hydrolysed to the water-soluble sulphonate.

15.3 Carbylamine reaction (isocyanide test)

Only 1° amines, aliphatic or aromatic, warmed with chloroform and alcoholic KOH give the isocyanide (carbylamine) R–N+≡C−, which has an extremely foul, penetrating odour. 2° and 3° amines do not react. It is a sensitive test for 1° amines (and, in reverse, a test for CHCl3).

CHCl₃, 3 KOH (alc.), heat⟶
Carbylamine reaction: RNH₂ + CHCl₃ + 3 KOH → RNC + 3 KCl + 3 H₂O

Mechanism. KOH removes H+ from CHCl3 (CCl3−), which loses Cl− to give dichlorocarbene :CCl2. The nucleophilic amine adds to the carbene; two successive E1cB-type losses of HCl (base-promoted) give the isocyanide.

Chloroform is deprotonated and loses chloride to give dichlorocarbene
Key idea Carbylamine reaction requires a free –NH2 group; it is positive for CH3NH2, tert-butylamine, aniline, benzylamine, amino acids with free α-NH2, but negative for (CH3)2NH, (CH3)3N, acetanilide, nitrobenzene.
Isocyanides are hydrolysed by acid (to RNH2 + HCOOH) but are stable to alkali – that is why the reaction can be carried out in KOH. (Part G.)
15.4 Hofmann mustard-oil reaction

A 1° amine is warmed with CS2 (forming a dithiocarbamic acid / salt) and then treated with HgCl2, which removes H2S: the product is an isothiocyanate R–N=C=S, with the pungent mustard-oil smell. 2° amines give a dithiocarbamate but cannot form an isothiocyanate (no second N–H).

15.5 Condensation with aldehydes and ketones
  • 1° amines + aldehydes/ketones (acid catalysis, mild) → imines (Schiff bases) with loss of water: ArNH2 + PhCHO → PhCH=N–Ar. Maximum rate at about pH 4–5 (acid needed to protonate carbonyl and expel water, but not so much that the amine is fully protonated).
  • 2° amines with an enolisable aldehyde/ketone → enamines, C=C–NR2 (used in Stork alkylation).
  • 3° amines do not react.

15.6 Reaction with epoxides, isocyanates and acrylonitrile
  • Epoxides open (SN2 at the less hindered C) to β-amino alcohols: ethylene oxide + NH3 → ethanolamine HOCH2CH2NH2 (and di-/tri-ethanolamine with excess oxide).
  • Isocyanates RN=C=O + R'NH2 → ureas RNHCONHR'.
  • Acrylonitrile: Michael addition of amines (cyanoethylation) → R–NH–CH2CH2CN.
