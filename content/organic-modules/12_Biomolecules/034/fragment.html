6.7 Edman degradation, step by step

+ Step 1 (pH 9): amine adds to C=S⟶ Step 2 (anhydrous acid): S attacks the first C=O⟶ +
Phenylthiocarbamoyl (PTC) peptide → cyclises → anilinothiazolinone (ATZ) + shortened peptide
Step 3: aqueous acid, rearrangement⟶
ATZ → PTH-glycine (phenylthiohydantoin), identified by HPLC
  • Step 1. In mild base the free N-terminal NH2 (not protonated) attacks the central carbon of PhN=C=S: the isothiocyanate carbon has a low π*(C=S) LUMO; the thiourea (PTC) forms.
  • Step 2. In anhydrous trifluoroacetic acid the thiourea sulfur (soft nucleophile, high-lying lone pair) attacks the first peptide C=O (5-exo-trig), the N–C(O) bond to the next residue breaks, giving the thiazolinone and the intact peptide shortened by one residue. Why only the first bond: the five-membered ring needs the sulfur two atoms from the carbonyl; only the N-terminal peptide bond is placed for that cyclisation.
  • Step 3. Aqueous acid isomerises the ATZ to the more stable PTH amino acid (a thiohydantoin with a conjugated N–C(=S)–N).
