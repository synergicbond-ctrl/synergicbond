19. Acetoacetic ester and malonic ester syntheses (alkylation of active methylene)

These are the standard JEE routes to substituted methyl ketones (acetoacetic ester) and substituted acetic acids (malonic ester). The pattern: deprotonate the active CH2 with NaOEt (pKa 11–13, so an alkoxide is enough); alkylate with a primary (or methyl, allyl, benzyl) alkyl halide by SN2; repeat if desired; then hydrolyse and heat so that the β-keto acid or malonic acid loses CO2. Why decarboxylation is easy: β-keto acids and malonic acids fragment through a cyclic six-membered transition state, releasing CO2 and giving an enol that tautomerises to the ketone or acid. Loss of CO2 is at 100–150 °C; the product keeps the other carbonyl.

Trap Concentrated NaOH (and then H+) breaks the acetoacetic ester at the C–C(=O) bond (acid cleavage) and gives carboxylic acids (two molecules of acid) instead of the ketone: CH3COCH(R)COOEt → CH3COOH + RCH2COOH. Dilute NaOH then acid and heat gives the ketone.

Example 27 Main

Synthesis target

Name the starting materials in the acetoacetic or malonic ester route to (a) hexan-2-one, (b) pentanoic acid, (c) 2-methylbutanoic acid.

Show solution
  1. (a) Hexan-2-one = CH3CO–CH2–C3H7: ethyl acetoacetate + NaOEt + 1-bromopropane; then dil. NaOH, H3O+, Δ.
  2. (b) Pentanoic acid = n-C3H7–CH2–COOH: diethyl malonate + NaOEt + 1-bromopropane; then hydrolysis and Δ.
  3. (c) 2-Methylbutanoic acid = CH3CH2CH(CH3)COOH has two substituents on the α-carbon: dialkylate diethyl malonate, first with CH3CH2Br and then with CH3I (each time NaOEt), then hydrolyse and heat.
Example 28 Adv

Cyclic products

Diethyl malonate is treated with two equivalents of NaOEt and 1,3-dibromopropane; the product is hydrolysed and heated. What is formed, and what would 1,2-dibromoethane give?

Show solution
  1. First alkylation: (EtOOC)2CH–(CH2)3Br. The second equivalent of base makes the second enolate, which closes the ring by intramolecular SN2: diethyl cyclobutane-1,1-dicarboxylate. Hydrolysis and decarboxylation: cyclobutanecarboxylic acid (Perkin alicyclic synthesis).
  2. 1,2-Dibromoethane similarly gives cyclopropanecarboxylic acid.
