24.2 Iodoform

Iodoform CHI3: yellow crystalline solid, mp 119 °C (392 K), characteristic antiseptic smell; prepared by the iodoform reaction: I2 + NaOH (NaOI) with ethanol, acetone, or any CH3CO–R / CH3CH(OH)R compound.

I2, NaOH⟶ +
Acetone to iodoform (yellow precipitate) and sodium acetate: the haloform (iodoform) test for CH3CO– or CH3CH(OH)– units.
Key idea Iodoform-positive: ethanol, acetaldehyde, methyl ketones (CH3COR), secondary alcohols of the type CH3CH(OH)R. Methanol, formaldehyde, ethers, diethyl ketone, 1° alcohols other than ethanol: negative. Mechanism: α-triiodination of the CH3 then hydroxide attack on the carbonyl, loss of CI3− (a good leaving group because of the three iodines).
<h4 class="sub">24.3 Freons (chlorofluorocarbons) and the ozone layer</h4>

Freons are chlorofluoroalkanes: CCl3F (Freon-11), CCl2F2 (Freon-12), CHClF2 (Freon-22). Prepared by the Swarts reaction (CCl4 + SbF3). Uses: refrigerants, aerosol propellants, foam blowing. Properties: chemically inert, non-toxic, non-flammable, volatile, liquefied under mild pressure. Ozone layer: in the stratosphere UV photolyses a C–Cl bond: CCl2F2 → •CClF2 + Cl•. Cl• + O3 → ClO• + O2; ClO• + O → Cl• + O2: a catalytic chain (one Cl• destroys thousands of O3 molecules). Banned under the Montreal Protocol (1987); replaced by HFCs/HCFCs.

24.4 DDT and its environmental effects

DDT is a stable, fat-soluble, non-biodegradable insecticide (effective against mosquitoes, lice; malaria control) that bioaccumulates up the food chain; effects on bird egg shells (thinning), banned or restricted in many countries. Its dehydrochlorination by base gives DDE (an E2 reaction).
