6. Grignard reaction (R–MgX as a carbon nucleophile)

Preparation: R–X + Mg, absolutely dry diethyl ether or THF (the ether oxygen lone pairs coordinate Mg and stabilise RMgX). Order of reactivity of the halide: R–I > R–Br > R–Cl >> R–F; aryl and vinyl chlorides need THF. The C–Mg bond is strongly polarised Cδ−–Mgδ+, so RMgX is simultaneously a strong nucleophile and a strong base (conjugate acid R–H, pKa ≈ 45–50). Mechanism with a carbonyl: the carbon of RMgX attacks the C=O carbon through a six-membered cyclic transition state in which a second RMgX or the solvent helps coordinate the oxygen; the alkoxide–MgX salt is protonated in a separate aqueous acid work-up (H3O+ or aq. NH4Cl).

Trap A Grignard reagent cannot be made from a molecule that also carries an acidic or electrophilic group (–OH, –COOH, –NH2, –C≡C–H, C=O, –NO2, –CN). BrCH2CH2OH + Mg does not give a Grignard; the alcohol must first be protected (for example as a tetrahydropyranyl ether). A ketone with a halogen cannot be turned into its own Grignard.
Trap The Grignard is also a base: with a hindered ketone it may simply remove an α-hydrogen (enolisation, recovered starting material after work-up) or transfer a β-hydride (reduction to the secondary alcohol, as with isobutylmagnesium bromide). With an acid chloride or ester the product ketone is *more* reactive than the starting carbonyl, so you cannot stop at the ketone.
Example 7 Main

Design a tertiary alcohol

Show how 3-methylpentan-3-ol can be made from (a) a ketone and one Grignard, (b) an ester and one Grignard.

Show solution
  1. Target: CH3CH2–C(OH)(CH3)–CH2CH3: central carbon with CH3, C2H5, C2H5.
  2. (a) Butanone (CH3COC2H5) + C2H5MgBr, or pentan-3-one + CH3MgBr.
  3. (b) An ester gives a tertiary alcohol in which two identical groups come from the Grignard: use ethyl ethanoate + 2 C2H5MgBr, which gives (C2H5)2C(OH)CH3. Check that the other route fails: ethyl propanoate + 2 CH3MgBr would give 2-methylbutan-2-ol, not the target.
Example 8 Adv

Count the carbons

Ethylene oxide is treated with CH3CH2MgBr; the product is oxidised with PCC; then CH3MgBr (excess, then work-up) is added. Name the final product and state its carbon count.

Show solution
  1. Step 1: attack at the CH2 of the epoxide: CH3CH2–CH2CH2OH = butan-1-ol (4 carbons).
  2. Step 2: PCC stops at the aldehyde: butanal.
  3. Step 3: CH3MgBr adds to the aldehyde: CH3CH2CH2CH(OH)CH3 = pentan-2-ol (5 carbons).
Example 9 Adv

Active hydrogen estimate

0.30 g of an organic compound C2H6O2 reacts with excess CH3MgI and evolves 224 mL of CH4 at STP. How many active hydrogens per molecule, and what is a possible structure?

Show solution
  1. Moles of compound = 0.30/62 = 4.84 × 10-3 mol. CH4 = 0.224 L / 22.4 L mol-1 = 0.0100 mol.
  2. Active H per molecule = 0.0100/0.00484 ≈ 2. Hence two –OH groups: ethane-1,2-diol (HOCH2CH2OH).
Example 10 Adv

Stereochemistry of Grignard addition

Racemic 2-phenylpropanal is treated with CH3MgBr. How many stereoisomers of the product alcohol form, and which is the Cram (Felkin–Anh) major?

Show solution
  1. The product 3-phenylbutan-2-ol has two stereocentres: up to 4 stereoisomers (two diastereomeric pairs of enantiomers).
  2. The carbonyl is attacked from the side of the smallest group on the neighbouring stereocentre (H), with the large phenyl anti to the incoming nucleophile (Felkin–Anh/Cram model). One diastereomeric pair therefore predominates, but both pairs form and the mixture is optically inactive (racemic starting material).
