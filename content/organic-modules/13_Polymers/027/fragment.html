E. IMP collection

  • IMP (§1): functionality ≥ 3 in any monomer leads to network polymers.
  • IMP (§3): Ziegler–Natta catalysts control tacticity; radicals give atactic polymers.
  • IMP (§5): match monomer substituent to mechanism: radical-stabilising X → radical; e-donating X → cationic; e-withdrawing X → anionic; plain α-olefins → coordination.
  • IMP (§5.1): head-to-tail addition dominates; termination is coupling or disproportionation; initiator concentration trades rate for chain length.
  • IMP (§5.4): living polymers give narrow distributions and block copolymers.
  • IMP (§6): cis-1,4-polyisoprene is natural rubber; trans is gutta-percha.
  • IMP (§12): high Xn needs p > 0.99 and exact stoichiometry.
  • IMP (§13): the amide group is planar; hydrogen bonds make polyamides fibres.
  • IMP (§15): resol (base, excess HCHO) cures on heating alone; novolac (acid, excess phenol) needs hexamethylenetetramine.
  • IMP (§18): backbone ester or amide links make a polymer biodegradable.
  • IMP (§21): Mn ≤ Mw; colligative properties give Mn; light scattering gives Mw.

F. HYF collection (no frequency claims are made)

  • LDPE: 350–570 K, 1000–2000 atm, peroxide/O2; HDPE: 333–343 K, 6–7 atm, TiCl4 + AlEt3 (§9).
  • Teflon: CF2=CF2, persulphate; non-stick (§9).
  • Orlon: acrylonitrile; artificial wool; carbon-fibre precursor (§9).
  • Perspex: methyl methacrylate (§9).
  • Buna-S: butadiene + styrene; Buna-N: butadiene + acrylonitrile; neoprene: chloroprene (§10.3).
  • Vulcanisation: sulphur, 3–5 % (typical tyre rubber) (§10.2).
  • Nylon-6,6: adipic acid + hexane-1,6-diamine, 553 K; nylon-6: caprolactam, 533 K with water (§13).
  • Terylene: ethylene glycol + terephthalic acid, 420–460 K, Zn(OAc)2/Sb2O3 (§14.1).
  • Bakelite: phenol + HCHO; melamine resin: melamine + HCHO (§15, §16).
  • PHBV: 3-hydroxybutanoic + 3-hydroxypentanoic acids; nylon-2-nylon-6: glycine + 6-aminohexanoic acid (§18).
  • Water lost in a linear chain: 2n − 1 for n repeat units of A–A + B–B (§12.3).
  • Carothers: Xn = 1/(1 − p) (§12.1).

G. MOT Lens and Bonding Insights

  • MOT Lens (§8): radical addition is SOMO–π* or SOMO–π controlled; attack at CH2 where the coefficient is largest and the remaining SOMO is delocalised into X.
  • MOT Lens (§8): alternating copolymerisation of an electron-rich and an electron-poor monomer follows from nucleophilic/electrophilic radical SOMO matching.
  • MOT Lens (§13.2): amine HOMO (nN) into the C=O π* at the Bürgi–Dunitz angle; why acid chlorides are more reactive than acids.
  • Bonding Insight (§13.2): the amide’s N–C–O three-centre, four-electron π system (resonance and MO views agree).
  • MOT Lens (§15): phenoxide HOMO has large coefficients at ortho/para; HCHO LUMO is the C=O π*.
  • MOT Lens (§21A): conjugated π system in polyacetylene forms bands; gap shrinks as the chain lengthens; doping gives carriers.
  • Limits: these are qualitative frontier-orbital arguments; they rank reactivity but do not give numerical rates.

J. Structure and geometry register

polythene polypropene polystyrene PVC PTFE PAN PMMA PVAc
Addition polymers: * is the open valence; each repeat unit has two chain carbons.
nylon-6,6 nylon-6 PET Kevlar polycarbonate
Condensation polymers: the amide, ester or carbonate link is in the chain.
cis-polyisoprene trans-polyisoprene cis-polybutadiene neoprene (trans)
Geometry about the in-chain C=C decides the rubber or plastic character.
  • Tacticity: isotactic (side groups same side), syndiotactic (alternating), atactic (random) (§3).
  • Amide group: planar, trans-like arrangement of the N–H and C=O around the C–N bond in the chain; partial double bond (§13.1).
  • Resol and novolac: phenol rings joined by –CH2– at ortho and para positions; resol also has –CH2OH and –CH2OCH2– links (§15).

N. Graphs and experimental observations

  • Molar-mass distribution curve (§21.1): skewed peak with Mn left of Mv and Mw to the right.
  • Xn versus p (§12.1): grows slowly at first (Xn = 2 at p = 0.5) and then steeply near p = 1: Xn = 10 at p = 0.90, 100 at 0.99, 1000 at 0.999.
  • Nylon rope trick (§13.2): a thin film forms at the interface of the two layers and can be pulled out continuously as a rope.
  • Rubber band warming and the “rubber heat engine” (§10, §19): warmth on stretching; shortening on heating under load.
  • Thermocol (§9): beads containing pentane expand and fuse when steamed.
  • Heating PVC strongly (§9): HCl is evolved and turns moist blue litmus red; the residue darkens (polyene).
  • Bakelite versus novolac melting (§15): resol/novolac melt and dissolve before curing; cured Bakelite does not.
  • Burning of nylon: a smell like burnt hair or horn is observed because of N-containing material; this is an identification clue, not a proof.
