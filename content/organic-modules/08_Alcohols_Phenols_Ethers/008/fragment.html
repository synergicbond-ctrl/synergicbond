3.2 Reduction of carbonyl compounds

Reduction by metal hydrides is nucleophilic attack of hydride (H−) on the carbonyl carbon, followed by protonation of the alkoxide.

Ketone → secondary alcohol
1. LiAlH₄, dry ether 2. H₃O⁺⟶
Aldehyde → primary alcohol
1. LiAlH₄ 2. H₃O⁺⟶ +
Ester → two alcohols (acid part gives 1° alcohol, alkoxy part is released)

Chemoselectivity is a favourite exam theme. NaBH4 reduces an aldehyde or ketone in the presence of an ester. LiAlH4 reduces everything polar. BH3 reduces an acid in the presence of an ester.

Stereochemistry. Reduction of a prochiral ketone, R–CO–R′, with achiral NaBH4 gives a racemic alcohol (hydride attacks either face of the planar C=O equally). Reduction of a cyclic ketone: small hydrides (NaBH4, LiAlH4) attack from the axial direction in unhindered cyclohexanones to give the equatorial alcohol (more stable); 4-tert-butylcyclohexanone gives mainly trans-4-tert-butylcyclohexanol (about 90 %). Bulky hydrides (L-Selectride) attack equatorially and give the axial alcohol. In a chiral molecule the existing stereocentre biases the attack (Cram’s rule), giving diastereomers in unequal amounts.

Trap Reduction of an α,β-unsaturated aldehyde or ketone: NaBH4 and LiAlH4 mostly give the allylic alcohol (1,2-reduction) without reducing C=C; H2/Pd reduces C=C first giving the saturated ketone or alcohol; LiAlH4 at higher temperature can partly reduce C=C. Cinnamaldehyde + NaBH4 gives cinnamyl alcohol (3-phenylprop-2-en-1-ol).
