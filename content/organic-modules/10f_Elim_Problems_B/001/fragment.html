Practice 1 L1 Foundation

Ethyl tert-butyl ether, (CH3)3C–O–CH2CH3, is to be made by a Williamson-type reaction. Which combination is the sensible choice?

(A) Sodium ethoxide with tert-butyl bromide in ethanol

(B) Sodium tert-butoxide with bromoethane

(C) Sodium ethoxide with tert-butyl chloride in DMSO

(D) A mixture of ethanol and tert-butanol with aqueous NaOH, warmed

Show answer
  1. The Williamson synthesis is an SN2 reaction, so the halide should be methyl or primary and the bulk can sit on the alkoxide.
  2. Options A and C put a strong base/nucleophile (ethoxide) next to a tertiary halide. A tertiary carbon cannot be approached from the back, so ethoxide acts as a base and removes a β-hydrogen (E2): the product is 2-methylpropene, not the ether.
  3. Option D has no leaving group at all (NaOH does not convert an alcohol into an ether).
  4. Option B uses the unhindered primary halide CH3CH2Br. Although tert-butoxide is bulky, attack at a primary carbon is still feasible and E2 is not competitive here (bromoethane has only the CH3 hydrogens as β-hydrogens and no stabilised alkene to gain). The ether is formed. Answer: B.

Answer: B

Practice 2 L2 Main

Cyclopentyl tert-butyl ether is required. Which approach is most sensible?

(A) Cyclopentyl bromide with sodium tert-butoxide in tert-butanol under reflux

(B) tert-Butyl bromide with sodium cyclopentoxide

(C) Cyclopentanol with 2-methylpropene and a catalytic amount of H2SO4 at low temperature

(D) Cyclopentanol with tert-butanol and concentrated H2SO4 at 170 °C

Show answer
  1. Both Williamson combinations (A and B) join a bulky alkoxide to a secondary or tertiary halide. A secondary halide with a bulky strong base, and a tertiary halide with any strong base, give E2: A gives cyclopentene, B gives 2-methylpropene.
  2. Option D is a high-temperature dehydration medium; it would convert the alcohols into alkenes.
  3. In option C the alkene is protonated to the tertiary tert-butyl cation (Markovnikov addition) and the alcohol attacks the cation. A cold, catalytic acid keeps E1 from competing, so the ether is the main product. Answer: C.

Answer: C

Practice 3 L1 Foundation

How many of the four constitutional isomers of C4H9Br give only one alkene (ignoring E/Z) when treated with ethanolic KOH?

Show answer
  1. The isomers are 1-bromobutane, 2-bromobutane, 1-bromo-2-methylpropane and 2-bromo-2-methylpropane.
  2. 1-Bromobutane: the only β-carbon is C2, so only but-1-ene forms. 1-Bromo-2-methylpropane: only the CH carbon is β, so only 2-methylpropene. 2-Bromo-2-methylpropane: three equivalent methyl groups, so only 2-methylpropene.
  3. 2-Bromobutane has two different β-carbons (C1 and C3) and gives but-1-ene and but-2-ene, so it does not qualify.
  4. Count = 3.

Answer: 3

Practice 4 L1 Foundation

A tertiary alkyl bromide reacts in aqueous ethanol by a pure E1 mechanism. The substrate concentration is doubled and, at the same time, a weak base is added that raises the base concentration from 0.010 M to 0.030 M (not enough to alter the mechanism). By what factor does the rate of alkene formation change?

Show answer
  1. The rate-determining step of E1 is ionisation of the substrate; the base takes part only in the later, fast proton-loss step.
  2. So rate = k[R–X] and the base concentration does not appear. Tripling the base changes nothing; doubling the substrate doubles the rate.
  3. Factor = 2.

Answer: 2

Practice 5 L1 Foundation

Excess KOH in ethanol converts 2,3-dibromopropanoic acid into potassium propiolate (the salt of propynoic acid). In the last step of the preparation, dilute H2SO4 is added. What is its function and what is the final organic compound?

Show answer
  1. Under the strongly basic conditions the carboxylic acid group exists as its potassium salt, HC≡C–COO− K+.
  2. Dilute H2SO4 is a source of H+; it protonates the carboxylate anion and frees the neutral acid (and converts surplus KOH to K2SO4).
  3. The final organic compound is propynoic (propiolic) acid, HC≡C–COOH.

Answer: It protonates the carboxylate; final compound is propiolic acid, HC≡C–COOH

Practice 6 L2 Main

How many moles of KOH are consumed per mole of 2,3-dibromopropanoic acid, BrCH2–CHBr–COOH, in its complete conversion to potassium propiolate, HC≡C–COO− K+?

Show answer
  1. Step 1 – acid–base: the carboxylic acid is neutralised first, BrCH2CHBrCOOH + KOH → BrCH2CHBrCOO−K+ + H2O. This uses 1 mol of KOH.
  2. Step 2 – first E2: the acidic H on C2 and Br on C3 are removed, giving CH2=C(Br)COO−. This uses 1 mol of KOH (and produces KBr).
  3. Step 3 – second E2 (vinylic): the C3–H and the C2–Br are lost, forming the triple bond HC≡C–COO−. This uses 1 mol of KOH.
  4. Total = 1 + 1 + 1 = 3 mol.

Answer: 3

Practice 7 L1 Foundation

2-Bromo-2-methylbutane (2.50 mol) is treated with sodium ethoxide in ethanol. Assume 80% of the halide undergoes elimination (the rest gives ether), and that the alkene mixture contains 2-methylbut-2-ene and 2-methylbut-1-ene in the ratio 4 : 1. How many millimoles of 2-methylbut-1-ene are formed?

Show answer
  1. Moles of alkene = 0.80 × 2.50 mol = 2.00 mol.
  2. The 4 : 1 ratio means that 1 part in 5 is the Hofmann alkene 2-methylbut-1-ene: 2.00 mol × 1/5 = 0.40 mol.
  3. 0.40 mol = 400 mmol.

Answer: 400

Practice 8 Worked example

Pentan-1-ol is heated with concentrated H2SO4. What is the main alkene obtained?

(A) Pent-1-ene only, because the OH is on C1

(B) Mainly (E)-pent-2-ene, with smaller amounts of (Z)-pent-2-ene and pent-1-ene

(C) 2-Methylbut-2-ene by a methyl shift

(D) Pentane, by reduction

Show answer
  1. A primary carbocation is too unstable to form. The protonated alcohol therefore loses water either in a concerted E2 step or with simultaneous 1,2-hydride shift, giving a secondary cation.
  2. In the acid medium any pent-1-ene formed is itself protonated at C1 (Markovnikov) to the same secondary pent-2-yl cation. Repeated protonation/deprotonation moves the double bond inward.
  3. Proton loss from the pent-2-yl cation follows Zaitsev: the disubstituted pent-2-ene is favoured over pent-1-ene, and the E isomer is more stable than Z, so (E)-pent-2-ene predominates.
  4. A methyl shift (C) is impossible without branching in the skeleton, and nothing reduces the alcohol (D). Answer: B.

Answer: B

Practice 9 L2 Main

Which of the following observations are in favour of an E1 rather than an E2 mechanism for the elimination of a tertiary alkyl halide? (Select all that apply.)

(A) The rate is first order in the halide and does not change when the concentration of a weak added base is varied

(B) Replacing the β-hydrogens by deuterium gives kH/kD close to 1

(C) The alkene : substitution product ratio is the same for the chloride and the bromide under identical solvolysis conditions

(D) The rate increases in direct proportion to the concentration of sodium ethoxide

(E) The geometry of the alkene is fixed by the configuration of the substrate (anti arrangement of H and X)

(F) Alkenes with a rearranged carbon skeleton are found among the products

Show answer
  1. E1 has a slow ionisation step involving only the substrate, so the rate law is rate = k[R–X] (A true; D describes E2 and is false).
  2. The C–H bond is broken after the rate-determining step, so no primary isotope effect is seen (B true).
  3. The product-determining step starts from the carbocation, which has already lost the leaving group, so chloride and bromide give the same ratio (C true).
  4. A carbocation can rearrange to a more stable one before it loses a proton (F true).
  5. Stereospecificity from the anti arrangement is a feature of E2 (E false). Answer: A, B, C, F.

Answer: A,B,C,F

Practice 10 L1 Foundation

A tertiary alkyl bromide reacts in aqueous ethanol to give SN1 (ether and alcohol) and E1 (alkene) products from the same carbocation. Which change will increase the fraction of alkene?

(A) Lowering the temperature from 25 °C to 0 °C

(B) Raising the temperature from 25 °C to 65 °C

(C) Replacing the bromide by the corresponding iodide

(D) Increasing the proportion of water in the solvent

Show answer
  1. Elimination turns one molecule into more particles (alkene + H+ + the other fragments), so it has the more favourable entropy term; raising the temperature increases the elimination fraction (B).
  2. The leaving group has already departed when the cation chooses between losing a proton and being captured by solvent, so changing Br to I does not change the alkene fraction (C wrong).
  3. A more ionising, more nucleophilic solvent (more water) favours capture of the cation, hence substitution (D wrong); lowering the temperature also favours substitution (A wrong). Answer: B.

Answer: B

Practice 11 L1 Foundation

Why is an alkene prepared from an alkyl halide by heating it with a concentrated solution of KOH in ethanol rather than in water? (Select all that apply.)

(A) The less ionising alcoholic medium disfavours the unimolecular SN1/E1 paths, which give mainly substitution, and favours E2

(B) In water, hydroxide is strongly hydrogen-bonded, so it is a poorer base and substitution (alcohol formation) competes more

(C) Elimination cannot occur in water because an alcohol is needed as a reactant

(D) In ethanol, KOH is in equilibrium with a small amount of ethoxide, a stronger base than hydroxide, which also favours elimination

(E) Ethanol turns the halide into a carbocation, which then loses a proton

Show answer
  1. A concentrated strong base in a solvent of low ionising power pushes the system towards bimolecular elimination (A true).
  2. Water solvates the anion strongly and promotes ionisation, so substitution competes (B true).
  3. Elimination does not require an alcohol; statements C and E are false.
  4. In ethanol, the equilibrium KOH + C2H5OH ⇌ C2H5O− + H2O supplies some ethoxide, a stronger base than hydroxide, which favours E2 (D true). Answer: A, B, D.

Answer: A,B,D

Practice 12 L1 Foundation

3-Bromopentane is allowed to undergo E2 elimination at the same temperature under three sets of conditions: (I) NaOH in water, (II) NaOEt in ethanol, (III) KOtBu in DMSO. Which order of increasing E2 rate is correct?

(A) I < II < III

(B) III < II < I

(C) II < I < III

(D) I < III < II

Show answer
  1. In water hydroxide is heavily solvated by hydrogen bonding, which blunts its basicity; it also promotes substitution. It is the slowest of the three (I).
  2. Ethoxide in ethanol is somewhat less solvated and a stronger base (II).
  3. In a polar aprotic solvent such as DMSO the cation is solvated but the anion is left almost bare, and tert-butoxide is the strongest base of the series. Its E2 rate is much higher (III). The bulk of tert-butoxide also suppresses SN2.
  4. Order: I < II < III. Answer: A.

Answer: A

Practice 13 L2 Main

1-Bromo-1-methylcyclohexane is dehydrobrominated with four different base/solvent systems at the same temperature. In which system is the fraction of the exocyclic alkene (methylenecyclohexane) highest?

(A) NaOEt in ethanol

(B) KOH in ethanol

(C) KOtBu in tert-butanol

(D) KOC(C2H5)3 in 3-ethylpentan-3-ol

Show answer
  1. Two alkenes are possible: 1-methylcyclohexene (endocyclic, more substituted, Zaitsev) and methylenecyclohexane (exocyclic, less substituted, Hofmann).
  2. Removing a ring β-hydrogen is sterically more demanding than removing a hydrogen of the freely rotating methyl group. The bulkier the base, the more it prefers the methyl hydrogens.
  3. The size order of the bases is ethoxide ≈ hydroxide < tert-butoxide < triethylcarbinolate, so the Hofmann fraction is highest with the last one. Answer: D.

Answer: D

Practice 14 Worked example

Which route is the best way to make methylenecyclohexane in good yield by an elimination reaction?

(A) 1-Bromo-1-methylcyclohexane with sodium ethoxide in ethanol

(B) (Bromomethyl)cyclohexane with potassium tert-butoxide in tert-butanol

(C) 1-Methylcyclohexanol with concentrated H2SO4, heat

(D) Cyclohexylmethanol with concentrated H2SO4, heat

Show answer
  1. A and C go through (or resemble) the tertiary 1-methylcyclohexyl system, where the more substituted, endocyclic 1-methylcyclohexene is the major product (Zaitsev).
  2. D requires ionisation of a primary alcohol; the cation rearranges (hydride shift to the tertiary 1-methylcyclohexyl cation, or ring expansion), so rearranged alkenes, mainly the endocyclic 1-methylcyclohexene, are obtained rather than methylenecyclohexane.
  3. In B the only β-carbon is the ring CH, so the only alkene possible is methylenecyclohexane. Because the halide is primary the bulky tert-butoxide favours E2 over SN2 (the β-branching also slows SN2). Answer: B.

Answer: B

Practice 15 L2 Main

2-Bromobutane is treated with various nucleophile/base combinations. For which pairs is the stated reagent expected to give the higher substitution : elimination ratio than the reagent it is compared with? (Select all that apply.)

(A) Ethanethiolate (C2H5S−) compared with ethoxide

(B) Acetate compared with ethoxide

(C) tert-Butoxide compared with ethoxide

(D) Azide (N3−) compared with hydroxide

Show answer
  1. Substitution is favoured by good nucleophiles that are weak bases; elimination is favoured by strong, especially hindered, bases.
  2. RS− is a far better nucleophile and a much weaker base than RO− (A true). Acetate is a weak base (conjugate acid pKa about 4.8), so it substitutes (B true). Azide (conjugate acid about 4.7) is a good nucleophile and a weak base compared with hydroxide (D true).
  3. tert-Butoxide is bulkier than ethoxide: it attacks carbon with difficulty and removes a proton readily, so it gives more elimination and a lower S : E ratio (C false). Answer: A, B, D.

Practice 16 Worked example

Compare the substitution : elimination ratio for the reactions of 1-bromopropane (CH3CH2CH2Br) and of its isotopomer CH3CD2CH2Br with sodium ethoxide in ethanol. The ratio for the deuterated bromide is

(A) Lower than for the ordinary bromide

(B) Higher than for the ordinary bromide

(C) The same, since both reactions are bimolecular

(D) Impossible to predict

Show answer
  1. Substitution (SN2) occurs at C1 and does not break any C–H(D) bond at C2. The effect of β-deuterium on its rate is very small (a secondary isotope effect, kH/kD close to 1).
  2. Elimination (E2) breaks the C2–H or C2–D bond in the rate-determining step. A C–D bond is stronger (lower zero-point energy), so this is a primary kinetic isotope effect with kH/kD clearly above 1; the deuterated compound eliminates more slowly.
  3. With the elimination rate lowered and the substitution rate essentially unchanged, kS/kE increases. Answer: B (the S : E ratio is higher).

Answer: B

Practice 18 L2 Main

4-Bromohex-1-ene (CH2=CH–CH2–CHBr–CH2CH3) is warmed with sodium ethoxide in ethanol. Give the major alkene.

Show answer

Practice 19 Worked example

3-Bromo-2,2-dimethylpentane is warmed in aqueous ethanol (no added base). Besides substitution products, a mixture of alkenes is obtained. Give the major alkene.

Show answer
  1. Step 1 – ionisation (E1/SN1 conditions: secondary halide, ionising solvent, no strong base): the C–Br bond breaks to give the secondary cation at C3.
  2. Step 2 – rearrangement: C2 is quaternary and carries three methyl groups. A 1,2-methyl shift from C2 to C3 turns the secondary cation into the tertiary cation (CH3)2C+–CH(CH3)–CH2CH3 (2,3-dimethylpentan-2-yl).
  3. Step 3 – proton loss from the tertiary cation: removal of the C3–H gives the tetrasubstituted alkene 2,3-dimethylpent-2-ene; removal of a CH3 hydrogen gives 2,3-dimethylpent-1-ene. (Proton loss from the unrearranged secondary cation could give a little 4,4-dimethylpent-2-ene, but this cation is short-lived.)
  4. The most substituted (Zaitsev) alkene is the most stable, so the major alkene is 2,3-dimethylpent-2-ene.

Practice 20 Worked example

2,2-Dimethylpropan-1-ol (neopentyl alcohol) is heated with concentrated H2SO4. Give the main alkene.

Show answer
  1. Protonation converts OH into the good leaving group OH2+. Loss of water would leave a primary cation, which is not formed independently; there is no β-hydrogen on the quaternary carbon, so a direct E2 is also impossible.
  2. As water leaves, a methyl group migrates with its bonding electrons from the quaternary carbon to C1 (1,2-methyl shift). The product is the tertiary 2-methylbutan-2-yl cation, (CH3)2C+CH2CH3.
  3. Proton loss from this cation follows Zaitsev: the trisubstituted 2-methylbut-2-ene is the main alkene (2-methylbut-1-ene is minor).

Practice 21 L3 Advanced

2-Methylpentan-3-ol is dehydrated with H2SO4. Consider the alkenes obtainable from the secondary carbocation and from the tertiary carbocation that it forms by a single 1,2-hydride shift (no other rearrangement). How many different alkenes are possible in total, counting E and Z isomers separately?

Practice 24 Worked example

3,3-Dimethylbutan-2-ol is treated with POCl3 in pyridine at 0 °C. Give the alkene formed.

Show answer
  1. POCl3 converts the OH into –OPOCl2, a good leaving group, without the need for strong acid.

Practice 25 L3 Advanced

The salt 2-methylbutan-2-yl-dimethylsulfonium bromide is treated in two ways: (i) it is heated in aqueous ethanol, and (ii) it is treated with concentrated sodium ethoxide in ethanol. The major alkenes are

Practice 28 L3 Advanced

Which sequence converts (Z)-but-2-ene into (E)-but-2-ene?

Practice 30 L2 Main

The three diastereomers of 2-bromo-1,3-diethylcyclohexane are drawn below, in the order I, II, III (I: both ethyl groups and Br on the same face; II: ethyl groups cis to each other, Br on the opposite face; III: ethyl groups trans to each other). Considering chair conformations only, which one cannot undergo E2 dehydrobromination?

(A) I

(B) II

(C) III

(D) All three can

Show answer
  1. E2 needs Br axial and an axial β-hydrogen on the other side of the ring (trans-diaxial).
  2. Isomer I (all cis): in the chair with both ethyl groups equatorial, Br is axial and the axial hydrogens on C1 and C3 are anti to it, so E2 is possible (it gives 1,3-diethylcyclohexene).
  3. Isomer II (ethyls cis, Br trans to both): the chair with both ethyls equatorial has Br equatorial; the ring-flipped chair has Br axial but both ethyl groups axial, so H1 and H3 are equatorial. In neither chair is there an axial β-hydrogen anti to an axial Br, so E2 cannot occur.
  4. Isomer III (ethyls trans): the chair with Br axial has one ethyl axial and one equatorial. The carbon with the equatorial ethyl holds an axial hydrogen that is anti to Br, so E2 gives 1,3-diethylcyclohexene. Answer: II (B).

Answer: B

Practice 31 Worked example

cis- and trans-1,2-Dichlorocyclohexane (first and second drawing, respectively) are each treated with a strong base. Considering only anti-periplanar (trans-diaxial) E2 in chair conformers, which statement is correct?

(A) Only the cis isomer can give 1-chlorocyclohexene; the trans isomer can give only 3-chlorocyclohexene

  1. Both molecules contain the amidine unit N–C=N. Protonation occurs on the imine-type nitrogen, and the resulting cation is delocalised over both nitrogens: N–C=N+H ↔ N+=C–NH. This resonance-stabilised amidinium ion is very stable, so the conjugate acid is very weak, and the free base is strong.
  2. The two rings make the molecule rigid and bulky around the nitrogen atoms, so attack on a carbon centre (SN2) is hindered while the proton can still be reached. Consequently these amidines serve as non-nucleophilic bases that favour E2.

Answer: Protonation gives a resonance-stabilised amidinium ion; the bulky bicyclic frame hinders attack at carbon

Practice 58 L2 Main

1-Chloro-1-ethylcyclopropane is treated with potassium tert-butoxide in tert-butanol. The main alkene is

(A) 1-Ethylcyclopropene

(B) Ethylidenecyclopropane

(C) Vinylcyclopropane

(D) Cyclobutene

Show answer
  1. The β-hydrogens are of two kinds: the CH2 hydrogens of the ethyl group and the hydrogens of the ring CH2 groups.
  2. Loss of a ring hydrogen would put a C=C inside the three-membered ring, forming a cyclopropene, which has a very large angle strain. That route is very unfavourable.
  3. Loss of a hydrogen from the ethyl group gives an exocyclic double bond, ethylidenecyclopropane, which has less additional strain. This is the main product; the less substituted-looking alkene wins because of ring strain, not because of base size. Answer: B.

Answer: B

Practice 59 L1 Foundation

Arrange in decreasing ease of acid-catalysed dehydration: (I) 2-methylpentan-2-ol, (II) pentan-2-ol, (III) pentan-1-ol.

(A) I > II > III

(B) III > II > I

(C) II > I > III

(D) I > III > II

Show answer
  1. The rate-determining step is formation of a carbocation (or a cation-like transition state) from the protonated alcohol.
  2. Stability of the cation: tertiary (I) > secondary (II) > primary (III). A primary alcohol dehydrates only under forcing conditions.
  3. Order: I > II > III. Answer: A.

Practice 60 L2 Main

Which of the following statements about the E2 : SN2 ratio with sodium ethoxide in ethanol are correct? (Select all that apply.)

(A) 2-Bromo-2-methylpropane gives a higher E2 : SN2 ratio than 2-bromopropane

(B) 2-Bromopropane gives a higher E2 : SN2 ratio than bromoethane

(C) 1-Bromo-2-methylpropane gives a higher E2 : SN2 ratio than 1-bromobutane

(D) Bromoethane gives a higher E2 : SN2 ratio than 2-bromopropane

Show answer
  1. SN2 is very sensitive to steric hindrance at the reaction centre and in the β-position; E2 is much less so, and also benefits from a more substituted alkene.
  2. Tertiary > secondary > primary in E2 : SN2 ratio (A and B true, D false).
  3. A methyl branch at the β-carbon (isobutyl) blocks the back-side approach, so the isobutyl halide shows much more elimination than the unbranched butyl halide (C true). Answer: A, B, C.

Answer: A,B,C

Practice 61 L3 Advanced

Which tertiary chloride gives the less substituted alkene as the main product when solvolysed (E1) in hot aqueous ethanol?

(A) 2-Chloro-2-methylbutane

(B) 1-Chloro-1-methylcyclopentane

(C) 2-Chloro-2,4,4-trimethylpentane

(D) 3-Chloro-3-methylpentane

Show answer
  1. E1 elimination from a carbocation normally follows Zaitsev's rule, because the more substituted alkene character in the transition state is more stable. This applies to A, B and D.
  2. Option C is the exception. The carbocation (CH3)2C+–CH2–C(CH3)3 can give 2,4,4-trimethylpent-2-ene, but in that alkene a methyl group on C2 is forced against the very bulky tert-butyl group on C3, and the transition state leading to it is strained.
  3. The less crowded 2,4,4-trimethylpent-1-ene (the Hofmann alkene) therefore predominates. Answer: C.

Answer: C

Practice 62 L2 Main

3-Bromo-3-ethyl-2-methylpentane undergoes E1 elimination (ignore any carbocation rearrangement). How many different alkenes, counting E and Z isomers separately, can be formed?

Show answer
  1. The cation is (C2H5)2C+–CH(CH3)2. Its β-carbons are the two equivalent ethyl CH2 groups and the isopropyl CH.
  2. Loss from an ethyl CH2 gives CH3CH=C(C2H5)–CH(CH3)2, 3-ethyl-4-methylpent-2-ene. The C3 substituents (ethyl, isopropyl) differ, as do those on C2 (CH3, H), so E and Z isomers exist: 2 alkenes.
  3. Loss from the isopropyl CH gives (C2H5)2C=C(CH3)2, 3-ethyl-2-methylpent-2-ene, which has two identical groups on each carbon and no E/Z: 1 alkene.
  4. Total = 2 + 1 = 3.

Answer: 3
