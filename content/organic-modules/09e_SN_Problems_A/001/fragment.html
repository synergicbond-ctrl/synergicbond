Practice 1 L1 Foundation

An SN2 reaction obeys rate = k[RBr][Nu–]. The reaction mixture is diluted with pure solvent until its volume is three times the original (temperature unchanged). By what factor does the initial rate decrease?

Show answer
  1. Dilution to three times the volume divides every concentration by 3: [RBr] → [RBr]/3 and [Nu–] → [Nu–]/3.
  2. The rate depends on the product of the two concentrations, so rate' = k([RBr]/3)([Nu–]/3) = rate/9.
  3. The initial rate decreases by a factor of 9.

Answer: 9

Practice 2 L1 Foundation

For a nucleophilic substitution RBr + Nu– → R–Nu + Br– the experimental rate law is rate = k[RBr][Nu–]. Which conclusion is justified by this kinetic information alone?

(A) The reaction must occur in a single concerted step.

(B) The species RBr and Nu– are both present in the transition state of the rate-determining step, but the absence of intermediates is not proved.

(C) The substrate must be a primary halide.

(D) The product must have the inverted configuration.

Show answer
  1. A second-order rate law tells only the composition of the transition state of the slowest step: one RBr and one Nu–.
  2. Several different multistep sequences (for example a fast pre-equilibrium followed by a slow step) can give the same rate law, so a concerted mechanism is not proved by kinetics alone.
  3. Substrate class and stereochemistry need separate evidence (structure–reactivity trends, stereochemical experiments). Hence only B follows.

Answer: B

Practice 3 L1 Foundation

In the transition state of the SN2 reaction I– + CH3Br → CH3I + Br–, what is the I···C···Br bond angle in degrees?

Show answer
  1. The nucleophile attacks along the C–Br axis from the side opposite the leaving group (backside attack).
  2. In the trigonal-bipyramidal transition state the entering and leaving groups occupy the two axial positions, collinear with the central carbon; the three hydrogens lie in the equatorial plane at 120° to each other.
  3. The I···C···Br angle is therefore 180°.

Answer: 180

Practice 4 L2 Main

The hydrolysis of tert-butyl bromide in aqueous acetone proceeds by the SN1 mechanism. Which description of its free-energy profile is correct?

(A) One transition state and no intermediate.

(B) Two transition states with one carbocation intermediate between them; the transition state of the ionisation step is the higher one.

(C) Two transition states with one carbocation intermediate; the transition state of the step in which water attacks the cation is the higher one.

(D) Two transition states, and the carbocation lies at the top of the profile.

Show answer
  1. SN1 is a stepwise process: R–Br → R+ + Br– (step 1), then R+ + H2O → ROH2+ (step 2), followed by fast proton loss.
  2. Ionisation separates charges and breaks a bond without compensating bond formation, so it has the larger barrier – it is the rate-determining step. Capture of the cation by the solvent needs almost no activation energy.
  3. The carbocation is a true intermediate: it sits in an energy valley between the two maxima, not at a peak. Only B is consistent with this.

Answer: B

Practice 5 Worked example

The effect of raising the solvent polarity (for example, by adding water) on the rate of an SN2 reaction depends on how charge is distributed between the reactants and the transition state. Which of the following predictions are correct?

(A) (CH3CH2)3N + CH3I → (CH3CH2)3NCH3+ I–: the rate increases markedly with polarity.

(B) CH3S– + CH3CH2Br → CH3SCH2CH3 + Br–: the rate decreases slightly with polarity.

(C) (CH3)3S+ + HO– → (CH3)2S + CH3OH: the rate decreases markedly with polarity.

(D) (CH3)2S + CH3I → (CH3)3S+ I–: the rate decreases slightly with polarity.

Show answer
  1. Rule: a polar solvent stabilises whichever state (reactants or transition state) carries the more concentrated charge. If the transition state is more polar than the reactants, rate rises; if it is less polar, rate falls.
  2. A: both reactants are neutral, but the transition state already has partial charges (Nδ+···C···Iδ–). It is stabilised more than the reactants, ΔG‡ drops and the rate rises strongly. Correct.
  3. B: the full negative charge on the thiolate is spread over two partial charges in the transition state (Sδ–···C···Brδ–). The reactants are better solvated than the transition state, so ΔG‡ rises a little and the rate falls slightly. Correct.
  4. C: a full positive and a full negative charge annihilate each other in the transition state, which is almost neutral. Reactants are strongly stabilised by polar solvent, the transition state is not, so the rate falls markedly. Correct.
  5. D: this is the same charge type as A (neutral + neutral → charge-separated transition state), so the rate increases with polarity; D is wrong.
  6. Answer: A, B and C.

Answer: A,B,C

Practice 6 L1 Foundation

For the SN2 reaction of ethyl bromide with a nucleophile at 25 °C, a run with [EtBr] = 0.050 M and [Nu–] = 0.020 M has an initial rate of 3.0 × 10–6 M s–1. A second run at the same temperature uses [EtBr] = 0.10 M and [Nu–] = 0.040 M. What is its initial rate, in units of 10–6 M s–1?

  1. The rate law is rate = k[EtBr][Nu–].

Practice 7 L2 Main

An SN2 reaction, rate = k[RX][Nu–], is run with equal initial concentrations [RX]0 = [Nu–]0 = 0.10 M and k = 5.0 × 10–3 M–1 s–1. Because the concentrations stay equal throughout, the integrated law 1/[RX] = 1/[RX]0 + kt applies. What is the half-life in seconds?

Show answer
  1. Because [RX] = [Nu–] at all times, d[RX]/dt = –k[RX]2, a second-order decay; hence 1/[RX] = 1/[RX]0 + kt.
  2. At the half-life [RX] = [RX]0/2, so 2/[RX]0 = 1/[RX]0 + kt½, i.e. t½ = 1/(k[RX]0).
  3. t½ = 1/(5.0 × 10–3 M–1 s–1 × 0.10 M) = 1/(5.0 × 10–4 s–1) = 2000 s.

Answer: 2000

Practice 8 L1 Foundation

The SN2 reaction of a chiral secondary halide is called stereospecific because

(A) it gives a single product no matter which stereoisomer of the substrate is used.

(B) two stereoisomeric substrates give stereochemically different products – each is converted with inversion.

(C) it always gives a racemic mixture.

(D) it requires a chiral nucleophile.

Show answer
  1. A reaction is stereospecific when the stereochemistry of the reactant determines the stereochemistry of the product. In SN2 every molecule is inverted, so the (R)-substrate gives the product of one spatial arrangement and the (S)-substrate the opposite one.
  2. It is not the case that the same product forms from both (A), nor does racemisation occur (C); a chiral nucleophile is not required (D). Answer B.

Practice 9 L1 Foundation

Which statement correctly distinguishes nucleophilicity from basicity?

(A) Nucleophilicity is a thermodynamic property and basicity a kinetic one.

(B) Nucleophilicity describes how fast a species attacks carbon (it depends on ΔG‡), whereas basicity describes the position of the equilibrium of proton transfer (it depends on ΔG°).

(C) Nucleophilicity and basicity are always identical in order.

(D) Basicity refers only to anions and nucleophilicity only to neutral molecules.

Show answer
  1. Nucleophilicity is kinetic: the rate of attack on a saturated carbon, governed by the free energy of activation.
  2. Basicity is thermodynamic: the equilibrium constant of the acid–base reaction with H+, governed by ΔG°.
  3. The two often parallel each other (same attacking atom) but diverge for atoms of different size in the same group, for sterically hindered species and for solvated ions. Answer B.

Answer: B

Practice 10 Worked example

Which order correctly lists the following in decreasing nucleophilicity toward methyl iodide in ethanol? PhS–, EtO–, PhO–, CH3COO–, EtOH

(A) EtO– > PhS– > PhO– > CH3COO– > EtOH

(B) PhS– > EtO– > PhO– > CH3COO– > EtOH

(C) PhS– > PhO– > EtO– > CH3COO– > EtOH

(D) PhS– > EtO– > CH3COO– > PhO– > EtOH

Show answer
  1. Sulfur is larger, less electronegative and therefore more polarisable than oxygen, and thiolate is much less tightly hydrogen-bonded in a protic solvent. PhS– heads the list.
  2. Among the three oxyanions the attacking atom is the same, so nucleophilicity follows basicity. The conjugate acids have pKa about 16 (EtOH), 10 (PhOH) and 4.8 (CH3COOH): EtO– > PhO– > CH3COO–.
  3. The neutral solvent molecule EtOH is a far weaker nucleophile than any anion. Order: PhS– > EtO– > PhO– > CH3COO– > EtOH (B).
  4. Take-away: compare the attacking atom first (period/size), then basicity within the same atom, and put neutral molecules last.

Answer: B

Practice 11 L2 Main

Hydroperoxide ion, HOO– (pKa of H2O2 ≈ 11.6), and hydroxide ion (pKa of H2O = 15.7) are compared as nucleophiles toward an alkyl halide in water. The correct statement is

(A) HO– is the better nucleophile because it is the stronger base.

(B) HOO– is the better nucleophile although it is the weaker base, because the lone pairs on the adjacent oxygen enhance the reactivity of the attacking atom (the α-effect).

(C) The two are equally nucleophilic because both attack through oxygen.

Practice 12 L3 Advanced

Which statements about the nucleophilicity of halide ions are correct?

(A) Toward CH3Br in the gas phase the order is F– > Cl– > Br– > I–.

(B) Toward CH3I in dimethyl sulfoxide the order is I– > Br– > Cl– > F–.

(C) In methanol, I– is a much better nucleophile than F–, because F– is strongly hydrogen-bonded and less polarisable.

(D) The order of basicity toward H+ (F– > Cl– > Br– > I–) is the reverse of the order of nucleophilicity found in methanol.

Show answer
  1. Gas phase: no solvent shells; the small, charge-dense F– is the most reactive, so the order follows basicity (A true).
  2. DMSO: anions are poorly solvated (no hydrogen bonds), so the protic-solvent order is reversed or strongly levelled: chloride is more reactive than iodide (fluoride is very sensitive to traces of water). Statement B states the protic-solvent order and is false for DMSO.
  3. Methanol: hydrogen bonding stabilises small ions most, and the cost of shedding the solvent shell dominates, so reactivity rises with size and polarisability: I– > Br– > Cl– > F– (C true).
  4. Basicity of the halides (F– strongest) is the reverse of this protic-solvent nucleophilicity order (D true). Answer: A, C, D.

Answer: A,C,D

The nucleophilic reactivity of fluoride ion toward an alkyl bromide in acetonitrile or acetone increases in the order

Practice 17 Worked example

The sodium salt of 2-pyridone is treated with CH3I in DMF, and in a separate experiment the silver salt of 2-pyridone is treated with CH3I in benzene. The principal products are, respectively,

(A) 2-methoxypyridine; 1-methylpyridin-2(1H)-one

(B) 1-methylpyridin-2(1H)-one; 2-methoxypyridine

(C) 1-methylpyridin-2(1H)-one in both cases

(D) 2-methoxypyridine in both cases

Show answer
  1. The 2-pyridone anion is ambident: the negative charge is shared by nitrogen and oxygen, so alkylation can occur on N (giving the N-methyl lactam) or on O (giving the aromatic ether 2-methoxypyridine).
  2. Sodium salt in a dipolar aprotic solvent: the anion is free and the reaction is a true SN2 on CH3I. Methyl carbon of CH3I is a soft electrophile and attack takes place through the softer, more polarisable and more nucleophilic nitrogen: N-methylation.
  3. Silver salt in a non-polar solvent: Ag+ binds iodine and precipitates AgI, so the reacting carbon acquires considerable carbocation (hard, positively polarised) character. It is attacked by the harder, more electronegative end of the anion – oxygen – giving 2-methoxypyridine.
  4. So the pair is N-methyl lactam (Na salt) and 2-methoxypyridine (Ag salt): B.
  5. Take-away: free anions in aprotic solvents attack through the softer, more nucleophilic atom; silver salts push the electrophile toward cation-like character, so the harder end attacks.

Answer: B

Practice 25 Worked example

The conjugate acids of five anions have approximate pKa values: CH3SO3H –2; CF3COOH 0.5; CH3COOH 4.8; C6H5OH 10; H2O 15.7. The decreasing order of leaving-group ability of the corresponding anions in SN2 reactions is

(A) CH3SO3– > CF3COO– > CH3COO– > C6H5O– > HO–

(B) HO– > C6H5O– > CH3COO– > CF3COO– > CH3SO3–

(C) CF3COO– > CH3SO3– > CH3COO– > HO– > C6H5O–

(D) CH3COO– > CF3COO– > CH3SO3– > C6H5O– > HO–

Show answer

Practice 30 Worked example

Four bromides are treated with sodium iodide in acetone under identical conditions. The correct order of decreasing SN2 rate is

(A) 1-bromobutane > 1-bromo-2-methylpropane > 2-bromobutane > 1-bromo-2,2-dimethylpropane

(B) 1-bromobutane > 2-bromobutane > 1-bromo-2-methylpropane > 1-bromo-2,2-dimethylpropane

(C) 2-bromobutane > 1-bromobutane > 1-bromo-2,2-dimethylpropane > 1-bromo-2-methylpropane

(D) 1-bromo-2,2-dimethylpropane > 1-bromo-2-methylpropane > 1-bromobutane > 2-bromobutane
