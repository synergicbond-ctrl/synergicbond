Example 44 Gallery 1 – ranking

Arrange in decreasing basic strength in water: (a) (CH3)2NH, (b) C6H5NH2, (c) CH3CONH2, (d) pyridine, (e) NH3, (f) p-CH3C6H4NH2, (g) C6H5CH2NH2.

Show solution
  1. Sort into families first. Aliphatic sp3 amines: (a), (g), (e). Aryl amines: (b), (f). sp2 ring nitrogen: (d). Amide: (c).
  2. Aliphatic family: dimethylamine (a), pKb 3.27, is the strongest (+I and two N–H bonds for hydration of the ion). Benzylamine (g), pKb 4.70, and ammonia (e), 4.75, are almost equal: the CH2 is +I but the phenyl is −I; on the data (g) is a hair stronger.
  3. Weak bases: pyridine (d) pKb 8.75; p-toluidine (f) 8.92; aniline (b) 9.38. Pyridine's lone pair sits in an sp2 orbital outside the π system, so it is available; in toluidine and aniline the lone pair is delocalised into the ring, and the p-CH3 (+I) makes (f) slightly stronger than (b).
  4. Non-base: acetamide (c) – lone pair delocalised into C=O.
  5. Answer: (a) > (g) > (e) > (d) > (f) > (b) > (c).
Example 45 Gallery 2 – a six-step conversion

Convert aniline into 4-bromobenzoic acid.

Ac₂O⟶ Br₂, AcOH⟶ H₃O⁺, heat⟶ NaNO₂/HCl, 273 K; CuCN⟶ H₃O⁺, heat⟶
Aniline → 4-bromobenzoic acid in six steps
Show solution
  1. Plan. The CO2H must end up para to Br. Bromination of aniline puts Br para to N; the NH2 is then replaced by CN (Sandmeyer) which is hydrolysed to COOH – so the carbon enters exactly where the nitrogen was.
  2. Why protect first. Free aniline with Br2 gives 2,4,6-tribromoaniline; acetanilide gives the para mono-bromo compound.
  3. Check of M: 4-bromobenzoic acid C7H5BrO2, M = 201.0.
Example 46 Gallery 3 – meta oxygen

Prepare m-nitrophenol from benzene. Explain why direct nitration of phenol cannot be used.

HNO₃/H₂SO₄; then fuming HNO₃, 373 K⟶ (NH₄)₂S (Zinin)⟶ NaNO₂/H₂SO₄, 273 K; H₂O, warm⟶
Benzene → m-dinitrobenzene → m-nitroaniline → m-nitrophenol
Show solution
  1. Why not nitrate phenol: OH is an o/p director, so phenol gives o- and p-nitrophenol (and is easily oxidised); the meta isomer is not formed.
  2. Plan: put two meta-related nitro groups on benzene (nitro directs meta); reduce only one with sulphide (Zinin); convert NH2 to OH through the diazonium salt (warm dilute H2SO4, to avoid chloride competing).
  3. Check formula: m-nitrophenol C6H5NO3, M = 139.
Example 47 Gallery 4 – selective acylation

Paracetamol is made by acetylating 4-aminophenol. Explain why N-acetylation occurs and not O-acetylation, and give the formula and molar mass of the product.

4-aminophenol paracetamol
Show solution
  1. The amino group is the better nucleophile: its lone pair (HOMO nN) is higher in energy and less tightly held than an O lone pair (O is more electronegative), and in neutral/mildly acidic conditions the phenolic OH is not ionised. N-Acylation therefore competes far better; the amide formed is stable and does not acylate further.
  2. Product: N-(4-hydroxyphenyl)acetamide, C8H9NO2, M = 151.2 g mol-1. In the presence of a base that converts ArOH to ArO−, O-acylation would compete.
Example 48 Gallery 5 – an azo dye from scratch

Orange II is made from sulphanilic acid and 2-naphthol. Write the diazotisation and coupling steps, the position of coupling and the formula of the dye (as the sodium salt).

+ NaOH, 273 K⟶
Orange II (anion): coupling at C-1 of 2-naphthol
Show solution
  1. Sulphanilic acid (zwitterion, insoluble in acid) is dissolved in aqueous Na2CO3; NaNO2 is added; the solution is poured on to ice/HCl at 273 K to give the diazonium sulphonate (a stable inner salt). 2-Naphthol is dissolved in NaOH (naphthoxide). Coupling occurs at C-1 (α position, ortho to O−, the position of largest HOMO coefficient and the one that keeps the second ring's aromaticity in the σ-complex).
  2. Orange II (sodium salt): C16H11N2NaO4S, M = 350.3.
Example 49 Gallery 6 – structure from numbers

Compound A (C3H9N) consumes 3 mol CH3I per mole. Its quaternary hydroxide, heated, gives an alkene B and trimethylamine. B reacts with HBr to give C (C3H7Br) that, on treatment with KCN and then LiAlH4, gives D. Identify A–D and the number of carbons in D.

Show solution
  1. 3 CH3I → A is a primary amine C3H9N: propan-1-amine or propan-2-amine. Each gives propene (Hofmann), so B = CH3CH=CH2 regardless of which amine was used.
  2. B + HBr (Markovnikov) → 2-bromopropane C = (CH3)2CHBr. KCN → (CH3)2CHCN (2-methylpropanenitrile, 4 C). LiAlH4 → D = (CH3)2CHCH2NH2 (2-methylpropan-1-amine), 4 carbons.
  3. The data do not fix A uniquely (both primary C3H9N amines fit); this is exactly why an exam must give an additional fact.
Example 50 Gallery 7 – numbers

1.07 g of a compound containing only C, H and N gave 3.08 g CO2 and 0.81 g H2O on combustion. In a Dumas experiment 0.214 g of the compound gave 22.4 mL of N2 at STP. Titration shows it is a monoacidic base with M = 107 g mol-1. It gives a stable diazonium salt at 273 K. Identify it (C = 12, H = 1, N = 14).

Show solution
  1. Carbon: 3.08 × 12/44 = 0.840 g → 0.0700 mol. Hydrogen: 0.81 × 2/18 = 0.0900 g → 0.0900 mol.
  2. Nitrogen (Dumas): 22.4 mL = 1.00 × 10−3 mol N2 = 0.0280 g N in 0.214 g, i.e. 13.1 %; in 1.07 g: 0.140 g → 0.0100 mol.
  3. Check: 0.840 + 0.090 + 0.140 = 1.070 g ✓ (no other element).
  4. Ratio C : H : N = 0.0700 : 0.0900 : 0.0100 = 7 : 9 : 1 → empirical formula C7H9N (M = 107 ✓, so molecular formula = empirical formula).
  5. A primary aromatic amine with a stable diazonium salt: a toluidine (o-, m- or p-). Benzylamine (C7H9N) is excluded because it gives no stable diazonium salt.
Example 51 Gallery 8 – separation by acid–base extraction

An ether solution contains benzoic acid, phenol, aniline and nitrobenzene. Describe how to separate the four, and give the species found in each aqueous layer.

Show solution
  1. 1. Extract with aqueous NaHCO3. Benzoic acid (pKa 4.2) is converted to sodium benzoate (aqueous). Phenol (pKa 10) is not deprotonated by bicarbonate (carbonic acid pKa 6.4) and stays in ether. Acidify the aqueous layer with HCl → benzoic acid precipitates.
  2. 2. Extract the ether with aqueous NaOH. Phenol → sodium phenoxide (aqueous). Acidify (CO2 or HCl) → phenol is regenerated.
  3. 3. Extract the ether with dilute HCl. Aniline → anilinium chloride (aqueous). Add NaOH → aniline separates as an oil.
  4. 4. The remaining ether layer contains nitrobenzene (neutral, no acidic or basic centre); evaporate the ether.
  5. Order matters: use the weakest base first for the acid (NaHCO3 before NaOH) so that the carboxylic acid is separated from the phenol.
Example 52 Gallery 9 – losing one carbon without losing the ring

Convert cyclohexanecarboxylic acid into cyclohexylamine. Give two methods and say what happens to the configuration if the ring carbon is a stereocentre.

Show solution
  1. Method 1 (Hofmann): acid → SOCl2 → acid chloride → NH3 → cyclohexanecarboxamide → Br2/NaOH → cyclohexylamine. Method 2 (Curtius): acid chloride → NaN3 → acyl azide → heat (−N2) → cyclohexyl isocyanate → H2O → cyclohexylamine + CO2. (Schmidt: acid + HN3/H2SO4 gives the same product directly.)
  2. In every case the migrating ring carbon moves from C to N with its three substituents intact: retention of configuration if it were a stereocentre. The carboxyl carbon is lost as CO2 or carbonate.
Example 53 Gallery 10 – a titration curve

20.0 mL of 0.100 M ethylamine (pKb 3.29) is titrated with 0.100 M HCl. Calculate the pH (a) before addition, (b) after 10.0 mL, (c) at the equivalence point, (d) after 30.0 mL.

Show solution
  1. pKa of C2H5NH3+ = 14 − 3.29 = 10.71, Kb = 5.13 × 10−4.
  2. (a) x2/(0.100 − x) = 5.13 × 10−4 → [OH−] = 6.8 × 10−3 M (exact quadratic), pOH 2.17, pH = 11.84.
  3. (b) Half-equivalence: [base] = [conjugate acid]; pH = pKa = 10.71.
  4. (c) All amine is C2H5NH3+ at 0.0500 M: [H+] = √(KaC) = √(1.95 × 10−11 × 0.0500) = 9.9 × 10−7; pH = 6.01.
  5. (d) Excess HCl: 1.00 mmol in 50.0 mL = 0.0200 M → pH = 1.70.
  6. The steep part of the curve lies between pH ≈ 8 and 4, so methyl red (4.4–6.2) would be suitable (phenolphthalein would not).
