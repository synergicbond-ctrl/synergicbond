6. Counting the monohalogenation products of an alkane

Procedure (works every time):

  • Step 1: Draw the skeleton and mark every set of equivalent hydrogens using symmetry (rotation about single bonds is free, so the three H's of any CH3 are equivalent; two CH3 on one carbon are equivalent; mirror/rotation symmetry may make groups equivalent).
  • Step 2: Each set gives one constitutional monohalide.
  • Step 3: For each product decide whether it has a stereocentre; chiral products count as 2 if stereoisomers are included (enantiomers; a second centre doubles again).
  • Step 4: Total structural isomers = number of sets of non-equivalent H; total stereoisomers = sum over products.

Isomer count with stereoisomers

How many monochlorination products (including stereoisomers) are possible for (a) 2-methylbutane, (b) 2-methylpentane, (c) 3-methylpentane, (d) 2,2-dimethylbutane?

Show solution
  1. (a) 2-Methylbutane: sets: C-1/C-1′ CH3 (6 H) → 1-chloro-2-methylbutane (chiral C-2: 2); C-2 H → 2-chloro-2-methylbutane (1); C-3 H2 → 2-chloro-3-methylbutane (chiral: 2); C-4 H3 → 1-chloro-3-methylbutane (1). Total 6 (4 structural).
  2. (b) 2-Methylpentane (CH3)2CHCH2CH2CH3: sets: CH3 (×2) → 1-chloro-2-methylpentane (chiral, 2); C-2 H → 2-chloro-2-methylpentane (1); C-3 H2 → 3-chloro-2-methylpentane (chiral, 2); C-4 H2 → 2-chloro-4-methylpentane (chiral, 2); C-5 H3 → 1-chloro-4-methylpentane (1). Total 8 (5 structural).

(c) 3-Methylpentane CH3CH2CH(CH3)CH2CH3: sets: C-1 H3 (two ends) → 1-chloro-3-methylpentane (chiral C-3, 2); C-2 H2 → 2-chloro-3-methylpentane (two stereocentres C-2, C-3: 4 stereoisomers, 4); C-3 H → 3-chloro-3-methylpentane (1); 3-CH3 → 1-chloro-2-ethylbutane (1, achiral: C-2 has two identical ethyl groups). 6.1 Using symmetry: a faster way

For hydrocarbons with a mirror plane or Cn axis, count the unique carbon positions with the number of 13C NMR signals idea: equivalent carbons carry equivalent hydrogens (the converse fails only for diastereotopic H's, which do not matter in the structural count but matter when stereoisomers are counted: if the two H's on a CH2 are diastereotopic, replacing either H gives a different diastereomer).
