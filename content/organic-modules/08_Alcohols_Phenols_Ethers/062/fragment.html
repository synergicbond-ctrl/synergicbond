14.2 Why phenol is less acidic than a carboxylic acid

In a carboxylate ion the negative charge is shared equally by two electronegative oxygens (both resonance structures are equivalent and favourable). In phenoxide the charge is shared with carbon atoms (less electronegative), and the resonance structures disrupt the aromatic sextet. Hence pKa of benzoic acid 4.2, phenol 10.0. Consequences: phenol dissolves in aqueous NaOH (forming sodium phenoxide) but does not liberate CO2 from NaHCO3 (H2CO3 is the stronger acid). Conversely CO2 bubbled through sodium phenoxide solution liberates phenol (the solution turns cloudy): C6H5ONa + H2O + CO2 → C6H5OH + NaHCO3. This is exactly the property used to separate carboxylic acids (soluble in NaHCO3) from phenols (soluble in NaOH only) from neutral compounds (soluble in neither).
