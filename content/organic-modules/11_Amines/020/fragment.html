20. Electrophilic substitution of aniline

20.1 Halogenation

Bromine water reacts with aniline at once at room temperature, without a catalyst, replacing all three activated positions to give a white precipitate of 2,4,6-tribromoaniline (M = 330). The reaction is so fast that it cannot be stopped at the mono-stage; it is used both as a test for aniline (and phenol) and to estimate it gravimetrically.

Br₂ (aq), 3 equiv.⟶
Aniline → 2,4,6-tribromoaniline (white ppt.) – no catalyst needed

To get mono-bromination the activation must be reduced by acetylation: acetanilide → Br2/CH3COOH → p-bromoacetanilide (major; the bulky NHCOCH3 blocks ortho) → H3O+ or OH− (hydrolysis) → p-bromoaniline.

(CH₃CO)₂O⟶ Br₂, CH₃COOH⟶ H₃O⁺, heat⟶
Protect → brominate → deprotect: p-bromoaniline
Key idea Acetylation does three jobs: (i) the lone pair is shared with C=O so the ring is less activated (mono-substitution), (ii) the bulky acetamido group favours para over ortho, (iii) the group is not protonated in nitrating acid, and not oxidised. The acetyl group is removed by hydrolysis at the end.
20.2 Nitration

Direct nitration of aniline with HNO3/H2SO4 fails to give clean p-nitroaniline for two reasons:

  • Nitric acid oxidises aniline to tarry products (and some nitrobenzene/quinones).
  • In the strongly acidic medium aniline is protonated to the anilinium ion (–NH3+), a strongly deactivating meta-director. At 288 K the product mixture is about para 51 %, meta 47 %, ortho 2 % – so a large amount of meta isomer forms even though –NH2 is a para director.
p-nitroaniline (51 %) m-nitroaniline (47 %) o-nitroaniline (2 %)
Direct nitration of aniline in conc. HNO₃/H₂SO₄

Clean route to p-nitroaniline: aniline → acetanilide → HNO3/H2SO4 (p-nitroacetanilide, with some ortho) → hydrolysis (aq. NaOH or H2SO4) → p-nitroaniline. o-Nitroaniline (minor) is separated by steam distillation because it is intramolecularly hydrogen bonded and volatile.

HNO₃, H₂SO₄, 288 K⟶ OH⁻ or H₃O⁺⟶
Acetanilide route to p-nitroaniline

To obtain m-nitroaniline in practice: nitrobenzene → Sn/HCl would reduce the nitro group; instead use m-dinitrobenzene (PhNO2 nitrated with fuming HNO3/H2SO4, 373 K) and then selectively reduce one nitro group with (NH4)2S, or Na2S (Zinin).

(NH₄)₂S or Na₂S⟶
m-Dinitrobenzene → m-nitroaniline (selective reduction of one NO₂)
20.3 Sulphonation

Aniline heated with conc. H2SO4 (453–473 K, "baking process") first forms anilinium hydrogensulphate, which rearranges on heating to p-aminobenzenesulphonic acid (sulphanilic acid). It exists as the dipolar ion (zwitterion) H3N+C6H4SO3−; hence it has a very high melting point (decomposes above 280 °C), is sparingly soluble in water and insoluble in organic solvents, and does not behave as a free amine or as a free sulphonic acid. Sulphanilic acid is the starting material for sulpha drugs (via sulphanilamide) and for methyl orange.

conc. H₂SO₄, 453–473 K⟶
Aniline → sulphanilic acid (zwitterion)
Trap The sulphonic acid group ends up para to N (the large –SO3H avoids the crowded ortho position). Sulphanilic acid will still diazotise (with NaNO2/HCl or NaNO2/Na2CO3 then HCl) and couple, giving methyl orange when coupled with N,N-dimethylaniline.
20.4 Friedel–Crafts reactions fail

Aniline does not undergo Friedel–Crafts alkylation or acylation. The AlCl3 catalyst (a Lewis acid) coordinates with the lone pair on N, forming an adduct with a positive charge on N – a very strongly deactivated ring. Thus the ring is inert, and the catalyst is consumed. The same applies to pyridine. Acetanilide is much less basic and does undergo Friedel–Crafts acylation.

AlCl₃⟶
The Lewis acid ties up the N lone pair, so the ring is deactivated; no Friedel–Crafts reaction
