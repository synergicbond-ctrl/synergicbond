11N · Graphs and experimental observations

No graph is needed for nomenclature. The observations below are standard qualitative tests that identify functional groups and therefore give the suffix of a name.

Experimental Connection Iodoform test. A methyl ketone (CH₃CO–R) and an alcohol of the type CH₃CH(OH)–R give a yellow precipitate of iodoform with iodine and base. The observation identifies the CH₃CO– or CH₃CH(OH)– unit, which is then named as a ketone or alcohol.
Experimental Connection Tollens test. An aldehyde reduces the silver-ammonia complex to a silver mirror; a ketone does not. The result decides whether the suffix is -al or -one.
Experimental Connection Sodium bicarbonate test. A carboxylic acid releases carbon dioxide with sodium bicarbonate, whereas a phenol does not. The observation confirms that the suffix is -oic acid rather than -ol.
