27. Reimer–Tiemann reaction (ortho-formylation of phenols)

Reagents: phenol + CHCl3 + aqueous NaOH (or KOH), 340 K, then H+. Product: salicylaldehyde (2-hydroxybenzaldehyde) as the major product, 4-hydroxybenzaldehyde as the minor product (ortho : para roughly 2 : 1 to 3 : 1; the ortho isomer is favoured by the interaction of the phenoxide Na+ with the incoming dichlorocarbene). Mechanism: (1) OH− deprotonates CHCl3 to −CCl3, which loses Cl− to give dichlorocarbene :CCl2 (the electrophile, with a vacant p orbital and an sp2 lone pair). (2) The phenoxide ring (very electron rich) attacks :CCl2 through its ortho carbon, giving –CHCl2 after proton transfer. (3) Two successive hydrolysis steps (OH− replaces Cl, then loss of Cl− again) convert the ArCHCl2 into the aldehyde ArCHO.

CHCl₃, aq. NaOH, 340 K; then H⁺⟶
Phenol → salicylaldehyde (major, ortho)
CHCl₃, aq. NaOH (minor path)⟶
Para isomer: 4-hydroxybenzaldehyde (minor)
CCl₄, aq. NaOH; then H⁺⟶
With CCl4 in place of CHCl3 the group introduced is –COOH: salicylic acid
Trap The reaction is of phenols (and naphthols, pyrrole, indole), not of benzene, toluene or anisole, because the phenoxide ion is the nucleophile. If both ortho and para are blocked the carbene attacks at the substituted position and a cyclohexadienone results (p-cresol gives 4-dichloromethyl-4-methylcyclohexa-2,5-dienone). With pyrrole the carbene inserts into the ring (Ciamician–Dennstedt) to give 3-chloropyridine.
Example 36 Main

Reimer–Tiemann products

Give the main organic product when (a) phenol, (b) p-cresol (4-methylphenol), (c) 2-naphthol react with CHCl3/NaOH and then H+. Which one gives only ortho product?

Show solution
  1. (a) salicylaldehyde (and a little 4-hydroxybenzaldehyde). (b) The para position is blocked, so only ortho: 2-hydroxy-5-methylbenzaldehyde (5-methylsalicylaldehyde). (c) At the ring position next to OH (C-1): 2-hydroxy-1-naphthaldehyde. Only (b) gives a single product.
