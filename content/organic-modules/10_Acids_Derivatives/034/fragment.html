34. Long-form synthesis and mechanism problems (Advanced)

Practice 380 L3 · Long form

Outline a synthesis of ibuprofen from isobutylbenzene using a Friedel–Crafts acylation and a Grignard carboxylation. Give reagents for each step and state the number of carbons added.

Show answer
  1. (1) Friedel–Crafts acylation: isobutylbenzene + CH3COCl/AlCl3 → 4-isobutylacetophenone (para, because the alkyl group is an ortho/para director and the para position is less hindered).
  2. (2) NaBH4 (or H2/Ni) reduces C=O to CH(OH): 1-(4-isobutylphenyl)ethanol.
  3. (3) HCl (or SOCl2) converts the benzylic alcohol to the benzylic chloride.
  4. (4) Mg, dry ether: Grignard reagent; then CO2; then H3O+: the COOH group is attached to the benzylic carbon. Net: one carbon from CO2 (the other two carbons of the –CH(CH3)COOH unit come from the acetyl chloride).
1. CH₃COCl, AlCl₃ 2. NaBH₄ 3. HCl 4. Mg, ether 5. CO₂, H₃O⁺⟶
Ibuprofen (racemic)
Practice 381 L3 · Long form

Show how to convert phenol into ε-caprolactam (the monomer of nylon-6) and name each intermediate.

Show answer
  1. (1) Phenol + H2/Ni → cyclohexanol. (2) CrO3 or HNO3-based oxidation → cyclohexanone. (3) NH2OH → cyclohexanone oxime. (4) H2SO4 (Beckmann rearrangement): the anti (ring) C–C bond migrates; the six-membered ring expands to the seven-membered lactam ε-caprolactam. (5) Heating caprolactam with a trace of water gives nylon-6.
1. H₂, Ni 2. CrO₃ 3. NH₂OH 4. H₂SO₄⟶
Phenol → caprolactam
Practice 382 L3 · Long form

Starting from benzene and succinic anhydride, prepare 1-tetralone (3,4-dihydronaphthalen-1(2H)-one) (the Haworth synthesis).

Show answer
  1. (1) Benzene + succinic anhydride, AlCl3: Friedel–Crafts acylation (ring-opening of the anhydride) → 4-oxo-4-phenylbutanoic acid. (2) Clemmensen reduction (Zn–Hg, HCl) of the aryl ketone → 4-phenylbutanoic acid. (3) Cyclisation: SOCl2 (acid chloride), then AlCl3 (intramolecular Friedel–Crafts) or polyphosphoric acid → 1-tetralone, a six-membered ring fused to benzene.
+ 1. AlCl₃ 2. Zn(Hg), HCl 3. SOCl₂; AlCl₃⟶

Prepare cinnamyl-type target 3-phenylpropan-1-ol from benzaldehyde and malonic acid (name each step).

Practice 386 L3 · Long form

Show the mechanism of the Dieckmann cyclisation of diethyl adipate step by step.

Show answer
  1. 1. EtO− removes an α-H of one ester (the other α-carbon is five atoms away) → ester enolate. 2. The enolate carbon attacks the carbonyl carbon of the other ester group (five-membered ring closes) → cyclic tetrahedral alkoxide. 3. Loss of EtO− → 2-ethoxycarbonylcyclopentanone. 4. EtO− removes the acidic α-H between the two C=O groups (pKa ≈ 11): this irreversible step drives the reaction. 5. H3O+ work-up gives the neutral β-keto ester.
1. NaOEt 2. cyclisation 3. − EtO⁻ 4. NaOEt (deprotonation) 5. H₃O⁺⟶
Dieckmann: the driving force is the last deprotonation
Practice 387 L3 · Long form

An acid A (C4H6O3) loses CO2 on warming to give a neutral liquid B (C3H6O) that gives a 2,4-DNP derivative and the iodoform test. The ethyl ester of A gives a violet colour with FeCl3. A is obtained from the ester by cold dilute alkali and acidification at 0 °C. Identify A, B and the ester, and explain why A is handled cold.

Show answer
  1. A: C4H6O3 (DoU = 2): COOH and C=O. B (C3H6O): CO2 lost: B = acetone (DNP-positive; CH3CO– gives iodoform). A = 3-oxobutanoic (acetoacetic) acid, CH3COCH2COOH. The ester is ethyl acetoacetate: its enol form gives violet FeCl3.
  2. A is a β-keto acid: it loses CO2 readily through a six-membered cyclic transition state, so it must be isolated cold.
warm, − CO₂⟶

Practice 391 L3 · Long form

A fat is treated first with excess NaOH to give soap and glycerol, then the soap solution is treated with CaCl2. (a) What is the precipitate? (b) What is the effect of adding NaCl to the original soap solution (“salting out”)? (c) Why does the precipitate form but the detergent analogue stays in solution?

Show answer
  1. (a) Calcium stearate (or palmitate/oleate), a water-insoluble scum. (b) NaCl decreases the solubility of the sodium soap (common-ion effect, and lower solvation), so soap precipitates (salting out), a step in industrial soap-making. (c) Calcium and magnesium salts of carboxylic acids are insoluble; the Ca2+ salts of alkylbenzenesulfonates (detergents) are soluble in water, so they remain clear.
Practice 392 L3 · Long form

The compound C5H8O3 is a keto acid that gives the iodoform test and on mild heating does not decarboxylate. When boiled with dilute H2SO4 with Zn(Hg) (Clemmensen conditions) it gives pentanoic acid. Identify it.

Show answer
  1. C5H8O3 (DoU = 2): COOH + C=O. Iodoform-positive: CH3CO–. Not decarboxylated on warming: not a β-keto acid. Clemmensen reduces C=O to CH2: pentanoic acid skeleton (unbranched chain C5) with the C=O at C-4: 4-oxopentanoic acid (levulinic acid), CH3COCH2CH2COOH. The γ-keto acid is stable to warming.
