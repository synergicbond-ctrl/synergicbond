6.12 Acid-catalysed amide and ester hydrolysis and the "tetrahedral intermediate" rule

For any acyl derivative, nucleophilic acyl substitution goes through a tetrahedral intermediate: (i) nucleophile (HOMO) adds to C=O (π*): the carbonyl carbon changes from sp2 to sp3; (ii) the intermediate collapses by expelling the better leaving group (the one whose conjugate acid is the stronger acid, i.e. the one with the lowest σ* energy). Leaving ability: Cl− > RCOO− > RO− ≈ HO− > R2N−: amides are least reactive because the leaving group (amide anion) is the worst and because the amide carbonyl is stabilised by the nitrogen lone pair (6.5). This ordering explains why peptides are stable and why activation (acid chloride, active ester, O-acylisourea) is needed for synthesis.
