10. Acid anhydrides

10.1 Preparation

  • Carboxylate + acyl chloride: CH3COONa + CH3COCl → (CH3CO)2O + NaCl (also mixed anhydrides).
  • Dehydration of two acids: P2O5 or heat (for succinic, glutaric, maleic, phthalic acid forming five- and six-membered cyclic anhydrides). Acetic anhydride is made industrially from ketene + acetic acid, or by carbonylation of methyl acetate.
Δ, − H₂O⟶
Succinic acid → succinic anhydride (five-membered ring)
Δ, − H₂O⟶
Phthalic acid → phthalic anhydride

Mixed anhydrides

In a mixed anhydride RCO–O–COR′, the nucleophile attacks the more electrophilic and less hindered carbonyl; the carboxylate of the other acid leaves (the better leaving group is the weaker base, i.e. the stronger acid’s anion). Formic acetic anhydride HCO–O–COCH3 therefore transfers the formyl group (the acetyl group is more hindered and less electrophilic): it formylates alcohols and amines.

10.3 Perkin reaction

An aromatic aldehyde with no α-hydrogen condenses with an anhydride that has α-hydrogens, in the presence of the sodium (or potassium) salt of the same acid, at 180 °C, to give an α,β-unsaturated acid (E-cinnamic acid from benzaldehyde and acetic anhydride).

+ CH₃COONa, 180 °C, then H₃O⁺⟶
Perkin: benzaldehyde → trans-cinnamic acid
  • The acetate ion removes an α-H of acetic anhydride, giving the enolate of the anhydride.
  • The enolate adds to PhCHO (aldol-type) to give a β-acyloxy alkoxide; acyl transfer and loss of acetate (E1cB/dehydration) give the mixed anhydride PhCH=CH–CO–O–COCH3.
  • Hydrolysis gives cinnamic acid; the more stable E isomer (phenyl trans to COOH) predominates.
Key idea Perkin synthesis: ArCHO + (RCH2CO)2O → ArCH=C(R)COOH. The carbons of the acid chain contribute two carbons (the CH2 and the C=O) to the new acid; the other acyl group is lost as RCH2COOH. With propanoic anhydride and benzaldehyde the product is α-methylcinnamic acid.
<h4>10.4 Fries rearrangement</h4>

An aryl ester (e.g., phenyl acetate) with AlCl3 rearranges to a hydroxyaryl ketone: low temperature (< 60 °C) gives mainly para (kinetic), high temperature (> 160 °C) gives mainly ortho (chelated with Al, thermodynamic).

AlCl₃, then H₃O⁺⟶
Phenyl acetate → 4-hydroxyacetophenone (low T); 2-hydroxyacetophenone at high T

10.5 Cyclic anhydrides and dicarboxylic acids

Succinic, glutaric, maleic and phthalic acids all dehydrate on heating to five- or six-membered cyclic anhydrides, which are hydrolysed back by warm water. Maleic anhydride, phthalic anhydride and succinic anhydride are the main cyclic anhydrides in exams. Phthalic anhydride + NH3, then heat → phthalimide; + resorcinol → fluorescein; + phenol/H2SO4 → phenolphthalein.

Example 21 Main

Anhydride reaction outcomes

Predict the product when acetic anhydride reacts with (a) excess ethanol, (b) NH3 in excess, (c) aniline, (d) salicylic acid and a drop of H2SO4, (e) water.

Show solution
  1. (a) Ethyl ethanoate + ethanoic acid. (b) Ethanamide + ammonium ethanoate. (c) Acetanilide (N-phenylethanamide) + ethanoic acid. (d) Aspirin (2-acetoxybenzoic acid) + ethanoic acid. (e) 2 CH3COOH.
  2. In every case one acyl group is transferred and the other leaves as a carboxylic acid (or its salt).
Example 22 Adv

Atom economy

Why is ethanoic anhydride a less atom-economical acetylating agent than acetyl chloride, yet it is preferred in industry for aspirin?

Show solution
  1. Only one of its two acetyl groups is incorporated; the other ends as acetic acid (which can be recovered). Acetyl chloride gives HCl, which is corrosive and would attack the acid-sensitive product, and it hydrolyses violently; the anhydride is cheaper to handle, less volatile, and gives a manageable by-product.
