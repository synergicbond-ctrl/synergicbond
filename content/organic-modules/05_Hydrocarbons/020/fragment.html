19. Anti-Markovnikov addition of HBr: the peroxide (Kharasch) effect

In the presence of peroxides (ROOR), light or other radical sources, HBr adds in the opposite regiochemistry by a radical chain mechanism:

  • Initiation: RO–OR → 2 RO• (heat/light); RO• + H–Br → ROH + Br•.
  • Propagation 1: Br• adds to the less substituted carbon of the alkene to form the more stable radical (2° radical for propene): CH3–CH=CH2 + Br• → CH3–C•H–CH2Br.
  • Propagation 2: the radical abstracts H from H–Br: CH3–C•H–CH2Br + HBr → CH3CH2CH2Br + Br•.
  • Termination: radical–radical couplings.

19.1 Why only HBr?

The two propagation steps must both be exothermic (or only slightly endothermic) for an efficient chain. Estimate with bond energies (kcal mol-1): π bond of C=C ≈ 63; C–H (secondary) 98; H–Cl 103, H–Br 87, H–I 71; C–Cl 84, C–Br 70, C–I 56.

Example 31 Advanced

Mechanism-based product prediction

Give the major product when (a) CH3CH=CH2 reacts with HBr in the dark in CCl4, (b) the same with benzoyl peroxide, (c) styrene with HBr/ROOR, (d) 1-methylcyclopentene with HBr/peroxide (stereochemistry).

Show solution
  1. (a) 2-bromopropane (ionic).
  2. (b) 1-bromopropane (radical).
  3. (c) Br• adds to CH2= giving the benzylic radical PhCH•CH2Br; product PhCH2CH2Br (2-bromoethylbenzene).
  4. (d) Br• adds to C-2 (the less substituted alkene carbon), giving the tertiary radical at C-1. HBr donates H to either face of that radical, so both cis- and trans-1-bromo-2-methylcyclopentane form (diastereomers, each racemic; the trans isomer predominates).
