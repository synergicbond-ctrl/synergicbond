Practice 52 Foundation · MCQ

Which compound gives a yellow precipitate with I2/NaOH? (A) methanol (B) propan-1-ol (C) propan-2-ol (D) 2-methylpropan-2-ol

Show answer
  1. The group CH3CH(OH)– is oxidised to CH3CO– and then haloformed. Only propan-2-ol has it. Methanol, propan-1-ol and tert-butyl alcohol do not.

Answer: (C)

Practice 53 Foundation · MCQ

Tollens' reagent is reduced by all EXCEPT: (A) formic acid (B) acetaldehyde (C) benzaldehyde (D) acetone

Show answer
  1. Acetone is a ketone with no easily oxidised C–H on the carbonyl carbon; it does not reduce Tollens' reagent.

Answer: (D)

Practice 54 Foundation · MCQ

Fehling's solution distinguishes: (A) acetaldehyde from benzaldehyde (B) benzaldehyde from acetophenone (C) acetone from butanone (D) phenol from benzoic acid

Show answer
  1. Aliphatic aldehydes reduce Fehling's solution (red Cu2O); aromatic aldehydes do not. The other pairs contain no aliphatic aldehyde.

Answer: (A)

Practice 55 Foundation · MCQ

Neutral FeCl3 gives a violet colour with: (A) ethanol (B) phenol (C) benzoic acid (D) aniline

Show answer
  1. Phenols form coloured Fe(III) phenoxide complexes.

Answer: (B)

Practice 56 Foundation · MCQ

Which pair is distinguished by the carbylamine test? (A) methylamine / dimethylamine (B) dimethylamine / trimethylamine (C) aniline / benzamide (D) both A and C

Show answer
  1. Carbylamine (CHCl3 + KOH) is given only by primary amines. (A): methylamine positive. (C): aniline positive, benzamide (amide) negative.

Answer: (D)

What is observed when propan-2-ol is shaken with Lucas reagent at room temperature?

Practice 60 Application · multi-correct

Which of the following form a bisulphite addition product readily? (A) acetaldehyde (B) acetone (C) benzophenone (D) cyclohexanone (E) acetophenone

Show answer

Practice 63 Application

Compound A, C3H6O, gives no reaction with Tollens' reagent but gives a yellow precipitate with I2/NaOH and an orange ppt with 2,4-DNP. Identify A and write the organic products of the iodoform reaction.

Show answer

  2. (CH3)2CO + 3I2 + 4NaOH → CHI3 + CH3COONa + 3NaI + 3H2O.

Practice 64 Application

A neutral compound B (C7H6O) forms an orange precipitate with 2,4-DNP, gives a silver mirror but no red precipitate with Fehling's solution, and gives a positive Cannizzaro reaction. Identify B.

Show answer
  1. C7H6O, DBE 5: benzene ring + C=O. Tollens' + but not Fehling's ⇒ aromatic aldehyde. Cannizzaro (no α-hydrogen) fits. B = benzaldehyde.

Practice 65 Application · MCQ

An organic compound (C7H6O3) gives effervescence with NaHCO3 and a violet colour with neutral FeCl3. It is: (A) 2,4-dihydroxybenzaldehyde (B) salicylic acid (C) methyl salicylate (D) 3-hydroxybenzoic acid

Show answer
  1. Effervescence ⇒ –COOH, excluding (A) and (C) (no COOH; C has an ester). The formula C7H6O3 excludes (C) C8H8O3 anyway. Between (B) and (D) (both C7H6O3 acids), the strong violet with neutral FeCl3 is the textbook result for salicylic acid, which chelates Fe3+ through its adjacent –OH and –COO−.

Answer: (B)

Practice 66 Application

Hinsberg's reagent was added to three amines X, Y, Z followed by NaOH. X: clear solution, which on acidification gave a ppt. Y: an insoluble solid remained. Z: no reaction, an oily layer remained which dissolves in dil. HCl. Classify X, Y, Z.

Show answer
  1. X: primary (sulphonamide N–H acidic, soluble in alkali, precipitates on acidification). Y: secondary (sulphonamide with no N–H, insoluble). Z: tertiary (no reaction; amine dissolves in acid).

Answer: X 1°, Y 2°, Z 3°

Practice 67 Application

An amine C7H9N gives a yellow oil with HNO2 at 0-5 °C (no gas) and an insoluble sulphonamide with Hinsberg's reagent. Identify it.

Show answer
  1. Yellow oil with HNO2 ⇒ N-nitroso compound ⇒ secondary amine. Insoluble sulphonamide ⇒ 2°. C7H9N, DBE 4 ⇒ aromatic: N-methylaniline, C6H5NHCH3.

Practice 68 Application

Aniline and ethylamine both react with HNO2. Distinguish their products by behaviour at 273-278 K and by a coupling test.

Practice 69 Foundation · MCQ

Which reagent distinguishes an aldehyde from a ketone? (A) 2,4-DNP (B) NaHSO3 (C) Tollens' reagent (D) hydroxylamine

Show answer
  1. Only Tollens' reagent (oxidising character) separates all aldehydes from ketones. 2,4-DNP, hydroxylamine and NaHSO3 react with both (NaHSO3 with few ketones).

Practice 70 Foundation

What is the Mulliken–Barker test for a nitro group? Give the reactions.

Show answer
  1. ArNO2 + Zn/NH4Cl (aq) → ArNHOH (N-arylhydroxylamine). ArNHOH + 2[Ag(NH3)2]+ → ArNO + 2Ag↓ + … A grey/black precipitate or silver mirror shows the nitro group (it is reduced to hydroxylamine, which reduces Tollens' reagent).

Answer: silver mirror

Practice 71 Application

Separate a mixture of benzoic acid, phenol and toluene (all in ether). Write the order of reagents and the compound extracted by each.

Show answer
  1. (1) Aq. NaHCO3: extracts benzoic acid as sodium benzoate (phenol, pKa 10, is not ionised enough). Acidify to recover benzoic acid.
  2. (2) Aq. NaOH: extracts phenol as sodium phenoxide. CO2 or HCl liberates phenol.
  3. (3) The ether layer retains toluene; evaporate the solvent.

Answer: NaHCO3 → NaOH

Practice 72 Application

Why can phenol be liberated from sodium phenoxide by bubbling CO2, whereas benzoic acid cannot be separated from a bicarbonate solution the same way?

Show answer
  1. pKa(phenol) ≈ 10 > pKa1(H2CO3) ≈ 6.4, so CO2 + H2O protonates PhO−: PhO− + H2CO3 → PhOH + HCO3−. Benzoic acid (pKa 4.2) is a stronger acid than H2CO3; its anion stays as benzoate and CO2 cannot protonate it; a strong acid (HCl) is needed.
Practice 73 Application

A mixture of aniline and nitrobenzene is dissolved in ether. Describe separation and recovery of both.

Show answer
  1. Shake with dilute HCl: aniline → anilinium chloride (aqueous); nitrobenzene stays in ether.
  2. Separate layers. Evaporate ether: nitrobenzene. Add NaOH to the aqueous layer: aniline separates (oil); extract or steam distil.

Answer: HCl extraction

Practice 74 Application

A mixture contains benzaldehyde and acetophenone. How can they be separated chemically?

Show answer
  1. Shake with saturated NaHSO3: benzaldehyde gives the crystalline bisulphite adduct; acetophenone (conjugated and more hindered ketone) does not add appreciably. Filter off adduct; decompose with Na2CO3 or dilute HCl to regain benzaldehyde.

Answer: NaHSO3 adduct

Practice 75 Application · MCQ

A mixture of o-nitrophenol and p-nitrophenol is separated by: (A) sublimation (B) steam distillation (C) fractional crystallisation (D) vacuum distillation

Show answer
  1. o-Nitrophenol (intramolecular H-bond) is steam-volatile; p-nitrophenol (intermolecular H-bond) is not.

Answer: (B)

Practice 76 Application · matching

Match the test (P to S) with the group detected (1 to 4). P: Br2/CCl4; Q: neutral FeCl3; R: NaHCO3; S: 2,4-DNP. 1: carbonyl; 2: carboxylic acid; 3: C=C; 4: phenolic –OH.

Practice 77 Application

A compound C4H8O gives a positive Tollens' test and an orange ppt with 2,4-DNP, but no iodoform reaction. Its ¹H NMR shows a 6H doublet. Identify it.

Show answer
  1. Positive Tollens' ⇒ aldehyde: butanal or 2-methylpropanal. No iodoform: neither has CH3CO– or CH3CH(OH)–. A 6H doublet means two equivalent CH3 groups on one CH: (CH3)2CH–CHO. (Butanal would show a 3H triplet instead.)

Answer: 2-methylpropanal

Practice 78 Application

An ester C4H8O2 is hydrolysed. The alcohol obtained gives a yellow precipitate with I2/NaOH; the acid obtained gives a silver mirror with Tollens'. Identify the ester.

Show answer
  1. Acid reducing Tollens' ⇒ HCOOH. Alcohol giving iodoform with 3 carbons: C4H8O2 − HCOOH = C3H7OH ⇒ propan-2-ol. Ester: isopropyl formate HCOOCH(CH3)2.

Answer: isopropyl formate

Practice 79 Application

A compound D (C3H7NO) on heating with NaOH evolves a gas which turns red litmus blue and gives dense white fumes with HCl. D is also neutral. Identify D.

  1. The gas is NH3. A neutral compound C3H7NO (DBE 1) releasing NH3 on alkaline hydrolysis is a primary amide: propanamide CH3CH2CONH2. (N-methylacetamide would release CH3NH2.)

Practice 81 Advanced · paragraph

Compound P (C8H8O2) is a solid that dissolves in aq. NaHCO3 with brisk effervescence. It does not give a violet colour with FeCl3. On oxidation with hot alkaline KMnO4 it gives a dicarboxylic acid C8H6O4, which forms a cyclic anhydride on heating only in one of its isomers. (a) Identify P. (b) What is the nature of the dicarboxylic acid if its anhydride forms on heating?

Show answer
  1. DBE of P = 5: ring + C=O. NaHCO3 ⇒ COOH, so P = CH3C6H4COOH (toluic acid). KMnO4 oxidises the CH3 to COOH, giving a benzenedicarboxylic acid C8H6O4.
  2. (b) Cyclic anhydride on heating means the two COOH groups are ortho: phthalic acid → phthalic anhydride. Hence P = o-toluic acid (2-methylbenzoic acid); the m- and p-isomers give isophthalic and terephthalic acids, which cannot form cyclic anhydrides.

Answer: o-toluic acid

Practice 82 Advanced

An unknown solid Q dissolves in dilute NaOH but not in NaHCO3; gives a white precipitate with bromine water; gives violet colour with FeCl3; Lassaigne shows only C, H, O (no N, S, X). Q (C6H6O) is therefore which compound, and what does the precipitate consist of?

Show answer
  1. C6H6O (DBE 4): benzene ring, no C=O. Dissolves in NaOH only ⇒ weakly acidic ⇒ phenol. White ppt with Br2 water: 2,4,6-tribromophenol; violet FeCl3: iron(III) phenoxide.

Practice 83 Application

Why does glucose give a positive Tollens' test even though it is mostly present in the cyclic hemiacetal form?

Show answer
  1. In aqueous solution the cyclic hemiacetal is in equilibrium (mutarotation) with a small amount of the free aldehyde form. As the aldehyde is oxidised, the equilibrium shifts and all of the sugar reacts.
Practice 84 Application

Explain why sucrose does not reduce Fehling's solution until it has been boiled with dilute acid.

Show answer
  1. Sucrose is α-D-glucopyranosyl-β-D-fructofuranoside: the anomeric carbons of both units are tied up in the glycosidic bond, so there is no free hemiacetal/aldehyde or ketone. Acid hydrolysis gives glucose and fructose (invert sugar), both reducing.
Practice 85 Application

A protein solution gives a violet colour with NaOH + CuSO4, a yellow colour with conc. HNO3 that turns orange with alkali, and a black precipitate on boiling with NaOH and lead acetate. What does each test indicate?

Show answer
  1. Violet (biuret): peptide linkages (≥ 2). Yellow → orange (xanthoproteic): aromatic amino-acid residues (Tyr, Phe, Trp). Black PbS: sulphur-containing residues (cysteine/cystine).

  1. α-Amino acids with a free α-NH2 (glycine) give Ruhemann's purple; proline (secondary amine) gives yellow; a tertiary amine and an amide do not react.

Practice 87 Application

Alcoholic AgNO3 gives an immediate white precipitate with A and slow with B, none with C. A = benzyl chloride, B = n-butyl chloride, C = chlorobenzene (not necessarily in this order). Assign and give the reason.

Show answer
  1. Reactivity by SN1 character: benzyl chloride (stabilised benzylic carbocation) immediate: A; n-butyl chloride (primary, no stabilisation) slow on warming: B; chlorobenzene (C(sp2)–Cl with partial double bond character from lone-pair delocalisation) none: C.

Answer: A = benzyl chloride; B = n-butyl chloride; C = chlorobenzene

Practice 88 Advanced · paragraph

Compound R has the molecular formula C9H10O. It gives an orange ppt with 2,4-DNP, no silver mirror, and a yellow ppt with I2/NaOH. Vigorous oxidation gives benzoic acid. ¹H NMR: δ 7.2 (m, 5H), 3.7 (s, 2H), 2.1 (s, 3H). Identify R. Why does it give the iodoform test even though the carbonyl is not attached to the ring?

Practice 92 Application

Explain the order of acidity that makes NaHCO3 dissolve benzoic acid but not phenol: pKa data are benzoic acid 4.2, phenol 10.0, H2CO3 6.4. Calculate the equilibrium constant for PhOH + HCO3− ⇌ PhO− + H2CO3.

Show answer
  1. K = Ka(PhOH)/Ka(H2CO3) = 10-10.0/10-6.4 = 10-3.6 = 2.5 × 10−4: negligible, so phenol does not dissolve in bicarbonate.
  2. For benzoic acid: K = 10-4.2/10-6.4 = 102.2 = 158 ≫ 1: complete reaction.

Answer: K = 2.5 × 10−4 (phenol); 158 (benzoic acid)
