9.2 Cleavage of vicinal diols: periodic acid and lead tetra-acetate

Vicinal diols (and α-hydroxy aldehydes/ketones, α-diketones, α-hydroxy acids) undergo oxidative cleavage of the C–C bond between the two oxygen-bearing carbons. Reagents: HIO4 (aqueous, Malaprade reaction), NaIO4, or Pb(OAc)4 (Criegee, in anhydrous benzene or acetic acid). Mechanism: the diol displaces water from the iodine to form a cyclic periodate ester (five-membered ring; I(VII)); the ring then fragments in a concerted way with C–C and I–O bond breaking: two carbonyl groups and iodate (I(V)). The need for a cyclic intermediate means that a cis diol on a ring reacts faster than the corresponding trans diol, and a rigid trans-diol with two equatorial OH groups (OH···OH too far apart) may not react at all.

Key idea Counting rule: the number of moles of HIO4 equals the number of C–C bonds that lie between two carbons each bearing an OH (or C=O adjacent to carbinol) in the chain. Secondary carbinol carbons (CHOH) in the middle of the chain become HCOOH; terminal CH2OH becomes HCHO; terminal CHO becomes HCOOH; CH(OH) carrying R becomes RCHO; C(OH)R2 becomes a ketone.
Example 27 L2 Main

Periodic acid analysis

A compound C4H10O3 consumes 2 mol of HIO4 and gives one mole each of HCHO, HCOOH and CH3CHO. Deduce its structure and check the carbon count.

Show solution
  1. Two moles of periodic acid mean two C–C cleavages, so three adjacent carbons each bear an OH group: a 1,2,3-triol.
  2. Fragments: HCHO (one carbon, a terminal CH2OH), HCOOH (one carbon, the middle CHOH), CH3CHO (two carbons, the terminal CH(OH)CH3). Carbon count 1 + 1 + 2 = 4 ✓.
  3. Structure: HOCH2–CH(OH)–CH(OH)–CH3, butane-1,2,3-triol (C4H10O3 ✓).
  4. Always check that the carbons in the fragments add up to the carbons of the substrate.
