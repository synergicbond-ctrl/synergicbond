13. Nitriles (cyanides)

13.1 Structure and preparation

R–C≡N: sp-hybridised carbon and nitrogen, linear, strong dipole (bp 82 °C for CH3CN despite M = 41), pKa of α-H ≈ 25. Preparation:

  • RX + KCN (SN2; ethanol–water; 1° and methyl; 3° gives alkene by E2). ArX does not react (except activated, or by CuCN at 200 °C, Rosenmund–von Braun).
  • Dehydration of primary amide (P2O5, SOCl2). Dehydration of an aldoxime RCH=NOH (Ac2O).
  • Cyanohydrin from aldehyde/ketone + HCN. Sandmeyer: ArN2+ + CuCN.
  • Addition of HCN to alkenes (hydrocyanation) and Michael addition of CN− to α,β-unsaturated carbonyls.

13.2 Reactions

H₂O, H⁺ (or OH⁻), Δ⟶ H₂O, Δ⟶
Hydrolysis proceeds through the amide; with controlled mild conditions the amide can be isolated
1. LiAlH₄ 2. H₂O⟶
LiAlH₄ or H₂/Ni, 100 atm: primary amine RCH₂NH₂ (adds CH₂ to the chain; the amine has the same carbon number as the nitrile)
1. SnCl₂, HCl (Stephen) 2. H₂O⟶
Stephen reduction gives aldehyde via imine hydrochloride; DIBAL-H at −78 °C also stops at the aldehyde
1. CH₃CH₂MgBr 2. H₃O⁺⟶
Grignard adds once to the C≡N; the imine salt is hydrolysed to the ketone (butan-2-one)
  • Because the Grignard reagent adds once and the intermediate imine anion is not electrophilic enough to react again, nitriles give ketones, not tertiary alcohols (unlike esters and acyl chlorides). Good route to ketones: ArCN + RMgX → ArCOR.
  • Hydrolysis mechanism (acid): protonation of N, water attacks C, tautomerisation gives the amide; further hydrolysis follows the amide route. (Base: OH− adds to C≡N, then proton transfer.) Overall: RCN + 2 H2O + H+ → RCOOH + NH4+.
  • α-Deprotonation (strong base, e.g. LDA): RCH2CN gives a nitrile-stabilised carbanion that can be alkylated (an alternative chain extension). Active methylene nitriles: malononitrile CH2(CN)2 (pKa 11), ethyl cyanoacetate (pKa 9).
Key idea Reduction pairs: RCN + 4[H] → RCH2NH2 (LiAlH4); RNC + 4[H] → RNHCH3 (N-methyl amine). Hydrolysis pairs: RCN → RCOOH + NH3 (acid or base); RNC → RNH2 + HCOOH (acid only; stable to alkali).

Example 34 Main

Nitrile chemistry

Starting from bromoethane, prepare (a) propan-1-amine, (b) propanal, (c) butan-2-one, (d) propanoic acid.

Show solution
  1. First step in each: CH3CH2Br + KCN → CH3CH2CN (propanenitrile), adding one carbon.
  2. (a) LiAlH4 or H2/Ni: CH3CH2CH2NH2. (b) DIBAL-H, −78 °C, then H2O (or SnCl2/HCl, then H2O): CH3CH2CHO. (c) CH3MgBr, then H3O+: CH3CH2COCH3. (d) H3O+, Δ: CH3CH2COOH.
Example 35 Adv

Nitrile or isonitrile

Two isomers A and B have the formula C3H5N. A is hydrolysed by hot aqueous acid to propanoic acid and ammonium ion. B has an offensive smell, resists alkali, and with dilute acid gives ethylamine and a second product. Identify A, B and the second product.

Show solution
  1. A: CH3CH2C≡N (propanenitrile). B: CH3CH2N+≡C− (ethyl isocyanide); acid hydrolysis gives CH3CH2NH3+ + HCOOH (formic acid).
