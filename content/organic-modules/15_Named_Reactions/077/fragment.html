70. Diels–Alder reaction ([4 + 2] cycloaddition)

Reaction: a conjugated diene (4 π electrons, must be able to adopt the s-cis conformation) + a dienophile (2 π electrons; best with electron-withdrawing groups: C=O, CN, NO2, CO2R, maleic anhydride) → cyclohexene (six-membered ring, two new σ bonds, one new π bond). It is a concerted, thermally allowed, suprafacial [π4s + π2s] cycloaddition through a single cyclic transition state: no intermediate. Stereochemistry (the exam questions):

  • Stereospecific in the dienophile: cis substituents on the dienophile stay cis in the ring (maleic anhydride, maleate → cis-1,2-disubstituted); trans (fumarate) stay trans.
  • Substituents on the diene termini: "outside" groups of an E,E-diene (or E-1-substituted diene) end up cis to each other on the new ring.
  • Endo rule: with cyclic dienes (cyclopentadiene) and dienophiles with π-substituents (maleic anhydride), the endo adduct (substituent under the C=C of the new bridge) is the kinetic product, because of favourable secondary orbital overlap in the transition state. The exo product is thermodynamically more stable.

Regiochemistry (the "ortho/para" rule): a diene with a donor at C-1 and an acceptor dienophile give the 1,2 ("ortho") product; a diene with a donor at C-2 gives the 1,4 ("para") product.

Buta-1,3-diene + ethene → cyclohexene (needs forcing conditions because ethene is a poor dienophile)
+ benzene, Δ⟶
Butadiene + maleic anhydride → cis-1,2,3,6-tetrahydrophthalic anhydride (the two anhydride bonds are cis)
+ 0 °C⟶
Cyclopentadiene + maleic anhydride → the endo norbornene dicarboxylic anhydride (bicyclo[2.2.1]hept-5-ene-2,3-dicarboxylic anhydride)
+ Δ⟶
Isoprene (donor at C-2) + acrolein → "para" product, 4-methylcyclohex-3-ene-1-carbaldehyde
+ Δ⟶
Penta-1,3-diene (donor at C-1) + acrolein → "ortho" product, 2-methylcyclohex-3-ene-1-carbaldehyde
Trap Diene that cannot reach s-cis fails: a diene locked s-trans (for example in a ring) does not react; a diene locked s-cis (cyclopentadiene, cyclohexa-1,3-diene) reacts very fast. Also needs a cis-diene at the termini: (E,E)- and (E,Z)-hexa-2,4-diene differ drastically in rate, since the Z-terminus clashes with the other terminus in the s-cis form. The retro-Diels–Alder (heat, 450 K) cracks cyclopentadiene dimer (dicyclopentadiene) back to the monomer and is the reverse of the reaction above.
Example 61 Main

Stereochemistry of an adduct

Give the stereochemical relationship of the ring substituents in the product of (a) buta-1,3-diene + dimethyl maleate, (b) buta-1,3-diene + dimethyl fumarate, and say how many stereoisomers (counting enantiomers) each product has.

Show solution
  1. (a) cis-Dimethyl cyclohex-4-ene-1,2-dicarboxylate (cis from the Z-dienophile). The cis product is meso (a mirror plane through the ring between C-1/C-2 and C-4/C-5): 1 stereoisomer.
  2. (b) trans-Dimethyl cyclohex-4-ene-1,2-dicarboxylate: a racemic pair (2 enantiomers, R,R and S,S), which show the product is chiral and both enantiomers form.
Example 62 Adv

Predict the adduct

Predict the main product, with regio- and stereo-chemistry, of (a) 2,3-dimethylbuta-1,3-diene + maleic anhydride, (b) anthracene + maleic anhydride, (c) furan + maleic anhydride (exo/endo at low temperature vs high temperature).

Show solution
  1. (a) 4,5-Dimethylcyclohex-4-ene-1,2-dicarboxylic anhydride (cis). (b) Anthracene reacts at the central ring (positions 9 and 10), because this keeps two intact benzene rings: 9,10-dihydro-9,10-ethanoanthracene-11,12-dicarboxylic anhydride. (c) Furan acts as the diene: the oxa-bridged (7-oxanorbornene) adduct; the endo adduct forms faster (kinetic, low T) but the adduct is thermally reversible (retro-DA), so on warming the exo adduct (more stable) accumulates.
