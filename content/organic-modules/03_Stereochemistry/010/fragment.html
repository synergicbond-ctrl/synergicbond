7. Special cases of geometrical isomerism that JEE likes

7.1 Exocyclic double bonds and cyclic alkenes

Cyclopropene to cycloheptene are only stable as the cis double bond; trans-cyclooctene is the smallest isolable trans-cycloalkene (chiral, planar-chiral in effect). Bredt's rule: a double bond cannot be placed at the bridgehead of a small bicyclic system (the twisted π-bond would have no overlap).

trans-cyclooctene (smallest stable trans-cycloalkene; chiral) cis-cyclooctene (E)-cyclodecene
Larger rings (≥ 8) can host a trans double bond

7.2 Allylic and conjugated systems: s-cis / s-trans are conformations

In buta-1,3-diene the C2–C3 single bond rotates (barrier ≈ 25–30 kJ mol-1); s-trans and s-cis are conformers, not geometrical isomers (the "s" means "single bond").

7.3 Fumaric/maleic reactions

Maleic acid, but not fumaric acid, loses water on heating to give the cyclic maleic anhydride (the two COOH are close). Fumaric acid needs a much higher temperature and isomerises first. Br2 adds to maleic acid to give (±)-2,3-dibromosuccinic acid and to fumaric acid to give the meso compound (anti addition; see section 38).

maleic acid maleic anhydride fumaric acid
Only the cis acid can close the five-membered anhydride ring

7.4 Summary sheet: geometrical isomerism

  • E/Z: rank groups at each carbon by CIP; same side → Z; opposite → E.
  • Needs (a,b)C=C(c,d) with a ≠ b and c ≠ d.
  • Symmetrical polyenes: (2n + 2⌈n/2⌉)/2; unsymmetrical: 2n.
  • Cumulenes: odd number of double bonds → E/Z; even → axial chirality.
  • Rings: cis/trans exist when ≥ 2 ring atoms carry two different groups; 1,2- and 1,3-disubstituted (identical groups) have 3 stereoisomers; 1,4-disubstituted 2.
  • Physical: cis higher polarity, higher boiling; trans higher melting and usually more stable.
