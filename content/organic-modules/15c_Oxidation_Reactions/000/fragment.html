Oxidation reactions: reagents and what they do

Oxidation usually means adding oxygen or removing hydrogen. The oxidising agent decides how far a substrate is oxidised.

Alcohols. A primary alcohol gives an aldehyde with PCC, Swern or Dess–Martin, and a carboxylic acid with chromic acid (K₂Cr₂O₇/H₂SO₄, Jones) or KMnO₄. A secondary alcohol gives a ketone. A tertiary alcohol is not oxidised (no α-hydrogen).
Alkenes. OsO₄ (or cold alkaline KMnO₄) gives the syn (cis) diol. mCPBA gives an epoxide, which opens in acid to the anti (trans) diol. Ozonolysis cleaves C=C: Zn/H₂O or Me₂S work-up gives aldehydes and ketones; H₂O₂ work-up gives acids and ketones.
Side chains and other. Hot alkaline KMnO₄ turns any alkyl side chain that has a benzylic hydrogen into COOH. Baeyer–Villiger (mCPBA) converts a ketone to an ester; the group that migrates is the one better able to bear positive charge (tertiary > secondary ≈ aryl > primary > methyl). HIO₄ cleaves 1,2-diols. Tollens' and Fehling's reagents oxidise aldehydes only.
