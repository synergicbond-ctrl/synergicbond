9.1 Stereochemistry of making vicinal diols from alkenes

Syn-dihydroxylation (OsO4 then NaHSO3, or catalytic OsO4/NMO, or cold dilute alkaline KMnO4): a cyclic osmate (or manganate) ester forms on one face, so both OH groups add to the same side. Anti-dihydroxylation: epoxidation with a peracid (e.g. mCPBA) and then acid- or base-catalysed ring opening by water, which occurs with inversion at one carbon, giving the trans (anti) diol.

Example 26 L2 Main

Stereochemistry of diols

How many stereoisomeric products form when trans-hex-3-ene reacts with (i) OsO4; (ii) peracid then H3O+? Describe them.

Show solution
  1. The product is hexane-3,4-diol, with two stereocentres and a plane of symmetry in one diastereomer (meso).
  2. (i) Syn addition to a trans-alkene gives the (3R,4R)/(3S,4S) pair (the same result as for trans-but-2-ene): a racemic mixture of two enantiomers, because OsO4 can attack either face of the planar alkene.
  3. (ii) Epoxidation retains the trans geometry (trans-epoxide); anti opening gives the meso (3R,4S) compound: one stereoisomer.
