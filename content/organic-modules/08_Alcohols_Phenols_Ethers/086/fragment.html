22.2 Other reactions of ethers

  • With PCl5: C2H5OC2H5 + PCl5 → 2C2H5Cl + POCl3. With acid chlorides and ZnCl2 (or anhydrides): R–O–R + R′COCl → R′COOR + RCl.
  • With BBr3 or BCl3 (and AlCl3, TMSI): cleavage of aryl methyl ethers under mild conditions (demethylation) to the phenol: ArOCH3 + BBr3 → ArOBBr2 + CH3Br; then water gives ArOH.
  • α-Halogenation: free-radical chlorination in light, CH3CH2OCH2CH3 + Cl2 (hv) → CH3CHClOCH2CH3 (α-chloro ether: the α radical is stabilised by the lone pair of O). Excess Cl2 gives perchloro-diethyl ether.
  • Autoxidation to peroxides (see 20.3).
  • Oxidation by strong oxidants (CrO3) is slow; ethers are stable to most oxidants and reductants.
  • Acetals and ketals (R2C(OR′)2) are special ethers: stable to base but hydrolysed by aqueous acid; they protect aldehydes and ketones.
