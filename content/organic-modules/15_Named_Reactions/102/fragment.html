Block H. Stereochemistry

Practice 163 Stereo

(R)-2-Bromobutane is treated with NaI in dry acetone. Give the configuration of the product and the effect of prolonged reaction.

Show answer
  1. Single SN2: (S)-2-iodobutane. With excess I− and long time the product is itself attacked by I− (inversion again), so the mixture racemises.
Practice 164 Stereo

(S)-Butan-2-ol is converted to 2-chlorobutane with (a) SOCl2 alone, (b) SOCl2 in pyridine. Give the configuration.

Show answer
  1. (a) SNi: retention: (S)-2-chlorobutane. (b) Pyridine converts the chlorosulfite's chloride into free Cl−, which attacks by SN2: inversion: (R)-2-chlorobutane. (The CIP priority order stays O/Cl > ethyl > methyl > H, so retention keeps the same label.)
Practice 165 Stereo

trans-1-Bromo-2-methylcyclohexane and cis-1-bromo-2-methylcyclohexane are each treated with NaOEt/EtOH. Which gives 1-methylcyclohexene and which 3-methylcyclohexene?

Show answer
  1. E2 needs the Br and the β-H to be trans-diaxial. Cis isomer: Br axial, CH3 equatorial, H on C-2 axial and anti to Br, so elimination to the more substituted 1-methylcyclohexene (Saytzeff) occurs. Trans isomer: Br axial forces CH3 axial and the C-2 H equatorial (not anti), so only the C-6 axial H is available: 3-methylcyclohexene.
Practice 166 Stereo

Butadiene + dimethyl maleate and butadiene + dimethyl fumarate: stereochemistry of each product.

Show answer
  1. Maleate (Z) gives the cis-4-cyclohexene-1,2-dicarboxylate, a meso compound (1 stereoisomer). Fumarate (E) gives the trans diester, a racemic pair (R,R/S,S).
Practice 167 Stereo

Cyclohexene is converted to cyclohexane-1,2-diol by (a) OsO4/NMO and (b) mCPBA then H3O+. Give the stereochemistry of each.

Show answer
  1. (a) syn addition: cis-diol (meso). (b) epoxide, then anti ring opening: trans-diol (racemic, (R,R) + (S,S)).
Practice 168 Stereo

(Z)-But-2-ene is treated with OsO4/NMO; (E)-but-2-ene likewise. Describe the butane-2,3-diol products.

Show answer
  1. syn addition: Z gives meso-butane-2,3-diol; E gives the racemic (2R,3R)/(2S,3S) diol.
Practice 169 Stereo

(Z)-But-2-ene + mCPBA, then aqueous acid. Give the diol stereochemistry.

Show answer
  1. cis-Epoxide (meso); anti ring opening at either carbon gives the racemic (±)-butane-2,3-diol: opposite result to syn dihydroxylation of the same alkene.
Practice 170 Stereo

Br2 adds to (E)-but-2-ene and to (Z)-but-2-ene in CCl4. Give the stereochemistry.

Show answer
  1. anti addition through a bromonium ion: E gives meso-2,3-dibromobutane; Z gives racemic (2R,3R)/(2S,3S).
Practice 171 Stereo

Hofmann rearrangement of (S)-2-methylbutanamide. Give the configuration of the amine.

Show answer
  1. Migration with retention: the amine butan-2-amine is (S) (priority order is preserved because N replaces C(=O)N).
Practice 172 Stereo

(S)-3-Phenylbutan-2-one + mCPBA. Give the product and its configuration.

Show answer
  1. The secondary benzylic carbon migrates (benzylic ≈ secondary alkyl > methyl) with retention: (S)-1-phenylethyl ethanoate. The stereocentre (C-3, PhCH(CH3)–) becomes the carbinol carbon of PhCH(CH3)–OAc; O replaces C(=O)CH3 at the same priority rank (highest), so the label stays (S).
Practice 173 Stereo

How many stereoisomers does the aldol addition product of propanal have?

Show answer
  1. 3-Hydroxy-2-methylpentanal has two stereocentres (C-2 and C-3): 4 stereoisomers (2 diastereomeric pairs).
Practice 174 Stereo

(S)-Octan-2-ol is treated with PPh3, DEAD and benzoic acid. Give the configuration of the ester and of the alcohol after hydrolysis.

Show answer
  1. Mitsunobu inverts: (R)-octan-2-yl benzoate. Base hydrolysis attacks the acyl carbon, so the stereocentre keeps its configuration: (R)-octan-2-ol. Net: inversion of the alcohol.
Practice 175 Stereo

How many stereoisomers exist for 2,3-dibromobutane, and which one is formed from (Z)-but-2-ene + Br2?

Show answer
  1. 3 stereoisomers: meso (2R,3S) and the enantiomeric pair (2R,3R)/(2S,3S). Z-but-2-ene + Br2 (anti) gives the racemic (R,R)/(S,S) pair.
Practice 176 Stereo

Benzoin is formed from benzaldehyde and cyanide. Is it optically active?

Show answer
  1. Benzoin has one stereocentre (the CHOH carbon) but is formed from achiral reagents without a chiral influence, so it is the racemate (optically inactive).
Practice 177 Stereo

The Claisen rearrangement of (E)-crotyl phenyl ether gives 2-(but-3-en-2-yl)phenol. Is the product chiral, and in what form is it obtained?

Show answer
  1. Yes, the benzylic carbon (attached to Ar, CH3, CH=CH2, H) is a stereocentre; it is formed as the racemate (no chiral influence).
Practice 178 Stereo

Wittig reaction of an aldehyde with a non-stabilised ylide gives mostly which alkene geometry, and which reagent change gives the opposite?

Show answer
  1. Z-alkene from the non-stabilised ylide under salt-free conditions; a stabilised ylide (or the Horner–Wadsworth–Emmons phosphonate) gives the E-alkene.
