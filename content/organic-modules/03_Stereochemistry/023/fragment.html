20. Racemisation, resolution and asymmetric synthesis

20.1 Racemic mixtures: why they appear

Rule of thumb: a reaction of achiral (or racemic) reactants in an achiral environment cannot produce an excess of one enantiomer. If the product has one stereocentre, it forms as a racemate. The two faces of a planar intermediate (carbocation, carbonyl, enol, radical) are attacked at equal rates, because the two transition states are mirror images with equal energy.

butanone (R)-butan-2-ol (S)-butan-2-ol
Reduction of butanone with NaBH₄ attacks the two faces of C=O equally: racemic butan-2-ol, optically inactive
Key idea Optical activity cannot be created from nothing. Optically inactive reactants + achiral reagents + achiral solvent → optically inactive products (racemates or meso compounds). The exceptions require a chiral influence: a chiral catalyst, enzyme, solvent, circularly polarised light, or a chiral reagent.

20.2 Racemisation

Racemisation converts an optically active compound into the racemate. Common mechanisms:

  • Carbocation (SN1): the planar cation is captured from both faces. Example: (R)-1-phenylethyl chloride in a polar solvent.
  • Enolisation: a stereocentre carrying an α-hydrogen next to C=O loses that H as a proton (acid or base) to give a planar enol/enolate, then re-protonates from either face. (S)-2-methyl-1-phenylbutan-1-one racemises in base.
  • Pyramidal inversion: amines R1R2R3N invert rapidly; not an issue for carbon.
  • Rotation about a hindered bond: biaryl atropisomers racemise on heating if the barrier can be overcome.
  • Radical or reversible bond cleavage.
Trap A stereocentre that bears no α-hydrogen to a carbonyl and cannot form a stable cation does not racemise under mild base. (R)-2-methyl-2-phenylbutanoic acid is configurationally stable (no α-H), (R)-2-phenylpropanoic acid racemises easily in base.
(S)-2-phenylpropanoic acid (α-H: racemises) (S)-2-methyl-2-phenylbutanoic acid (no α-H: stable)
Example 60 Main

Racemisation in the lab

(S)-3-Methylpentan-2-one (CH3COCH(CH3)CH2CH3) is dissolved in dilute NaOH. Predict what happens to its optical activity, and compare with (S)-4-methylhexan-2-one (CH3COCH2CH(CH3)CH2CH3).

Show solution
  1. In 3-methylpentan-2-one the stereocentre C-3 carries an α-hydrogen next to the C=O: base removes it to give a planar enolate and the stereocentre is destroyed; re-protonation gives both enantiomers. Optical activity is lost (racemisation).
  2. In 4-methylhexan-2-one the stereocentre C-4 is β to the carbonyl; the α-hydrogens at C-3 are on a CH2, enolisation does not touch C-4. The compound retains its optical activity.

20.3 Resolution: separating enantiomers

Enantiomers have identical physical properties, so ordinary distillation or crystallisation cannot separate them. Methods:

  • 1. Mechanical separation (Pasteur, 1848): hand-picking mirror-image crystals of sodium ammonium tartrate (a conglomerate) crystallised below 27 °C. Rare, only for conglomerates.
  • 2. Diastereomeric salt formation (the standard method): convert the enantiomers into diastereomers by combining the racemate with a single pure enantiomer of another chiral compound (the resolving agent). Diastereomers differ in solubility and can be separated by fractional crystallisation; then the pure enantiomer is regenerated.
  • 3. Enzymatic / biochemical resolution: enzymes recognise only one enantiomer (a lipase hydrolyses only one enantiomer of an ester; a mould consumes only one enantiomer).
  • 4. Chromatography on a chiral stationary phase (chiral HPLC).
  • 5. Kinetic resolution: one enantiomer reacts faster with a chiral reagent/catalyst than the other.
+ salt formation⟶
(R)-lactic acid with (S)-1-phenylethylamine: one diastereomeric salt (R,S)
+ salt formation⟶
(S)-lactic acid with the same amine: the other diastereomeric salt (S,S)
Trap Resolving agents must themselves be enantiomerically pure. (±)-Acid + (±)-base gives two pairs of enantiomeric salts, not separable by crystallisation. Racemic acids are resolved with optically active bases (brucine, strychnine, quinine, (S)-1-phenylethylamine); racemic bases with optically active acids ((+)-tartaric acid, (−)-malic acid, (+)-camphor-10-sulfonic acid, (−)-mandelic acid). Racemic alcohols are first converted to the half-ester with phthalic or succinic anhydride (an acid), resolved with a chiral base, then hydrolysed.
Example 61 Main

Steps of a resolution

(±)-Mandelic acid is resolved with (−)-brucine. Describe the steps and name the relationship between the two salts formed.

Show solution
  1. (1) Mix (±)-acid with one enantiomer of the base: two salts, (R)-acid·(−)-base and (S)-acid·(−)-base, form. They are diastereomers (not mirror images, because the base part is identical).
  2. (2) They differ in solubility; fractional crystallisation isolates the less soluble salt.
  3. (3) Treat each salt with a strong acid (HCl): the free (R)- or (S)-mandelic acid is regenerated and the base is recovered as its salt.
  4. The two enantiomers of the acid are thereby obtained pure. (Maximum yield of each enantiomer is 50 % from the racemate unless the unwanted enantiomer is racemised and recycled.)

20.4 Asymmetric (enantioselective) synthesis

A reaction that creates a new stereocentre with unequal amounts of the stereoisomers is asymmetric: enantioselective if enantiomers are made unequally, diastereoselective if diastereomers are. Strategies: chiral pool (start from natural chiral compounds such as amino acids, sugars, terpenes), chiral auxiliaries, chiral catalysts (Sharpless epoxidation with a titanium tartrate catalyst, Noyori hydrogenation with Ru–BINAP, CBS reduction, organocatalysts such as proline), and biocatalysis. The measure of success is ee (or de for diastereomers).

Formula de (diastereomeric excess) = |D1 − D2| / (D1 + D2) × 100 %

20.5 Chirality in nature and medicine

Natural amino acids are L ((S) except cysteine), natural sugars are D. Enzymes and receptors are chiral, so enantiomers of a drug often act differently.

L-DOPA (S) (active in Parkinson's disease) (S,S)-ethambutol (S,S) (anti-tuberculosis; the (R,R)-isomer causes blindness) (S)-propranolol (S) (S)-ibuprofen (S)

Other facts: (R)-limonene smells of orange, (S)-limonene of lemon/turpentine; (R)-carvone of spearmint, (S)-carvone of caraway; thalidomide's enantiomers interconvert in the body, so using a single enantiomer would not have prevented its teratogenic effects.
