14. Counting hydrogen and hydride equivalents

Questions about the amount of H2 or hydride consumed need a systematic count. Under forcing hydrogenation conditions (Pt, high pressure) every unsaturation is saturated; under mild conditions (Pd/C, 1 atm) only the groups named as reactive are reduced, and an aromatic ring, ester or acid is left alone.

  • Moles of H2 per unit: C=C 1; C≡C 2; benzene ring 3; naphthalene 5; C=O (aldehyde/ketone) 1; C≡N 2; NO2 → NH2 3.
  • Moles of hydride (H−) per unit: aldehyde or ketone 1; ester 2 (to two alcohols); amide 2 (to amine); nitrile 2; acid chloride 2 (to alcohol); anhydride 4 (to two alcohols); carboxylic acid 2 for reduction plus 1 lost as H2 (deprotonation of OH).
  • LiAlH4 and NaBH4 each contain four hydrides, so the theoretical minimum is (total hydride required) ÷ 4 moles of reagent.

1-Phenylbut-1-en-3-yne: full hydrogenation uses 2 + 1 + 3 = 6 mol H2; Pd/C at 1 atm would use 3 mol; Lindlar uses 1 mol.

  1. Write down each reducible group and the reaction conditions.
  2. Assign the H2 or hydride count to each group from the list above.
  3. Add the counts. For a hydrocarbon CcHhNn the maximum H2 uptake equals the degrees of unsaturation, (2c + 2 + n − h) ÷ 2.
Formula. Degrees of unsaturation DoU = (2C + 2 + N − H − X) ÷ 2. For a hydrocarbon, mol H2 for complete hydrogenation = DoU − number of rings left intact.
