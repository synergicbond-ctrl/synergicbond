6.8 Ninhydrin and periodate: two reagents with a clean mechanism

  • Ninhydrin: (1) the amino acid's NH2 condenses with a ninhydrin carbonyl to give an imine; (2) decarboxylation of the imine (the imine N+ is an electron sink): gives an azomethine ylide; (3) hydrolysis of that species gives the aldehyde RCHO and 2-amino-indane-1,3-dione (the amine formed from ninhydrin); (4) this amine condenses with a second ninhydrin to give the Ruhemann's purple anion, a fully conjugated 18-carbon anionic dye whose HOMO–LUMO gap falls in the visible (λmax 570 nm). Proline cannot give the neutral amine, so only the iminium-type chromophore (yellow, λmax 440 nm) forms.
  • Periodate: (1) the vicinal diol attacks I(VII) to form a five-membered cyclic periodate ester (this explains why cis diols in rigid rings react faster than trans diols, which can't reach the cyclic geometry, and why 1,3-diols are inert); (2) electrons flow from the O–I bonds: C–C bond cleaves and the two carbonyls form as I(VII) is reduced to I(V). MO view: the C–C σ bond is the HOMO that donates into the low-lying σ*(O–I) as the two C=O π bonds form synchronously.
