80. Level 3, set F — mechanism and orbital-reasoning problems

Practice 296 L3 MO reasoning

Explain, using orbitals, why the N lone pair of aniline is less basic but the ring is more reactive than benzene toward electrophiles.

Show answer
  1. The same interaction is responsible for both effects: it is a donor–acceptor mixing in which the π system takes electron density from N and returns it to the ring.

The N lone pair (nN) overlaps with the ring π system. This (i) lowers its energy and spreads it over N and the ring (less available to H+: pKa 4.6 vs 10.6 for cyclohexylammonium), and (ii) raises the energy of the highest occupied ring π orbital, giving it large coefficients at ortho/para carbons – a better nucleophile toward E+.

Practice 297 L3 Mechanism

Write the steps of the acid-catalysed formation of an imine from benzaldehyde and aniline and say which step is rate-limiting at pH 7 and which at pH 2.

Show answer

(1) N attacks C=O → carbinolamine; (2) protonation of OH and loss of water → iminium; (3) loss of H+ → imine. At pH 7 the dehydration (step 2) is rate-limiting (too little acid to protonate the OH); at pH 2 the addition step (1) is rate-limiting because most aniline is protonated and cannot attack.

Practice 298 L3 Mechanism

In the Hofmann bromamide reaction, why does the intermediate N-bromoamide need a second equivalent of base?

Show answer

The rearrangement requires an anionic nitrogen: loss of Br− is assisted by the push of the R–C bonding electrons toward N only when the nitrogen carries a negative charge (the bromoamidate). The neutral N-bromoamide is not a good enough electron source to trigger migration; the second deprotonation also makes Br− a better leaving group by providing the push.

Practice 299 L3 Mechanism

The carbylamine reaction of aniline does not work with the 2° amine N-methylaniline. Draw the point in the mechanism where the reaction stops.

Show answer

After addition of :CCl2 and proton transfer, N-methylaniline gives PhN(CH3)–CHCl2. To reach an isocyanide the nitrogen must lose two protons (to form N≡C); this 2° N has no N–H, so no elimination to –N+≡C− is possible.

Practice 300 L3 Mechanism

Show with arrows why the diazonium ion is attacked by phenoxide at the terminal nitrogen and at the para position of the phenoxide.

Show answer

In ArN≡N+ the LUMO is π*(N≡N) with its largest lobe on the terminal N; in phenoxide the HOMO (a ring π orbital strongly mixed with the O lone pair) has its largest coefficient at the para (and ortho) carbons. The best overlap of HOMO and LUMO bonds the para carbon to the terminal N, giving the cyclohexadienone-type σ-complex; loss of H+ gives the azo compound.

Practice 301 L3 Mechanism

Why does the Hofmann elimination require anti-periplanar geometry, and what orbital interaction does it use?

Show answer

The base removes the β-H as the C–H bonding pair becomes the new π bond while the C–N+ bond breaks. The filled σ(C–H) donates into the empty σ*(C–N+); maximum overlap occurs when the two bonds are anti-periplanar (180°). Syn elimination (Cope) instead uses a cyclic five-membered transition state.

Practice 302 L3 Mechanism

Explain why the Sandmeyer reaction proceeds through Cu(I) and not Cu(II).

Show answer

Cu(I) is a one-electron donor that transfers an electron into π*/σ* of ArN2+, giving Ar• + N2 + Cu(II). Cu(II) lacks the reducing power to initiate; it only serves to deliver the halide to the aryl radical in the second step and is reduced back to Cu(I).

Practice 303 L3 Mechanism

Why is the aryl cation formed in the Balz–Schiemann reaction trapped by BF4− but not usually formed in water?

Show answer

The aryl cation (an empty sp2 orbital in the ring plane, orthogonal to the π system) is extremely unstable and indiscriminately reactive. In a non-nucleophilic environment (dry BF4− salt) it can only abstract fluoride from BF4− (BF3 leaves). In water it is not formed free: water attacks the diazonium ion (SN1/SN2-like) or the rate is slow.

Practice 304 L3 Mechanism

Draw the Meisenheimer complex from 2,4-dinitrofluorobenzene and methylamine and identify where the negative charge resides.

Meisenheimer complex
Show answer

The complex has the sp3 carbon bearing F and NH2+CH3; the negative charge is delocalised onto the ortho and para carbons and into the oxygens of both nitro groups (o- and p-quinoid resonance forms). Loss of F− and H+ gives N-methyl-2,4-dinitroaniline.

Practice 305 L3 Mechanism

The nitrile C≡N is attacked by Grignard reagents but not by NaBH4. Explain using LUMO energies.

Show answer

The nitrile π* is higher in energy than π*(C=O), so only very strong nucleophiles (carbanion equivalents, AlH4−) attack it, particularly when the N is activated by Mg2+ coordination (Lewis acid). BH4− is a weaker hydride donor (the B–H bond is less polarised) and does not reduce nitriles.

Practice 306 L3 Mechanism

Why is ortho/para-chloronitrobenzene hydrolysed by NaOH at 130–170 °C while the Dow process for chlorobenzene needs about 350 °C?

Show answer

In the nitro-activated ring the Meisenheimer complex is stabilised by delocalisation of the charge onto the NO2 oxygens (the LUMO of the nitroarene is lowered, with large coefficients at o/p carbons). In chlorobenzene there is no such stabilisation (the elimination–addition benzyne path needs the stronger conditions).

Practice 307 L3 Mechanism

How many electrons are transferred in the complete reduction of one molecule of nitrobenzene to aniline and what is the order of intermediates? Give the electrons per step.

Show answer

Six electrons: PhNO2 → PhNO (2 e−) → PhNHOH (2 e−) → PhNH2 (2 e−); each step is accompanied by two protons, and the first and third by loss of water.

Practice 308 L3 Mechanism

The nitronate ion from nitromethane can react at C (Henry) or at O (nitronate esters). Why does C-alkylation/addition to carbonyl predominate?

Show answer

The HOMO of the nitronate has its largest coefficient on carbon, which is the soft, more nucleophilic site; reaction at C forms a strong C–C bond and a stable nitro compound. O-attack gives less stable nitronic esters (observed only with hard electrophiles such as silyl halides).

Practice 309 L3 Mechanism

The N-oxide of triethylamine decomposes when heated to ethene and Et2NOH. Describe the transition state and the number of electrons in the cyclic array.

Show answer

A five-membered cyclic transition state: O−, N+, Cα, Cβ and H. Six electrons move: the C–H σ pair, the C–N σ pair and a lone pair on O. It is a suprafacial, thermally allowed, syn elimination (Ei).
