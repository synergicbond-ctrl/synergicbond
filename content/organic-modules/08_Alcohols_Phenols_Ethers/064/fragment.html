15. Electrophilic substitution of phenol

The OH group is a strong activating o,p-director: the oxygen lone pair is donated to the ring (+M, which outweighs its −I effect). The arenium ion formed by attack at o or p has an extra resonance structure in which every atom (apart from H) has an octet (the oxonium form). Phenol is so reactive that it needs no Lewis-acid catalyst for halogenation and reacts with dilute nitric acid; phenoxide ion (O−) is even more reactive (it reacts with CO2, CHCl3/:CCl2 and ArN2+).

Example 42 L1 Foundation

Products of EAS

Give the major organic product when phenol reacts with (a) Br2/H2O (excess), (b) Br2/CS2, 273 K, (c) dil. HNO3, (d) conc. H2SO4, 373 K, (e) benzenediazonium chloride/NaOH at 273 K.

Example 43 L2 Main

Picric acid and sulphonation

Why is picric acid not made by direct nitration of phenol with concentrated HNO3? How many moles of HNO3 are consumed in forming 1 mol of picric acid by the two-step route?

Show solution
  1. Concentrated nitric acid oxidises the very electron-rich phenol ring to tarry quinones and ring-cleavage products, giving a poor yield.
  2. In the two-step route, phenol is sulphonated (conc. H2SO4, 2,4-disulphonic acid), and then conc. HNO3 replaces both SO3H groups and adds NO2 at the third position (ipso nitration followed by loss of SO3). Stoichiometrically, three NO2 groups need 3 mol HNO3 per mole of picric acid (the sulphonic acid groups act as protecting groups).
Example 44 L2 Main

Reactivity comparison

Rank the rate of bromination in water: benzene, phenol, nitrobenzene, sodium phenoxide, toluene.

Show solution
  1. Activation: O− (+M, a full negative charge) > OH (+M) > CH3 (+I, hyperconjugation) > H > NO2 (−M, −I).
  2. Order (decreasing): sodium phenoxide > phenol > toluene > benzene > nitrobenzene.
Example 45 L3 Advanced

Which position?

Predict the principal product of mono-bromination (Br2, CS2, 273 K) of (a) p-cresol, (b) 3-methylphenol, (c) 4-nitrophenol.

Show solution
  1. (a) The para position is blocked; Br enters ortho to the OH (the more powerful director): 2-bromo-4-methylphenol.
  2. (b) The OH directs to 2, 4 and 6. Position 4 (para to OH, ortho to CH3) and 6 (ortho to OH, para to CH3) are both activated by both groups (cooperative); position 2 is hindered between OH and CH3. Major: 4-bromo-3-methylphenol (para to OH) with some 2-bromo-5-methylphenol (ortho to OH, para to CH3).
  3. (c) The ring is deactivated by NO2, but OH still dominates and Br enters ortho to the OH (meta to NO2): 2-bromo-4-nitrophenol.
