AC-027 [Application] · Akshay topic Q27 · Status: Verified (standard radical stability; structures read from the image)

[Akshay Chaudhary topic, master-sheet page 18] Relative stability of radicals: (i) CH₃CH=CH–CH₂• (ii) •CH=CHCH₃ (iii) CH₃CH•CH₃. Options: (a) i>ii>iii (b) ii>iii>i (c) i>iii>ii (d) iii>i>ii

Full solution
  1. (i) is an allylic radical: resonance-stabilised.
  2. (iii) is a secondary alkyl radical: hyperconjugation only.
  3. (ii) is a vinyl radical (σ-type, unstable).
  4. Order i > iii > ii = option (c).

Answer: (c)

AC-038 [Application] · Akshay topic Q38 · Status: Partially verified (site correct; the stated reason is only partly complete)

[Akshay Chaudhary topic, page 18] Imidazole (pKₐ of conjugate acid ≈ 7): which N is protonated and why? (a) N1, aromatic heterocycle with n = 1 (b) N1, it is sp² (c) N3, it is sp² (d) N1, its lone pair is part of the aromatic π system

Full solution
  1. Protonation occurs at the pyridine-type N (N3). Its lone pair lies in the plane and is not part of the aromatic sextet.
  2. The N1 lone pair is in the aromatic sextet, so protonating N1 would disturb the aromatic system (option (d) is the correct reasoning for N1 not protonating).
  3. Option (c) gives the correct site; its reason (sp²) is partly right: the lone pair sits in an sp² orbital outside the π system.

Answer: (c)
