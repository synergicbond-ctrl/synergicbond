21. Nucleophilic aromatic substitution: addition–elimination (SNAr)

21.1 Mechanism

  • Step 1 (slow, rate-determining): the nucleophile adds to the ring carbon bearing X. The aromaticity is lost: an sp3 carbon (bearing X and Nu) is formed, and the negative charge is delocalised over the ortho and para carbons. This anionic σ-complex is the Meisenheimer complex (isolable in some cases, e.g. picryl ethers).
  • Step 2 (fast): X− leaves, aromaticity is restored.
  • Rate = k [ArX][Nu−] (second order).
+ slow, addition⟶ fast, −Cl−⟶
p-Nitrochlorobenzene to p-nitrophenol. The negative charge of the Meisenheimer complex is delocalised onto the nitro group (nitronate form shown).
Key idea Why ortho/para and not meta? Attack at C–X puts the negative charge on the ortho and para carbons (relative to the attacked carbon). A –NO2, –CN, –COR or –SO2R group at o/p can accept this charge directly (through resonance, onto the electronegative oxygens). A meta group cannot: its activation is only inductive and weak.
Trap The groups that activate SNAr are the same as those that deactivate and meta-direct in electrophilic substitution. A methyl or methoxy group at ortho/para decreases the rate of SNAr (destabilises the anion). Two nitro groups o,p to the halogen (2,4-dinitro-) are far more powerful than one; three (2,4,6-trinitro-, picryl chloride) make the halide as reactive as an acid chloride.

21.2 The leaving-group order is reversed: F ≫ Cl ≈ Br > I

In SNAr the first step (addition) is rate-determining, and the C–X bond is intact in that step. Fluorine, the most electronegative halogen, makes the ipso carbon most electrophilic and stabilises the Meisenheimer complex by its –I effect. Thus rate: ArF > ArNO2(ipso) > ArCl ≈ ArBr > ArI: opposite to SN2. In the benzyne mechanism, in contrast, C–X cleavage is involved in the rate-determining elimination and the order is Br > I > Cl > F.

Trap Check which mechanism a question implies. If strong EWG at o/p and a nucleophile: SNAr, F fastest. If NaNH2 and no EWG: benzyne (aryne), Br ≥ I > Cl > F (fluoride poor). If only alkali at ordinary temperature with no EWG: no reaction.

Example 34 Main

Rank

Arrange in increasing order of reactivity towards aqueous NaOH: chlorobenzene (A), 4-nitrochlorobenzene (B), 2,4-dinitrochlorobenzene (C), 2,4,6-trinitrochlorobenzene (D), 3-nitrochlorobenzene (E).

Show solution
  1. Activation by –NO2 at ortho/para is additive: D (three) > C (two) > B (one). A has none. A meta nitro (E) only withdraws inductively and lies between A and B.
  2. A < E < B < C < D.

Example 36 Adv

Mechanistic reasoning

The rate of reaction of 1-X-2,4-dinitrobenzene with piperidine is F : Cl : Br : I ≈ 3300 : 4.3 : 4.3 : 1 (relative). Explain and state what this shows about the rate-determining step.

Show solution
  1. F is far faster, and Cl and Br are equal: in SNAr the C–X bond is not broken in the rate-determining step. The rate follows the ability of X to stabilise the negative charge in the Meisenheimer complex and make the ipso carbon electrophilic (electronegativity: F ≫ Cl ≈ Br ≈ I). If C–X breaking were rate-determining, I would be fastest and F slowest (as in SN2).
  2. Thus the addition step is slow and the elimination is fast.
Example 37 Adv

Product of SNAr and ring position

Which chloro compound reacts faster with NaOEt: 1-chloro-2,4-dinitrobenzene or 1-chloro-3,5-dinitrobenzene? Explain.

Show solution
  1. 1-Chloro-2,4-dinitrobenzene: both NO2 groups are ortho and para to Cl; they delocalise the negative charge of the Meisenheimer complex. Reactive.
  2. 1-Chloro-3,5-dinitrobenzene: both NO2 are meta to Cl; the charge in the intermediate lies on carbons ortho and para to the attacked carbon, not on the nitro-bearing carbons; only inductive stabilisation. Far less reactive (comparable to a single meta substituent).
