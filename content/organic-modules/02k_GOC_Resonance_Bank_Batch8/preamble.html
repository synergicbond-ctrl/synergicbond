RM-129 [Application] · Master sheet Q129 · Status: Verified (counting)

[textbook-style] Carbonate ion (three equivalent contributors). Which is correct? (a) each C–O has bond order 4/3 (b) one C–O is double and two are single in the hybrid (c) 2 O⁻ and 1 O= on definite oxygens (d) carbon carries the negative charge

Full solution
  1. The hybrid has three equal C–O bonds of order 4/3 (no single localised double bond).
  2. (b) and (c) describe one contributor, not the hybrid; (d) is wrong (charge on O).

Answer: (a)

RM-131 [Application] · Master sheet Q131 · Status: Verified (arrow pushing)

[textbook-style] In the O⁻-containing resonance structures of penta-2,4-dienal (C-1 = CHO carbon), the positive charge resides on: (a) C-2 and C-4 (b) C-3 and C-5 (c) C-1 only (d) C-2 and C-5

Full solution
  1. Pushing the C=O π pair to O⁻ leaves C-1 positive.
  2. Further push along C2=C3 and C4=C5 moves the positive charge to C-3 and then C-5.
  3. Among the options, the charged positions beyond C-1 are C-3 and C-5: option (b).

Answer: (b)

RM-132 [Foundation] · Master sheet Q132 · Status: Verified

[textbook-style] Which resonance form contributes most to acrolein CH₂=CH–CH=O? (a) CH₂=CH–CH=O (b) ⁺CH₂–CH=CH–O⁻ (c) CH₂=CH–C⁺H–O⁻ (d) ⁻CH₂–CH=CH–O⁺

Full solution
  1. (a) is neutral with all octets and no formal charge: the most important contributor.
  2. (b), (c), (d) carry charges or unfavourable charge placement.

Answer: (a)

RM-133 [Application] · Master sheet Q133 · Status: Verified

[textbook-style] Most stable carbocation on protonation of 2-methyl-1-butene: (a) (CH₃)₂C⁺–CH₂CH₃ (b) CH₃CH(CH₂⁺)CH₂CH₃ (c) (CH₃)₂CH–C⁺H–CH₃ (d) CH₂=C(CH₃)CH₂CH₂⁺

Full solution
  1. Protonating C-1 of 2-methyl-1-butene gives the tertiary cation (CH₃)₂C⁺–CH₂CH₃.
  2. Tertiary is the most stable of the listed cations: option (a).

Answer: (a)

RM-134 [Foundation] · Master sheet Q134 · Status: Verified (arrow pushing)

[textbook-style] Curly-arrow push that converts CH₂=CH–CH₂⁺ into its resonance contributor: (a) π electrons of C=C move to form a new π bond to C3 (b) σ(C–H) moves (c) lone pair on C2 moves (d) carbon atom moves

Full solution
  1. The C1=C2 π pair moves toward C2–C3, which places the positive charge on C1.
  2. Only electrons move; no σ-bond or atom moves: (a).

Answer: (a)

RM-135 [Application] · Master sheet Q135 · Status: Verified

[textbook-style] Which ion is most stabilised by delocalisation into more than one electronegative atom? (a) CH₃COO⁻ (b) CH₃CH₂O⁻ (c) CH₃⁻ (d) (CH₃)₃C⁻

Full solution
  1. Acetate delocalises its charge over two O atoms (two equivalent forms).
  2. Ethoxide localises on one O; methyl and t-butyl carbanions have no electronegative delocalisation.

Answer: (a)

RM-136 [Application] · Master sheet Q136 · Status: Verified (sheet data; direction consistent with Ref. [2], p. 344)

[textbook-style] Heats of hydrogenation of 1-butene (30.3) vs trans-2-butene (27.6) differ mainly because: (a) more α-C–H hyperconjugation and alkyl substitution on the double bond (b) ring strain (c) hydrogen bonding (d) larger dipole moment

Full solution
  1. trans-2-butene has more substituents on the C=C (six α-H vs two in 1-butene), so it is more stable and releases less heat.
  2. Option (a).

Answer: (a)

RM-137 [Application] · Master sheet Q137 · Status: Verified (Ref. [2], Table 20-4 gives 2.86 for chloroacetic acid; sheet gives 2.85)

[textbook-style] Chloroacetic acid (pKₐ 2.85) is stronger than acetic acid (4.76) because: (a) the –I effect of Cl stabilises the carboxylate anion (b) Cl has +M (c) hyperconjugation of CH₂Cl (d) steric strain in the acid

Full solution
  1. Cl withdraws σ-electron density (−I), stabilising the carboxylate.
  2. (b) is wrong (Cl is −I dominant); (c) and (d) do not explain the trend.

Answer: (a)

RM-138 [Application] · Master sheet Q138 · Status: Verified (standard EAS activation order)

[textbook-style] Reactivity toward electrophilic attack on the ring: (a) PhOH > PhCH₃ > PhH > PhNO₂ (b) PhNO₂ > PhH > PhCH₃ > PhOH (c) PhCH₃ > PhOH > PhH > PhNO₂ (d) PhH > PhOH > PhCH₃ > PhNO₂

Full solution
  1. Activating: OH (strong +M) > CH₃ (+I, hyperconjugation) > H.
  2. Deactivating: NO₂ (strong −M) is least reactive.
  3. Order (a).

Answer: (a)

RM-139 [Foundation] · Master sheet Q139 · Status: Verified (Ref. [3] Scheme 1.1)

[textbook-style] Inductive effect of alkyl groups on a C=C (propene dipole 0.36 D): the methyl group is: (a) electron releasing (+I and hyperconjugation) (b) electron withdrawing (c) neutral (d) only +M

Full solution
  1. Propene’s dipole has CH₃ at the positive end (Ref. [3] Scheme 1.1: 0.366 D), so CH₃ releases electrons.

Answer: (a)

RM-140 [Application] · Master sheet Q140 · Status: Verified against sheet passage data

[textbook-style] Gas-phase carbocation with the lowest hydride-ion affinity (HIA, lower = more stable): (a) (CH₃)₃C⁺ (b) CH₃CH₂⁺ (c) CH₃⁺ (d) CH₂=CH⁺

Full solution
  1. Sheet HIA values: t-Bu 231, ethyl 270, methyl 312; vinyl is least stable (highest HIA).
  2. Lowest = t-Bu, option (a).

Answer: (a)

RM-143 [Foundation] · Master sheet Q143 · Status: Verified (standard NMR reasoning; the 335 ppm value is the sheet’s, not checked against an opened source)

[textbook-style] ¹³C shift of the C⁺ in tert-butyl cation ≈ 335 ppm because: (a) C⁺ is strongly deshielded (electron-deficient) (b) C⁺ is shielded (c) it is sp³ (d) it is a radical

Full solution
  1. An empty p orbital on a sp² cation carbon leaves the nucleus strongly deshielded.
  2. Option (a); (c) and (d) are wrong (sp², not radical).

Answer: (a)
