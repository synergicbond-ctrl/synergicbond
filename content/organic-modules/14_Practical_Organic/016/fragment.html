5.1 Column chromatography

A glass column is packed with a slurry of the adsorbent (silica gel, alumina). The mixture, dissolved in a minimum of solvent, is placed on top. An eluting solvent is poured through and the components travel down at different rates. Components that are least strongly adsorbed (least polar on silica) come out first. Fractions are collected, each is checked by TLC, the pure ones are combined and the solvent is evaporated. A more polar eluent washes off strongly adsorbed compounds faster.

IMP On a polar adsorbent (silica gel, alumina) the order of elution with a non-polar solvent is: hydrocarbons < alkyl halides < ethers < esters < ketones, aldehydes < alcohols < amines < acids. The more polar a compound, the stronger it sticks and the lower its Rf.
