14.1 Why phenol is more acidic than alcohols

  • Resonance. The negative charge of phenoxide is delocalised over the ortho and para carbons (three extra canonical structures besides the one with the charge on oxygen; five contributors with the two Kekulé forms). Alkoxide ions have no such delocalisation.
  • Inductive effect. The ring carbon attached to O is sp2 (33 % s character), more electronegative than sp3 carbon; it withdraws electron density through σ bonds (−I), stabilising the anion.
  • Destabilisation of the neutral molecule by resonance (donation of the lone pair into the ring puts + on oxygen, strengthening the O–H polarity) makes the neutral phenol slightly less stable, so ionisation is easier.
