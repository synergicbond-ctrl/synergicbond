22. Epoxidation and the two routes to diols

22.1 Epoxidation with peroxy acids

Alkene + RCO3H (peracetic acid, mCPBA, perbenzoic acid, monoperphthalic acid; or H2O2 with a catalyst) → epoxide (oxirane) + RCOOH. The peracid transfers its electrophilic oxygen to the π bond in a single concerted "butterfly" step: no cation, no rearrangement. Result: syn addition of O and retention of alkene geometry: a cis alkene gives a cis-epoxide; a trans alkene gives a trans-epoxide (stereospecific). Electron-rich alkenes react faster (R2C=CR2 > R2C=CHR > RCH=CH2).

Industrial: ethene + O2, Ag, 250 °C → ethylene oxide (not a peracid method). Epichlorohydrin and propylene oxide are made through halohydrins (Cl2/H2O then base, section 21).

22.2 Opening of epoxides: acid vs base

Epoxides are strained (≈ 115 kJ) and open easily with nucleophiles, always with inversion at the attacked carbon (SN2-like, anti addition across the C–O).

Example 38 Advanced

Stereochemical outcome of epoxide hydrolysis

Predict the diol obtained from (a) cis-2,3-dimethyloxirane (b) trans-2,3-dimethyloxirane with dilute H3O+. Is each product optically active?

Show solution
  1. Water attacks one carbon with inversion; the other carbon keeps its configuration.
  2. (a) The cis (meso) epoxide has (2R,3S) configuration. Attack at C-2 gives (2S,3S); attack at C-3 gives (2R,3R). Equal probability: racemic (2R,3R)- and (2S,3S)-butane-2,3-diol; optically inactive as a mixture.
  3. (b) The trans epoxide, say (2R,3R): inversion at either carbon gives (2S,3R) = meso-butane-2,3-diol (the same compound from either attack); optically inactive.
  4. Compare: cis-2-butene + OsO4 → meso; trans-2-butene + OsO4 → racemic. The epoxide route gives the opposite result.

22.3 Syn-dihydroxylation: OsO4 and cold KMnO4

  • OsO4 (catalytic, with NMO or K3Fe(CN)6 as the re-oxidant; or stoichiometric then NaHSO3/H2S) and cold, dilute, alkaline KMnO4 (Baeyer's reagent) add two OH groups to the same face: they proceed through a cyclic osmate (or manganate) ester, formed by concerted [3+2] addition of the metal-oxo to the alkene; hydrolysis (reductive for OsO4) liberates the cis-diol with no C–O bond breakage.
  • KMnO4 gives lower yields (over-oxidation); the colour change purple → brown (MnO2) is the Baeyer test for unsaturation. OsO4 is toxic and expensive but gives high yields.

Anti-dihydroxylation: (i) epoxidation then H3O+; (ii) peroxyformic acid (HCO3H, from HCOOH + H2O2) or HCO2H/H2O2 which epoxidises and opens in situ; (iii) Prévost reaction (I2, silver benzoate, anhydrous, then hydrolysis: anti) as opposed to Woodward (same reagents with water: syn).

Example 39 Advanced

Stereo bookkeeping

Complete the table: which stereoisomer(s) of 2,3-dibromobutane or butane-2,3-diol are formed from: (a) cis-but-2-ene + Br2 (b) trans-but-2-ene + cold KMnO4 (c) trans-but-2-ene + mCPBA then H3O+ (d) cis-but-2-ene + OsO4.

Show solution
  1. (a) anti on cis → (±) racemic. (b) syn on trans → (±) racemic diol. (c) epoxidation retains trans geometry (trans epoxide), then anti opening: anti total on trans gives meso diol. (d) syn on cis → meso.
