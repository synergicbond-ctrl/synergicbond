10. Family 5: aromatic acylation and formylation

10.1 Friedel–Crafts acylation Benzene + acid chloride (or anhydride) + anhydrous AlCl3 gives an aryl ketone. The electrophile is the acylium ion RCO+. Unlike alkylation, it does not rearrange and it does not poly-substitute, because the product is deactivated by C=O; a stoichiometric amount (more than 1 equiv) of AlCl3 is needed because the ketone complexes the Lewis acid.

Benzene with maleic anhydride gives 3-benzoylacrylic acid.

The reaction fails on rings carrying strongly deactivating groups (NO2, COR, CN, SO3H), with aromatic amines (they complex AlCl3) and with phenols under normal conditions (O-acylation competes). 10.2 Gattermann–Koch formylation Benzene (or alkylbenzene) + CO + HCl, with AlCl3/CuCl under pressure, gives benzaldehyde. The electrophile is the formyl cation HCO+, formed from CO and HCl (formyl chloride, HCOCl, cannot itself be isolated). It is not applicable to phenols and phenyl ethers, nor to deactivated rings, and not to nitrobenzene.

Toluene → p-tolualdehyde (para, para-directing and bulky electrophile)

10.3 Gattermann reaction Benzene + HCN + HCl + AlCl3 (or Zn(CN)2/HCl), then hydrolysis, gives benzaldehyde. It can be used with phenols and their ethers because the electrophile (HC≡NH+, formimidoyl cation) is milder. 10.4 Reimer–Tiemann reaction Phenol + CHCl3 + aqueous NaOH (about 340 K), then acidification gives mostly salicylaldehyde (2-hydroxybenzaldehyde). The electrophile is dichlorocarbene :CCl2, generated by α-elimination from CCl3−. The phenoxide ring attacks the carbene at ortho (para minor); the dichloromethyl compound is hydrolysed to CHO. With CCl4 the product is salicylic acid.

10.5 Vilsmeier–Haack formylation Electron-rich arenes (phenols, N,N-dimethylaniline, pyrrole) + DMF + POCl3 give aryl aldehydes (iminium salt hydrolysed). The electrophile is the chloroiminium ion (Vilsmeier reagent). Useful where Gattermann–Koch fails.

10.6 Fries rearrangement and Houben–Hoesch Phenyl esters rearrange with AlCl3 to hydroxyaryl ketones (Fries): low temperature favours para, high temperature favours ortho (the ortho product is chelated and thermodynamically stable). Nitriles + HCl + ZnCl2 acylate electron-rich phenols (Houben–Hoesch), giving hydroxyaryl ketones.
