3. Physical properties and spectroscopic fingerprints

  • Benzene: colourless liquid, b.p. 353 K (80 °C), m.p. 279.5 K (5.5 °C); non-polar, immiscible with water, burns with a sooty (smoky, luminous) flame because of high C : H ratio. Benzene is carcinogenic: toluene is the usual safe replacement solvent.
  • Toluene, xylenes and ethylbenzene are liquids; naphthalene (m.p. 353 K) and anthracene (m.p. 489 K) are crystalline solids; naphthalene sublimes (moth balls).
  • Of the xylenes, p-xylene has the highest melting point (286 K) because it packs best; o-xylene (248 K) and m-xylene (225 K) are lower. The same para-high-melting trend holds for most disubstituted benzenes (p-dichlorobenzene 326 K, o 256 K, m 248 K). Boiling points follow polarity: ortho > meta > para (o-xylene 417 K, m 412 K, p 411 K).
  • 1H NMR: ring protons at δ 6.5–8.5 (benzene δ 7.27) because the induced ring current deshields them (a diagnostic of aromaticity); a benzylic CH3 or CH2 at δ 2.2–2.7. Protons held above the plane of the ring (as in [18]annulene's inner H, or a cyclophane) are shifted upfield, even negative δ.
  • 13C NMR: aromatic carbons 120–150 ppm. A para-disubstituted ring with two identical groups shows only 2 aromatic C signals; mono-substituted shows 4; o-disubstituted (identical groups) shows 3; m-disubstituted shows 4; 1,3,5 shows 2.
  • IR: aromatic C–H stretch just above 3000 cm-1; ring C=C stretches near 1600 and 1500 cm-1; out-of-plane C–H bends (900–690 cm-1) indicate substitution pattern (monosubstituted: 750 and 700, strong; para: 830).

A little spectroscopy

A compound C8H10 shows two aromatic 13C signals and one aliphatic 13C signal. Identify it. Another isomer shows four aromatic signals and two aliphatic ones: identify it.

Show solution
  1. C8H10 has 4 degrees of unsaturation: a benzene ring plus saturated side chains: ethylbenzene or a xylene.
  2. Two aromatic signals + one CH3 signal → p-xylene (high symmetry: both CH3 equivalent).
  3. Four aromatic + two aliphatic: ethylbenzene has 4 aromatic (ipso, o, m, p) and 2 aliphatic (CH2, CH3) → ethylbenzene. (m-Xylene has 4 aromatic but only 1 CH3 signal, so it does not fit the second description.)
