Practice 328 Adv

Isobutyl bromide

Predict the product(s) when 1-bromo-2-methylpropane reacts with sodium cyanide in DMSO and with potassium tert-butoxide.

Show answer
NaCN, DMSO⟶
SN2 despite the β-branch (CN− is a good nucleophile, weak base): 3-methylbutanenitrile.
t-BuOK⟶
With the bulky strong base: E2 gives 2-methylpropene.
Practice 329 Adv

Tertiary halide solvolysis

Give the major organic product of tert-butyl chloride in methanol at room temperature and the mechanism.

Show answer
CH3OH⟶
SN1: tert-butyl methyl ether (MTBE), with a little 2-methylpropene (E1).
Practice 330 Adv

Thiol

Write the product of bromoethane with sodium hydrogen sulfide and say why a large excess of NaSH is used.

Show answer
NaSH, excess⟶
Ethanethiol. HS− is an excellent nucleophile; the thiol product (as thiolate) can react again with EtBr to give diethyl sulfide, so excess NaSH is used to suppress this.

Wurtz–Fittig

Which products form when chlorobenzene and chloromethane react with sodium in dry ether? Which is the main one?

Show answer
+ 2 Na, dry ether⟶
Toluene (cross-coupling) as the Wurtz–Fittig product, with biphenyl (PhPh) and ethane (CH3CH3) as by-products.
Practice 333 Adv

Nitro compound

Give the product of iodoethane with silver nitrite and with sodium nitrite in DMF (major, textbook rule).

AgNO2⟶

NaNO2⟶

Practice 335 Adv

Grignard and an aldehyde

Give the product of ethylmagnesium bromide with acetaldehyde after acid work-up, and its stereochemistry.

Show answer
+ H3O+⟶
Butan-2-ol, formed as a racemic mixture (the carbonyl group is planar and is attacked from either face).
Practice 336 Adv

Cyclic halohydrin

Predict the product of 2-bromoethanol with NaOH (1 equiv) and then with excess ammonia.

Show answer
NaOH⟶
Ethylene oxide by intramolecular SN2.
+ excess NH3⟶
Ammonia opens the epoxide: 2-aminoethanol (ethanolamine) with some di- and triethanolamine.
Practice 337 Adv

Neopentyl halide

Neopentyl chloride is treated with sodium ethoxide in ethanol at 55 °C. What happens, and why?

Show answer
NaOEt, EtOH, 55 °C⟶
Essentially no reaction: SN2 is blocked by the t-butyl group and E2 is impossible because the β-carbon has no hydrogens. (Forcing conditions with ionisation would give rearranged products.)
