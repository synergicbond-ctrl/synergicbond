7. Allylic, benzylic, vinylic, aryl and bridgehead halides

Allylic and benzylic halides are unusually reactive in both mechanisms. For SN1 the allyl or benzyl cation is stabilised by resonance delocalisation; for SN2 the transition state is stabilised by overlap of the p orbitals of the adjacent π system with the p-like orbital at the reacting carbon. For example allyl chloride and benzyl chloride react with iodide in acetone far faster than a simple primary chloride.

Allyl and benzyl chloride (reactive); vinyl and phenyl chloride (essentially inert to SN1 and SN2).

Vinylic and aryl halides are inert under ordinary substitution conditions. The halogen is attached to an sp2 carbon, so the C–X bond is shorter and stronger (partial double-bond character through overlap of the halogen lone pair with the π system); a vinyl or phenyl cation is very unstable (the empty orbital lies in the plane, not conjugated); and backside attack is blocked by the π electrons and by the geometry of the ring or alkene. Bridgehead halides such as 1-bromobicyclo[2.2.1]heptane are also inert: backside attack is impossible because the carbon is held in a cage, and the cation cannot become planar.

1-Bromobicyclo[2.2.1]heptane: neither SN2 (no backside) nor SN1 (cannot flatten).

Trap. An allylic halide that ionises gives a delocalised cation, so the nucleophile may attack either terminus (allylic transposition). Under SN1 solvolysis in water, 3-chlorobut-1-ene and 1-chlorobut-2-ene both give the same two alcohols, but-3-en-2-ol and but-2-en-1-ol.
