6. Alkenes: epoxidation and syn or anti dihydroxylation

Epoxidation with a peroxy acid (mCPBA) is a concerted, single-step transfer of oxygen to the C=C bond, often drawn as a ‘butterfly’ transition state. Both C–O bonds form on the same face, so the reaction is stereospecific: a cis alkene gives a cis (meso, for cis-but-2-ene) epoxide and a trans alkene gives the trans epoxide. Electron-rich (more substituted) double bonds react faster than electron-poor ones, which allows selective epoxidation of one double bond in a diene.

Two stereochemically opposite routes lead from an alkene to a 1,2-diol. Syn-dihydroxylation delivers both OH groups to the same face. OsO4 adds in a concerted [3+2] step to give a cyclic osmate(VI) ester; hydrolysis (NaHSO3 or H2S) frees the diol. Because OsO4 is toxic and expensive it is used catalytically with a co-oxidant, usually N-methylmorpholine N-oxide (NMO), which reoxidises Os(VI) to Os(VIII). Cold, dilute, alkaline KMnO4 also gives syn-diols, via a cyclic manganate(V) ester; it is less efficient and over-oxidises on warming or acidifying.

Anti-dihydroxylation is a two-step sequence: epoxidation, then ring-opening with aqueous acid (H3O+) or hydroxide. Water attacks the protonated epoxide from the side opposite to the leaving oxygen (SN2-like inversion), so the two OH groups end up anti. The overall result for stereochemistry is summarised below.

Key idea. cis alkene + syn → meso (if the two ends are identical); cis + anti → racemate; trans + syn → racemate; trans + anti → meso. In a ring, syn gives cis-diol and anti gives trans-diol.
Trap. Cold KMnO4 must be dilute, cold and alkaline to stop at the diol. Hot, concentrated or acidified KMnO4 cleaves the C=C bond (next section).
