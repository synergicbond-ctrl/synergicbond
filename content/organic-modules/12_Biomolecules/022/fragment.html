4.1 Components: bases, sugars, nucleosides, nucleotides

Nucleic acids are polymers of nucleotides. A nucleotide has three parts: a nitrogenous base, a pentose (D-ribose in RNA, 2-deoxy-D-ribose in DNA) and phosphate.

adenine (A) guanine (G) cytosine (C) thymine (T, DNA only) uracil (U, RNA only)
Purines (A, G): fused six- and five-membered rings, 9 ring atoms. Pyrimidines (C, T, U): one six-membered ring. Thymine = 5-methyluracil
Key idea Purines are two-ring; pyrimidines are one-ring. Mnemonic: "CUT the Py": Cytosine, Uracil, Thymine are pyrimidines; "PURe As Gold": Adenine, Guanine are purines. Purine: C5H4N4; the four bases and their formulas are A C5H5N5, G C5H5N5O, C C4H5N3O, T C5H6N2O2, U C4H4N2O2.
  • Lactam–lactim tautomerism: the keto (lactam) forms C=O/N–H predominate; the rare enol (lactim) and imino tautomers mispair (e.g. enol-T pairs with G) and are one source of spontaneous mutation.
  • Methylated bases: 5-methylcytosine (epigenetic mark), 1-methyladenine, etc.

Trap Ring numbering: the sugar carbons are primed (1′ to 5′) to distinguish them from the base atoms (1 to 9). So the glycosidic bond is C-1′–N-9 (purines) or C-1′–N-1 (pyrimidines), the phosphate in a mononucleotide is esterified at 5′-OH, and the 2′-OH is absent in DNA (hence "deoxy").
AMP (adenosine 5′-monophosphate) ATP (adenosine 5′-triphosphate)
Nucleotides. ATP, the energy currency: hydrolysis of the terminal phosphoanhydride bond, ATP + H₂O → ADP + Pi, releases ≈ 30.5 kJ mol⁻¹ under standard conditions (more in the cell)
  • A nucleotide's phosphate carries a negative charge at pH 7 (pKa2 ≈ 6.5 for the monoester): DNA and RNA are polyanions.
  • Other roles: NAD+, FAD, coenzyme A (adenine nucleotides), cAMP (second messenger, a 3′,5′-cyclic phosphate), GTP (G-protein signalling).
