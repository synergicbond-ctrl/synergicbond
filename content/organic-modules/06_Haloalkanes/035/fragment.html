34. Practice set, Level 3 (Advanced): integer-type problems

The answer to each problem in this set is a single non-negative integer. Where the question asks for stereoisomers, count enantiomers separately and treat E and Z as different compounds.

Practice 207 Integer

Monochlorination count

How many monochlorinated products (all stereoisomers counted) are possible from the free-radical chlorination of 3-methylpentane?

Show answer
  1. Distinct carbon environments in CH3CH2–CH(CH3)–CH2CH3: C1/C5 (CH3), C2/C4 (CH2), C3 (CH), and the methyl on C3.
  2. 1-Chloro-3-methylpentane: stereocentre at C3 → 2. 2-Chloro-3-methylpentane: two stereocentres (C2, C3) → 4. 3-Chloro-3-methylpentane: achiral → 1. 1-Chloro-2-ethylbutane: achiral → 1.
  3. Total = 2 + 4 + 1 + 1 = 8.
Practice 208 Integer

Monochlorination count

How many monochlorinated products, including stereoisomers, form from methylcyclopentane with Cl2/hν?

Show answer
  1. Four constitutional isomers: (chloromethyl)cyclopentane (achiral: 1); 1-chloro-1-methylcyclopentane (achiral: 1); 1-chloro-2-methylcyclopentane (two stereocentres, cis and trans, each chiral: 4); 1-chloro-3-methylcyclopentane (cis and trans, each chiral: 4).
  2. Total = 1 + 1 + 4 + 4 = 10.

Practice 210 Integer

Monochlorination count

2,2,3-Trimethylbutane is chlorinated (Cl2, hν). How many monochloro products, counting stereoisomers?

Show answer
  1. (CH3)3C–CH(CH3)2. Three environments: the nine H on the tert-butyl methyls (→ ClCH2C(CH3)2CH(CH3)2, achiral), the one tertiary H (→ (CH3)3C–CCl(CH3)2, achiral), and the six H of the isopropyl methyls (→ (CH3)3C–CH(CH3)–CH2Cl, chiral: 2 enantiomers).
  2. Total = 1 + 1 + 2 = 4.

Practice 238 Integer

First-order kinetics

tert-Butyl chloride hydrolyses in aqueous acetone with k = 2.31 × 10−2 min−1. How many minutes are needed for 87.5 % of the chloride to be consumed?

Show answer
  1. 87.5 % consumed = 3 half-lives (50 → 75 → 87.5 %). t½ = 0.693/2.31 × 10−2 = 30 min. t = 3 × 30 = 90 min.

E2 constraints
