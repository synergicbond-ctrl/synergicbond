IMP Two rules cover most questions: (1) the more electronegative the neighbouring atom/group, the larger δ (CH3–CH3 0.9; CH3–O 3.3; CH3–Cl 3.0); effect falls off over about three bonds; (2) π-electron systems create their own local fields: protons on the periphery of a benzene ring are deshielded (δ 7), aldehyde H (δ 9-10) very strongly deshielded (the C=O acts similarly), alkenyl H δ 5-6. Acid protons (O–H) exchange: add D2O, shake, and the O–H (and N–H) signal disappears, since R–OH + D2O ⇌ R–OD + HDO.
Bonding Insight Why ring protons are at δ 7 (orbital picture). In benzene the six π electrons occupy delocalised molecular orbitals. In the applied field B0 they circulate around the ring (a *ring current*); the induced field opposes B0 inside the ring but reinforces it outside, at the position of the ring hydrogens, so these protons feel a larger effective field and are deshielded (δ ≈ 7 rather than 5-6 for an isolated alkene). The same anisotropy argument, together with the electronegative oxygen, places the aldehyde H at δ 9-10. Aromatic stabilisation (4n + 2 π electrons) is thus visible in NMR: [18]annulene inner protons even appear at negative δ.
