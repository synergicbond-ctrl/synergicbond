1. Orientation: what this chapter covers and how it is examined

Nitrogen compounds sit at the junction of three big ideas in JEE Advanced organic chemistry: (i) electronic effects decide basicity, (ii) named reactions decide synthesis and identification, (iii) diazonium salts are the most flexible intermediate in aromatic chemistry because the group –N2+ can be replaced by almost anything. Questions fall into five repeating types.

  • Order questions – arrange amines in order of basic strength (aqueous or gas phase), or compare pKb/pKa of substituted anilines. These test the electronic-effect toolkit of Chapter 2.
  • Reagent/product questions – Hinsberg, carbylamine, HNO2, Hofmann bromamide, Gabriel, Sandmeyer, coupling. These test reagent knowledge and the special conditions (temperature, pH, medium).
  • Count questions – how many isomeric amines, how many give N2, how many react with Hinsberg reagent and give a KOH-soluble product. These need systematic enumeration.
  • Multi-step synthesis and identification – an unknown C7H9N or C3H9N is identified from a chain of tests; or a four-step conversion of benzene to a tri-substituted benzene with a fixed substitution pattern.
  • Numerical – mass of tribromoaniline, equivalent mass of an amine from the chloroplatinate, nitrogen estimation (Dumas, Kjeldahl, Duma's).

The chapter is organised as: Part A (structure, nomenclature, physical properties, basicity), Part B (all preparations), Part C (reactions of amines, identification), Part D (aniline and aromatic substitution), Part E (diazonium salts and azo dyes), Part F (nitro compounds), Part G (cyanides and isocyanides), Part H (summary sheets and distinction tests), Part I (graded problem bank with full solutions).

Key idea Always ask three questions about any nitrogen compound: (1) is the lone pair on N free (basic/nucleophilic) or delocalised (aniline, amide, pyrrole)? (2) How many H atoms are on N (1°, 2° or 3°)? – this decides acylation, Hinsberg, carbylamine, HNO2. (3) Is N attached to an aromatic ring? – this decides diazonium stability and electrophilic substitution.
