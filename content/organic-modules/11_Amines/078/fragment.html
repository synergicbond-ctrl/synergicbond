78. Level 3, set D — multi-step identification

Practice 274 L3 Identify

A chiral primary amine A (C8H11N) gives a positive carbylamine test. With NaNO2/HCl it evolves N2 and gives B (C8H10O), which gives the iodoform test and on oxidation gives acetophenone. Identify A and B, and say what Hofmann degradation of which amide would give A.

A: 1-phenylethanamine B: 1-phenylethanol
Show answer
  1. Carbylamine → 1° amine; N2 → N on sp3 carbon; B contains the CH3CH(OH)– unit (iodoform) and oxidises to PhCOCH3, so B = PhCH(OH)CH3. Then A = PhCH(NH2)CH3, chiral (stereocentre). The amide PhCH(CH3)CONH2 (C9H11NO) loses one carbon.

A = 1-phenylethanamine; B = 1-phenylethanol. A is obtained from 2-phenylpropanamide by Br2/KOH.

Practice 275 L3 Identify

An amide A (C4H9NO) is heated with Br2/KOH to give amine B (C3H9N), which on treatment with NaNO2/HCl yields C (C3H8O). C gives a positive iodoform test and on oxidation gives acetone. Identify A, B, C.

Show answer
  1. Oxidation of C to acetone gives C = (CH3)2CHOH (iodoform positive), B = (CH3)2CHNH2, A = (CH3)2CHCONH2 (C4H9NO ✓).

A = 2-methylpropanamide; B = propan-2-amine; C = propan-2-ol.

Practice 276 L3 Identify

A yellow, steam-volatile solid A (C6H6N2O2) is a much weaker base than aniline. Diazotisation followed by H3PO2 gives nitrobenzene. Identify A and explain the steam volatility.

o-nitroaniline
Show answer
  1. The diazotisation/deamination sequence only shows that the compound is a nitroaniline; steam volatility selects the ortho isomer.

A = o-nitroaniline. Its intramolecular N–H···O hydrogen bond reduces intermolecular association, so it is volatile in steam (the m- and p-isomers, intermolecularly hydrogen-bonded, are not).

Practice 277 L3 Identify

A tertiary amine A (C3H9N) forms a salt with HCl, does not react with benzenesulphonyl chloride, and gives no gas with HNO2. Its methiodide on treatment with moist Ag2O and heat gives no alkene. Identify A and explain the last observation.

Show answer

A = trimethylamine. The methiodide is (CH3)4N+I−; it has no β-hydrogen so Hofmann elimination is impossible (the hydroxide on heating gives CH3OH + (CH3)3N by substitution).

Practice 278 L3 Identify

Which of the following amines give 3-methylbut-1-ene as the Hofmann elimination product after exhaustive methylation? (A) 3-methylbutan-1-amine, (B) 3-methylbutan-2-amine, (C) 2-methylbutan-2-amine, (D) 2-methylbutan-1-amine.

Show answer
  1. (A) (CH3)2CHCH2CH2N+Me3: β-H on C2 gives (CH3)2CHCH=CH2. (B) (CH3)2CH–CH(CH3)–N+Me3: Hofmann loss from the CH3 gives (CH3)2CH–CH=CH2. (C) gives 2-methylbut-1-ene (and 2-methylbut-2-ene minor). (D) gives 2-methylbut-1-ene.

A and B.

Practice 279 L3 Identify

Benzaldehyde → (NH2OH) → B → (Ac2O, heat) → C → (LiAlH4) → D → (NaNO2/HCl, 273 K) → E. Identify B–E.

Show answer
  1. Oxime formation; dehydration of the aldoxime (Ac2O); full reduction of the nitrile; benzyl diazonium ion is unstable and gives PhCH2OH (and some PhCH2OAc/PhCH2Cl).

B = benzaldehyde oxime; C = benzonitrile; D = benzylamine; E = benzyl alcohol (with N2 evolved).

Practice 280 L3 Identify

Nitrobenzene is reduced in the presence of Zn/NaOH to a yellow-orange compound A (C12H10N2O), which is reduced further to B (C12H10N2) and then to C (C12H12N2). C with aqueous HCl gives its isomer D (C12H12N2), which can be tetrazotised and coupled with naphthionic acid to give a red dye. Identify A–D.

A: azoxybenzene B: azobenzene C: hydrazobenzene D: benzidine
Show answer
  1. Alkaline reduction stages as in the notes; hydrazobenzene rearranges in acid (benzidine rearrangement) to 4,4'-diaminobiphenyl, which has two NH2 groups to diazotise (tetrazotisation).

A = azoxybenzene, B = azobenzene, C = hydrazobenzene, D = benzidine (the dye is Congo red).

Practice 281 L3 Identify

Ethyl bromide is divided into two portions. Portion 1 is warmed with ethanolic KCN to give P; portion 2 is warmed with AgCN to give Q. P and Q are each treated with LiAlH4 to give R and S, respectively. R and S are each treated with NaNO2/HCl at 273 K. Identify P, Q, R, S and describe the observation with each of R and S.

P Q R S
Show answer
  1. KCN (ionic) attacks through C; AgCN (covalent) through N. Reduction: nitrile → RCH2NH2 (1°); isocyanide → R–NH–CH3 (2°).

P = propanenitrile; Q = ethyl isocyanide; R = propan-1-amine; S = N-methylethanamine. R + HNO2: brisk evolution of N2 (propanols/propene); S + HNO2: yellow oil N-nitroso-N-methylethanamine, no gas.

Practice 282 L3 Identify

A compound has C 77.4 %, H 7.5 %, N 15.1 % by mass. Its molar mass is 93 g mol-1 (titration with HCl as a monoacidic base). It gives a white precipitate with bromine water and a foul odour with CHCl3/KOH. Identify it.

Show answer
  1. Ratios: C 77.4/12 = 6.45, H 7.5/1 = 7.5, N 15.1/14 = 1.08; dividing by 1.08: C 5.97, H 6.94, N 1 → C6H7N (M = 93 ✓). Bromine water precipitate and carbylamine → 1° aromatic amine = aniline.

Aniline, C6H5NH2.

Practice 283 L3 Identify

A primary aromatic amine A (C7H9N) is acetylated to B; B is oxidised with alkaline KMnO4 to 4-acetamidobenzoic acid. A itself, on acetylation followed by bromination (Br2/AcOH) and hydrolysis, gives a monobromo-amine C in which Br is ortho to NH2. Identify A, and write the structure of C.

A: p-toluidine C: 2-bromo-4-methylaniline
Show answer
  1. Oxidation of the CH3 of B to COOH gives 4-acetamidobenzoic acid, so CH3 and NHAc are para: A = 4-methylaniline (C7H9N ✓). With the para position blocked by CH3, the NHAc group directs bromine ortho; hydrolysis gives 2-bromo-4-methylaniline.

A = p-toluidine; C = 2-bromo-4-methylaniline.

Practice 284 L3 Identify

Aniline is treated with excess CH3I, then Ag2O/H2O, then heated. What is the organic product, and how does it differ from the product of the same sequence applied to cyclohexylamine?

Show answer

Aniline: phenyltrimethylammonium hydroxide on heating gives N,N-dimethylaniline + methanol (no β-hydrogen, so no alkene; SN2 attack on a methyl group). Cyclohexylamine: cyclohexyltrimethylammonium hydroxide gives cyclohexene + trimethylamine.

Practice 285 L3 Identify

A secondary amine A (C5H13N) consumes two moles of CH3I. The product, converted to the hydroxide and heated, gives trimethylamine and a single alkene whose ozonolysis gives only formaldehyde and acetone. Give both possible structures of A.

Show answer
  1. Ozonolysis giving HCHO + (CH3)2C=O identifies the alkene as 2-methylpropene (CH2=C(CH3)2). Two methyl groups are added to the 2° amine, so the quaternary ion is C4H9–N+(CH3)3; the loss of NMe3 gives isobutene when the C4H9 group is tert-butyl or isobutyl. Hence A = CH3NH–C(CH3)3 or CH3NH–CH2CH(CH3)2 (both C5H13N ✓).

A = N-methyl-2-methylpropan-2-amine (N-methyl-tert-butylamine) or N-methyl-2-methylpropan-1-amine (N-methylisobutylamine).
