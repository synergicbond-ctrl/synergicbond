1. Definition and conditions

A conjugated substituent contributes to the π-system only when its p-orbitals overlap with the orbitals of the ring or of the reacting centre. Steric inhibition of resonance (SIR) is the loss of that overlap when bulky neighbours force the group out of the plane. The group then acts only through σ-bonds (inductive effect), and its mesomeric effect is reduced or removed.

Conditions: a conjugating group (–COOH, –OH, –NR₂, –NO₂, –CH=O) attached to a ring, and one or two ortho substituents large enough to rotate it. Classic cases: 2,6-disubstituted benzoic acids, N,N-dimethylanilines with ortho methyls, 3,5-dimethyl-4-nitrophenol, and tetra-ortho-substituted biphenyls.
