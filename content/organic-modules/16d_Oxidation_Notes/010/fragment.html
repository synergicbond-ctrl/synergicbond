11. Oxidation of sulfur and nitrogen compounds

Heteroatoms are often more easily oxidised than C–H or C=C bonds. Sulfides are oxidised in two controlled stages. One equivalent of H2O2 (acetic acid), NaIO4 or one equivalent of mCPBA at low temperature gives the sulfoxide R2S=O; a second equivalent (or excess H2O2/heat, KMnO4, two equivalents of mCPBA) gives the sulfone R2SO2. Each stage adds one oxygen atom and takes 2 e−. Sulfur is oxidised in preference to a C=C bond by peracids, so a sulfide–alkene is oxidised at S first.

Reactant:

Reagent: 1 equiv NaIO4, H2O/MeOH, 0 °C

Product:

Sulfide to sulfoxide.

Reactant:

Reagent: 2.2 equiv mCPBA, CH2Cl2

Product:

Sulfide to sulfone.

Thiols are oxidised by mild reagents (I2, air with base) to disulfides, 2 RSH → RS–SR, a one-electron-per-thiol process; strong oxidants (HNO3) take thiols to sulfonic acids RSO3H. Tertiary amines react with H2O2 or peracids to give amine N-oxides R3N+–O−; pyridine gives pyridine N-oxide. When an N-oxide that has a β-hydrogen is heated, it undergoes the Cope elimination: an intramolecular syn elimination through a five-membered cyclic transition state gives an alkene and a N,N-dialkylhydroxylamine, with no external base needed.

Reactant:

Reagent: mCPBA or H2O2/AcOH

Product:

Pyridine N-oxide.

Reactant:

Reagent: I2, NaOH (or air, base)

Product:

Thiol to disulfide.

Aniline is converted by acidic dichromate or MnO2/H2SO4 to p-benzoquinone, and peracids take aromatic amines through hydroxylamines and nitroso compounds to nitro compounds. Such oxidations of the NH2 group are not clean, which is why aniline is acylated before reactions in which an oxidant is present. Triphenylphosphine is oxidised to Ph3P=O; the strong P=O bond is the driving force in the reductive work-up of ozonides and in the Wittig reaction.

Key idea. Sulfide → sulfoxide → sulfone takes 1 then 2 equivalents of peracid in total; amine → N-oxide takes 1 equivalent.
Trap. Do not assume that excess peracid stops at the sulfoxide: for full oxidation the number of equivalents is the sum for every oxidisable site (S, N, C=C).
