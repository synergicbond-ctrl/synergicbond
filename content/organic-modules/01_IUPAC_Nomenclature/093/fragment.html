11E · Important-point collection

  • §1.1 Every IUPAC name answers four questions, in the order in which the name is built: (1) which group is senior, and so becomes the suffix? (2) which chain or ring carries it (the parent)? (3) how is the parent numbered (the locants)? (4) which groups hang off it (the prefixes, in alphabetical order)?
  • §2.1 The chain is chosen before the numbering, and the numbering is chosen before the alphabetical order. Alphabetical order decides the numbering only when every earlier criterion is tied (§2.4).
  • §3.1 For a hydrocarbon the order of numbering decisions is: (1) all multiple bonds together as a set; (2) if still tied, the double bond; (3) all substituent prefixes as a set; (4) the first prefix in alphabetical order.
  • §4.1 The order is acid > anhydride > ester > acid halide > amide > nitrile > aldehyde > ketone > alcohol > thiol > amine > imine > C=C > C≡C, with ethers, halides, nitro compounds and alkyl groups as prefixes only. Carboxylate salts rank just above anhydrides (§9.1).
  • §6.2 To name a bicyclic skeleton from the structure: (1) mark the two bridgeheads (atoms with three ring bonds); (2) count the carbons in each of the three bridges; (3) write them in decreasing order in brackets; (4) add all carbons: the alkane name. Bicyclo[2.2.1]: bridges 2, 2, 1; atoms 2 + 2 + 1 + 2 = 7 → heptane.
  • §7.2 Seniority still applies. Phenol and aniline are parents only when OH or NH₂ is the principal group. If a higher group (COOH, CHO, CN, CONH₂, SO₃H) is present, the compound is named as a benzoic acid, benzaldehyde and so on, and the OH or NH₂ becomes a prefix: 2-hydroxybenzoic acid, 4-aminobenzaldehyde.
  • §8.1 Three different kinds of descriptor exist: E/Z for double bonds (based on priorities), cis/trans for ring substituents or for a clearly defined same-side relationship (based on geometry), and R/S for stereocentres (priorities and handedness). The signs (+) and (−) record the measured direction of rotation of plane-polarised light and have no fixed relation to R and S.
