32. Diazotisation and the Sandmeyer, Gattermann, Balz–Schiemann and related replacements

Diazotisation: ArNH2 + NaNO2 + 2 HX, 273–278 K (0–5 °C) → ArN2+X−. Above 10 °C the salt loses N2 and gives the phenol. Aliphatic primary amines give N2 + a mixture (carbocation products) and do not form stable diazonium salts. Arenediazonium salts are stabilised by resonance of the ring with the N≡N+ group.

Key idea Why use the diazonium route? It is the only way to place Cl, Br, CN, I, F and H at positions that direct substitution cannot reach (for example 1,3,5-tribromobenzene: brominate aniline fully to 2,4,6-tribromoaniline, diazotise, treat with H3PO2; m-bromotoluene, m-chlorophenol and similar). Cyanation by Sandmeyer then hydrolysis is a one-carbon chain extension ring → –COOH.
Example 38 Main

1,3,5-Tribromobenzene

Prepare 1,3,5-tribromobenzene from aniline in three steps.

Show solution
  1. (i) Aniline + Br2/H2O (room temperature) → 2,4,6-tribromoaniline (white precipitate; the strongly activating –NH2 cannot be stopped at mono-bromination).
  2. (ii) NaNO2/HCl, 273–278 K → 2,4,6-tribromobenzenediazonium chloride.
  3. (iii) H3PO2 (or CH3CH2OH, Δ) → 1,3,5-tribromobenzene (N2 lost, H introduced).
Example 39 Adv

Is the Sandmeyer route needed?

Give the shortest route from benzene to m-bromonitrobenzene, and say why a Sandmeyer route would be longer.

Show solution
  1. Benzene → HNO3/H2SO4 → nitrobenzene → Br2/FeBr3 (–NO2 is a meta director) → m-bromonitrobenzene: two steps, no Sandmeyer needed.
  2. A Sandmeyer route (nitrobenzene → m-dinitrobenzene → partial reduction with (NH4)2S or NH4HS → m-nitroaniline → NaNO2/HCl → CuBr) takes five steps. It is used only when the directing effects of the groups already present cannot place the substituent where it is wanted.
