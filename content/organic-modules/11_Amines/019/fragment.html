19. Aniline: structure, preparation, and what makes it special

Aniline, C6H5NH2 (M = 93), is the parent aromatic amine: a colourless oily liquid (b.p. 184 °C, density ≈ 1.02 g mL-1) that darkens on standing through air oxidation, is toxic and absorbed through skin, and is only sparingly soluble in water. It is manufactured by catalytic hydrogenation of nitrobenzene and in the laboratory by Sn/HCl (or Fe/HCl) reduction followed by NaOH; it is also obtained from benzamide by Hofmann bromamide. It is purified by steam distillation (it is volatile with steam and immiscible with water), or via its hydrochloride. The –NH2 group is a strongly activating, ortho/para-directing group because the N lone pair donates into the ring (+M). That effect makes aniline far more reactive than benzene toward electrophiles, but it also causes three recurring difficulties: over-substitution, protonation in acidic media (anilinium is deactivating and meta-directing) and oxidation. Every named solution in this part is a way around one of these.
