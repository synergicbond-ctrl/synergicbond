38. Gabriel phthalimide synthesis (primary amines only)

Reagents: phthalimide + KOH → potassium phthalimide (the N–H is acidic, pKa ≈ 8.3, because the anion is stabilised by two C=O groups) → + R–X (SN2, primary halide, DMF) → N-alkylphthalimide → NaOH/H2O (or hydrazine, N2H4) → primary amine R–NH2 + phthalate (or phthalhydrazide). What it achieves: a pure 1° amine without 2°/3° contamination (the N-alkylphthalimide cannot alkylate again). Compare: ammonolysis of RX gives a mixture of 1°, 2°, 3° amines and quaternary salt.

Trap Fails for aryl amines (aryl halides give no SN2), for secondary and tertiary alkyl halides (E2 elimination dominates), and cannot give 2°/3° amines. So Gabriel makes CH3CH2NH2 or benzylamine but not aniline, not isopropylamine in good yield, not tert-butylamine.

Can Gabriel make it?

Which of these amines can be made by the Gabriel synthesis: (a) benzylamine, (b) aniline, (c) tert-butylamine, (d) N-methylethanamine, (e) butan-1-amine?

Show solution
  1. (a) Yes (benzylic halide, SN2). (b) No (aryl halide). (c) No (tertiary halide eliminates). (d) No (a secondary amine). (e) Yes. So (a) and (e).
