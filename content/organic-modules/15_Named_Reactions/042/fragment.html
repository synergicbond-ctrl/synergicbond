37. Hell–Volhard–Zelinsky (HVZ) α-halogenation of acids

Reagents: RCH2COOH + Br2 + catalytic red P (or PBr3), then H2O. Product: α-bromo acid RCHBrCOOH. Mechanism: P + Br2 → PBr3; PBr3 converts the acid to the acyl bromide RCH2COBr; an acyl bromide enolises far more easily than the acid; the enol is brominated at the α-carbon (Br2); the α-bromo acyl bromide exchanges with more RCH2COOH (or is hydrolysed) to give the α-bromo acid.

Br₂, red P; then H₂O⟶
Propanoic acid → 2-bromopropanoic acid
excess NH₃⟶
α-Bromo acid + NH3 → α-amino acid (racemic alanine): the standard laboratory route to α-amino acids
alc. KOH⟶
Dehydrohalogenation gives propenoic (acrylic) acid; with α-bromo acids containing β-H, the α,β-unsaturated acid forms
Key idea HVZ needs at least one α-H; formic acid, benzoic acid and 2,2-dimethylpropanoic acid do not react. Only the α position brominates (not β). Excess Br2 gives the α,α-dibromo acid. Acids with two α-H give mono and then di-bromination.

Product and further steps

Butanoic acid is treated with Br2/P, then with (i) excess NH3, (ii) alcoholic KOH. Name the products.

Show solution
  1. 2-Bromobutanoic acid first. (i) NH3 (SN2 on the α-carbon): 2-aminobutanoic acid. (ii) Alcoholic KOH (E2): but-2-enoic acid (crotonic acid), CH3CH=CHCOOH.
