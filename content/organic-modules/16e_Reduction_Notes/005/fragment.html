6. Hydrogenation of arenes and of other functional groups

Benzene rings need forcing conditions: H2 at high pressure (about 50–100 atm) over Pt, Rh or Ru, or Raney Ni at 150–200 °C. Mild conditions (Pd/C, 1 atm, room temperature) therefore reduce C=C and C≡C bonds and leave the aromatic ring intact, so styrene gives ethylbenzene and only forcing conditions give ethylcyclohexane. Naphthalene is reduced in stages, first to tetralin and then to decalin (five moles of H2 in all).

Over Pd/C at low pressure, alkynes, alkenes and nitro groups are reduced readily; aldehydes, ketones and nitriles need more vigorous conditions; the benzene ring, esters, acids and amides are not reduced without extreme conditions (copper chromite, high temperature and pressure). Acyl chlorides are reduced only to the aldehyde when the catalyst is poisoned (Rosenmund).

Palladium also causes hydrogenolysis of benzylic and allylic C–X, C–O and C–N bonds. Benzyl ethers, benzyl esters and benzylamines are cleaved to toluene plus the free alcohol, acid or amine, which makes the benzyl group a convenient protecting group. Aryl ketones may be reduced all the way to CH2 over Pd/C in acid.

Reactant:

Reagent: H2, Pd/C

Product:

Hydrogenolysis of the benzylic C–O bond.

Trap. A benzyl ether, benzyl ester or benzylic halide does not survive H2/Pd–C. If a C=C must be reduced while keeping such a group, use a reagent without hydrogenolysis activity such as diimide (Section 11).
