12. Conformations of cycloalkanes

12.1 Cyclobutane and cyclopentane

Cyclobutane is puckered (butterfly, fold angle about 25–35°): puckering reduces eclipsing of the C–H bonds at a small cost in angle strain (C–C–C ≈ 88°). Cyclopentane is an envelope (one carbon out of the plane of the four others, the flap moving around the ring: pseudorotation); a half-chair form is also accessible; torsional strain about 26 kJ mol-1.

12.2 Cyclohexane: chair, half-chair, twist-boat and boat

The chair has all C–C–C angles at about 111° (nearly tetrahedral), all C–H bonds staggered (dihedral 60°), so it has no angle and no torsional strain. It is the global minimum. Its D3d symmetry shows two types of C–H bonds:

  • Axial (a): six bonds parallel to the C3 axis (three up, three down, alternating around the ring).
  • Equatorial (e): six bonds pointing outward roughly in the plane of the ring, each parallel to two ring bonds.

Key idea Drawing rule: on carbon 1 (axial up), carbon 2 has axial down, carbon 3 axial up … The equatorial bond on a carbon is opposite in sense to the axial bond on the same carbon (if axial is up, equatorial points slightly down) and is parallel to the ring bonds once removed.

At room temperature the ring flip (chair ⇌ chair) occurs about 105 times per second, and axial and equatorial positions interchange. Cyclohexane at 25 °C: > 99.9 % chair.

Trap In the boat conformation the two "flagpole" hydrogens clash (steric), and the four other C–C bonds are eclipsed (torsional): both strains are present; the twist-boat is 7 kJ more stable than the boat because it relieves both partly.

12.3 Monosubstituted cyclohexane: axial vs equatorial

An axial substituent is gauche to two ring carbons (C-3 and C-5, in a 1,3-diaxial relation with the axial H's on those carbons). Each such 1,3-diaxial interaction costs about 3.8 kJ (0.9 kcal) for CH3: two interactions ≈ 7.6 kJ (1.8 kcal). This is the A-value (axial → equatorial free-energy preference).

Example 17 Advanced

Axial/equatorial ratio

Calculate the percentage of equatorial methylcyclohexane at 298 K given ΔG° = 7.3 kJ mol-1. What does a tert-butyl group (ΔG ≈ 20 kJ) do?

Show solution
  1. K = exp(7300/(8.314 × 298)) = exp(2.946) = 19.0. Equatorial fraction = 19/20 = 95 %.
  2. tert-Butyl: K = exp(20000/2478) = exp(8.07) = 3.2 × 103; equatorial fraction = 99.97 %. The ring is locked: tert-butyl is used as a conformational anchor to study the other substituents.

12.4 Disubstituted cyclohexanes: the cis/trans rules

In a chair, two substituents on adjacent carbons (1,2): trans = e,e or a,a; cis = a,e. On 1,3: cis = e,e or a,a; trans = a,e. On 1,4: trans = e,e or a,a; cis = a,e.

Example 18 Advanced

cis-1-tert-Butyl-4-methylcyclohexane

Draw the preferred chair of cis-1-tert-butyl-4-methylcyclohexane and of the trans isomer. Estimate the energy difference using A(t-Bu) ≈ 20, A(CH3) ≈ 7.6 kJ.

cis: t-Bu equatorial, CH₃ axial (cis-1,4 is a,e)
trans: both equatorial (trans-1,4 is e,e)
Show solution
  1. cis-1,4: one group must be axial. The ring flips so that t-Bu is equatorial and CH3 axial: energy = A(CH3) = 7.6 kJ mol-1. The alternative (t-Bu axial) costs 20 kJ and is negligible.
  2. trans-1,4: both equatorial: energy 0.
  3. The trans isomer is more stable by ≈ 7.6 kJ mol-1 (the cis isomer exists almost entirely as the t-Bu-equatorial chair).
Example 19 Advanced

cis-1,2: which group goes axial?

In cis-1-chloro-2-methylcyclohexane, which chair is preferred and what is the approximate ΔG between the two chairs (A-values: CH3 7.3 kJ, Cl 2.1 kJ; ignore gauche terms)?

Show solution
  1. cis-1,2 is always a,e. Chair A: Cl axial, CH3 equatorial: strain ≈ 2.1 kJ. Chair B: CH3 axial, Cl equatorial: ≈ 7.3 kJ.
  2. The preferred chair has the larger group (CH3) equatorial and Cl axial; ΔG ≈ 7.3 − 2.1 = 5.2 kJ mol-1.

12.5 Why trans-1,2-diequatorial is sometimes beaten: polar groups

With large polar groups such as Br or Cl in a 1,2-trans relationship the diaxial conformer competes: in the ee form the two C–X dipoles are gauche and repel, and the groups are close. trans-1,2-Dibromocyclohexane in a non-polar solvent has a substantial diaxial population. Steric-only counting (A-values) predicts ee; real answers depend on the groups and the solvent, so for JEE use the steric rule unless the question gives a hint (H-bonding or dipoles).
