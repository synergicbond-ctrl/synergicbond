34. Dow process and cumene process (phenol manufacture)

Dow process: chlorobenzene + NaOH (aq.), 623 K, 300 atm → sodium phenoxide → H+ → phenol. Mechanism: elimination–addition through benzyne (a 14C-labelled chloro compound gives a 50 : 50 mixture of labelled positions: cine substitution); the *activated* aryl halide (o/p-NO2) reacts by the addition–elimination SNAr path under mild conditions instead. Cumene process (industrial route to phenol and acetone): benzene + propene (H3PO4 catalyst) → cumene (isopropylbenzene) → autoxidation with O2 → cumene hydroperoxide → dilute H2SO4 → phenol + acetone (the Hock rearrangement of Part E).

Trap Aryl halides do not undergo SN1 or SN2. They need forcing conditions (Dow, benzyne with NaNH2/liquid NH3) or o/p-activation by –NO2 (SNAr). 2,4-Dinitrochlorobenzene reacts with aqueous Na2CO3 at 360 K; 2,4,6-trinitrochlorobenzene (picryl chloride) with water at room temperature.
