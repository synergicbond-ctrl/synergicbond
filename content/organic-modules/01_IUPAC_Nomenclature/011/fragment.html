Degree of unsaturation (DoU). For a compound CcHhNnXxOo (X = halogen):

Formula DoU = (2c + 2 + n – h – x) / 2
Oxygen and sulphur do not enter the formula. A ring or a double bond counts 1 and a triple bond 2. DoU = 0 means a saturated acyclic compound.
Validity Check The formula assumes neutral molecules with normal valences (C 4, N 3, O 2, X 1). It does not distinguish rings from double bonds, so DoU = 1 fits an alkene or a cycloalkane equally; it says how many, not which.
Example 1 Direct application

Reading a skeletal formula

How many carbon and hydrogen atoms are in the skeletal structure of 2,3-dimethylbutan-2-ol?

Show solution
  1. Carbons: 4 in the longest chain (butane) and 2 methyl branches make 6.
  2. The skeleton has no ring and no multiple bond, so it is saturated. A saturated acyclic alcohol is CnH2n+2O, so for n = 6 the hydrogens number 14 (13 on carbon and 1 on oxygen).
  3. Check by DoU: (2·6 + 2 – 14)/2 = 0.

Example 2 Standard

Unsaturation from a formula

How many degrees of unsaturation does C8H9ClO have?

Show solution
  1. The halogen counts like a hydrogen and oxygen is ignored: DoU = (2·8 + 2 – 9 – 1)/2 = (18 – 10)/2 = 4.
  2. Four is exactly the value for a benzene ring (three C=C and one ring), so a substituted benzene is the natural candidate.

Concept Check

Is a compound of formula C₅H₈ necessarily an alkyne?

Show answer

No. DoU = (12 – 8)/2 = 2, which fits an alkyne, a diene, a cycloalkene or a bicyclic compound. The formula gives the number of units of unsaturation, not their kind.

Concept Check

Which is the attachment carbon in isobutyl, and what is its class?

Show answer

The CH₂ carbon of (CH₃)₂CH–CH₂–; it is primary.
