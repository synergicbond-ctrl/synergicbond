25. The aldol reaction

25.1 Aldol addition: mechanism With dilute NaOH (about 10 %) at 5–25 °C, two molecules of ethanal combine to give 3-hydroxybutanal (aldol = aldehyde + alcohol). The enolate of one molecule adds to the carbonyl of another.

  • Step 1: OH− removes an α-H of CH3CHO: CH2=CH–O− (enolate; formed in small equilibrium amount).
  • Step 2: the enolate carbon attacks the C=O carbon of a second ethanal: alkoxide CH3CH(O−)CH2CHO (a C–C bond forms).
  • Step 3: the alkoxide takes H+ from water: CH3CH(OH)CH2CHO and regenerates OH− (catalytic).
dil. NaOH, 283 K⟶ + CH₃CHO (C–C bond formation)⟶ H₂O⟶
Aldol addition of ethanal: 3-hydroxybutanal (aldol)
Key idea The aldol product has a carbonyl group and a hydroxyl group on the β-carbon (β-hydroxy aldehyde or ketone). Formation of a new C–C bond between the α-carbon of one molecule and the carbonyl carbon of another is the defining feature.
25.2 Dehydration: aldol condensation
Heating (or using stronger base or acid) causes loss of H2O from the β-hydroxy carbonyl compound by E1cB: the α-H is removed (it is acidic), giving an enolate that expels OH− from the β-carbon. The product is an α,β-unsaturated carbonyl compound, stabilised by conjugation; this drives the reaction to completion.
Δ, −H₂O (E1cB)⟶
3-Hydroxybutanal → but-2-enal (crotonaldehyde), trans
+ (i) dil. NaOH, 283 K (ii) Δ⟶

Trap "Aldol addition" keeps the OH (product is β-hydroxy carbonyl); "aldol condensation" loses water (product is α,β-unsaturated carbonyl). Ask which one the question wants. Dehydration is accelerated by heat and acid, and is spontaneous when it creates extended conjugation (for example with an aryl group at the β-carbon).
25.3 Aldol of propanone and the equilibrium problem
For ketones the aldol equilibrium is unfavourable: at equilibrium (25 °C) only about 5 % of the acetone is converted into 4-hydroxy-4-methylpentan-2-one (diacetone alcohol), because the tetrahedral addition product of a ketone is more crowded. To get a good yield, the product is removed from the base as it forms (Soxhlet extraction over Ba(OH)2). Dehydration of diacetone alcohol (traces of I2 or acid, heat) gives mesityl oxide, and further reaction with acetone gives phorone (2,6-dimethylhepta-2,5-dien-4-one) and, by cyclisation, isophorone.

25.4 Acid-catalysed aldol In dilute acid the enol (not the enolate) attacks the protonated carbonyl. Aldol condensation by acid usually gives the unsaturated product directly (dehydration is acid-promoted). Example: 3 acetone → mesitylene (1,3,5-trimethylbenzene) with conc. H2SO4: triple aldol condensation then cyclisation.

+ + conc. H₂SO₄, −3 H₂O⟶
Acetone → mesitylene (benzene synthesis by condensation, historical)

25.5 Cross aldol reactions When two different carbonyl compounds, each with α-H, are mixed in base, four products are formed (two self-aldols and two crossed aldols). The cross aldol is useful only if one of the following controls the reaction:

  • One partner has no α-hydrogen (benzaldehyde, HCHO, 2,2-dimethylpropanal, furfural). It can only be the electrophile. Then the enolisable partner is added slowly to a mixture of the other partner and base, so that the enolate meets the non-enolisable aldehyde (Claisen–Schmidt condensation).
  • Preformed enolate (directed aldol): LDA, −78 °C converts ketone A completely to its enolate; adding aldehyde B gives the cross-aldol product only.
  • Reactivity difference: aldehydes are more electrophilic than ketones; HCHO is the most electrophilic. A ketone enolate (from acetone) adds to an aldehyde rather than to another acetone: acetone + PhCHO is clean.
  • Silyl enol ether + Lewis acid (Mukaiyama aldol): TiCl4 activates the aldehyde; the silyl enol ether (an enolate equivalent) attacks.

Shortcut Count of products for a mixed aldol: with carbonyl A (has α-H) and B (has α-H): 2 self + 2 cross = 4 aldol addition products (ignoring stereochemistry); if B has no α-H: 1 self (of A) + 1 cross (A's enolate + B) = 2, and the cross is the major one when B is in excess and A is added slowly. If neither has α-H: no aldol.
25.6 Aldol with formaldehyde: methylol compounds and pentaerythritol
HCHO has no α-H but is highly electrophilic. An enolisable aldehyde with three α-H reacts with HCHO three times (each replacing one H by CH2OH); then a fourth HCHO acts as hydride donor (crossed Cannizzaro) to reduce the CHO.

Ethanal + 4 HCHO → pentaerythritol C(CH₂OH)₄ plus formate

Three-, four- and seven-membered rings form much less readily (strain or poor probability).

Pentane-2,4-dione does not undergo intramolecular aldol: the only possible rings would be three- or four-membered.

Key idea Method for intramolecular aldol: (1) number the chain; (2) list each α-carbon that has H; (3) count the ring that would form if that α-carbon attacks each other C=O; (4) accept only 5- and 6-membered rings; (5) dehydrate if possible (the new ring usually ends up with a conjugated C=C–C=O); (6) if two answers remain, the more stable (or kinetically preferred) one is the one with the smaller ring if conjugation is the same, or the more substituted alkene.
Example 27 Main

Aldol products

Give the products of aldol addition and aldol condensation of (a) propanal, (b) butanone (only the product from the CH2 side), (c) benzaldehyde + ethanal.

Show solution
  1. (a) Addition: 3-hydroxy-2-methylpentanal CH3CH2CH(OH)CH(CH3)CHO. Condensation: 2-methylpent-2-enal.
  2. (b) The enolate from the CH2 side (more substituted) attacks the C=O of another butanone: CH3CH2C(CH3)(OH)–CH(CH3)–COCH3, 4-hydroxy-3,4-dimethylhexan-2-one. Dehydration (the only α-H left is on C-3) gives 3,4-dimethylhex-3-en-2-one.
  3. (c) 3-phenylprop-2-enal (cinnamaldehyde): PhCHO is the non-enolisable electrophile; ethanal is the enolate source.

Example 30 Adv

Stereochemistry of the aldol

How many stereoisomers does 3-hydroxy-2-methylpentanal have, and how many racemic diastereomers (pairs) are formed in the aldol addition of propanal?

Show solution
  1. The product has two stereocentres (C-2 and C-3, each with four different groups): 22 = 4 stereoisomers = 2 pairs of enantiomers = 2 racemic diastereomers (syn and anti, also called erythro and threo). With no chiral influence each pair forms as a racemate.
