6.2 The Lucas test (ZnCl2/conc. HCl)

Lucas reagent is anhydrous ZnCl2 in concentrated HCl (about 1:1 mol/mol or 136 g ZnCl2 in 105 g conc. HCl). ZnCl2 is a Lewis acid: it coordinates to the alcohol oxygen, by coordinating to the oxygen, which turns –OH into a far better leaving group and speeds up SN1 ionisation. The alkyl chloride formed is insoluble in the aqueous medium, so the clear solution turns turbid / cloudy, and finally separates as a second layer.

Alcohol typeObservation at room temperatureMechanism
Benzylic, allylic, 3°cloudy immediately (within seconds)SN1 via stable cation
2°cloudy after about 5 minutes (heating needed to speed)SN1 via 2° cation
1° (and CH3OH)no turbidity at room temperature; may react on heatingSN2 (slow)

Limits of the test: (i) it works only for monofunctional alcohols soluble in the reagent (up to about six carbons); (ii) allylic/benzylic 1° alcohols behave like 3°; (iii) it gives false results for alcohols which rearrange; (iv) the test uses the speed of formation of the insoluble alkyl chloride, it is not a chemical colour test.

Example 14 L1 Foundation

Lucas reagent order

Arrange in order of decreasing speed of reaction with Lucas reagent: butan-1-ol, butan-2-ol, 2-methylpropan-2-ol, benzyl alcohol, propan-1-ol.

Show solution
  1. The rate-determining step is formation of the carbocation. Benzyl cation (resonance) and tert-butyl cation (3°, three hyperconjugating methyls) form fastest. Benzyl alcohol is a primary alcohol but reacts instantaneously, like a tertiary.
  2. Between benzyl and tert-butyl, resonance stabilisation by the ring versus hyperconjugation: both are “immediate”; experimentally both give instant turbidity and are usually listed as 2-methylpropan-2-ol ≈ benzyl alcohol.
  3. Then secondary (butan-2-ol) after about 5 minutes; the two primary alcohols show nothing at room temperature, with propan-1-ol practically the same as butan-1-ol.
  4. Order: 2-methylpropan-2-ol ≈ benzyl alcohol > butan-2-ol > butan-1-ol ≈ propan-1-ol.
Example 15 L2 Main

Lucas test differentiation

Which one gives turbidity immediately with Lucas reagent: (i) CH3CH2CH(OH)CH3; (ii) CH2=CHCH2OH; (iii) (CH3)2C(OH)CH2CH3; (iv) C6H5CH2CH2OH? Give reasons.

Show solution
  1. Immediate turbidity needs a stable carbocation: allyl cation (ii) is resonance stabilised; (iii) gives the 3° cation.
  2. (i) is secondary (about 5 min); (iv) is 2-phenylethanol, a primary alcohol with the ring separated from the carbinol carbon by CH2 (the phenonium ion forms only slowly): no immediate turbidity.
  3. Answer: (ii) and (iii).
