13. Stoichiometry and equivalent counting in redox problems

Redox stoichiometry reduces to one equation: moles of electrons lost by the substrate = moles of electrons gained by the oxidant. For a substrate that loses x electrons per molecule and an oxidant that gains n electrons per formula unit, the mole ratio substrate : oxidant = n : x. In a titration, equivalents (n-factor times moles) of oxidant at the end point equal the equivalents of reductant; for solutions, N = M × n-factor and N1V1 = N2V2.

  • n-factors of oxidants: MnO4− in acid 5; in neutral or weakly basic medium 3; in strongly basic medium 1. Cr2O72− 6; CrO3 3. H2O2 (as oxidant), peracid, HIO4, Pb(OAc)4, I2 (→ 2I−) 2 each.
  • n-factors of substrates: primary alcohol → aldehyde 2, → acid 4; secondary alcohol → ketone 2; aldehyde → acid 2; alkene → diol 2; C=C → epoxide 2; sulfide → sulfoxide 2; Fe2+ → Fe3+ 1; C2O42− → 2CO2 2; CH3 of toluene → COOH 6.
  1. Worked example 1: how many moles of KMnO4 in acid oxidise 5 mol of ethanol to ethanoic acid? Electrons lost: 5 × 4 = 20 mol. Each MnO4− accepts 5 e−, so 20 ÷ 5 = 4 mol.
  2. Worked example 2: 25.0 mL of an Fe2+ solution needs 20.0 mL of 0.0200 M KMnO4 in acid. Moles MnO4− = 0.400 mmol, which accept 2.00 mmol of electrons, so Fe2+ = 2.00 mmol and its concentration is 2.00 ÷ 25.0 = 0.0800 M.
  3. Worked example 3: acetone and iodoform: CH3COCH3 + 3I2 + 4NaOH → CHI3 + CH3COONa + 3NaI + 3H2O, so 3 mol of I2 (equivalently 3 mol of NaOI) are consumed per mole of acetone.
Formula. moles of oxidant = (moles of substrate × electrons lost per molecule) ÷ (electrons gained per oxidant unit)

Two further cases require care. First, the oxidant n-factor depends on the medium, so the medium must be read from the question. Second, when several functional groups react, add the electron counts of every group that is oxidised. For example, glycerol cleaved by HIO4 loses 2 e− per C–C cleavage, so 2 mol of HIO4 are used; if the substrate also carried another group that is oxidised by the same reagent, its electrons would be added.

Trap. A back-titration, where excess oxidant is determined with iodide/thiosulfate, requires subtracting the excess from the total added before applying the substrate ratio.
