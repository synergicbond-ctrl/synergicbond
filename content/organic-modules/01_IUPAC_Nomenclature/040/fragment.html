5.1 The master procedure

Definition. The master procedure is the sequence of seven decisions that produces the name of any compound with more than one functional group. The steps have a fixed order; most incorrect names result from skipping a step or reordering two of them.

  • 1. Mark every functional group and every multiple bond in the structure.
  • 2. Rank them with the seniority table (Section 4.1). The highest-ranking group is the principal group: it gives the suffix. The others become prefixes (hydroxy, oxo, amino, cyano, carboxy …) except C=C and C≡C, which are expressed by endings.
  • 3. Select the parent chain or ring by these criteria, in order — each applied only if the previous gave a tie:

(a) the maximum number of principal groups (even if the resulting chain is shorter); (b) the maximum number of multiple bonds (C=C and C≡C counted together, 1979/1993 convention); (c) the longest chain; (d) the maximum number of C=C; (e) the maximum number of substituents.

  • 4. Number the chain so that, in order: (a) the principal group(s) get the lowest locants; (b) multiple bonds get the lowest locants (as one set; on a tie the double bond is lower); (c) all prefixes together get the lowest set; (d) the prefix cited first alphabetically gets the lowest locant.
  • 5. Name the prefixes: choose the correct prefix form of each lower group (Section 5.3).
  • 6. Arrange the prefixes alphabetically and assemble: prefixes + root + ene/yne + suffix.
  • 7. Add stereodescriptors at the very front if the structure specifies them (Part 8).
Why It Happens Criterion 3(a) comes first because the suffix is the main message of the name: a chain that carries more principal groups expresses more of that message in the suffix. Length and branching only decide among chains that carry the same number of principal groups.
Compare & Distinguish The 2013 recommendations keep 3(a) but then apply chain length before the number of multiple bonds. The 1979/1993 convention used in school problems applies the multiple bonds first. For compounds with no multiple bonds the two coincide.
Exam Trap Steps 3 and 4 are different decisions. The parent chain is chosen by criteria 3(a)–(e) before any numbering; only then is the chain numbered by step 4. The usual error is to choose the chain by asking which numbering looks lowest.
