44. Fifty common traps and exceptions

1. Aldehydes and ketones do not hydrogen-bond to themselves; they accept H-bonds from water. 2. Bisulphite adducts: aldehydes and methyl ketones; not aryl ketones (acetophenone), not diaryl, not di-isopropyl. 3. Reactivity: HCHO > CH3CHO > RCHO > PhCHO > acetone > acetophenone > benzophenone. 4. Cyanohydrin pH: slight base best; strong acid kills CN−. 5. Hydrate stability only for HCHO, chloral, ninhydrin, hexafluoroacetone, glyoxal. 6. Hemiacetals are unstable open-chain but stable in cyclic sugars. 7. Acetals are stable to base, labile to dilute acid. 8. Grignard + C=O compound that contains OH, COOH, NH, ≡C–H: reagent is destroyed. 9. Ester + 2 RMgX gives a tertiary alcohol with two identical R groups; the ketone cannot be isolated. 10. RCN + RMgX stops at the ketone; RCOCl + RMgX does not (over-addition) but R2CuLi does. 11. Semicarbazide reacts through the hydrazine NH2. 12. 2,4-DNP is positive for all aldehydes/ketones, not for esters or acids. 13. Oxime of acetone: one isomer; of butanone: two (E/Z). 14. Beckmann: the group anti to OH migrates. 15. Wittig: unstabilised ylide gives Z; stabilised gives E; ylide made from primary halide. 16. Clemmensen needs acid-stable substrates; Wolff–Kishner base-stable. 17. NaBH4 does not reduce esters, acids, amides, nitriles. 18. LiAlH4 is destroyed by water; do not use in protic solvent. 19. Luche (NaBH4/CeCl3) gives 1,2-reduction of enones. 20. H2/Pd reduces C=C of enones before C=O. 21. PCC stops at the aldehyde; aqueous CrO3 does not. 22. Wacker of propene gives acetone, not propanal. 23. Hg2+/H3O+ hydration: Markovnikov (ketone); R2BH/H2O2/OH−: aldehyde. 24. Rosenmund: not for HCHO. 25. Gattermann–Koch: not for phenols, phenyl ethers, nitro or acyl-deactivated rings. 26. Reimer–Tiemann: mostly ortho (salicylaldehyde); CCl4 gives salicylic acid. 27. Friedel–Crafts acylation: no rearrangement, no polyacylation; fails with strongly deactivated rings and with amines. 28. Tollens': all aldehydes; Fehling's: aliphatic aldehydes only. 29. α-Hydroxyketones (benzoin, fructose) give positive Tollens'. 30. Iodoform: CH3CO–, CH3CH(OH)–; ethanal yes, methanal no; CH3COOH, CH3COOR no. 31. Haloform gives an acid with one carbon less. 32. Acid-catalysed halogenation: mono, more substituted α-carbon; base: poly, methyl first. 33. LDA/−78 °C: kinetic enolate (less substituted); NaH/equilibrating: thermodynamic. 34. Aldol: requires α-H; dehydration by E1cB and acid; ketone aldols are unfavourable at equilibrium. 35. Crossed aldol of two enolisable partners: four products. 36. Claisen–Schmidt: ArCHO + enolisable carbonyl, dilute NaOH; no Cannizzaro in dilute base. 37. Cannizzaro: no α-H, conc. NaOH; 1 : 1 acid : alcohol. 38. Crossed Cannizzaro with HCHO: HCHO becomes formate. 39. Benzoin: ArCHO only, CN− catalyst; aliphatic aldehydes do aldol instead. 40. Perkin: anhydride with ≥ 2 α-H; sodium salt of the same acid. 41. Knoevenagel: weak base; Doebner adds decarboxylation. 42. Michael: 1,4; donors are stabilised carbanions. 43. Robinson = Michael + intramolecular aldol. 44. Baeyer–Villiger: O inserted on the side of the better-migrating group; product ester, phenol precursors. 45. Intramolecular aldol: only 5- and 6-membered rings. 46. Hard Nu: 1,2; soft Nu: 1,4. 47. Pinacol: diol → ketone with C-skeleton rearrangement. 48. Darzens: epoxide-ester; hydrolysis/decarboxylation → aldehyde with one more carbon. 49. Hexamine from HCHO + NH3; aldehyde-ammonia from ethanal + NH3. 50. Bredt-bridgehead α-H cannot enolise; no exchange.
