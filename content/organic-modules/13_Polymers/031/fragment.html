Q195 Advanced

Cross-link counting

Each cross-link in a rubber uses two sulphur atoms. A 1.00 mol sample of isoprene units has cross-links on 4 % of the units (one cross-link per two units involved, i.e. each cross-link uses two units). How many moles of S atoms are used?

Show answer

Units involved in cross-links = 0.04 × 1.00 = 0.04 mol; each cross-link joins 2 units → 0.02 mol cross-links; 2 S each → 0.04 mol S (about 1.3 g, i.e. ≈ 1.8 % by mass of the 68.1 g of rubber: consistent with soft rubber).

Q201 Advanced

Q1 of 3: identify the monomer

A liquid hydrocarbon X (C8H8) decolourises Br2/CCl4, gives benzoic acid on vigorous oxidation (hot KMnO4) and polymerises with benzoyl peroxide. (i) Identify X. (ii) Name the polymer and mechanism. (iii) What structural unit is obtained?

Show answer

(i) X = styrene: C8H8, benzene ring + vinyl group (five degrees of unsaturation: ring 4 + C=C 1); hot KMnO4 cleaves the side chain to benzoic acid. (ii) Polystyrene by free-radical chain-growth (initiator benzoyl peroxide → C6H5COO•). (iii) –[CH2–CH(C6H5)]– , atactic, head-to-tail.

Q202 Advanced

Q2 of 3: from rubber to monomer

An organic polymer R, on ozonolysis (reductive work-up), gives only CH3COCH2CH2CHO. (i) Identify R. (ii) What monomer and what configuration is present in the natural form? (iii) Name the substance obtained by heating R with 3–5 % sulphur.

Show answer

(i) R = polyisoprene, head-to-tail 1,4-linked (each unit gives one 4-oxopentanal). (ii) Isoprene, cis-1,4 in natural rubber (trans in gutta-percha). (iii) Vulcanised rubber (S–S cross-links).

Q203 Advanced

Show answer

Q204 Advanced

A polymer from three ozonolysis products

A polymer made from a hydrocarbon Y (C4H6) by sodium initiator has the 1,4-cis structure about 40 %, trans 50 % and 1,2 about 10 %. (i) Identify Y. (ii) Which product(s) would ozonolysis (reductive work-up) of the 1,2-units alone give? (iii) Which commercial rubber is made by copolymerising Y with styrene?

Show answer

(i) Y = buta-1,3-diene. (ii) A 1,2-unit –CH2–CH(CH=CH2)– has a pendant vinyl: ozonolysis cleaves it to –CH2–CH(CHO)– (plus formaldehyde from the =CH2); 1,4-units give succinaldehyde OHC–CH2CH2–CHO. (iii) Buna-S (SBR).

Q205 Advanced

Resol or novolac

Phenol (1.0 mol) and formaldehyde (0.8 mol) are heated with oxalic acid; the product is a brittle, fusible resin which melts, dissolves in ethanol and does not set when heated alone. (i) Name the resin. (ii) What must be added to cure it? (iii) Why does the resin not set on its own?

Show answer

(i) Novolac (acid catalyst, HCHO : phenol < 1). (ii) Hexamethylenetetramine (a source of HCHO and NH3) or extra formaldehyde. (iii) It has –CH2– bridges but no –CH2OH groups, so there is no group to condense on heating; the chain ends are free phenol rings.

Q206 Advanced

A mass-spectrum question

A nylon oligomer H–[NH(CH2)6NHCO(CH2)4CO]n–OH shows a molecular ion at m/z 2 281 (average masses). (i) Find n. (ii) How many amide bonds?

Show answer

M = 226.32n + 18.015 = 2281 → n = (2281 − 18.015)/226.32 = 10.00 → n = 10; amide bonds = 2n − 1 = 19.

Q207 Advanced

A living polymerisation

A student wants a polystyrene of Mn = 52 000 with PDI < 1.05. (i) Which mechanism? (ii) How many moles of n-BuLi per 100 g of styrene? (iii) What happens if there is a trace of water?

Show answer

(i) Living anionic (n-BuLi, THF or benzene, dry, inert gas). (ii) Xn = 52 000/104.15 = 499.3; moles styrene = 100/104.15 = 0.9601; n-BuLi = 0.9601/499.3 = 1.92 × 10-3 mol. (iii) Water protonates carbanion ends (R–CH(Ph)- + H2O → R–CH2Ph + OH-), killing chains early; Mn falls and PDI broadens.
