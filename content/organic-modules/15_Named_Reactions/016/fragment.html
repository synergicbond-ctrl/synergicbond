13. Cannizzaro reaction (and crossed Cannizzaro, Tishchenko)

Reagents: an aldehyde without α-hydrogen (HCHO, ArCHO, R3C–CHO, glyoxal, furfural) + concentrated NaOH or KOH. Disproportionation: one molecule is reduced to the alcohol, the other oxidised to the carboxylate (acid after H+). Yield of each ≈ 50 %. Mechanism: (1) OH− adds to the C=O of one molecule giving a tetrahedral anion; its second deprotonation gives a dianion (with strong base); (2) this species transfers a hydride (H−) from the carbon to the carbonyl carbon of a second molecule (rate-determining step; second order in aldehyde, first order in base for the dianionic path); (3) the carboxylic acid so formed is deprotonated by the alkoxide. The hydride comes from C–H and goes to C: the H on the alcohol carbon does not come from the solvent (D2O experiments leave the C–H unlabelled).

Key idea Crossed Cannizzaro rule: in HCHO + ArCHO, HCHO is always the one oxidised (to formate), because it is the most electrophilic aldehyde and so adds OH− fastest, and ArCHO is reduced to ArCH2OH. It is the practical way to convert ArCHO completely into ArCH2OH. Two different aromatic aldehydes give a mixture of four products.
Trap Aldehydes with α-hydrogens (CH3CHO, CH3CH2CHO) give aldol, not Cannizzaro, with NaOH. In the question "which of these undergo Cannizzaro": HCHO, PhCHO, (CH3)3CCHO, furfural, glyoxal, and 2,2-disubstituted aldehydes yes; CH3CHO, (CH3)2CHCHO, PhCH2CHO no.
Example 21 Main

Cannizzaro products

State the products (after acidification) when each is heated with conc. NaOH: (a) p-chlorobenzaldehyde, (b) 2,2-dimethylpropanal, (c) 2-phenylethanal (PhCH2CHO), (d) HCHO + p-nitrobenzaldehyde.

Show solution
  1. (a) p-ClC6H4CH2OH + p-ClC6H4COOH. (b) (CH3)3CCH2OH + (CH3)3CCOOH. (c) No Cannizzaro: PhCH2CHO has α-H and gives aldol-type products. (d) p-O2NC6H4CH2OH + HCOOH (formate oxidised, p-nitrobenzaldehyde reduced).
Example 22 Adv

Number of moles

2.12 g of benzaldehyde (M = 106) is converted by Cannizzaro with 100 % yield of each product. Find the mass of benzyl alcohol and of benzoic acid formed.

Show solution
  1. n(PhCHO) = 2.12/106 = 0.0200 mol. Half is reduced, half oxidised: 0.0100 mol each.
  2. Benzyl alcohol (M = 108): 0.0100 × 108 = 1.08 g. Benzoic acid (M = 122): 0.0100 × 122 = 1.22 g.
