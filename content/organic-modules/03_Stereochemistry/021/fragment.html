18. Cyclohexane: chair, boat, twist-boat

18.1 The chair

The chair has all C–C–C angles close to 111° (almost tetrahedral) and every C–H bond staggered with respect to its neighbours: there is no angle strain and no torsional strain. It is the most stable conformation. The twelve hydrogens are of two kinds:

  • Axial (6): parallel to the ring axis, three pointing up and three pointing down, alternating around the ring.
  • Equatorial (6): pointing outward roughly along the ring "equator", each slightly up or slightly down, the opposite of the axial bond on the same carbon.

Key idea On any one ring carbon, one bond is axial and the other equatorial. If the axial one points up, the equatorial one points slightly down. Adjacent carbons alternate: axial up, axial down, axial up...
Chair flip (ring inversion): the chair interconverts with the other chair through the half-chair and twist-boat (barrier about 45 kJ mol-1, a rate of about 105–106 s-1 at 298 K). The flip turns every axial bond into an equatorial one and vice versa. It does not change cis/trans relationships or R/S descriptors, because no bond is broken: "up" remains "up".

18.2 Other conformations of cyclohexane

ConformationEnergy (kJ/mol, relative to chair)Features
Chair0strain-free
Twist-boat (skew-boat)≈ 23boat twisted to relieve flagpole and eclipsing strain: a true minimum
Boat≈ 29–30a maximum between two twist-boats; flagpole H···H clash and four eclipsed C–C bonds
Half-chair≈ 45transition state on the way from chair to twist-boat

The usual interconversion sequence: chair → half-chair → twist-boat → boat → twist-boat → half-chair → chair. At room temperature more than 99.9 % of cyclohexane is in the chair form.

Trap The boat is not a minimum but a transition state between twist-boats; the twist-boat is a minimum about 7 kJ mol-1 below the boat. The energy order of stability: chair > twist-boat > boat > half-chair.

18.3 Drawing chairs correctly (what to check)

  • The ring bonds come in three parallel pairs (opposite sides parallel).
  • Each axial bond is vertical; axial bonds alternate up/down, starting with the carbon at the "up" tip.
  • An equatorial bond on a carbon is parallel to the ring bonds that are one bond away from that carbon.
  • In a chair, any two groups on adjacent carbons that are trans are either both axial (anti-periplanar, 180°) or both equatorial (gauche, 60°); cis groups on adjacent carbons are axial/equatorial.
