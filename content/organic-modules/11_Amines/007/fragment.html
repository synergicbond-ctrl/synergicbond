7. Reduction methods

7.1 Nitro compounds

Nitro compounds (aliphatic and aromatic) are reduced to primary amines by:

  • Sn/HCl, Fe/HCl (Béchamp reduction; with Fe only a little HCl is needed because the FeCl2 formed is re-used) or Zn/HCl.
  • H2 with Ni, Pd/C or Pt – the cleanest industrial route to aniline.
  • LiAlH4 reduces aliphatic nitro compounds to amines but aromatic nitro compounds to azo compounds, ArN=NAr.
  • Na2S, (NH4)2S or NaHS (Zinin reduction) reduces one nitro group of a dinitroarene selectively.
Sn, conc. HCl; then NaOH⟶
Nitrobenzene to aniline (Sn/HCl gives anilinium chloride; NaOH liberates the free base)

Stoichiometry: C6H5NO2 + 3Sn + 7HCl → C6H5NH3+Cl− + 3SnCl2 + 2H2O. Each NO2 → NH2 change is a 6-electron reduction; Sn → Sn2+ supplies 2 e−, so 3 Sn per nitro group. The medium decides the product from nitrobenzene (a standard JEE Advanced table):

7.2 Nitriles

R–C≡N is reduced to R–CH2NH2 by LiAlH4/ether, H2/Ni, or Na/C2H5OH (Mendius reduction). The amine has one carbon more than the alkyl halide used to make the nitrile.

KCN⟶ LiAlH₄; then H₂O⟶
Ethyl bromide → propanenitrile → propan-1-amine (one-carbon homologation)
Shortcut Milder reagents stop earlier: DIBAL-H, or SnCl2/HCl (Stephen reduction), give the aldehyde via the imine. Only strong reducing conditions give the amine.
7.3 Amides

Primary, secondary and tertiary amides are reduced by LiAlH4 to amines with the same number of carbons: RCONH2 → RCH2NH2; RCONHR' → RCH2NHR'; RCONR'2 → RCH2NR'2. C=O becomes CH2. NaBH4 does not reduce amides.

N-Methylethanamide → N-methylethanamine: C=O → CH₂, carbon number unchanged
7.4 Oximes and azides
  • Oximes: LiAlH4 or H2/Ni gives primary amines, R2C=NOH → R2CHNH2.
  • Alkyl azides RN3 + LiAlH4 (or H2/Pd, or PPh3 then H2O) → RNH2 cleanly with no over-alkylation.
7.5 Reductive amination

Aldehydes and ketones condense with NH3 or an amine to an imine/iminium ion, which is reduced in situ (H2/Ni or NaBH3CN). NaBH3CN reduces the iminium ion much faster than the carbonyl group at pH 6–7.

Acetone with NH₃ gives propan-2-amine (a primary amine)

A primary amine + aldehyde gives a secondary amine

Eschweiler–Clarke methylation: a 1° or 2° amine is exhaustively N-methylated by HCHO + HCOOH (formic acid is the hydride donor, CO2 is lost). It stops at the 3° stage – no quaternary salt – because a 3° amine cannot form an iminium ion.
