2.7 Peptide sequencing

Sequence determination = (i) amino acid composition, (ii) N- and C-terminal residues, (iii) cleavage into overlapping fragments and (iv) ordering of the fragments.

Composition

Hydrolyse with 6 M HCl at 110 °C, 24 h (this destroys Trp, converts Asn → Asp and Gln → Glu; Ser and Thr partly decompose); separate by ion-exchange or HPLC and detect with ninhydrin. The result is a table of the moles of each amino acid per mole of peptide.

The peptide is destroyed

Edman degradation. PhNCS reacts with the N-terminal NH₂; anhydrous acid cleaves only the first peptide bond (as the thiazolinone, which rearranges to the PTH-amino acid, here PTH-glycine); the shortened peptide is intact and can be degraded again. Automated sequencers read ≈ 50 residues

Key idea Trypsin cuts after K and R. A protein with k Lys + Arg residues (none followed by Pro) gives k + 1 fragments. CNBr with m methionines gives m + 1 fragments. Two different cleavage agents produce overlapping fragments; overlaps fix the order (Sanger used this for insulin).
Formula Number of fragments from cleavage at k sites in a linear chain: k + 1. If the chain contains an intra-chain disulfide (cyclic), the number of pieces can be fewer until the disulfide is broken.
<h4>Disulfide bonds</h4>

Cystine (–CH2S–SCH2–) bridges are cleaved by performic acid (oxidation to two cysteic acid residues, –SO3H) or by reduction with 2-mercaptoethanol/dithiothreitol (to two –SH), which are then capped with iodoacetamide or iodoacetate. Insulin (Sanger 1955; Nobel 1958): A chain 21 residues + B chain 30 residues = 51 residues, M ≈ 5808; three disulfides: A7–B7 and A20–B19 (inter-chain) and A6–A11 (intra-chain).

Trap Cleavage specificity is often tested with a "does not cut before Pro" detail and with "trypsin cuts after the basic residue, i.e. on the carbonyl side". Remember also that Trp is destroyed by acid hydrolysis, so it is missing from a 6 M HCl composition analysis.
Example 51 Adv

Sequence from enzymatic fragments

A hexapeptide contains Ala, Arg, Gly, Leu, Lys, Phe (one each). DNP-Gly is the Sanger product. Trypsin gives three fragments: Gly–Arg, Leu–Lys and a dipeptide containing Ala and Phe (order unknown). Chymotrypsin gives Gly–Arg–Leu–Lys–Phe and Ala. Find the sequence.

Show solution
  1. N-terminal = Gly. Chymotrypsin cuts after Phe (C-side): so Phe is the C-terminal of the five-residue fragment, leaving Ala as the C-terminal residue of the hexapeptide: ... –Phe–Ala. (Chymotrypsin fragment 'Ala' alone means Ala follows Phe at the C-end.)
  2. Trypsin cuts after Arg and Lys: fragments end in Arg, Lys, and the C-terminal one: (Gly, Arg) = Gly–Arg (Gly is N-terminal); (Leu, Lys) = Leu–Lys; (Ala, Phe) = Phe–Ala (since Phe–Ala is the C-end).
  3. Order of fragments: Gly–Arg first; Phe–Ala last; Leu–Lys in the middle. Gly–Arg–Leu–Lys–Phe–Ala. Check with chymotrypsin: Gly–Arg–Leu–Lys–Phe | Ala ✓ (cuts after Phe).
Example 52 Adv

CNBr fragments

A decapeptide contains Met at positions 4 and 8 only. CNBr treatment gives how many fragments, and what is the C-terminal group of the fragment that precedes each cut?

Show solution
  1. Two cuts → 3 fragments (residues 1–4, 5–8, 9–10). The fragments ending at Met-4 and Met-8 have a homoserine lactone at the C-terminus (the Met side chain's S–CH3 is lost as CH3SCN).
Example 53 Main

Reagent identification

Name the method: (a) uses FDNB, (b) uses PhN=C=S and can be repeated, (c) uses carboxypeptidase.

Show solution
  1. (a) Sanger N-terminal analysis; (b) Edman degradation; (c) C-terminal analysis.
Example 54 Adv

Trypsin and Pro

The peptide Ala–Lys–Pro–Gly–Arg–Val is treated with trypsin. Predict the products.

Show solution
  1. Trypsin cuts after Lys and Arg, but not if the next residue is Pro. Lys is followed by Pro: no cut there. Arg is followed by Val: cut.
  2. Products: Ala–Lys–Pro–Gly–Arg and Val.
Example 55 Adv

How many peptide fragments?

A protein has 11 Lys, 8 Arg and 5 Met residues; two of the Lys are followed by Pro. How many fragments are obtained with (a) trypsin, (b) CNBr (assuming a single polypeptide chain, no disulfide)?

Show solution
  1. (a) Cleavage sites = 11 + 8 − 2 = 17 → 18 fragments.
  2. (b) 5 Met → 5 cuts → 6 fragments.
