13.1 Chemical separation by acid–base extraction

Validity Check Acid–base extraction works when one component is ionised and the other is not at the chosen pH; as a rule of thumb the pKa values must differ by at least about two units. Do not use a stronger reagent than needed: NaOH would extract carboxylic acid and phenol together, and prolonged strong acid or base may hydrolyse esters or amides.

The syllabus topic "chemical methods of separation of monofunctional organic compounds from binary mixtures" always reduces to one idea: use an aqueous acid or base to convert one component into a water-soluble salt, separate it from the other (which stays in the organic solvent), then regenerate it.

Standard binary mixtures:

Typical binary mixture separations
MixtureProcedure
Benzoic acid + naphthaleneDissolve in ether; shake with aq. NaHCO3: acid → sodium benzoate (aqueous); ether layer keeps naphthalene. Acidify aqueous layer with HCl: benzoic acid ppt
Phenol + toluene (neutral)Shake with aq. NaOH: sodium phenoxide dissolves; toluene stays. Pass CO2 or add HCl to recover phenol
Benzoic acid + phenolShake with aq. NaHCO3: benzoic acid extracted (phenol not); phenol then from ether layer with NaOH
Aniline + nitrobenzene (or toluene)Shake with dilute HCl: aniline → anilinium chloride (aqueous); nitrobenzene remains. Add NaOH to recover aniline
Aniline + phenolDilute HCl extracts aniline; NaOH extracts phenol from the ether
Benzoic acid + anilineBoth ionise: add NaHCO3 (extract acid) then HCl (extract amine); or add acid to precipitate amine salt
Benzaldehyde + acetophenoneShake with saturated NaHSO3: benzaldehyde gives a solid adduct (acetophenone does not, because of conjugation and steric hindrance); regenerate with dilute acid or Na2CO3
Ethanol + diethyl etherFractional distillation / extract alcohol with water or CaCl2 (forms adduct with alcohols)
Nitrobenzene + anilineSteam distillation separates neither (both volatile); use acid extraction
o-Nitrophenol + p-nitrophenolSteam distillation (ortho distils)

Separating 1°, 2° and 3° amines (Hinsberg method). Add C6H5SO2Cl and excess aq. NaOH: 1° gives a soluble sodium salt of the sulphonamide; 2° gives an insoluble sulphonamide; 3° does not react. Distil the 3° amine (steam). Filter the solid 2° sulphonamide; acidify the filtrate to get the 1° sulphonamide; each sulphonamide is hydrolysed by acid to return the amine.

Exam Trap The order of extraction matters: if a mixture contains acid, phenol and amine, use NaHCO3 first (only the carboxylic acid), then NaOH (the phenol), then HCl (the amine); using NaOH first would extract the acid and the phenol together. Never use strong acid on an ester/amide-containing neutral if you plan to recover it intact: hydrolysis can occur on prolonged heating.
Example 20 Adv

Separation scheme

A solid mixture of benzoic acid, p-cresol, aniline and naphthalene is dissolved in ether. Write the sequence of reagents to separate the four.

Show solution
  1. 1. Shake the ether solution with saturated aqueous NaHCO3: benzoic acid → sodium benzoate in the aqueous layer (pKa 4.2 < carbonic acid 6.4). Acidify with HCl to recover benzoic acid.
  2. 2. Shake the ether layer with aqueous NaOH: p-cresol (pKa 10.2) → sodium p-cresoxide. Pass CO2 to recover p-cresol.
  3. 3. Shake the ether layer with dilute HCl: aniline → anilinium chloride (aqueous). Add NaOH to liberate aniline.
  4. 4. Evaporate the ether layer: naphthalene.
