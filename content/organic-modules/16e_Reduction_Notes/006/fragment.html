7. Dissolving-metal reductions

Sodium or lithium in liquid ammonia (−33 °C) dissolves to give solvated electrons (deep blue solution). These electrons reduce alkynes and aromatic rings; ammonia (or an added alcohol) is the proton source.

  1. An electron adds to the alkyne to give a radical anion, which is protonated by NH3 to give a vinyl radical.
  2. A second electron gives a vinyl anion. The trans vinyl anion is more stable because the alkyl groups are far apart; it is protonated by NH3.
  3. Net result: anti addition of H2, giving the trans (E) alkene. The alkene is not reduced further.

Reactant:

Reagent: Na, NH3(l), −33 °C

Product:

Contrast: H2/Lindlar gives the (Z) alkene.

Terminal alkynes are deprotonated by the amide formed in situ to give acetylides, which are not reduced. Isolated alkenes are inert to Na/NH3. LiAlH4 reduces propargylic alcohols to (E)-allylic alcohols by a related anti addition.

The Birch reduction converts a benzene ring into a 1,4-cyclohexadiene (Na or Li, NH3, ROH). Regiochemistry depends on the substituent:

  • Electron-donating groups (alkyl, OR, NR2) end up on carbons of the remaining double bonds (sp2).
  • Electron-withdrawing groups (COOH, CONH2, aryl) end up on an sp3 carbon: the ipso carbon and the para carbon are the ones that are reduced.

Reactant:

Reagent: Na, NH3(l), EtOH

Reactant:

Reagent: Li, NH3(l), EtOH

Product:

Trap. Birch gives the 1,4-diene (non-conjugated), never the 1,3-diene. Anisole hydrolysed with mild acid gives cyclohex-3-enone; stronger acid moves the double bond into conjugation to give cyclohex-2-enone.
