Chapter tests

Two tests of twenty questions each, to be attempted without the text. Allow 60 minutes per test. Single-correct questions have exactly one correct option; multiple-correct questions may have one or more; numerical answers are rounded as stated. No question repeats a problem of the exercise sets. Keys follow each test, and each solution is shown under its question.

Test 1: concepts, structures and mechanisms (Questions 330 to 349)

Test 1 · Q1 Test 1, Single

Most stable cation

Which of the following cations is the most stable in the gas phase? (A) CH2=CHCH2+ (B) C6H5CH2+ (C) (CH3)2CH+ (D) CH3CH2+

Show answer

(B). The hydride affinities are benzyl 233, isopropyl 247, allyl 256 and ethyl 274 kcal mol−1; the smaller the value the more stable the cation. The benzyl cation delocalises the charge into the ring.

Test 1 · Q2 Test 1, Single

Hydrobromination with rearrangement

The major product of 3-methylbut-1-ene with HBr in the absence of peroxide is (A) 1-bromo-3-methylbutane (B) 2-bromo-3-methylbutane (C) 2-bromo-2-methylbutane (D) 1-bromo-2-methylbutane.

Show answer

(C). Protonation gives the secondary cation; a 1,2-hydride shift gives the tertiary cation, and bromide adds to it.

Test 1 · Q3 Test 1, Single

Peroxide effect

Propene reacts with HBr in the presence of benzoyl peroxide. The major product is (A) 2-bromopropane (B) 1-bromopropane (C) 1,2-dibromopropane (D) propane.

Show answer

(B). Br• adds to the terminal carbon to give the secondary radical, which abstracts H from HBr.

Test 1 · Q4 Test 1, Multiple

Singlet carbenes

Which statements about singlet carbenes are correct? (A) Both non-bonding electrons occupy one sp2 orbital. (B) Addition to a Z-alkene is stereospecific. (C) They give an ESR signal. (D) The bond angle is about 100 to 110°.

Show answer

A, B and D. A singlet has paired electrons, so it shows no ESR signal (C is false); it adds in one concerted step (B) and its angle is small (D).

Test 1 · Q5 Test 1, Single

Arynes from m-chlorotoluene

How many different toluidines form when m-chlorotoluene reacts with sodium amide in liquid ammonia? (A) 1 (B) 2 (C) 3 (D) 4

Show answer

(C). 3-Methylbenzyne gives o- and m-toluidine, and 4-methylbenzyne gives m- and p-toluidine; the three different compounds are o-, m- and p-toluidine.

Test 1 · Q6 Test 1, Numerical

Per-hydrogen reactivity

In the photochlorination of propane the secondary hydrogens are 4.5 times as reactive as the primary hydrogens. What percentage of the monochloride is 2-chloropropane? (nearest integer)

Show answer

60. Weights: primary 6 × 1 = 6; secondary 2 × 4.5 = 9; 9/(6 + 9) = 60 %. (This is the 40 : 60 distribution of propane chlorination.)

Test 1 · Q7 Test 1, Single

Skeleton retained or rearranged

In which reaction is the carbon skeleton retained? (A) neopentyl chloride with AgNO3 in ethanol (B) neopentyl chloride with Cl2 and light (C) 3,3-dimethylbutan-2-ol with H2SO4 (D) cyclobutylmethyl cation formed in solution

Show answer

(B). Radicals do not undergo 1,2-methyl shifts. A, C and D involve cations that rearrange.

Test 1 · Q8 Test 1, Single

Hofmann degradation

3-Phenylpropanamide is treated with Br2 and NaOH. The organic product is (A) PhCH2CH2NH2 (B) PhCH2CH2CH2NH2 (C) PhCH2NH2 (D) PhCH2CH2COOH.

Show answer

(A). The carbonyl carbon is lost; the amine has one carbon fewer than the amide.

Test 1 · Q9 Test 1, Multiple

Carbon acids in DMSO

Which compounds are more acidic than acetone (pKa 26.5 in DMSO)? (A) pentane-2,4-dione (B) ethyl acetate (C) nitromethane (D) acetonitrile

Show answer

A and C. pKa: pentane-2,4-dione 13.3, nitromethane 17.2 (both below 26.5); ethyl acetate 30.5 and acetonitrile 31.3 are above it.

Test 1 · Q10 Test 1, Single

Kinetic enolate

The less substituted (kinetic) enolate of 2-methylcyclohexanone is formed with (A) NaOEt in ethanol at 25 °C (B) LDA in THF at −78 °C (C) NaH under equilibrating conditions (D) aqueous acid.

Show answer

(B). A strong, hindered base in an aprotic solvent at low temperature removes the more accessible proton irreversibly.

Test 1 · Q11 Test 1, Numerical

Barrier difference

A product forms 500 times faster through one transition state than through another. Calculate ΔΔG‡ in kJ mol−1 at 298 K (nearest integer).

Show answer

15. ΔΔG‡ = RT ln 500 = 8.314 × 298 × 6.215 = 15.4 kJ mol−1.

Test 1 · Q12 Test 1, Single

E2 and ring conformation

trans-1-Bromo-2-methylcyclohexane reacts with sodium ethoxide. The major alkene is (A) 1-methylcyclohexene (B) 3-methylcyclohexene (C) methylenecyclohexane (D) cyclohexene.

Show answer

(B). Br axial in the trans isomer puts CH3 axial too, so the C-2 hydrogen is equatorial and not anti to Br; the only anti hydrogen is on C-6, which gives 3-methylcyclohexene.

Test 1 · Q13 Test 1, Single

Aromatic ion

Which is aromatic according to Hückel's rule? (A) C5H5+ (B) C5H5− (C) C3H3− (D) C8H8

Show answer

(B). The cyclopentadienide anion has six π electrons in a planar ring. C5H5+ (4 π electrons) and C3H3− (4 π electrons) are antiaromatic; planar C8H8 has 8.

Test 1 · Q14 Test 1, Single

Limits of the peroxide effect

The radical chain addition of HCl to alkenes is not observed mainly because (A) the H–Cl bond is too weak (B) the step R• + HCl → RH + Cl• is endothermic (C) Cl• does not add to alkenes (D) HCl is a weak acid.

Show answer

(B). H–Cl is 103 kcal mol−1, stronger than a secondary C–H bond (99), so the hydrogen-transfer step is endothermic by about 4 kcal mol−1.

Test 1 · Q15 Test 1, Multiple

Isocyanates

Which rearrangements give an isocyanate as the key intermediate? (A) Hofmann (B) Curtius (C) Beckmann (D) Schmidt (E) Lossen

Show answer

A, B, D and E. The Beckmann rearrangement gives a nitrilium ion and an amide.

Test 1 · Q16 Test 1, Single

Evidence for benzyne

Chlorobenzene labelled with 14C at C-1 gives aniline labelled equally at C-1 and C-2. This shows (A) SNAr (B) elimination–addition through benzyne (C) a radical chain (D) SN1 through an aryl cation.

Show answer

(B). A symmetrical intermediate is required; direct substitution would leave all the label at C-1.

Test 1 · Q17 Test 1, Numerical

Monochlorination products

How many monochlorination products, including stereoisomers, does 2,2-dimethylbutane give?

Show answer

4. 1-Chloro-2,2-dimethylbutane, 3-chloro-2,2-dimethylbutane (two enantiomers) and 1-chloro-3,3-dimethylbutane.

Test 1 · Q18 Test 1, Single

Baeyer–Villiger migration

Methyl cyclohexyl ketone is oxidised with a peracid. The product is (A) cyclohexyl ethanoate (B) methyl cyclohexanecarboxylate (C) cyclohexanol (D) cyclohexanone.

Show answer

(A). The secondary alkyl group migrates in preference to methyl, giving CH3COOC6H11.

Test 1 · Q19 Test 1, Single

Fastest SNAr

Which compound reacts fastest with piperidine in an SNAr reaction? (A) 4-nitrofluorobenzene (B) 4-nitrochlorobenzene (C) 4-nitrobromobenzene (D) 4-nitroiodobenzene

Show answer

(A). The rate-determining step is the addition of the nucleophile, which the electronegative fluorine accelerates.

Test 1 · Q20 Test 1, Single

Stereochemistry of carbene addition

Z-but-2-ene reacts with dibromocarbene (from CHBr3 and t-BuOK). The product is (A) trans-1,1-dibromo-2,3-dimethylcyclopropane (B) cis-1,1-dibromo-2,3-dimethylcyclopropane (C) an equal mixture of both (D) 1,1-dibromo-2-ethylcyclopropane.

Show answer

(B). A singlet carbene adds in one step, so the two methyl groups remain cis.

Test 2: data, calculations and integrated questions (Questions 350 to 369)

Test 2 · Q1 Test 2, Numerical

Half-life from a barrier

A unimolecular reaction has ΔG‡ = 80 kJ mol−1 at 298 K. Using k = (kBT/h)e−ΔG‡/RT with kBT/h = 6.2 × 1012 s−1, calculate the half-life in seconds (nearest integer).

Show answer

12. k = 6.2 × 1012 × exp(−80000/(8.314 × 298)) = 0.059 s−1; t1/2 = 0.693/0.059 = 11.8 s.

Test 2 · Q2 Test 2, Numerical

Equilibrium constant

Two isomers differ by 5.7 kJ mol−1 in free energy at 298 K, the more stable one being favoured. What is the ratio of the more stable to the less stable at equilibrium (nearest integer)?

Show answer

10. K = exp(5700/(8.314 × 298)) = 9.98.

Test 2 · Q3 Test 2, Numerical

Hydrogen abstraction by bromine

Using bond dissociation enthalpies of 101 kcal mol−1 for C2H5–H and 88 for H–Br, calculate ΔH (kcal mol−1) of Br• + C2H6 → C2H5• + HBr.

Show answer

+13. ΔH = 101 − 88 = +13 kcal mol−1; the step is endothermic, so the transition state is late.

Test 2 · Q4 Test 2, Numerical

Tropylium in water

The pKR+ of the tropylium ion is +4.7. What is [C7H7+]/[C7H7OH] at pH 3.0 (nearest integer)?

Show answer

50. The ratio is 104.7 − 3.0 = 101.7 = 50.

Test 2 · Q5 Test 2, Numerical

Nucleophilic constants

The nucleophilic constants toward methyl iodide in methanol are 9.9 for C6H5S− and 6.3 for CH3O−. Thiophenoxide reacts 10x times faster than methoxide. Find x (one decimal place).

Show answer

3.6. x = 9.9 − 6.3.

Test 2 · Q6 Test 2, Numerical

Yield of aniline

12.1 g of benzamide (M = 121.1) is converted to aniline (M = 93.1) in a Hofmann reaction. The yield is 75 %. What mass of aniline (g, two decimal places) is obtained?

Show answer

6.98 g. 0.100 mol × 93.1 × 0.75 = 6.98 g.

Test 2 · Q7 Test 2, Numerical

Quantum yield

A photochemical chlorination absorbs 4.0 × 10−6 mol of photons and gives 2.0 × 10−2 mol of chloroalkane. What is the quantum yield?

Show answer

5000. Φ = 2.0 × 10−2/4.0 × 10−6 = 5.0 × 103.

Test 2 · Q8 Test 2, Numerical

Thermodynamic control at higher temperature

Two isomers differ by 8.0 kJ mol−1. By what factor does the more stable one predominate at equilibrium at 400 K (nearest integer)?

Show answer

11. exp(8000/(8.314 × 400)) = 11.1.

Test 2 · Q9 Test 2, Single

Order of acidity

Which order of decreasing acidity in DMSO is correct? (A) diethyl malonate > cyclopentadiene > acetone > toluene (B) acetone > diethyl malonate > cyclopentadiene > toluene (C) cyclopentadiene > diethyl malonate > toluene > acetone (D) toluene > acetone > cyclopentadiene > diethyl malonate

Show answer

(A). pKa: 16.4, 18.1, 26.5, about 43.

Test 2 · Q10 Test 2, Single

Why bromination is selective

The selectivity of bromination compared with chlorination is explained by (A) the greater strength of the C–Br bond (B) a late transition state for the endothermic hydrogen abstraction (C) the higher energy of Br• (D) the absence of chain termination.

Show answer

(B). The transition state resembles the product radical, so differences in radical stability are felt in the activation energy.

Test 2 · Q11 Test 2, Single

Stable carbene

The stable, isolable carbene with an X-ray bond angle of 102° is best described as (A) a triplet diradical (B) a nucleophilic singlet stabilised by two nitrogen lone pairs (C) an electrophilic singlet (D) a carbenoid.

Show answer

(B). N-Heterocyclic carbenes are singlets stabilised by donation from two adjacent nitrogen atoms.

Test 2 · Q12 Test 2, Single

Which orbital interaction explains the E2 stereochemistry?

The preference for anti-periplanar elimination arises from overlap of (A) σ(C–H) with σ*(C–X) (B) π(C=C) with σ*(C–X) (C) the lone pair of the base with the π bond (D) σ(C–X) with σ*(C–H) from the same side.

Show answer

(A). The C–H bonding orbital donates into the back lobe of the C–X antibonding orbital when the bonds are anti.

Test 2 · Q13 Test 2, Single

Product of an anionic rearrangement

2-Chlorocyclohexanone with sodium methoxide gives (A) 2-methoxycyclohexanone (B) methyl cyclopentanecarboxylate (C) cyclohexanone (D) methyl cyclohexanecarboxylate.

Show answer

(B). Favorskii rearrangement with ring contraction through a cyclopropanone.

Test 2 · Q14 Test 2, Single

Selectivity of enolate alkylation

An enolate is alkylated with (CH3)2SO4 in DMSO. The main site of reaction is (A) carbon (B) oxygen (C) both equally (D) neither.

Show answer

(B). Dimethyl sulfate is a hard electrophile and DMSO separates the ion pair, favouring O-alkylation.

Test 2 · Q15 Test 2, Multiple

Conditions for kinetic control

Which conditions favour kinetic control in enolate formation? (A) LDA (B) −78 °C (C) a protic solvent (D) an excess of ketone

Show answer

A and B. A strong hindered base at low temperature deprotonates irreversibly; a protic solvent or an excess of ketone allows proton exchange (equilibration).

Test 2 · Q16 Test 2, Multiple

Radical or cation?

Which of the following observations point to a radical intermediate? (A) the carbon skeleton is retained in a neopentyl system (B) a quantum yield of 104 (C) racemisation of a chiral product (D) an induction period caused by oxygen

Show answer

A, B and D. C is also found for cations and does not distinguish.

Test 2 · Q17 Test 2, Multiple

Correct statements

Which statements are correct? (A) The tropylium ion is aromatic. (B) The cyclopentadienyl cation is antiaromatic. (C) The vinyl cation is more stable than the methyl cation in the gas phase. (D) The phenyl cation is more stable than the ethyl cation.

A, B and C. D is false: ethyl (274) is more stable than phenyl (298).
