10. Directing and activating effects: the reasons

10.1 Two separate questions

Every substituent Z on a benzene ring raises two distinct questions:

  • 1. Rate: is the substituted ring more or less reactive than benzene? (Activating or deactivating.)
  • 2. Orientation: which positions of the ring does the incoming electrophile prefer? (Ortho/para-directing or meta-directing.)

These two are often correlated but are not the same property. Halogens are the standard exceptions: they deactivate yet direct o/p. The tool for both questions is the stability of the arenium ion and the stability of the transition state leading to it; the conjugate effects (–I, +I, +M, –M, hyperconjugation) alter that stability.

10.2 Activating groups: the arenium-ion argument

Take a donor group Z with a lone pair (OMe, NH2, OH). When E+ attacks the ortho or para position, one resonance contributor places the positive charge on the carbon bearing Z, and the lone pair of Z can then delocalise onto that carbon: a fourth, fully octet-satisfied resonance structure (an oxonium or iminium ion). Attack at meta never puts the charge on C–Z: the extra structure is unavailable.

+charge on C2 +charge on C4 (bears OMe) oxonium form (all octets full)
Anisole, attack at the position para to OMe: four contributors; the last two are specially stable because the charge is carried by the carbon holding OMe, and then by O itself.
+charge on C2 +charge on C4 +charge on C6
Anisole, attack at the position meta to OMe: three contributors only, none with the charge on the carbon bearing OMe (no oxonium form).
+charge on C2 (bears OMe) oxonium form +charge on C4 +charge on C6
Anisole, ortho attack: also four contributors, including the oxonium ion.

Result: ortho and para attack give arenium ions stabilised by the donor lone pair (the oxonium resonance form); meta attack does not. The transition states mirror this, so ortho/para substitution is much faster than meta, and much faster than substitution in benzene itself (the cation is more stable). A donor group is therefore an activating, o/p-director.

Key idea Test for an o/p-director: does Z have a lone pair (or π bond, or filled σ orbital) on the atom attached to the ring that can donate to an adjacent carbocation? If yes (OR, NR2, X, alkyl by hyperconjugation, aryl, vinyl) → ortho/para.

Trap Acetanilide (PhNHCOCH3) and phenyl acetate (PhOCOCH3) are weaker activators than aniline and phenol: that is the point of protecting an amine as the amide, to moderate its reactivity, prevent over-substitution and stop oxidation. They are still o/p-directing and activating relative to benzene.

10.3 Deactivating meta directors

Take a –M group, such as C=O, NO2, CN, SO3H. The atom attached to the ring carries a positive (δ+) charge: C of C=O, N+ of NO2, C of C≡N, S of SO3H. When E+ attacks ortho or para, one contributor puts the positive charge on the ring carbon that carries Z, directly adjacent to another positive centre: strongly destabilising. Meta attack never places the charge on C–Z.

nitrobenzene, para attack: bad contributor, + on C–NO₂ para: other contributor meta attack: charge never on C–NO₂
Nitrobenzene: all three contributors for meta attack avoid C–NO₂; one of the three for ortho or para attack is very unfavourable (positive charge next to N⁺).
  • All positions of the ring are deactivated (the ring is poorer in electrons and the cation has a –M/–I group nearby); meta attack is simply the least unfavourable, not a favourable position. This is why meta directors also slow the reaction down by factors of 104–108.
  • Nitration of nitrobenzene gives ≈ 6 % ortho, 93 % meta, < 1 % para; benzaldehyde gives ≈ 19 % ortho, 72 % meta, 9 % para; trifluoromethylbenzene ≈ 6 : 91 : 3 (approximate figures).
  • Para is generally the least formed isomer for meta directors, even though para is not sterically hindered: its contributor with charge on C–Z is the worst (charge-charge repulsion at the furthest-reaching position).
Remember Strong –I groups without π-conjugation (–CF3, –CCl3, –NR3+, –NH3+) are meta directors for the same reason (charge adjacent to δ+ centre). –CH2Cl is only weakly –I (insulated by one carbon) and is an o/p director (with a notable meta share); –CH2NO2, –CH2COOH, –CH2CN are o/p directors or mixed; −CH=CH–NO2 and –CH=CH–COOH are o/p (the vinyl π bond donates, the EWG is at the far end).

10.4 Halogens: deactivating but ortho/para directing

Halogens (X = F, Cl, Br, I) show two opposing effects. –I: the electronegative X pulls σ-electrons from the ring and destabilises any cationic TS: this slows the reaction. +M: a lone pair on X can donate into an adjacent cation (halonium ion contributor) in ortho/para attack, but cannot do so in meta attack. The –I effect operates at all positions and is dominant for rate; the +M effect operates selectively at o/p and is decisive for orientation.

  • Fluorine: 2p–2p overlap is excellent, so +M is nearly as strong as –I at para: the para position of fluorobenzene is as reactive as a single position in benzene (partial rate factor ≈ 1), while the ortho position is strongly deactivated by proximity of the –I effect: para : ortho ratio is the highest of the halogens.

  • Para : ortho ratios are higher for halobenzenes than the statistical value (1 : 2) because of the combination of the electronic effect (o is closer to the –I group) and, for the larger halogens, sterics.
Trap "Which is more reactive in EAS, chlorobenzene or toluene or benzene?" Toluene > benzene > chlorobenzene. Halogens are among the very few groups that are deactivating but o/p-directing. The standard 'exception list' also includes –CH2Cl, –CH=CH–COOH and –CH=CH–NO2 (vinyl with EWG at the far end) at the weak end.

10.5 Hyperconjugation and the alkyl groups

Alkyl groups direct o/p and activate because (i) the +I effect pushes electron density to the ring carbon, and (ii) in o/p attack one contributor has the charge on C–R, a tertiary-like cation, stabilised by hyperconjugation with the C–H or C–C bonds of R (in meta attack the charge is on secondary-like carbons only).

ortho/para attack on toluene: tertiary-type contributor the same contributor written with hyperconjugation (C=CH₂ type) meta attack: no contributor with charge on C–CH₃
The para-attack contributor with the charge on C–CH₃ is a tertiary carbocation; the meta attack has only secondary-type contributors.
Example 18 Main

Rank the reactivity

Arrange in decreasing rate of nitration: (a) benzene, toluene, p-xylene, mesitylene (b) benzene, chlorobenzene, nitrobenzene, phenol, acetanilide (c) anisole, acetanilide, benzene, benzaldehyde.

Show solution
  1. (a) Each CH3 adds activation (about 25× for the first, and roughly additive in log): mesitylene > p-xylene > toluene > benzene (mesitylene is near diffusion-controlled).
  2. (b) Phenol > acetanilide > benzene > chlorobenzene > nitrobenzene.
  3. (c) Anisole > acetanilide > benzene > benzaldehyde. OMe (full +M with an O lone pair not shared) is a stronger activator than NHCOCH3 (N lone pair partly used by C=O).

Predict the nitration products

Give the major mononitration product(s): (a) PhCF3 (b) PhNHCOCH3 (c) PhCH2Cl (d) PhNH3+ HSO4− (e) PhOCOCH3 (f) PhCH=CH–COOH (g) PhCOCl (h) PhNMe2 in dil. acid vs in HNO3/H2SO4.

Show solution

  2. (b) p-Nitroacetanilide (with some ortho).
  3. (c) CH2Cl is weakly deactivating, o/p: o- and p-nitrobenzyl chloride (mostly p).
  4. (d) anilinium: –NH3+ strongly –I, no π: m-nitroanilinium (m-nitroaniline after neutralisation). Aniline in HNO3/H2SO4 gives ≈ 47 % meta.

Example 20 Adv

Resonance picture

Draw the contributor of the arenium ion for para-nitration of chlorobenzene that shows how Cl stabilises the cation, and explain why this contributor is absent in meta attack.

Show solution
  1. Para attack puts the positive charge at C1 (the carbon carrying Cl) in one contributor. A chlorine lone pair then donates to C1 to make a chloronium double bond C1=Cl+: every atom has an octet.
  2. For meta attack the charge is on C2, C4 and C6 (counting from the sp3 carbon C3, with Cl at C1) and never on C1: the Cl lone pair is not conjugated with the cationic centres, so only –I operates and the cation is destabilised.

10.6 Quantitative support: partial rate factors and σ+

The reactivity of each ring position relative to one position of benzene is the partial rate factor f. For toluene nitration (with nitration relative rate 25): fo ≈ 42, fm ≈ 2.5, fp ≈ 58. Because fm > 1, methyl activates even meta (a little), but o and p much more. For chlorobenzene: fo ≈ 0.03, fm ≈ 0.0006, fp ≈ 0.14 (all < 1: every position is deactivated, but ortho/para are less deactivated than meta). For nitrobenzene all f are tiny and fm is the largest. Brown–Okamoto σ+ constants correlate log f with substituent: log f = ρσ+, with ρ negative (≈ −6 for nitration and −12 for bromination in acetic acid) showing the strong build-up of positive charge in the TS. Typical σp+: OCH3 −0.78; NH2 −1.3; CH3 −0.31; F −0.07; Cl +0.11; Br +0.15; NO2 +0.79; σm+: CH3 −0.07; OCH3 +0.05; Cl +0.40; NO2 +0.71.

Shortcut Use σ+ only for ranking. A negative σp+ = activating at para; a positive value = deactivating. Halogens have σp+ ≈ 0 (F −0.07, Cl +0.11) but σm+ ≈ +0.35 (strongly positive): para much less deactivated than meta: o/p direction.
Example 21 Adv

Product prediction by σ+

Use the σ+ values to explain why chlorobenzene reacts mainly at para although the overall rate is lower than benzene's.

Show solution
  1. The rate at a position is f = 10ρσ+. With ρ ≈ −6 (nitration): para: σp+ = +0.11 → fp ≈ 10−0.66 ≈ 0.2 (i.e. ≈ 0.14 measured); meta: σm+ = +0.40 → fm ≈ 10−2.4 ≈ 0.004.
  2. Both < 1: Cl deactivates every position. But para is ~35–50× more reactive than meta, so the product is 70 % para, ≈ 30 % ortho, ≈ 1 % meta.
