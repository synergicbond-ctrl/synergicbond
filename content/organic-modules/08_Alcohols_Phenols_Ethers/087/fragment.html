22.3 Electrophilic substitution of aryl ethers

The OR group is a strong activating o,p-director (+M outweighs −I), a little less activating than OH. Anisole (methoxybenzene) reacts orders of magnitude faster than benzene in nitration and bromination. The para product usually predominates over ortho because of sterics.

Example 62 L2 Main

Anisole versus phenol

Compare phenol and anisole in respect of (a) reaction with NaOH; (b) bromination; (c) reaction with HI; (d) FeCl3 test.

Show solution
  1. (a) Phenol dissolves (phenoxide); anisole does not (no acidic H).
  2. (b) Both are activated o,p-directors. Phenol with Br2/H2O goes at once to 2,4,6-tribromophenol; anisole in acetic acid gives mainly p-bromoanisole (monobromination), since OCH3 is a little less activating and cannot ionise to the much more reactive phenoxide.
  3. (c) Phenol: no reaction (no C–O cleavage); anisole: phenol + CH3I.
  4. (d) Phenol: violet; anisole: none.
Example 63 L3 Advanced

Peroxide hazards

An old bottle of diethyl ether is to be distilled. (a) What test should be done first? (b) Why is it dangerous to distil to dryness? (c) How is the peroxide removed and why does that reagent work?

Show solution
  1. (a) Test with acidified aqueous KI (yellow/brown I2, starch blue) or FeSO4/KSCN (red Fe(SCN)3).
  2. (b) Peroxides (hydroperoxides and polymeric peroxides, less volatile than ether) concentrate in the pot residue; they are shock- and heat-sensitive and detonate.
  3. (c) Shake with a freshly prepared aqueous solution of FeSO4 (or Na2SO3, NaHSO3) which reduces the peroxide O–O bond (Fe2+ → Fe3+), then dry over CaCl2 and distil from sodium wire with a trace of benzophenone (the blue ketyl indicates dry, peroxide-free ether).
