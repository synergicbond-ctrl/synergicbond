63. Cope and oxy-Cope rearrangements

Cope: a 1,5-diene ⇌ an isomeric 1,5-diene by a [3,3] shift through a chair transition state (heated at 150–250 °C). The equilibrium favours the diene with the more substituted double bonds (more stable). Oxy-Cope: a 1,5-dien-3-ol rearranges to an enol, which tautomerises to a δ,ε-unsaturated aldehyde or ketone; the tautomerisation makes it irreversible. The anionic oxy-Cope (KH or the potassium alkoxide, 18-crown-6) accelerates the rate by a factor of 1010–1017.

Δ (Cope)⟶
3-Methylhexa-1,5-diene ⇌ hepta-1,5-diene (the disubstituted internal alkene is more stable than the monosubstituted one, so the equilibrium lies to the right)
Δ or KH (oxy-Cope)⟶
Hexa-1,5-dien-3-ol → hex-5-enal (enol tautomerises)
Trap Do not confuse: Cope rearrangement (1,5-diene, carbon-only [3,3]) with Cope elimination (an amine oxide to an alkene and a hydroxylamine, Section 68). The same name stands for two unrelated reactions.
