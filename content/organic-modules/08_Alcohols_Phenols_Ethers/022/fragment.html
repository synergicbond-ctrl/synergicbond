Practice 151 L2 MIX

Complete the sequence and name each product: propene —(HBr, ROOR)→ A —(aq. KOH)→ B —(PCC)→ C.

Show answer
  1. Peroxide effect: anti-Markovnikov addition gives A = 1-bromopropane. Aq. KOH (SN2): B = propan-1-ol. PCC: C = propanal.

Practice 167 L2 MIX

Balance: C2H5OH + I2 + NaOH → CHI3 + HCOONa + NaI + H2O.

Show answer
  1. C2H5OH + 4I2 + 6NaOH → CHI3 + HCOONa + 5NaI + 5H2O. Check: C 2 = 1 + 1; H: 6 + 6 = 12 = 1 + 1 + 10; I: 8 = 3 + 5; Na 6 = 1 + 5; O: 1 + 6 = 7 = 2 + 5 ✓.

How many moles of I2 are needed to convert 1 mol of acetone into iodoform?

Practice 170 L2 MIX

0.92 g of a monohydric alcohol ROH reacts with excess Na to give 224 mL of H2 at STP. Calculate its molar mass and identify it.

Show answer
  1. 224 mL H2 = 0.0100 mol → 0.0200 mol alcohol (2 ROH → H2) → M = 0.92/0.0200 = 46 g mol−1 → ethanol.
Practice 171 L2 MIX

1.52 g of a dihydric alcohol, treated with excess Na, gives 0.448 L H2 at STP. Find M and the formula for a saturated acyclic diol.

Show answer
  1. Each molecule gives one H2: 0.02 mol diol → M = 1.52/0.02 = 76. CnH2n+2O2: 14n + 34 = 76 → n = 3: C3H8O2 (propane-1,2-diol or propane-1,3-diol).
Practice 172 L2 MIX

An organic compound X (C3H8O2) consumes 1 mol of HIO4 and gives HCHO and CH3CHO. Identify X.

  1. One cleavage with these fragments: HOCH2–CH(OH)–CH3, propane-1,2-diol. (Propane-1,3-diol would not react.)
Practice 173 L2 MIX

A compound C4H10O2 with HIO4 gives only CH3CHO (2 mol per mol compound). Identify it. How many stereoisomers does it have?

Show answer
  1. Butane-2,3-diol, CH3CH(OH)CH(OH)CH3. Three stereoisomers: (2R,3R), (2S,3S) and meso (2R,3S).
Practice 174 L2 MIX

Convert glycerol to (a) acrolein; (b) trinitrate; (c) 2-iodopropane; (d) 1,2,3-trichloropropane.

Show answer
  1. (a) KHSO4, Δ. (b) conc. HNO3/conc. H2SO4, below 288 K. (c) excess HI, Δ. (d) PCl5 (3 equiv.) or SOCl2.
Practice 175 L2 MIX

Why does glycerol not dissolve in ether but does in water? Why is it so viscous?

Show answer
  1. Three OH groups make it strongly polar and extensively H-bonded; water offers more H-bonding sites than ether. The extensive H-bond network between glycerol molecules causes high viscosity (and high bp, 290 °C).
Practice 176 L2 MIX

Draw the mechanism for the formation of diethyl ether from ethanol and H2SO4 and state why 2-methylpropan-2-ol does not give a good ether under the same conditions.

Show answer
  1. EtOH is protonated; a second EtOH attacks the CH2 of EtOH2+ (SN2) to give Et2OH+; deprotonation gives Et2O. For the tertiary alcohol SN2 is impossible and the 3° cation loses a proton to form 2-methylpropene (E1).
Practice 177 L2 MIX

Differentiate with one reagent each: (a) phenol and benzoic acid; (b) benzyl alcohol and benzaldehyde; (c) anisole and phenol; (d) ethanol and ethanal.

Show answer
  1. (a) NaHCO3 (CO2 from benzoic acid). (b) Tollens’ (silver mirror from benzaldehyde). (c) NaOH solubility or neutral FeCl3 (phenol). (d) Tollens’/Fehling’s (ethanal), or Na (ethanol gives H2).
Practice 178 L2 MIX

An organic compound (C7H8O) dissolves in NaOH but not in NaHCO3; with Br2/H2O it gives a precipitate C7H5OBr3. Identify it.

Show answer
  1. A tribromo derivative from a cresol needs three free o/p positions: m-cresol (2,4,6-tribromo-3-methylphenol, C7H5Br3O).
Practice 179 L2 MIX

Why does o-hydroxybenzaldehyde have a lower boiling point than p-hydroxybenzaldehyde? Which is steam volatile?

Show answer
  1. Intramolecular H-bond in the ortho isomer; the para isomer associates intermolecularly (bp higher, mp 116 °C). The ortho isomer is steam volatile.
Practice 180 L2 MIX

Complete the following with mechanism type: (a) (CH3)3COH + HCl; (b) CH3CH2CH2OH + SOCl2; (c) (CH3)2CHOH + PBr3.

Show answer
  1. (a) SN1: (CH3)3CCl. (b) SN2/SNi: CH3CH2CH2Cl + SO2 + HCl. (c) SN2: (CH3)2CHBr (inversion if chiral; no rearrangement).
Practice 181 L2 MIX

Why do PBr3 and SOCl2 avoid the rearrangements that occur with HBr?

Show answer
  1. They convert OH into a good leaving group (–OPBr2, –OS(O)Cl) without protonation to a free carbocation; Br−/Cl− displaces it by SN2 (or SNi), so no cation intermediate forms.
Practice 182 L2 MIX

Identify the species X, Y and Z: ethene —(Br2/H2O)→ X —(NaOH)→ Y —(H3O+)→ Z.

Show answer
  1. X = 2-bromoethanol; Y = ethylene oxide; Z = ethylene glycol.
Practice 183 L2 MIX

Write the mechanism of X → Y above and state why the ring closure is fast.

Show answer
  1. NaOH removes the O–H proton; the alkoxide attacks the carbon bearing Br from the back (intramolecular SN2, 3-exo-tet), expelling Br−. The reacting centres are held close together in the same molecule (high effective molarity), so closure is fast in spite of ring strain.
Practice 184 L2 MIX

Explain: epoxides undergo ring-opening with weak nucleophiles under acid catalysis, but ethers are cleaved only by HI/HBr under forcing conditions.

Show answer
  1. The three-membered ring has about 114 kJ mol−1 of strain, which is released as the ring opens; the protonated epoxide is therefore a very good electrophile. Acyclic ethers have no strain, so cleavage requires strong acid and a good nucleophile (I−) and heat.
Practice 185 L2 MIX

Which is more reactive towards ring opening with NaOH/H2O: ethylene oxide or oxetane or THF? Give the reason.

Show answer
  1. Ethylene oxide (strain about 114 kJ mol−1) > oxetane (about 107 kJ mol−1, slower) > THF (about 25 kJ mol−1; essentially unreactive).
Practice 186 L2 MIX

What are the products of the following: trans-2-butene + Br2/H2O, then NaOH? Name the epoxide and comment on stereochemistry.

Show answer
  1. Anti addition gives a racemic pair of bromohydrins (3-bromobutan-2-ol); intramolecular SN2 closes the ring with inversion at C–Br: trans-2,3-dimethyloxirane (racemic).
Practice 187 L2 MIX

Draw the product when 1-methylcyclohexene is treated with (a) mCPBA then H3O+; (b) OsO4; and compare their stereochemistry.

Show answer
  1. (a) Anti: trans-1-methylcyclohexane-1,2-diol. (b) Syn: cis-1-methylcyclohexane-1,2-diol. In the acid opening the water attacks the tertiary carbon of the protonated epoxide with inversion, giving the trans diol.
Practice 188 L2 MIX

Which epoxides C4H8O give butan-2-ol on reduction with LiAlH4?

Show answer
  1. Hydride attacks the less hindered carbon and the OH remains on the other. To obtain CH3CH2CH(OH)CH3, either 2-ethyloxirane (H− at CH2, OH stays at C-2) or 2,3-dimethyloxirane (either carbon, cis or trans) works; 2,2-dimethyloxirane would give 2-methylpropan-2-ol instead.
Practice 189 L2 MIX

Write the sequence for preparing ethylene glycol dimethyl ether (1,2-dimethoxyethane) from ethylene glycol.

Show answer
  1. HOCH2CH2OH + 2NaH → NaOCH2CH2ONa; then 2CH3I (Williamson): CH3OCH2CH2OCH3.
Practice 190 L2 MIX

Why do crown ethers solvate cations but not anions, and why does this activate the anion?

Show answer
  1. The cation fits into the cavity and is held by ion–dipole interactions with the oxygens. The anion has no such interaction with the hydrocarbon exterior, so it is poorly solvated (“naked”) and more nucleophilic/basic than in protic solvents.

Show answer

Practice 192 L2 MIX

How would you convert C2H5Br into (a) C2H5SH; (b) C2H5SC2H5; (c) C2H5SSC2H5?

Show answer
  1. (a) NaSH (excess). (b) C2H5SNa + C2H5Br (or Na2S, 2 C2H5Br). (c) Oxidise C2H5SH with I2 (or air/Br2).
Practice 193 L2 MIX

Why is a thioether alkylated by CH3I to a sulphonium salt, whereas an ether is not under the same conditions?

Show answer
  1. Sulphur is larger and more polarisable (a better nucleophile; lone pair in a 3p-based orbital, less tightly held); the sulphonium ion is stable. An ether oxygen is a poor nucleophile and the trialkyloxonium ion is highly reactive (Meerwein salts must be made with special reagents).
Practice 194 L2 MIX

Order the following by nucleophilicity in ethanol: CH3O−, CH3S−, CH3COO−, I−. Explain the first two.

Show answer
  1. CH3S− > I− > CH3O− > CH3COO−. In a protic solvent, small hard O− is strongly solvated by H-bonding, while the large polarisable S− is not.
