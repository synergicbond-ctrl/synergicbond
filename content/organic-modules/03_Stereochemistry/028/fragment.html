25. Stereochemistry of carbonyl additions

25.1 Attack on a prochiral carbonyl

A ketone R–CO–R′ (R ≠ R′) is trigonal planar with two enantiotopic faces. Attack of a non-chiral nucleophile (NaBH4, RMgX, HCN) from either face has the same rate, so the new stereocentre is formed as a racemate. If the molecule already has a stereocentre, the two faces are diastereotopic and the diastereomeric products form in unequal amounts: diastereoselective (Cram's rule: the nucleophile approaches past the smallest group on the adjacent stereocentre, with the largest group held perpendicular to the carbonyl).

butanone (R)-butan-2-ol (S)-butan-2-ol
NaBH₄ reduction of butanone: 1:1
Example 71 Main

Racemic or not?

For each reaction decide if the product is optically active: (a) propanal + CH3MgBr, then H3O+; (b) benzaldehyde + HCN; (c) acetone + NaBH4; (d) (R)-2-phenylpropanal + NaBH4.

Show solution
  1. (a) Butan-2-ol: one new stereocentre, achiral reagents: racemic, inactive. (b) Mandelonitrile: racemic. (c) Propan-2-ol: no stereocentre, inactive. (d) The substrate already has a stereocentre: the reduction creates a CH2OH group (no new stereocentre), so the product 2-phenylpropan-1-ol keeps the stereocentre untouched: optically active ((S) or (R) product depending on priorities).

25.2 Cyclohexanones: axial versus equatorial attack

A small nucleophile (LiAlH4, NaBH4) attacks 4-tert-butylcyclohexanone preferentially from the axial direction, giving the equatorial alcohol (about 90 % trans). A bulky hydride (L-Selectride) attacks from the equatorial direction (axial attack is blocked by the axial H atoms on C-3 and C-5), giving the axial alcohol (about 95 % cis). (The ratios are standard literature values.)

trans-4-tert-butylcyclohexanol (OH equatorial): major with LiAlH₄
cis-4-tert-butylcyclohexanol (OH axial): major with L-Selectride
