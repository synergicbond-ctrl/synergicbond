6.11 Serine protease (chymotrypsin): a peptide bond hydrolysed in two stages

The active site has a catalytic triad Ser-195, His-57, Asp-102 and an "oxyanion hole" formed by backbone N–H groups.

  • Step 1 (binding). The side chain of Phe/Tyr/Trp sits in a hydrophobic pocket: this is why chymotrypsin cuts after aromatic residues.
  • Step 2 (acylation, nucleophilic attack). His-57 (general base, itself held by Asp-102) removes the Ser-195 OH proton; the alkoxide attacks the peptide C=O (HOMO → π*). The tetrahedral intermediate's O− is stabilised in the oxyanion hole by two N–H hydrogen bonds. The N–H···O− bonds lower the energy of the transition state more than the ground state: this is what catalysis is.
  • Step 3 (collapse). The tetrahedral intermediate collapses, His-57H+ donates its proton to the leaving amine N (making it a good leaving group), and the C-terminal fragment is released: an acyl–enzyme (ester of Ser-195).
  • Step 4 (deacylation). Water replaces the amine: His-57 deprotonates water, HO− attacks the acyl–enzyme ester, a second tetrahedral intermediate forms and collapses, releasing the N-terminal fragment (as a carboxylic acid) and regenerating the enzyme.
Key idea Why the mechanism has two tetrahedral intermediates and two stages: the first is the attack of a protein nucleophile (Ser) on the peptide; the second is the attack of water on the resulting ester. Each uses the same general-base/general-acid pair and the same oxyanion hole. An organophosphate (DFP, sarin) phosphorylates Ser-195 irreversibly and so poisons the enzyme.
