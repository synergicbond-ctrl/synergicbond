5. Free-radical halogenation of alkanes

5.1 The reaction and the three-stage mechanism

Alkane + halogen, in the presence of light (hν) or heat (above about 250 °C), gives alkyl halide + hydrogen halide: R–H + X2 → R–X + H–X. This is a radical chain reaction. The mechanism for chlorination of methane:

  • Initiation: Cl–Cl → 2 Cl• (homolysis of the weakest bond by light; BDE 58 kcal mol-1 (243 kJ) only needs visible/UV photons).
  • Propagation 1: Cl• + CH4 → HCl + •CH3 (H abstraction).
  • Propagation 2: •CH3 + Cl2 → CH3Cl + Cl• (the new Cl• restarts step 1: this is the chain).
  • Termination: Cl• + Cl• → Cl2; •CH3 + •CH3 → C2H6; •CH3 + Cl• → CH3Cl.

Observations explained by the mechanism: (i) a quantum yield ≫ 1 (one photon triggers thousands of cycles); (ii) inhibitors such as O2 or quinone trap radicals and give an induction period; (iii) trace ethane in the products proves the termination step •CH3 + •CH3; (iv) further substitution gives CH2Cl2, CHCl3, CCl4, since CH3Cl also has H's. Using a large excess of CH4 gives mostly monochloride; excess Cl2 gives CCl4.

5.2 Thermochemistry and the reactivity of the halogens

Use bond dissociation energies (kcal mol-1): C–H(CH3) 105; X–X: F 38, Cl 58, Br 46, I 36; H–X: F 136, Cl 103, Br 87, I 71; CH3–X: F 115, Cl 85, Br 70, I 57.

  • Fluorination is so exothermic that C–C bonds break; it is violent and non-selective.
  • Chlorination is smooth, mildly exothermic in the first step (small activation energy, about 17 kJ mol-1).
  • Bromination: first step is endothermic (+18), Ea ≈ 75 kJ; works only with heat/light and is very selective.
  • Iodination: overall endothermic, and HI is a strong reducing agent which reverses it (RI + HI → RH + I2); alkane iodination does not work unless an oxidising agent (HIO3, HNO3) removes HI.

Order of reactivity toward alkanes: F2 > Cl2 > Br2 ≫ I2. Order of selectivity: I2 > Br2 > Cl2 > F2 (the reverse).

Chlorination: step 1 is nearly thermoneutral with an early TS (Hammond), the chain is driven by the exothermic second step

5.3 Why bromine is selective and chlorine is not: the Hammond postulate

The Hammond postulate: in an exothermic step the transition state resembles the reactants (early); in an endothermic step the transition state resembles the products (late).

  • Chlorine abstraction (step 1) is almost thermoneutral; its TS is early (little C–H breaking, little radical character on carbon), so the stability difference of the product radicals (3° > 2° > 1°) is only partly felt: selectivity is modest.
  • Bromine abstraction is endothermic (+18); the TS is late, with the C–H bond nearly fully broken and a nearly free alkyl radical; the full stability difference between radicals (3° vs 2° vs 1°) is felt: selectivity is high.

Product distribution

Calculate the % of each monochloride from (a) propane, (b) 2-methylpropane, (c) butane with Cl2/hν (relative rates 1° : 2° : 3° = 1 : 3.8 : 5). Repeat (b) for bromination (1 : 82 : 1640).

Show solution
  1. (a) 6 × 1 = 6 (1-chloropropane); 2 × 3.8 = 7.6 (2-chloropropane). Total 13.6. 1-Cl: 44.1 %, 2-Cl: 55.9 %.
  2. (b) 9 × 1 = 9 (1-chloro-2-methylpropane); 1 × 5 = 5 (2-chloro-2-methylpropane). Total 14. 1°: 64.3 %, 3°: 35.7 %.
  3. (c) 6 × 1 = 6 (1-chlorobutane); 4 × 3.8 = 15.2 (2-chlorobutane). Total 21.2: 1-Cl 28.3 %, 2-Cl 71.7 %.
  4. Bromination of 2-methylpropane: 9 × 1 = 9; 1 × 1640 = 1640. 3° share = 1640/1649 = 99.45 % 2-bromo-2-methylpropane.

5.5 Stereochemistry of radical halogenation

The alkyl radical is planar (or a very rapidly inverting shallow pyramid), so halogen transfer from X2 takes place on either face with equal probability.

  • If the radical carbon becomes a new stereocentre in the product and the molecule had no other stereocentre, the product is a racemic mixture (50 : 50), optically inactive.
  • If the H abstracted is at an existing stereocentre, that centre is destroyed in the radical and its configuration is lost: an optically active substrate gives a racemic product (e.g. (S)-3-methylhexane + Br2/hν → racemic 3-bromo-3-methylhexane).
  • If the radical forms elsewhere in a molecule that already has a stereocentre, the new centre is formed on either face and the products are diastereomers, normally in unequal amounts.

Stereochemistry at a stereocentre

Optically pure (S)-3-methylhexane is brominated with Br2/hν. The major product is 3-bromo-3-methylhexane. Is it optically active?

Br₂, hν⟶
Radical at C-3 is planar: attack on both faces gives the racemate
Show solution
  1. Br• abstracts the tertiary H on C-3 (the weakest C–H, high selectivity for Br).
  2. The radical CH3CH2–C•(CH3)–CH2CH2CH3 is planar; the original configuration is lost.
  3. Br2 attacks both faces equally: (R)- and (S)-3-bromo-3-methylhexane in 1 : 1. The product is optically inactive (racemic).
Example 9 Advanced

Radical at a different carbon of a chiral molecule

(S)-2-Chlorobutane is chlorinated further at C-3 (Cl2, hν). What 2,3-dichlorobutanes form, and are they all optically active?

Show solution
  1. C-2 keeps its spatial arrangement. The two hydrogens on C-3 are diastereotopic (the molecule is chiral), so abstraction of one H or the other, and attack on either face of the planar radical, give both possible configurations at C-3.
  2. Products: the chiral (2S,3S)-2,3-dichlorobutane and the meso (2R,3S)-compound; they are diastereomers formed in unequal amounts and are separable.
  3. Only the (2S,3S) isomer is optically active; the meso product is not.
