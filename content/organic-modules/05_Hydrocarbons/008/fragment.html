7. Other reactions of alkanes

7.1 Combustion

Complete: CnH2n+2 + (3n+1)/2 O2 → n CO2 + (n+1) H2O, strongly exothermic; heat of combustion rises by about 650 kJ mol-1 per added CH2. Incomplete combustion (limited O2) gives CO or soot. Methane + O2 (Cu tube, 100 atm) → methanol; (Mo2O3) → methanal.

Formula O2 needed per mole alkane = (3n + 1)/2. Methane 2, ethane 3.5, propane 5, butane 6.5.
Heats of combustion are used to compare isomer stability: the more branched isomer has the smaller heat of combustion (it is more stable). n-Butane (about 2877 kJ mol-1) releases more heat than isobutane (about 2868 kJ mol-1); both give 4 CO2 + 5 H2O, so the 9 kJ difference is the extra stability of the branched isomer.

KMnO4 oxidises a tertiary C–H of an alkane to tertiary alcohol; hot, it can degrade the chain. This is a test for tertiary hydrogen: (CH3)3CH + KMnO4 (pink) → purple colour lost with brown MnO2.

7.3 Nitration, sulphonation and related substitutions

  • Vapour-phase nitration (conc. HNO3, 400 °C): RH + HONO2 → RNO2 + H2O, a radical chain. C–C cleavage also occurs, so propane gives a mixture of four nitro compounds: 1-nitropropane, 2-nitropropane, nitroethane and nitromethane (Hass process, industrial). It is not a clean laboratory method.
  • Sulphochlorination (Reed reaction): RH + SO2 + Cl2, hν → RSO2Cl + HCl (radical chain: R• + SO2 → RSO2•; RSO2• + Cl2 → RSO2Cl + Cl•). Used in detergent manufacture (sodium alkane sulphonates).
  • Sulphonation with conc. H2SO4 does not occur with simple alkanes (they are inert to cold conc. acid); that inertness is why conc. H2SO4 is used to remove alkenes from alkane mixtures.

7.4 Pyrolysis (cracking), isomerisation, aromatisation

  • Pyrolysis/cracking (heat 700–1000 K, no catalyst; or zeolite): large alkane → smaller alkane + alkene. C10H22 → C5H12 + C5H10. Mechanism: C–C homolysis then β-scission of radicals. Gives ethene, propene for petrochemicals.
  • Isomerisation: n-butane → isobutane with anhyd. AlCl3/HCl, 300 K; n-pentane → isopentane; carbocation mechanism (hydride and methyl shifts). Used to raise octane number.
  • Aromatisation (hydroforming/reforming): n-hexane → benzene (Cr2O3/Al2O3 or Pt/Al2O3, 773 K, 10–20 atm); n-heptane → toluene + 4H2. Dehydrogenation, cyclisation, dehydrogenation.
