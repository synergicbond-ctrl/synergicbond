25. Replacement reactions (loss of N2)

The diazonium group is a super-leaving group (N2); it is replaced by nucleophiles or H. These reactions are the heart of the chapter's synthetic planning.

25.1 Sandmeyer and Gattermann

Sandmeyer: ArN2+X− + CuX → ArX + N2. The Cu(I) salt acts through single-electron transfer: Cu(I) transfers an electron to ArN2+ giving the aryl radical, N2 and Cu(II)X2; the aryl radical takes X from Cu(II)X2 and regenerates Cu(I). Because of this radical pathway aryl radicals can also couple to biaryls (by-products). The reaction needs Cu(I) with the same halide as the product.

CuCl/HCl⟶
Sandmeyer: p-toluenediazonium → p-chlorotoluene
  • Gattermann (modification): Cu powder with HX gives the same products with lower yield (the older method). Do not confuse with the Gattermann–Koch (CO/HCl/AlCl3/CuCl formylation of arenes) and Gattermann aldehyde synthesis (HCN/HCl).
  • Cyanide: ArN2+ + CuCN → ArCN; then H3O+ → ArCOOH: this adds one carbon.
25.2 Balz–Schiemann and iodination

Fluorine cannot be introduced via Cu(I) or direct halogenation, so: ArNH2 → NaNO2/HBF4 → ArN2+BF4− (precipitates) → dry and heat → ArF + N2 + BF3. Iodine is introduced simply by adding aqueous KI (iodide is itself a good electron donor, so no copper catalyst is needed).

25.3 Hydrolysis and deamination
  • Warm water: ArN2+ → ArOH + N2 + H+. In the presence of Cl− some chlorobenzene forms, so H2SO4 (HSO4− is non-nucleophilic) is used to give clean phenol; the dilute sulfuric acid is boiled.
  • Deamination: H3PO2 (or EtOH) reduces ArN2+ to Ar–H. The synthetic point: the NH2 group is used as a director (very strong o/p activator) and is removed afterwards: synthesis of 1,3,5-tribromobenzene, m-bromotoluene, 3,5-dibromotoluene, etc.
