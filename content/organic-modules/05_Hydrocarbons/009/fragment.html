8. Conformations of alkanes

Rotation about a C–C σ bond is not completely free; different spatial arrangements (conformations) have different energies. The two extremes for ethane are staggered (lowest energy) and eclipsed (highest energy). The energy cost of eclipsing is the torsional strain, ≈ 12 kJ mol-1 (2.9 kcal) in ethane, about 4 kJ per H/H eclipsing pair. The cause is the loss of stabilising σ C–H → σ* C–H hyperconjugation in the eclipsed form (with a small steric contribution).

Key idea Stability order for butane conformers: anti > gauche > partially eclipsed > fully eclipsed. The anti conformer predominates (about 70 % at 298 K, gauche ≈ 30 %, split between two enantiomeric gauche forms).
Trap "Gauche" is staggered, not eclipsed. Its extra energy (3.8 kJ) is steric (van der Waals repulsion of the two CH3 groups), whereas eclipsed forms have both torsional and steric strain. In molecules where an intramolecular H-bond exists (ethylene glycol, 2-fluoroethanol, 2-chloroethanol), the gauche conformer is more stable than anti. This is a standard JEE trap.
Example 12 Advanced

Population ratio

At 298 K, ΔG(gauche − anti) = 3.8 kJ mol-1. Estimate the ratio of gauche to anti populations. (R = 8.314 J mol-1K-1; there are two gauche forms.)

Show solution
  1. K = e−ΔG/RT = exp(−3800/(8.314 × 298)) = exp(−1.534) = 0.216 (per gauche conformer).
  2. Two enantiomeric gauche conformers: total gauche/anti = 2 × 0.216 = 0.43.
  3. Mole fractions: anti = 1/(1 + 0.43) = 70 %, gauche = 30 %.

8.2 Propane and higher alkanes

Propane: the eclipsed barrier is 14 kJ mol-1 (one H/CH3 eclipse of ≈ 6 kJ replaces one H/H of 4 kJ). Isobutane: 16 kJ. Highly branched alkanes (e.g. 2,2,3,3-tetramethylbutane) have large torsional barriers and, in the gauche forms, large steric strain.

Key idea Eclipsing strain energy values commonly used: H/H eclipse ≈ 4 kJ (1.0 kcal); H/CH3 ≈ 6 kJ (1.4 kcal); CH3/CH3 ≈ 11 kJ (2.6 kcal); gauche CH3/CH3 ≈ 3.8 kJ (0.9 kcal). Full eclipsed butane = 2 × 4 + 11 = 19 kJ: matches the table.
