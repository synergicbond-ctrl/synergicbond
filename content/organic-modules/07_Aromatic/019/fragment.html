17. Benzyne: elimination–addition

Aryl halides without activating groups react with a very strong base (NaNH2 or KNH2 in liquid NH3, ≈ 240 K; or LDA) by a completely different pathway, elimination–addition, through a short-lived neutral intermediate: benzyne (1,2-didehydrobenzene).

NaNH₂, NH₃(l), 240 K (–HCl)⟶ NH₃ (or NH₂⁻ then H⁺)⟶
Chlorobenzene → benzyne → aniline: the amide ion abstracts the ortho H, Cl⁻ leaves, then NH₂⁻ adds to either carbon of the "triple bond"
  • Benzyne structure. Two adjacent ring carbons each use an sp2 orbital in the plane (not part of the aromatic sextet) to form a very weak, strained 'triple' bond; the in-plane overlap of these two orbitals is poor, so the LUMO is low in energy and benzyne is extremely electrophilic. The aromatic π sextet is unaffected: benzyne remains aromatic. It is not isolable (observed at ≈ 10 K in matrices or by mass spectrometry).
  • Evidence (Roberts, 1953). Chlorobenzene labelled with 14C at C1 gives aniline in which the label is split 50 : 50 between C1 and C2: the amino group enters either end of the benzyne.
  • A required ortho hydrogen. Elimination needs an H ortho to the halogen: 2-chloro-1,3-dimethylbenzene (both ortho carbons carry CH3) does not react with NaNH2, and 2,6-disubstituted aryl halides give no benzyne (but, instead, very slow SNAr if activated).
  • Leaving-group order in benzyne formation (base-induced elimination): Br > I > Cl > F (a mixture of acidity of the ortho C–H and ability to leave).
Example 39 Adv

Cine substitution

Predict the products of NaNH2/NH3(l) on (a) p-chlorotoluene (b) m-bromoanisole (c) o-bromotoluene (d) 2-bromo-1,3-dimethylbenzene.

Show solution
  1. (a) p-Chlorotoluene: removing the ortho H gives 4-methylbenzyne (the triple bond between C1 and C2; the CH3 is para to C1, meta to C2). NH2− adds at either carbon: p-toluidine and m-toluidine, in about equal amounts (cine substitution gives the meta isomer).
  2. (b) m-Bromoanisole: H abstraction at C2 (between the two groups; the most acidic H because of the –I effect of OMe and Br) gives 3-methoxybenzyne. The nucleophile adds at C1 (meta to OMe), which puts the carbanion on C2, next to the electron-withdrawing OMe (stabilised by –I): the product is m-anisidine (3-methoxyaniline), essentially only.
  3. (c) o-Bromotoluene: the only ortho H is at C6 (C2 carries CH3), giving 3-methylbenzyne; NH2− adds at either end: a mixture of o-toluidine (direct substitution) and m-toluidine (cine), roughly 1 : 1.
  4. (d) 2-Bromo-1,3-dimethylbenzene has no ortho H: no benzyne, no reaction.

Substituted benzynes

Regiochemistry rule. In a 3-substituted benzyne the nucleophile adds so that the carbanion forms next to an electron-withdrawing (–I) substituent. This gives the meta product from 3-(OMe, F, CF3, Cl) benzynes. For a 4-substituted benzyne the two ends are nearly equivalent and about equal amounts of m- and p-products form (somewhat more meta for –I groups). Trapping benzyne. Benzyne is trapped by dienes (Diels–Alder) and by nucleophiles; the trapping proves its existence.

Mg, THF⟶ furan⟶
o-Bromofluorobenzene + Mg → benzyne (loss of MgBrF) → [4+2] with furan: 1,4-dihydro-1,4-epoxynaphthalene
+ Diels–Alder⟶
Benzyne + anthracene → triptycene

Methods to generate benzyne: (1) ArX + NaNH2/NH3; (2) o-fluorobromobenzene + Mg or Li (halogen–metal exchange then loss of MgBrF/LiF); (3) benzenediazonium-2-carboxylate (from anthranilic acid + HNO2) on gentle heating: loses N2 and CO2 (explosive when dry); (4) o-(trimethylsilyl)phenyl triflate + F− (Kobayashi, mild).
