Example 65 Adv

Chain 1: from iodoform to ozonolysis

A (C3H6O) gives a yellow precipitate with I2/NaOH and an orange precipitate with 2,4-DNP, but does not reduce Tollens' reagent. A + CH3MgBr, then H3O+ → B (C4H10O, does not oxidise with K2Cr2O7). B + conc. H2SO4, Δ → C (C4H8). C + O3, then Zn/H2O → D + E. Identify A–E.

Show solution
  1. A: positive 2,4-DNP = C=O; positive iodoform = CH3CO–; negative Tollens = ketone. C3H6O: A = propanone.
  2. B: propanone + CH3MgBr → tertiary alcohol (CH3)3C–OH (does not oxidise): B = 2-methylpropan-2-ol.
  3. C = dehydration of B: 2-methylpropene, (CH3)2C=CH2. D + E = ozonolysis fragments: propanone and methanal (HCHO).
CH₃MgBr; H₃O⁺⟶ H₂SO₄, Δ⟶ O₃; Zn/H₂O⟶ +
Chain 1 in structures (A → B → C → D + E)
Example 66 Adv

Chain 2: Cannizzaro, SOCl2, KCN

A (C7H6O) gives no Fehling test, gives a silver mirror, and with conc. NaOH gives B (C7H8O) and C (C7H6O2). B + SOCl2 → D (C7H7Cl); D + KCN → E (C8H7N); E + H3O+, Δ → F (C8H8O2). Identify all and give the carbon change from A to F.

Show solution
  1. A: aromatic aldehyde (Tollens positive, Fehling negative), C7H6O = benzaldehyde. Cannizzaro: B = benzyl alcohol, C = benzoic acid (after acidification).
  2. D = benzyl chloride (SNi/SN2 on SOCl2); E = phenylacetonitrile (benzyl cyanide, SN2 with CN−); F = phenylethanoic acid (PhCH2COOH).
  3. Carbon count: 7 (A) → 8 (F): one carbon added by CN−.
SOCl₂⟶ KCN⟶ H₃O⁺, Δ⟶
B → D → E → F
Example 67 Adv

Chain 3: Hofmann and nitrous acid

A (C4H9NO), on treatment with Br2/NaOH, gives B (C3H9N), which gives a foul-smelling compound with CHCl3/alc. KOH. B + NaNO2/HCl (273 K) gives C (C3H8O) with evolution of N2. A is unbranched. Identify A, B, C.

Show solution
  1. A: primary amide that on Hofmann gives an amine with one carbon less: C4H9NO unbranched = butanamide, CH3CH2CH2CONH2. B = propan-1-amine (the carbylamine test shows a primary amine). C = propan-1-ol (the answer expected from simple substitution of N2+ by OH; in practice the n-propyl cation partly rearranges by a 1,2-hydride shift, so propan-2-ol and propene are also formed).
Br₂, NaOH⟶ NaNO₂, HCl, 273 K⟶
A → B → C (loses CO then N2)
Example 68 Adv

Chain 4: oxidation, Beckmann, polymer

A (C6H12O) is oxidised by CrO3/H2SO4 to B (C6H10O). B + NH2OH → C (C6H11NO); C + conc. H2SO4, 390 K → D (C6H11NO, a cyclic amide); D, heated with water and an acid catalyst, polymerises. Give A, B, C, D and the polymer.

Show solution
  1. A = cyclohexanol (secondary alcohol, Jones gives ketone). B = cyclohexanone. C = cyclohexanone oxime. D = ε-caprolactam (Beckmann rearrangement). The ring-opening polymerisation of caprolactam gives nylon-6, [–NH(CH2)5CO–]n.
CrO₃, H₂SO₄⟶ NH₂OH⟶ H₂SO₄⟶
A → B → C → D
Example 69 Adv

Chain 5: acetophenone and the reagent bank

A (C8H8O) gives iodoform and does not give Tollens' or Fehling's. A + Zn–Hg/HCl → B; A + mCPBA → C; C + aq. NaOH, then H+ → D + E. D + CHCl3/NaOH, then H+ → F. Identify all.

Show solution
  1. A = acetophenone. B = ethylbenzene (Clemmensen). C = phenyl ethanoate (Baeyer–Villiger, phenyl migrates in preference to methyl). Hydrolysis: D = phenol and E = ethanoic acid. Reimer–Tiemann of phenol: F = salicylaldehyde.
mCPBA⟶ NaOH; H⁺⟶ CHCl₃, NaOH; H⁺⟶
A → C → D → F (the ethanoic acid E is the other hydrolysis product)
Example 70 Adv

Chain 6: the mass-balance clue

A hydrocarbon X (C9H12) is oxidised by air to Y. Y with dilute H2SO4 gives two products, Z1 (M = 94, gives violet colour with neutral FeCl3) and Z2 (M = 58, gives iodoform). Identify X, Y, Z1, Z2, and the industrial process.

Show solution
  1. M = 94 and FeCl3 violet: phenol (Z1). M = 58 with iodoform positive: propanone (Z2). C6H6O + C3H6O = C9H12O2: Y is cumene hydroperoxide, C6H5C(CH3)2OOH, from air oxidation of X = cumene (isopropylbenzene), C9H12. This is the cumene (Hock) process.
