19. Related carbon–carbon forming reactions of acid derivatives

19.1 Knoevenagel and Doebner condensation

An aldehyde (or ketone) with an active methylene compound in the presence of a weak amine base (piperidine, pyridine) gives the α,β-unsaturated compound after elimination of water. In the Doebner modification the active methylene compound is malonic acid and pyridine is the solvent: condensation is followed by decarboxylation, giving the α,β-unsaturated monocarboxylic acid (cinnamic acid from benzaldehyde).

+ pyridine, piperidine, Δ (− H₂O, − CO₂)⟶
Doebner: benzaldehyde + malonic acid → trans-cinnamic acid
+ piperidine⟶
Knoevenagel with malononitrile

19.2 Reformatsky reaction

α-Halo ester + Zn (an organozinc enolate, not basic enough to attack the ester) + aldehyde or ketone, then H3O+ → β-hydroxy ester (which dehydrates to the α,β-unsaturated ester on acid treatment).

+ 1. Zn, THF 2. H₃O⁺⟶
Reformatsky: acetone + ethyl bromoacetate → ethyl 3-hydroxy-3-methylbutanoate

19.3 Darzens condensation

Ketone/aldehyde + α-chloro ester + NaOEt → glycidic ester (α,β-epoxy ester), which is saponified and decarboxylated to give an aldehyde or ketone with one more carbon.

+ NaOEt⟶
Darzens: benzaldehyde + ethyl chloroacetate → ethyl 3-phenylglycidate

19.4 Michael additions of active methylene compounds

The enolate of malonic ester, EAA or nitroalkanes adds in a conjugate (1,4) fashion to α,β-unsaturated ketones, esters or nitriles (Michael acceptors). Followed by hydrolysis and decarboxylation this gives 1,5-dicarbonyl type products (acids/ketones with the Michael acceptor’s β-carbon bonded to the CH2).

+ NaOEt (cat.)⟶
Malonate + methyl vinyl ketone: 1,4-addition (Michael)
Shortcut How to choose among these: (1) aldehyde + active CH2, amine base → Knoevenagel; (2) ArCHO + anhydride + carboxylate → Perkin; (3) α-haloester + Zn + C=O → Reformatsky (β-hydroxy ester); (4) α-chloro ester + base + C=O → Darzens (epoxy ester); (5) enolate + enone → Michael.
