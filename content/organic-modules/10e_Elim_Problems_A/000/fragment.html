Further problems: E1, E2 and E1cB (part A)

This set extends the notes with problems that train the habits needed for elimination questions: decide first which mechanism the conditions point to (base strength, solvent, temperature, substrate class), then ask which hydrogens are really available, and finally check the three-dimensional requirement. Many items turn on a single stereoelectronic fact, namely that a concerted elimination wants the departing hydrogen and the leaving group in one plane, usually 180° apart.

Structures that depend on three-dimensional arrangement are drawn with wedge bonds, and the solutions describe the relevant Newman projection or chair in words. Worked examples carry the longer, step-by-step solutions; the remaining items are graded from L1 (recall and direct application) to L3 (multi-step reasoning). Some multiple-choice items have more than one correct option; where the stem asks which statements are correct, select every one that applies.
