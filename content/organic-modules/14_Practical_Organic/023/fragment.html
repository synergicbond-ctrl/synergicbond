7.3 Test for sulphur

Two independent tests on separate portions of the SFE:

  • Lead acetate test. Acidify a portion with acetic acid (not strong acid, which would dissolve some of the sulphide as H2S gas) and add lead acetate: a black precipitate of PbS appears. S2− + (CH3COO)2Pb → PbS↓(black) + 2CH3COO−
  • Sodium nitroprusside test. Add a few drops of freshly prepared sodium nitroprusside Na2[Fe(CN)5NO] to the alkaline extract: a violet (purple) colour appears because of the complex ion [Fe(CN)5NOS]4−. S2− + [Fe(CN)5NO]2− → [Fe(CN)5NOS]4−

The colour fades on standing. Nitroprusside is the more characteristic test; the black PbS test is the simplest.

Exam Trap If the extract was acidified with HNO3 (e.g. in the halogen test) the S2− has been lost as H2S, so the sulphur tests must be done on fresh extract that has not been boiled with HNO3.

Alternative (Carius type) test: oxidise the compound with Na2O2 (or fuming HNO3) to sulphate, acidify with HCl, add BaCl2: white BaSO4.
