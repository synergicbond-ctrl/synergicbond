22. Proton NMR (¹H NMR) spectroscopy

Principle. A nucleus with spin ½ (¹H, ¹³C) in a magnetic field aligns with or against it; radio-frequency radiation of the right frequency flips it (resonance). The exact frequency depends on the electron density around the nucleus: circulating electrons create a small opposing field, shielding the nucleus; a nucleus with less electron density is deshielded and resonates at a higher frequency (downfield, larger δ).

Formula δ (ppm) = [(νsample − νTMS)/νoperating] × 106 TMS = (CH3)4Si, δ = 0

TMS is the standard because (i) its 12 equivalent protons give a single sharp signal, (ii) Si is electropositive so the H's are strongly shielded and the signal lies upfield of nearly all organic protons, (iii) it is chemically inert and volatile (b.p. 300 K) so it is easily removed. Four pieces of information are read from a ¹H NMR spectrum:

  • Number of signals = number of sets of chemically equivalent protons (protons interchanged by symmetry).
  • Chemical shift δ = the electronic environment (functional-group information).
  • Integration (relative area) = ratio of the number of protons in each set.
  • Splitting (multiplicity) = the number of neighbouring protons (n + 1 rule).
