26. The Cannizzaro reaction

26.1 Statement and scope Aldehydes without α-hydrogen (HCHO, benzaldehyde and substituted benzaldehydes, 2,2-dimethylpropanal, furfural, glyoxylic acid) cannot form an enolate, so concentrated alkali does not trigger an aldol reaction. Instead, two molecules of the aldehyde disproportionate: one is oxidised to the carboxylate, the other reduced to the primary alcohol.

+ 50 % NaOH, then H₃O⁺⟶ +
Cannizzaro: 2 PhCHO → PhCH₂OH + PhCOOH (as PhCOO⁻ before acidification)
+ conc. NaOH⟶ +
2 HCHO → CH₃OH + HCOO⁻
+ conc. NaOH⟶ +
Pivaldehyde also has no α-H

The maximum yield of each product is 50 % with respect to the aldehyde. 26.2 Mechanism

  • Step 1: OH− adds to the C=O of one aldehyde molecule: ArCH(O−)OH, the hydrate anion.
  • Step 2: in strong base this loses a second proton: ArCH(O−)2, a dianion (this dianion form is a much better hydride donor).
  • Step 3: the dianion transfers hydride (H−) from carbon to the carbonyl carbon of a second aldehyde molecule (rate-determining): ArCOO− (after loss of the hydride) and the alkoxide ArCH2O−.
  • Step 4: the alkoxide takes H+ from water: ArCH2OH.
OH⁻ (addition)⟶ − H⁺ (OH⁻)⟶ H⁻ transfer to second PhCHO⟶ +
Steps 1-3: the dianion is the hydride donor; the second molecule of aldehyde is the acceptor

The kinetics are second order in aldehyde, and first order in hydroxide at moderate base concentration and second order in hydroxide at high base concentration (the dianion pathway).

Key idea Evidence for hydride transfer: when the reaction is run in D2O, the benzyl alcohol formed contains no deuterium on carbon (PhCH2OH, not PhCHDOH); the hydrogen on the CH2 came from the C–H of another aldehyde molecule, not from the solvent. In contrast, an enolisation mechanism would incorporate D from the solvent.
26.3 Crossed Cannizzaro
Two different non-enolisable aldehydes give four products unless one is much more reactive. HCHO is both the best acceptor (most electrophilic) and, once converted to its hydrate dianion, the best hydride donor; but in a mixture with ArCHO the HCHO hydrate forms first and gives hydride to the ArCHO: HCHO is oxidised to formate; ArCHO is reduced to the alcohol.
+ conc. NaOH⟶ +
Crossed Cannizzaro: benzyl alcohol and formate, in high yield (all the aromatic aldehyde is converted to alcohol)
Shortcut Crossed Cannizzaro with HCHO is the preferred way to reduce an aromatic aldehyde (with no waste of half as the acid) and also the last step of the pentaerythritol synthesis (Section 25.6).
26.4 Intramolecular Cannizzaro
Dialdehydes and keto-aldehydes without α-H undergo an intramolecular version in which one carbonyl is oxidised and the other reduced, giving a hydroxy-acid.

26.5 Conditions and traps

Trap Aldehydes with α-hydrogen (ethanal, propanal, phenylacetaldehyde, isobutyraldehyde) do not show the Cannizzaro reaction in alkali: enolate formation (aldol) is much faster. Aldehydes with α-H would undergo Cannizzaro only if the α-H could not be removed (bridgehead).
Trap Dilute NaOH does not trigger Cannizzaro; concentrated (about 40–50 %) NaOH is needed because the dianion must form. Dilute NaOH with benzaldehyde and acetone is Claisen–Schmidt, not Cannizzaro.
Trap Cannizzaro reduces one molecule and oxidises another: the product ratio is 1 : 1. A question may ask for the "percentage of benzaldehyde converted to alcohol": 50 %.
Substituent effects. Electron-withdrawing groups speed up the addition of OH− (step 1) and the acceptance of hydride, so p-nitrobenzaldehyde reacts faster than benzaldehyde; strongly donating groups (p-NMe2, p-OH as phenoxide) retard or stop the reaction because the aldehyde is less electrophilic (and p-hydroxybenzaldehyde is converted to a phenoxide that deactivates the carbonyl).
26.6 Tishchenko reaction
Aldehydes with an aluminium alkoxide (Al(OEt)3) combine to give the ester: 2 RCHO → RCOOCH2R. The mechanism is also a hydride transfer, but a hemiacetal alkoxide is the donor.
+ Al(OEt)₃⟶
Tishchenko: 2 CH₃CHO → ethyl ethanoate (industrial)

26.7 Benzilic acid rearrangement Related to Cannizzaro in outcome (a 1,2-dicarbonyl converted into an α-hydroxy acid): benzil (PhCOCOPh) on heating with KOH undergoes a 1,2-phenyl shift. OH− adds to one C=O; the adjacent carbon accepts a migrating aryl group; the product is potassium benzilate; acidification gives benzilic acid (diphenylhydroxyacetic acid).

Example 31 Main

Cannizzaro or aldol?

State whether each aldehyde gives (i) Cannizzaro, (ii) aldol, (iii) neither in the stated conditions: (a) PhCHO + conc. NaOH; (b) CH3CH2CHO + dilute NaOH; (c) HCHO + conc. NaOH; (d) (CH3)2CHCHO + conc. NaOH; (e) PhCHO + CH3CHO + dilute NaOH; (f) 2,2-dimethylpropanal + dilute NaOH.

Show solution
  1. (a) Cannizzaro (no α-H, strong base). (b) Aldol (has α-H). (c) Cannizzaro (HCHO → CH3OH + HCOO−). (d) 2-Methylpropanal has one α-H, so enolate formation (aldol) outcompetes Cannizzaro: aldol addition. The product, 3-hydroxy-2,2,4-trimethylpentanal, cannot dehydrate because C-2 has no H. (e) Crossed aldol (Claisen–Schmidt): cinnamaldehyde. (f) Dilute base: neither (no α-H for aldol; Cannizzaro needs concentrated alkali), the aldehyde is largely unreacted.
Example 32 Adv

Products and mass

What is obtained when 5.3 g of benzaldehyde (M = 106) undergoes complete Cannizzaro reaction? Give the masses of benzyl alcohol (M = 108) and benzoic acid (M = 122) after acidification.

Show solution
  1. n(PhCHO) = 5.3/106 = 0.050 mol. Half is oxidised, half reduced. n(PhCH2OH) = n(PhCOOH) = 0.025 mol.
  2. Mass of benzyl alcohol = 0.025 × 108 = 2.70 g. Mass of benzoic acid = 0.025 × 122 = 3.05 g.
Example 33 Adv

Crossed Cannizzaro: predict

Predict the products of (a) p-nitrobenzaldehyde + HCHO, conc. NaOH; (b) PhCHO + p-O2NC6H4CHO (equimolar).

Show solution
  1. (a) 4-Nitrobenzyl alcohol + HCOO−. (b) A mixture of up to four products (two alcohols, two acids): neither partner is a much better hydride donor, so the crossed reaction is not selective. A clean crossed Cannizzaro needs HCHO as one partner.
Example 34 Adv

Intramolecular

Terephthalaldehyde (benzene-1,4-dicarbaldehyde) is treated with conc. NaOH. Does it undergo an intramolecular Cannizzaro like phthalaldehyde? What is the product?

Show solution
  1. The two CHO groups are para and cannot reach each other, so hydride transfer is intermolecular: one CHO is oxidised (to COO−) and a CHO of another molecule is reduced (to CH2OH). The isolated product after acidification is 4-(hydroxymethyl)benzoic acid.
