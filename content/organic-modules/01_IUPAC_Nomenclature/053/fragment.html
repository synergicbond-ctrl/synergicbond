6.6 Ring substituent groups

  • Cycloalkyl: cyclohexyl; with attachment as C-1 and further locants: 2-methylcyclohexyl; with a double bond: cyclohex-2-en-1-yl.
  • Phenyl (C₆H₅–), substituted: 4-chlorophenyl; naphthalen-1-yl (1-naphthyl); pyridin-2-yl; furan-2-yl.
  • Benzyl is C₆H₅CH₂–; tolyl is CH₃C₆H₄– (o-, m-, p-); aryl is the generic term for any aromatic substituent.
Example 44 Standard

Name the compound.

Show solution
  1. Benzene ring bearing two groups: chlorine and a 1-chloroethyl group (the chlorine is on the carbon attached to the ring).
  2. Locants {1,4}: the lower locant is given to the substituent cited first in alphabetical order: chloro (c) vs (1-chloroethyl) (c…), compare letter by letter: "chloro" < "chloroethyl", so chloro gets 1.

1-Chloro-4-(1-chloroethyl)benzene.

Think Further

How many rings does a compound named tricyclo[...]alkane have, and how can the number of rings be found from a structure?

Show solution

Three. The number of rings equals the number of bonds that must be cut to turn the skeleton into an acyclic chain; the prefix bi-, tri-, tetra- in the von Baeyer name states that number. Equivalently, rings = bonds – atoms + 1 for the carbon skeleton.
