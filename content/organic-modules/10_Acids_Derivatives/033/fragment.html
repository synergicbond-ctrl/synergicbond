Practice 348 L2 · Pharmacology

Benzoic acid has pKa 4.20. Calculate the ratio [benzoate]/[benzoic acid] in blood (pH 7.4) and in the stomach (pH 2.0). Which form crosses a lipid membrane more easily?

Show answer
  1. pH − pKa = log([A−]/[HA]). Blood: 7.4 − 4.20 = 3.20 → ratio = 103.2 = 1.6 × 103 (almost entirely benzoate anion). Stomach: 2.0 − 4.20 = −2.20 → ratio = 10−2.2 = 6.3 × 10−3 (almost entirely un-ionised acid).
  2. The neutral acid HA crosses the membrane more easily, so absorption of acids is favoured in the stomach.
Practice 349 L2 · Pharmacology

Aspirin has pKa 3.5. What percentage is ionised at pH 2.0?

Show answer
  1. Fraction ionised = 1/(1 + 10pKa − pH) = 1/(1 + 101.5) = 1/(1 + 31.6) = 0.0307: 3.1 % ionised.
Practice 350 L2 · Vinegar

A commercial vinegar contains 5.0 % w/v acetic acid. Calculate (a) its molarity, (b) its pH (Ka = 1.8 × 10−5), (c) the volume of 0.500 M NaOH needed to neutralise 10.0 mL.

Show answer
  1. (a) 50.0 g/L ÷ 60.05 = 0.833 M. (b) [H+] = √(1.8 × 10−5 × 0.833) = 3.87 × 10−3; pH = 2.41. (c) 10.0 mL × 0.833 M = 8.33 mmol → 8.33/0.500 = 16.7 mL.

Practice 352 L2 · Combustion

A compound (0.300 g) on combustion gave 0.440 g CO2 and 0.180 g H2O. Its molar mass is 60. Find the molecular formula and give two isomers that can be distinguished by NaHCO3.

Show answer
  1. n(C) = 0.440/44 = 0.0100 mol (0.120 g C). n(H) = 2 × 0.180/18 = 0.0200 mol (0.0202 g H). O = 0.300 − 0.120 − 0.020 = 0.160 g = 0.0100 mol. Empirical formula CH2O (M = 30); molecular formula C2H4O2 (M = 60).
  2. Acetic acid evolves CO2 with NaHCO3; its isomer methyl methanoate does not. (HOCH2CHO is a third isomer.)
Practice 353 L2 · Zerewitinoff

0.192 g of citric acid (M = 192) is treated with excess CH3MgI. What volume of CH4 (STP) is liberated, and how many active hydrogen atoms does this correspond to?

Show answer
  1. 0.192/192 = 1.00 mmol. Citric acid has three COOH and one tertiary OH: four active H, 4.00 mmol CH4 = 4.00 × 22.4 = 89.6 mL.
Practice 354 L2 · Soap

Calculate (a) the mass of NaOH needed to saponify 100 g of tristearin (M = 891.5), (b) the mass of sodium stearate (M = 306.5) formed, (c) the mass of glycerol (M = 92.1).

Show answer
  1. 100/891.5 = 0.1122 mol tristearin. (a) NaOH: 3 × 0.1122 = 0.3365 mol × 40.0 = 13.5 g. (b) Sodium stearate: 0.3365 × 306.5 = 103 g. (c) Glycerol: 0.1122 × 92.1 = 10.3 g. Mass balance: 100 + 13.5 = 113.5 g = 103.1 + 10.3.

Practice 357 L2 · Equilibrium

1.0 mol of ethyl acetate and 10 mol of water react (acid catalyst) to equilibrium; Kc for esterification is 4. What fraction of the ester is hydrolysed?

Show answer
  1. Hydrolysis constant K′ = 1/4. Ester → acid + alcohol: x²/[(1 − x)(10 − x)] = 0.25 → 0.75x² + 2.75x − 2.5 = 0 → x = 0.754.
  2. 75 % hydrolysed (excess water drives hydrolysis, just as excess alcohol drives esterification).
Practice 358 L2 · Salt hydrolysis

Calculate the pH of 1.0 M sodium formate (pKa of formic acid = 3.75).

Show answer
  1. Formate is the conjugate base: pKb = 14 − 3.75 = 10.25. pOH = ½(pKb − log c) = ½(10.25 − 0) = 5.125; pH = 14 − 5.125 = 8.88.
Practice 359 L2 · Drug structure

Ibuprofen is (RS)-2-(4-isobutylphenyl)propanoic acid. Give its formula and molar mass; why is it sold as a racemate although only the (S)-form is active?

Show answer
  1. C13H18O2, M = 206.3 g mol−1. The body contains an isomerase that converts the inactive (R)-enantiomer into the active (S)-form, so resolution is unnecessary.
Practice 360 L2 · LiAlH4 stoichiometry

How many moles of LiAlH4 are needed (minimum) to reduce 1 mol of ethyl benzoate to benzyl alcohol and ethanol? And 1 mol of acetophenone?

Show answer
  1. Each LiAlH4 supplies four hydrides. An ester needs two hydride equivalents (two successive additions): 0.5 mol LiAlH4. A ketone needs one: 0.25 mol LiAlH4. (Practical reductions use an excess.)
Practice 361 L2 · IR

A compound C4H6O3 has strong IR bands at 1820 and 1750 cm−1 and no O–H band. It reacts with water to give a single acid (M = 60). Identify it.

Show answer
  1. Two C=O bands near 1820 and 1760 are the signature of an acid anhydride; hydrolysis gives two molecules of one acid of M = 60: ethanoic acid. The compound is ethanoic anhydride, (CH3CO)2O, C4H6O3.
Practice 362 L2 · IR

A liquid C3H5N has an IR band at 2250 cm−1 and no C=O band, and gives propanoic acid on boiling with dilute acid. Identify it.

Show answer
  1. C≡N stretch at 2250 cm−1 → a nitrile; hydrolysis to propanoic acid shows that the chain has three carbons including the nitrile carbon: propanenitrile, CH3CH2CN.
Practice 363 L2 · NMR

An ester shows two signals only, both singlets in the ratio 3 : 3: δ 3.7 and δ 2.0. Identify it, and one isomer with three signals.

Show answer
  1. OCH3 (3.7) and CH3C=O (2.0): methyl ethanoate. Isomers: ethyl methanoate shows a triplet, quartet and a formyl singlet (three signals); propanoic acid shows a triplet, a quartet and a broad COOH.
Practice 364 L2 · Order

Arrange in increasing ease of decarboxylation on heating: benzoic acid, malonic acid, acetoacetic acid, trichloroacetic acid.

Show answer
  1. Benzoic acid requires soda lime at high temperature (hardest). Malonic acid decarboxylates at about 140 °C through the cyclic TS. Trichloroacetic acid loses CO2 at about 100 °C in water. Acetoacetic acid loses CO2 at room temperature or on very gentle warming (β-keto acid, best cyclic TS and an enol product).
  2. benzoic < malonic < trichloroacetic < acetoacetic (approximate).

A single organic reagent that converts propanoic acid into propanoyl chloride AND ethyl propanoate into propanoyl chloride + ethyl chloride is

Show answer

Practice 372 L2 · Conceptual

Why is the hydrolysis of an amide in dilute acid effectively irreversible, whereas the acid hydrolysis of an ester is reversible?

Show answer
  1. The amine product is protonated under the acidic conditions (RNH3+, pKaH ≈ 10), so it is removed from the equilibrium and cannot attack the acid; the alcohol from an ester has no such sink and recombines with the carboxylic acid.
Practice 373 L2 · Conceptual

Why does the conjugate-base stability explain the order of acid chloride > anhydride > ester > amide reactivity, even though amide N is a better π donor than ester O?

Show answer
  1. Both the leaving group ability and the resonance stabilisation of the carbonyl point the same way. Better donors (N > O > Cl) stabilise the ground state and make the carbonyl less electrophilic, and a poor leaving group (NH2−) is expelled with difficulty. The poorest donor and best leaving group (Cl) gives the most reactive derivative.
Practice 374 L2 · Conceptual

Which acid is formed when cyclopentanone is oxidised vigorously with HNO3, and what is its further behaviour on heating with acetic anhydride?

Show answer
  1. Cyclopentanone gives glutaric acid, HOOC(CH2)3COOH (ring cleavage at the C=O–CH2 bond); with acetic anhydride (or on heating) it forms the six-membered glutaric anhydride.
Practice 375 L2 · Conceptual

Why does ethyl acetoacetate give a violet colour with FeCl3 but diethyl malonate does not?

Show answer
  1. Ethyl acetoacetate has a significant enol fraction (≈ 8 %), the enol being stabilised by conjugation and an intramolecular H-bond; Fe3+ complexes the enolic OH. Diethyl malonate is almost entirely in the keto form (enol content ≈ 10−4 %), so there is no enol to complex.
Practice 376 L2 · Conceptual

Explain why Cl3CCOOH decarboxylates easily on warming but CH3COOH does not.

Show answer
  1. Decarboxylation of the carboxylate leaves a carbanion. Three electronegative Cl atoms stabilise CCl3− (by induction, and by negative hyperconjugation into σ* C–Cl), whereas CH3− is extremely unstable. The CCl3− then takes a proton to give CHCl3.
Practice 377 L2 · Conceptual

Why is NaOEt used in the Claisen condensation of ethyl acetate rather than the stronger base NaNH2 or NaH?

Show answer
  1. NaNH2 or NaH would work to make the enolate (LDA, NaH are used for difficult cases) but also attack the ester (NH2− as a nucleophile gives amide); NaOEt is conveniently matched to the ester (no transesterification), cheap, and its basicity is just sufficient because the final deprotonation drives the sequence.
Practice 378 L2 · Conceptual

Why can a cyclic anhydride such as succinic anhydride be used to acylate an arene by Friedel–Crafts reaction but give only one acyl group on the ring?

Show answer
  1. AlCl3 opens the anhydride to the acylium–carboxylate; the aryl ketone product is deactivated (like all Friedel–Crafts acylation products) and the carboxylate stays attached to the chain as a –COOH after work-up, so the product is the 4-aryl-4-oxobutanoic acid with a single acyl group on the ring.
Practice 379 L2 · Conceptual

A carboxylic acid reacts with aqueous NaHCO3 but does not dissolve in water. Explain how the acid is separated from neutral organic impurities.

Show answer
  1. Shake the mixture in ether with aqueous NaHCO3: the acid is converted into its water-soluble sodium salt and moves to the aqueous layer; neutral impurities stay in the ether. Separate the layers, acidify the aqueous layer with HCl and filter or extract the regenerated acid.
