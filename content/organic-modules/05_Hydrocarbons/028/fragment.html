27. Other reactions of alkenes

27.1 Polymerisation

Alkenes polymerise by addition (chain growth): radical, cationic, anionic, or coordination (Ziegler–Natta).

Radical mechanism: initiation (R• from peroxide); propagation: R–CH2–C•H2 + CH2=CH2 → R–(CH2)3–C•H2...; termination by coupling or disproportionation. The radical adds to the less substituted carbon so that the more stable radical forms (head-to-tail polymer).

27.2 Hydroformylation (oxo process) and Wacker oxidation

  • Hydroformylation: RCH=CH2 + CO + H2, Co2(CO)8 (or Rh), 100–150 °C, 100 atm → RCH2CH2CHO (linear, major) + RCH(CH3)CHO (branched). Gives aldehydes with one more carbon.
  • Wacker process: CH2=CH2 + ½ O2 → CH3CHO (PdCl2/CuCl2, H2O). Higher terminal alkenes give methyl ketones.

27.3 Alkenes as nucleophiles in Friedel–Crafts type reactions

Alkene + H+ (H2SO4/HF) → carbocation, which alkylates an arene: benzene + propene, AlCl3/HCl or H3PO4 → cumene (isopropylbenzene), the first step of the cumene process to phenol and acetone.

27.4 Dimerisation and the "isooctane" route

2-Methylpropene + 2-methylpropene (H2SO4, 60 %) → 2,4,4-trimethylpent-1-ene + 2,4,4-trimethylpent-2-ene; hydrogenation gives 2,2,4-trimethylpentane (iso-octane), the 100-octane standard.
