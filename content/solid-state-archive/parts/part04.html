2. Ionic and Molecular Solids

◆Concept

Ionic solids are held together by long-range Coulombic forces. Their brittleness follows from the fact that a sufficiently large displacement can bring like charges adjacent, producing strong repulsion and fracture.

Molecular solids contain discrete molecules. Their properties depend on intermolecular forces rather than an extended ionic or covalent framework.

3. Metallic and Covalent/Network Solids

◆Concept

Metallic solids possess delocalized valence electrons, allowing electronic conduction and non-directional bonding that supports plastic deformation.

Network solids contain an extended covalent framework. Diamond and SiC are rigid three-dimensional networks. Graphite is a key exception to the simple “covalent solid = insulator” rule because electrons are delocalized within its layers.

4. Quick Visual

⚠JEE Advanced traps & edge cases

  • Graphite is covalent/network but conducts along its layers.
  • Ionic solids conduct by mobile ions only when ions can migrate; a rigid perfect ionic lattice is not an ordinary electronic conductor.
  • Do not classify only by melting point; identify constituent particles and bonding.

▶Solved examples

Worked example 1

Example 1 — Classification

Classify K₂SO₄, solid CO₂, Cu and SiC.

  1. K₂SO₄ contains ions → ionic.
  2. CO₂ contains discrete non-polar molecules → molecular non-polar.
  3. Cu → metallic.
  4. SiC → covalent/network.

Ionic; molecular; metallic; covalent/network.

Worked example 2

Example 2 — Multi-correct

Which may conduct electricity in the solid state? (A) Cu (B) graphite (C) NaCl crystal (D) diamond

  1. Cu has mobile electrons.
  2. Graphite has delocalized electrons within layers.
  3. NaCl crystal lacks mobile charge carriers under ordinary conditions.
  4. Diamond has localized σ bonds.

Correct options: A and B.
