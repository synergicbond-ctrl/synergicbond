16. Conformational analysis of open-chain molecules

16.1 Conformations and conformers

Rotation about a σ-bond changes the conformation of a molecule without breaking any bond. The infinitely many arrangements are called conformations; those at energy minima are conformers (rotamers). The barrier between conformers of simple alkanes is small (about 12 kJ mol-1 for ethane), so they interconvert millions of times per second at room temperature and cannot be separated. We describe the arrangement by the dihedral (torsion) angle θ between two bonds on adjacent atoms.

Key idea Conformers are not configurational isomers: they interconvert by rotation alone. Configurational isomers (enantiomers, diastereomers, cis/trans) need bond breaking.

16.2 Ethane

Looking down the C–C bond (Newman projection) we see two sets of three C–H bonds. Staggered (θ = 60°): bonds as far apart as possible, the lowest energy. Eclipsed (θ = 0°): bonds directly behind each other, the highest energy. The energy difference is about 12 kJ mol-1 (2.9 kcal mol-1). Three eclipsing H/H interactions of about 4 kJ mol-1 each account for it. The extra energy is called torsional strain. (Its physical origin is still discussed: the staggered form gains hyperconjugative σ C–H → σ* C–H stabilisation, and the eclipsed form has more Pauli repulsion; for JEE it is enough to say "repulsion between eclipsed bonds".)

Propane: barrier about 14 kJ mol-1 (3.3 kcal): the CH3/H eclipsing costs about 6 kJ mol-1, so H/H + H/H + CH3/H = 4 + 4 + 6 = 14.

16.3 Butane: the textbook case

Rotation about the C-2–C-3 bond of butane gives four kinds of arrangement:

  • Anti (θ = 180°): the two methyl groups opposite each other. The most stable: 0 kJ mol-1.
  • Gauche (θ = 60° and 300°): methyls 60° apart, staggered: about 3.8 kJ mol-1 above anti (steric repulsion between the methyl groups, "gauche butane interaction"). There are two gauche conformers, which are enantiomeric (mirror images, rapidly interconverting).
  • Partially eclipsed (θ = 120° and 240°): CH3 eclipses H, H eclipses CH3, H eclipses H: 6 + 6 + 4 = about 16 kJ mol-1.
  • Totally (fully) eclipsed (θ = 0°): CH3 eclipses CH3 and two H/H: 11 + 4 + 4 = about 19 kJ mol-1. The highest energy.

Example 50 Adv

Populations of butane conformers

Estimate the percentage of gauche conformers of butane at 298 K, taking the gauche energy as 3.8 kJ mol-1 (two gauche conformers).

Show solution
  1. Gauche/anti = 2 × exp(−3.8/2.478) = 2 × 0.216 = 0.432.
  2. Fraction gauche = 0.432/(1 + 0.432) = 30 %; anti = 70 %.
Trap Only the minima are conformers (anti and the two gauche). Eclipsed arrangements are transition states for rotation, not isolable conformers. "Which butane conformer is the most stable?" → anti. "Which has the highest energy?" → fully eclipsed. The gauche conformer has the second lowest energy.

16.4 When gauche wins: intramolecular H-bonding and the gauche effect

Anti is the default winner for large groups, but it can lose:

  • Intramolecular hydrogen bond: ethane-1,2-diol (ethylene glycol), 2-chloroethanol, 2-aminoethanol and the lactic-acid type compounds prefer the gauche conformation, because O–H···O (or O–H···Cl, N) hydrogen bonding stabilises it. This is a standard reason in JEE questions.
  • The gauche effect: 1,2-difluoroethane is more stable gauche than anti by about 2–3 kJ mol-1 (hyperconjugation σC–H → σ*C–F), even though fluorines are electronegative and bulky enough in principle. 1,2-Dichloroethane and 1,2-dibromoethane are mostly anti in the gas phase and non-polar solvents.
  • Dipole effects: in polar solvents polar conformers (gauche X–C–C–X) gain stability. The dipole moment of 1,2-dichloroethane rises with temperature and polarity of solvent because the polar gauche population rises.

16.6 Chirality of conformers; interconversion of Newman, sawhorse, Fischer

  • A molecule that is chiral in a fixed conformation is not necessarily chiral as a substance: the gauche forms of butane are chiral conformers, but they interconvert rapidly and butane is achiral (the average has a mirror plane). The compound is judged in its most symmetric accessible conformation, which for butane is anti (centre of symmetry).
  • Meso-tartaric acid is achiral because in the anti conformation it has a centre of symmetry (and in the eclipsed one a mirror plane); (R,R)-tartaric acid has only a C2 axis in every conformation.
  • For Newman → Fischer conversions, bring the Newman projection to the eclipsed conformation first (see the section on projections).

16.7 Substituted ethanes and the Newman-projection exam routine

  • 1. Draw the Newman projection (front carbon: three groups; rear: three groups).
  • 2. Decide the dihedral angle between the two groups of interest (0°, 60°, 120°, 180°).
  • 3. Rank the conformers: anti is best unless H-bonding or a gauche effect applies; eclipsed forms are maxima.
  • 4. For rotamers of 2-substituted systems, count gauche interactions.
Example 53 Adv

Gauche interactions

2-Methylbutane is viewed along the C-2–C-3 bond. Front carbon C-2: CH3, CH3, H. Rear carbon C-3: CH3, H, H. How many CH3/CH3 gauche interactions does the best staggered conformer have, and what is the energy gap to the worst staggered one?

rear CH₃ anti to H of C-2 (2 gauche)
rear CH₃ anti to one CH₃ (1 gauche)
Show solution
  1. In a staggered conformer the rear CH3 is anti to one front group and gauche (60°) to the other two.
  2. If it is anti to the front H, it is gauche to both front methyls: 2 gauche CH3/CH3 interactions (about 7.6 kJ mol-1).
  3. If it is anti to one front CH3, it is gauche to the other CH3 and to H: 1 gauche CH3/CH3 interaction (3.8 kJ mol-1).
  4. The best conformer has 1; the gap to the worst staggered conformer is about 3.8 kJ mol-1.

16.8 Summary sheet: acyclic conformations

  • Ethane: staggered 0, eclipsed +12 kJ/mol (3 H/H eclipsings of 4). Propane barrier 14.
  • Butane: anti 0 < gauche 3.8 < partly eclipsed 16 < fully eclipsed 19 (kJ/mol); minima are anti and two (enantiomeric) gauche.
  • Gauche is favoured by intramolecular H-bonding (ethylene glycol, 2-chloroethanol) and by the gauche effect (1,2-difluoroethane).
  • Conformers interconvert by rotation; the molecule's chirality is judged in its most symmetric conformation.
  • At 298 K RT = 2.48 kJ/mol; butane is about 70 % anti, 30 % gauche.
