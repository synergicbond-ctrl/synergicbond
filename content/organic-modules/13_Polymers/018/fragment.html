14. Polyesters

14.1 Terylene (Dacron, PET)

terephthalic acid ethylene glycol (ethane-1,2-diol) PET repeat unit
Condensation at 420–460 K with Zn(OAc)₂ / Sb₂O₃ catalyst; water (or methanol if dimethyl terephthalate is used) distils out.

Industrially the first stage is transesterification of dimethyl terephthalate with ethylene glycol (Zn or Mn acetate), then polycondensation near 550 K under vacuum with Sb2O3, removing the excess glycol. The mechanism is the acid-catalysed (Fischer-type) or metal-activated acyl substitution: the alcohol O lone pair attacks the C=O π*, a tetrahedral intermediate forms, then –OH2+ (or –OCH3) leaves. Ester links are less polar and have no N–H, so PET depends on the stacked benzene rings and dipoles (no hydrogen bonding) for its strength; m.p. ≈ 260 °C. Uses: terylene fabric (blends with cotton), sails, tyre cord, seat belts, magnetic tapes, PET bottles (soft-drink, oriented, high clarity), films (Mylar).

14.2 Glyptal, alkyd resins

Glyptal: ethylene glycol + phthalic acid (or anhydride); the version from glycerol + phthalic anhydride is cross-linked because glycerol is trifunctional. Used in paints and lacquers (alkyd resins), adhesives.

phthalic acid ethylene glycol glyptal repeat unit
Ortho-phthalate linkages (kinked); from glycerol the third –OH makes cross-links.
