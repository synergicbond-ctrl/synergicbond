12. Reactions of epoxides with nucleophiles; polymerisation

A wide range of nucleophiles open the strained ring. Each reaction adds a two-carbon fragment carrying an alcohol, which makes epoxides valuable building blocks.

Reactant:

Reagent: 1) C6H5MgBr; 2) H3O+

Product:

Grignard reagents extend the carbon chain by two carbons and give a primary alcohol: 2-phenylethanol. With a substituted epoxide the carbanion attacks the less substituted carbon.

Reactant:

Reagent: 1) LiAlH4; 2) H3O+

Product:

Hydride attacks the CH2 carbon, so the more substituted oxygen is retained: propan-2-ol (a Markovnikov-type alcohol).

Reactant:

Reagent: excess NH3

Product:

2-Aminoethanol. The product amine is itself nucleophilic and reacts with more ethylene oxide, so diethanolamine and triethanolamine form unless ammonia is in large excess.

  • H2O, H+ or HO−: 1,2-diols (ethylene glycol from ethylene oxide).
  • ROH, H+: 2-alkoxyethanols (for example 2-ethoxyethanol from ethanol).
  • HX: halohydrins (2-chloroethanol from HCl).
  • CN−: β-hydroxy nitriles.

Epoxides undergo ring-opening polymerisation. An anionic initiator (HO− or RO−) opens one ring, and the resulting alkoxide attacks the next monomer, again at the CH2 carbon. The product is poly(ethylene glycol), HO(CH2CH2O)nH. Cationic polymerisation with Lewis acids also occurs.

  1. Initiation: HO− + ethylene oxide → HOCH2CH2O−.
  2. Propagation: the alkoxide attacks another ethylene oxide molecule to give HO(CH2CH2O)2−, and so on.
  3. Termination: proton transfer from water or an alcohol gives HO(CH2CH2O)nH.
Formula. A chain HO(CH2CH2O)nH has n + 1 oxygen atoms: two are in terminal OH groups and n − 1 are ether oxygens.
