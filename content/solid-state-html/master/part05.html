Voids, Interstitial Sites and Diamond Geometry

10-part continuous textbook chapter

5.1 Meaning of a void

A void is an interstitial region enclosed by neighbouring packed particles. It is not an empty lattice point in the Bravais sense. The size and coordination of a void depend on the geometry of the surrounding spheres.

VoidNumber of surrounding spheresCoordination geometry
Triangular void3Planar triangle
Tetrahedral void4Tetrahedron
Octahedral void6Octahedron
Cubic void8Cube
Void count is tied to packed-particle count

For a close-packed array containing NNN host particles, there are NNN octahedral and 2N2N2N tetrahedral holes. This statement is independent of whether the close packing is HCP or CCP.

5.2 Formation of tetrahedral and octahedral holes

When a B-layer sphere covers a triangular depression in an A layer, the enclosed region between the three A spheres and one B sphere is a tetrahedral hole. When an upward triangular depression in one layer lies opposite a downward depression in the next, six spheres enclose an octahedral hole.

Ntet=2N,Noct=NN_{tet}=2N,\qquad N_{oct}=NNtet​=2N,Noct​=N
Derivation · Void count from one close-packed layer
triangular depressions=2N\text{triangular depressions}=2Ntriangular depressions=2N

A large triangular layer of NNN spheres has approximately 2N2N2N triangular depressions, boundary effects neglected.

N depressions are covered by the next layerN\text{ depressions are covered by the next layer}N depressions are covered by the next layer

Each covered depression produces one tetrahedral hole on that side.

N opposite pairs form octahedral holes across layersN\text{ opposite pairs form octahedral holes across layers}N opposite pairs form octahedral holes across layers

Layer stacking gives the final 2:1 tetrahedral-to-octahedral count.

Hole counting

Worked example

Mixed-hole occupancy

Anions are close packed. Cations occupy one-quarter of tetrahedral holes and one-half of octahedral holes. Determine the formula.

  1. Cation/anion =2(1/4)+(1/2)=1=2(1/4)+(1/2)=1=2(1/4)+(1/2)=1.

5.5 Sizes of interstitial holes

The largest sphere that fits a hole while touching all host spheres has a limiting radius. For host radius RRR and interstitial radius rrr, the classical values follow from regular polyhedral geometry.

HoleLimiting r/Rr/Rr/RDerivation result
Triangular0.1552/3−12/\sqrt3-12/3​−1
Tetrahedral0.2253/2−1\sqrt{3/2}-13/2​−1
Octahedral0.4142−1\sqrt2-12​−1
Cubic0.7323−1\sqrt3-13​−1

These are geometrical lower limits for a cation to maintain contact with surrounding anions without forcing them apart. They are not exact universal boundaries for real ionic compounds.

Distortion and covalency

Polarisation, directional covalent bonding, unequal compressibility and temperature can stabilise a coordination number outside the simple radius-ratio prediction.

5.6 Diamond structure

Diamond can be described as an FCC lattice with the basis (0,0,0)(0,0,0)(0,0,0) and (1/4,1/4,1/4)(1/4,1/4,1/4)(1/4,1/4,1/4). Equivalently, one carbon sublattice is displaced from another by one-quarter of a body diagonal. Each carbon is tetrahedrally bonded to four neighbours.

5.7 Interstitial sites in the conventional HCP cell

The conventional HCP prism contains six host atoms. The universal close-packing rule therefore gives twelve tetrahedral and six octahedral holes. These sites occur in alternating planes between the lower A, middle B and upper A layers.

Ntet=2(6)=12,Noct=6N_{tet}=2(6)=12,\qquad N_{oct}=6Ntet​=2(6)=12,Noct​=6

Layer-by-layer sketches often mark interstitial planes at fractional heights such as h/8h/8h/8, 2h/82h/82h/8, 3h/83h/83h/8 and so on. Those labels are a visual bookkeeping device; the invariant result is the total count and coordination of the holes.

Worked example

HCP hole occupancy

An HCP anion cell contains six anions. If one-third of its octahedral holes and one-quarter of its tetrahedral holes are occupied, how many cations belong to the cell?

  1. Octahedral cations =(1/3)(6)=2=(1/3)(6)=2=(1/3)(6)=2.
  2. Tetrahedral cations =(1/4)(12)=3=(1/4)(12)=3=(1/4)(12)=3.
  3. Total cations =5=5=5.
The cell contains 5 cations for 6 anions.

Part review

1

Close packing gives 2N2N2N tetrahedral and NNN octahedral holes for NNN host particles.

2

An FCC conventional cell contains 8 tetrahedral and 4 octahedral holes.

3

Hole occupancy directly determines stoichiometry.

4

Limiting hole ratios are 0.225 (tetrahedral), 0.414 (octahedral) and 0.732 (cubic).

5

Diamond is an open sp3sp^3sp3 network with 8 atoms per conventional cell and about 34% packing.
