Practice 56 L1 Hinsberg

An amine A dissolves in aqueous KOH after reaction with benzenesulphonyl chloride. A second amine B gives an insoluble product, and a third amine C gives no product. Classify A, B, C.

Show answer
  1. 1°: RNHSO2Ph has an acidic N–H and dissolves in KOH. 2°: R2NSO2Ph has no N–H and is insoluble. 3°: no stable sulphonamide.

A = 1°, B = 2°, C = 3°.

Practice 57 L1 Hinsberg

Write the product of C2H5NH2 with C6H5SO2Cl. Why is it soluble in alkali?

Show answer
  1. Acidification of the clear solution reprecipitates the sulphonamide.

N-Ethylbenzenesulphonamide, C6H5SO2NHC2H5. The N–H is acidic (the sulphonyl group stabilises the conjugate base), so KOH forms the soluble salt.

Practice 60 L1 HNO2

What are the products when ethylamine reacts with NaNO2/HCl at 273 K? What is observed with diethylamine and triethylamine?

Show answer
  1. 1° aliphatic diazonium ions are unstable; 2° amines give N-nitroso compounds; 3° amines only form salts (nitrite).

Ethylamine: ethanol + N2 (brisk effervescence). Diethylamine: N-nitrosodiethylamine (yellow oil). Triethylamine: forms a soluble salt, no gas, no oil.

Practice 61 L1 Alkylation

What is the final product when methylamine reacts with excess CH3I, and how many moles of CH3I are consumed?

Show answer
  1. CH3NH2 → (CH3)2NH → (CH3)3N → (CH3)4N+; each step adds one CH3.

Tetramethylammonium iodide, (CH3)4N+I−; 3 mol CH3I per mole of CH3NH2.

Practice 62 L1 Hofmann elimination

Predict the products on heating tetramethyl- and ethyltrimethyl-ammonium hydroxide separately.

Show answer
  1. Hofmann elimination requires a β-hydrogen. The ethyl group has β-H (the CH3); the methyl groups do not.

(CH3)4N+OH−: methanol + trimethylamine (no β-hydrogen; SN2 by OH− on a methyl). (CH3)3N+CH2CH3OH−: ethene + trimethylamine + water.

Practice 63 L1 Acylation

Write the product of aniline with acetic anhydride, and give two reasons for carrying out this reaction before nitrating aniline.

acetanilide
Show answer
  1. The amide N lone pair is shared with C=O, so the ring is less activated and the group is not protonated in HNO3/H2SO4.

Acetanilide (C6H5NHCOCH3). It prevents oxidation of the amino group and avoids the anilinium ion (meta product); it also moderates the activating power and favours para.

Practice 64 L1 Schotten–Baumann

What is formed when aniline is shaken with benzoyl chloride and aqueous NaOH?

benzanilide
Show answer
  1. This is the Schotten–Baumann benzoylation.

Benzanilide (N-phenylbenzamide), C6H5CONHC6H5; NaOH neutralises the HCl formed so that the amine is not lost as salt.

Practice 67 L1 Hinsberg

Which class of amine does not react with benzenesulphonyl chloride? What do you do to separate it from the others?

Show answer
  1. Acid dissolves the 3° amine as its salt but does not dissolve the 2° sulphonamide; base dissolves the 1° sulphonamide.

Tertiary amines. After the reaction with PhSO2Cl/KOH the 3° amine remains as an insoluble oil that dissolves on adding acid, which separates it from the insoluble 2° sulphonamide (which stays insoluble in acid).

methyl isothiocyanate

Practice 70 L1 Tests

Name a reagent that distinguishes between (a) ethylamine and diethylamine, (b) aniline and nitrobenzene, (c) aniline and benzylamine.

Show answer
  1. Each test uses the property that differs: N–H count, basicity, or aliphatic/aromatic diazonium stability.

(a) CHCl3/KOH (carbylamine) or HNO2; (b) dilute HCl (aniline dissolves); (c) NaNO2/HCl at 273 K followed by β-naphthol: aniline gives a red azo dye, benzylamine evolves N2.

Practice 73 L1 Salt formation

Why are amine hydrochlorides soluble in water but insoluble in diethyl ether? How can a water-insoluble amine be recovered from its hydrochloride?

Show answer
  1. RNH3+Cl− + NaOH → RNH2 + NaCl + H2O.

The salts are ionic and hydrate well; ether is non-polar. Adding NaOH liberates the free amine, which separates as an oily layer or can be extracted into ether.

Practice 74 L1 Aniline test

How can aniline be detected by bromine water? Give the formula and the molar mass of the product.

2,4,6-tribromoaniline
Show answer
  1. The –NH2 group activates o- and p-positions strongly; no catalyst is needed.

A white precipitate of 2,4,6-tribromoaniline (C6H4Br3N, M = 330) forms at once.
