14.3 Effect of ring substituents

Substituents change the acidity through the stability of the phenoxide ion.

  • Electron-withdrawing groups (NO2, CN, CHO, COR, X) at the ortho and para positions increase acidity because they delocalise the negative charge by −M (and −I). At the meta position only −I operates (the charge on the ring cannot be placed on the meta carbon), so the effect is smaller.
  • Electron-donating groups (CH3, OCH3 at para, NH2) decrease acidity (+I, +M intensify the negative charge).

Key idea Order of acidity of nitrophenols: p ≳ o > m > phenol. The ortho isomer should be strongest on −I grounds (nearest), but its intramolecular H-bond stabilises the un-ionised form and so decreases acidity; the net result is that p-nitrophenol (7.15) and o-nitrophenol (7.23) are almost equal and m-nitrophenol (8.40) is clearly weaker. Methyl-substituted phenols are all weaker than phenol; a +M or +I group lowers acidity, a −M or −I group raises it.
Trap Steric inhibition of resonance. In 3,5-dimethyl-4-nitrophenol the two methyl groups ortho to the NO2 twist the nitro group out of the plane of the ring, so it cannot withdraw by −M and the pKa is 8.25 compared with 7.15 for p-nitrophenol. When a question puts bulky groups ortho to a −M group, suspect steric inhibition of resonance: the −M group is switched off.
Salicylic acid. The carboxyl group of salicylic acid is much stronger (pKa1 2.97) than that of benzoic acid (4.20): the carboxylate is stabilised by an intramolecular H-bond from the o-OH. The phenolic OH, conversely, is much weaker (pKa2 13.6) because the dianion is destabilised (negative charges close together; the OH’s H-bond is already in use). p-Hydroxybenzoic acid (4.54) is less acidic than benzoic acid (+M of OH dominates at para).
Example 38 L1 Foundation

Order of acidity

Arrange in increasing acidity: phenol, p-cresol, p-nitrophenol, m-nitrophenol, 2,4,6-trinitrophenol, ethanol.

Show solution
  1. Ethanol (16) is weakest. Among phenols: p-cresol (10.3) < phenol (10.0) < m-nitrophenol (8.4) < p-nitrophenol (7.15) < picric acid (0.4).
  2. Order (increasing): ethanol < p-cresol < phenol < m-nitrophenol < p-nitrophenol < 2,4,6-trinitrophenol.
Example 39 L2 Main

NaHCO3 test

Which of these dissolve in aqueous NaOH, and which of them liberate CO2 from aqueous NaHCO3: phenol, picric acid, salicylic acid, 2,4-dinitrophenol, benzyl alcohol, p-nitrophenol?

Show solution
  1. Dissolve in NaOH: all except benzyl alcohol: phenol, picric acid, salicylic acid, 2,4-dinitrophenol, p-nitrophenol.
  2. CO2 from NaHCO3 requires a pKa below that of H2CO3 (6.35): picric acid (0.4) ✓, 2,4-dinitrophenol (4.1) ✓, salicylic acid (COOH, 2.97) ✓. Phenol (10) ✗; p-nitrophenol (7.15) ✗, since 7.15 > 6.35.
  3. Answer: NaOH: five; NaHCO3: picric acid, 2,4-dinitrophenol, salicylic acid.
Example 40 L2 Main

Separation scheme

A mixture of benzoic acid, p-cresol, naphthalene and aniline is dissolved in ether. Outline the separation using extractions.

Show solution
  1. Extract with dilute HCl: the aniline (as anilinium chloride) goes into the aqueous layer; regenerate with NaOH.
  2. Extract the ether layer with aqueous NaHCO3: benzoic acid (as sodium benzoate; CO2 evolved) goes into the aqueous layer; acidify to recover. p-Cresol is too weak an acid (10.3) to dissolve in NaHCO3.
  3. Extract with aqueous NaOH: p-cresol (as sodium p-cresolate) goes into the aqueous layer; acidify (or bubble CO2) to recover.
  4. The ether layer now contains only naphthalene (evaporate).
Example 41 L3 Advanced

Steric inhibition and pKa

Explain why 3,5-dimethyl-4-nitrophenol (pKa 8.25) is a weaker acid than 4-nitrophenol (pKa 7.15), although methyl groups at the meta positions are far from the OH.

Show solution
  1. In 4-nitrophenol the NO2 group is coplanar with the ring and withdraws charge from the phenoxide oxygen by −M (and −I): pKa 7.15.
  2. In 3,5-dimethyl-4-nitrophenol the two methyl groups flank the NO2 group and force it to rotate out of the ring plane. Its p-orbitals can no longer overlap with the ring π system, so the −M effect is largely lost and only −I remains. The phenoxide is less stabilised and the acidity falls (pKa 8.25).
  3. This is steric inhibition of resonance: the methyls act through geometry, not through their +I effect.
