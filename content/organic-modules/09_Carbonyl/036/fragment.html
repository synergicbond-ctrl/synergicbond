31. The Michael addition

The conjugate (1,4-)addition of a stabilised carbanion (the Michael donor) to an α,β-unsaturated carbonyl compound (the Michael acceptor) forms a new C–C bond. Donors: malonic ester, ethyl acetoacetate, β-diketones, nitroalkanes, cyanoacetates, enamines, enolates. Acceptors: enones, enals, acrylates, acrylonitrile, nitroalkenes, vinyl sulfones.

  • Step 1: a base (NaOEt, piperidine) generates the stabilised enolate from the donor.
  • Step 2: the enolate attacks the β-carbon of the acceptor (the electrophilic site, δ+), giving a new enolate (stabilised by the acceptor group).
  • Step 3: protonation of this enolate gives the 1,5-dicarbonyl (or 1,5-functionalised) product.

Shortcut Quick identification: in the product, the new C–C bond is between the donor carbon and the β-carbon of the acceptor; the two carbonyl groups end up 1,5 to each other (counting carbons C=O...C...C...C...C=O when the donor was a ketone enolate). 1,5-Dicarbonyl compounds can cyclise by intramolecular aldol to six-membered rings: this is the Robinson annulation.
Trap A thermodynamic point: Michael addition is reversible (retro-Michael) at high temperature. 1,2-addition (carbonyl attack) is faster but reversible for stabilised carbanions; 1,4-addition gives the more stable product. Hard, strongly basic nucleophiles (RLi, RMgX with enals, LiAlH4) prefer 1,2; soft ones (cuprates, malonate, thiolates, amines, cyanide) prefer 1,4.
